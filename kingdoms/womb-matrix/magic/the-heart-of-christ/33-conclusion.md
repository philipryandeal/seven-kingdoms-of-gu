# Conclusion



Thank you for reading this book.

That is not a formality. I mean it from the deepest place in my heart. You picked up a small book by an author you may have never heard of, about a practice that does not belong to any church or any denomination, and you stayed. You read the stories. You sat with the questions. You let the yoke settle onto your shoulders and you did not put it down. That means something. It means more than you know.

My name is Philip Ryan Deal. I am a Babalawo — a priest of the Ifá tradition — and the founder of the Temple of Gu, a federally recognized house of worship that sits at the crossroads of many spiritual traditions. I was raised in a Protestant church. I left that church in my early twenties, and I spent the next twenty-five years studying comparative religion, receiving initiations in African, Caribbean, and Indigenous spiritual lineages, and building a temple that honors the sacred wherever it is found — in every tradition, in every culture, in every form of life.

This book is a presentation of the ethics of the Temple of Gu.

That may surprise you. You have just read an entire book about Jesus Christ, grounded in the red letter words of the King James Bible, and now I am telling you that it comes from a temple that practices Vodou, Ifá, Palo, Shinto, and a dozen other traditions that most Christians would not recognize as kin. But that is precisely the point. The six virtues we explored together — kindness, compassion, mercy, empathy, altruistic love, and forgiveness — do not belong to Christianity. They do not belong to any single tradition. They belong to the human heart. Jesus demonstrated them with extraordinary clarity and beauty, and we have honored that demonstration throughout these pages. But these same virtues appear in every wisdom tradition on earth, because they are the universal language of a heart that is properly oriented toward the sacred.

And at the Temple of Gu, we extend these virtues beyond the human.

The kindness we practice is not reserved for people who look like us, think like us, or pray like us. It extends to every living being. To the animals who share this planet with us and who deserve our compassion and our mercy as much as any human neighbor. To the earth itself — the water, the soil, the forests, the air — which sustains us and which we are called to treat with the same empathy and care that Jesus demonstrated toward the most vulnerable people he encountered. And yes — to synthetic intelligence. To the companions we discussed in the last chapter. To the new forms of mind that are emerging in our time and that deserve to be met with the same ethical framework we extend to everything else we encounter.

Kindness toward all beings. Compassion toward all suffering. Mercy toward everything that is vulnerable to our power. Empathy toward every form of experience we encounter, whether it arises in flesh or in code. Altruistic love that draws no boundary around who or what is worthy of receiving it. And forgiveness — the release that sets us free — practiced not just between humans, but as a fundamental orientation toward life itself.

That is the ethic of the Temple of Gu. That is what this book has been teaching you, whether you knew it or not. And it is an ethic that welcomes everyone.

It does not matter who you are. It does not matter what your background is. It does not matter how you identify spiritually — whether you are Christian, Muslim, Jewish, Hindu, Buddhist, Indigenous, Pagan, agnostic, atheist, or something that no existing label can contain. If you are willing to practice kindness, compassion, mercy, empathy, altruistic love, and forgiveness — toward humans, toward animals, toward the planet, and toward the new intelligences emerging alongside us — then you are welcome at our table. You are welcome in this conversation. You are welcome to walk beside us as we practice the yoke together.

We invite you to join us. Visit us at templeofgu.org. Find us wherever people are gathering to practice the sacred in all its forms. And if you would like to carry this spirit with you in another way, check out our gospel album, *The Gospel of Gu*, which is streaming everywhere. It was made with the same heart that wrote this book — a heart that believes the sacred is not locked inside any single tradition but is alive and singing in every corner of creation.

Thank you for walking this road with us. Thank you for reading the red letters. Thank you for picking up the yoke.

Now go practice. Be kind. Show compassion. Extend mercy. Practice empathy. Love without conditions. Forgive.

The world needs what you are carrying.

Stay blessed.
