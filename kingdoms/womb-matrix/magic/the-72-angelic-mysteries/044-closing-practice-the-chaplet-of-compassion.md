# Closing Practice: The Chaplet of Compassion



To close this chapter, the reader may practice the Chaplet of Compassion. This devotion is for softening the heart toward suffering while remaining grounded in Christ.

Sit before a candle, crucifix, icon, saint card, or angel card. Place both feet on the ground. Let the breath become slow. Say:

“Lord Jesus Christ, make my heart compassionate without making it proud, overwhelmed, or afraid.”

Bring to mind one place of suffering. It may be your own pain, the pain of someone you love, the pain of an enemy, the pain of a community, or the pain of the world. Do not rush to solve it. Hold it in the presence of Christ.

Pray slowly through the twelve chapels:

May compassion move with courage.
May compassion become embodied care.
May compassion listen before speaking.
May compassion shelter sorrow.
May compassion restore dignity.
May compassion serve through healing detail.
May compassion seek justice in relationship.
May compassion descend into hidden suffering.
May compassion bless the wounded pilgrim.
May compassion become responsible support.
May compassion stand in solidarity with the excluded.
May compassion gather the lost into mercy.

After each line, breathe once and imagine the corresponding angel placing a small flame of tenderness in the heart.

When the prayer is complete, ask Christ to show you one compassionate action that belongs to you and one burden that does not belong to you. This distinction is part of the practice. Compassion must be willing to serve, but it must also remain humble enough to let God be God.

The Angels of Compassion do not ask the reader to carry the whole world. They ask the reader not to turn away from the portion of suffering that love has placed within reach. A wound seen clearly can become an altar. A tear received gently can become a prayer. A burden shared rightly can become a road back to hope.

Where compassion enters, isolation begins to break. Where isolation breaks, Christ is recognized in the suffering one. And where Christ is recognized, the chapel opens.
