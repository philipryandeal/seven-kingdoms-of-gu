# Closing Practice: The Chaplet of Empathy



To close this chapter, the reader may practice the Chaplet of Empathy. This devotion is for cultivating holy perception, humble listening, and grounded emotional understanding.

Sit before a candle, crucifix, icon, saint card, or angel card. Place both feet on the ground. Let the breath become slow. Say:

“Lord Jesus Christ, teach me to see others without possession, projection, or contempt.”

Bring to mind one person you are trying to understand. This may be someone you love, someone who confuses you, someone who hurt you, someone you have judged, or someone whose experience is very different from your own. Imagine them standing in the light of Christ. Do not force an interpretation. Do not invade their mystery. Simply hold them reverently before God.

Pray slowly through the twelve chapels:

May empathy perceive the heart beneath the fire.
May empathy listen through the body and senses.
May empathy ask before assuming.
May empathy hear the echo of memory.
May empathy recognize dignity and the need to be seen.
May empathy notice the small signs of need.
May empathy mirror truthfully in relationship.
May empathy honor hidden depths without intrusion.
May empathy understand the pilgrim’s road.
May empathy recognize burden and responsibility.
May empathy make room for difference.
May empathy feel with creation without drowning.

After each line, breathe once and imagine the corresponding angel placing a clear mirror of light in the heart.

When the prayer is complete, ask Christ three questions. First: “What am I projecting?” Second: “What am I refusing to see?” Third: “What is not mine to know?” These questions protect empathy from pride. They remind the soul that another person’s inner life is sacred ground.

If an insight comes, hold it lightly. Empathy should make you more humble, not more certain. If action is needed, let it be simple and respectful: ask, listen, apologize, adjust, make room, or release the need to understand everything.

The Angels of Empathy do not ask the reader to become a mind-reader. They ask the reader to become a more faithful witness. They do not ask the reader to absorb every emotion. They ask the reader to love with perception and boundaries. They do not ask the reader to erase difference. They ask the reader to approach difference as holy mystery.

Where empathy enters, loneliness begins to break. Where loneliness breaks, the person is no longer reduced to a role, wound, label, or mistake. And where the person is seen more truly, the chapel opens.
