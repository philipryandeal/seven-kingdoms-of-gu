# The Rite of the Sixfold Heart: A Grand Angelic Ritual for Invoking the Six Virtues



The Rite of the Sixfold Heart is the crown practice of this manual. It gathers the twelve apostolic chapels, the seventy-two angelic virtues, and the contemplative heart of Christ into one ritual pattern. It is not a rite of fortune-telling. It is not a rite of command. It is not a rite of spectacle. It is a devotional ritual of alignment, purification, and embodied Christian virtue.

The purpose of the rite is simple: to invite the six virtues of Kindness, Compassion, Mercy, Empathy, Altruistic Love, and Forgiveness to awaken in the practitioner through the grace of Christ and the witness of the apostolic chapels.

The cards are used here as prayer doors. The saints open the temple. The angels reveal the movement of grace. Christ remains the center. The practitioner does not ask, “What will happen to me?” The practitioner asks, “What virtue is Christ asking me to embody, and how shall I live it?”

This is the heart of the entire oracle.

The Saint cards represent the chapels, the sacred environments through which the soul approaches Christ. The Angel cards represent the virtues, the actions of grace moving through those chapels. When they are placed together in ritual, the practitioner creates a symbolic temple: Christ at the center, the six virtues as the inner flame, and the twelve apostolic chapels as the outer sanctuary.

The Rite of the Sixfold Heart may be performed when the practitioner seeks moral clarity, spiritual renewal, reconciliation, emotional healing, preparation for service, consecration of the deck, guidance before an important decision, or restoration after conflict. It may also be performed seasonally, monthly, during Lent, Advent, Eastertide, on a personal feast day, or whenever the heart feels divided and must be returned to Christ.

This rite is intentionally simple. It does not require elaborate ceremonial equipment. A candle, the deck, a clean surface, and a sincere heart are enough. The rite may be performed at a home altar, a desk, a chapel, a quiet room, or any place where the practitioner can pray with attention. The beauty of the rite comes not from complexity, but from sincerity, order, and reverence.

The practitioner is not summoning angels as servants. The practitioner is entering a contemplative relationship with the virtues those angels carry. The angels are approached as ministers of grace, messengers of divine virtue, and companions in prayer. The practitioner does not command them. The practitioner asks to be instructed, corrected, softened, strengthened, and transformed.

**The Sixfold Heart**

Before performing the rite, it is helpful to understand the six virtues as one movement of Christic love.

Kindness opens the heart.
Compassion moves toward suffering.
Mercy restores what has failed.
Empathy sees the other truthfully.
Altruistic Love gives without possession.
Forgiveness releases hatred into God.

These six virtues are not separate decorations placed upon the soul. They are six movements of one redeemed heart. A heart that is kind but not compassionate may remain polite at a distance. A heart that is compassionate but not merciful may feel suffering without knowing how to restore. A heart that is merciful but not empathetic may pardon without understanding. A heart that is empathetic but not altruistic may perceive others without giving itself in love. A heart that loves altruistically but cannot forgive may eventually become bound by resentment. A heart that forgives but lacks kindness may become severe and abstract.

The six virtues complete one another.

Together, they form the Sixfold Heart: a contemplative image of the Heart of Christ living in the practitioner. This is the heart that opens, approaches, restores, understands, gives, and releases. It is not sentimental. It is not weak. It is not vague. It is a disciplined heart, a prayerful heart, a heart rooted in God.

The goal of the rite is not to feel holy for an hour. The goal is to receive a pattern of life. The cards show the practitioner how the six virtues wish to become embodied now, in this season, in this body, in these relationships, in these responsibilities, and in this particular soul.

**When to Perform the Rite**

The full Rite of the Sixfold Heart may be performed whenever the practitioner desires a complete spiritual reading and devotional invocation of the six virtues. It is especially suitable for:

A new beginning.
A birthday or anniversary.
The beginning of a liturgical season.
A time of conflict or reconciliation.
A period of discernment.
A retreat day.
A monthly spiritual review.
The consecration of the oracle deck.
The beginning or end of a forty-day practice.
A moment when the heart feels hard, confused, wounded, or divided.

The rite may be performed alone or with a small prayer circle. If performed in a group, one person may serve as reader or ritual guide, while the others pray and journal. Each participant may draw their own six angel cards, or the group may draw one Sixfold Heart Mandala for the collective.

If the rite is performed for another person, the practitioner should remember that this is devotional guidance, not coercion. The cards do not remove another person’s freedom. They do not replace conscience, therapy, pastoral care, medical care, or ordinary wisdom. They are instruments of contemplation. They help the soul listen.

**Materials**

For the full rite, gather:

The complete oracle deck.
A white candle.
A crucifix, Sacred Heart image, icon of Christ, or open Gospel.
A clean cloth or altar surface.
A bowl of clean water.
A small dish of salt or blessed salt.
A rosary, prayer rope, or simple beads, if desired.
A journal and pen.
Optional incense.
Optional flowers.
Optional images of Mary, the apostles, or guardian angels.

The only essential materials are the deck, a candle, and a sincere heart. The other items help the body enter prayer.

The bowl of water represents receptivity, cleansing, and the living mercy of God. The salt represents preservation, truth, and the purification of intention. The candle represents Christ as the light at the center of the temple. The deck represents the visible pattern of the chapels and virtues. The journal represents embodiment, because the rite must become action.

**Preparing the Deck**

Separate the deck into seven groups:

The twelve Saint cards.
The twelve Angels of Kindness.
The twelve Angels of Compassion.
The twelve Angels of Mercy.
The twelve Angels of Empathy.
The twelve Angels of Altruistic Love.
The twelve Angels of Forgiveness.

Place the twelve Saint cards in order, beginning with Saint John of Aries and continuing through Saint Matthias of Pisces. These will form the outer circle of the rite.

Shuffle each virtue group separately. The practitioner will draw one card from each virtue group during the rite. These six cards will form the inner mandala.

This means the final reading will contain six angel cards:

One Angel of Kindness.
One Angel of Compassion.
One Angel of Mercy.
One Angel of Empathy.
One Angel of Altruistic Love.
One Angel of Forgiveness.

Together, these six cards reveal the practitioner’s Sixfold Heart Mandala.

**The Altar Layout**

Place the candle at the center of the altar.

Behind or beneath the candle, place the crucifix, Sacred Heart image, icon of Christ, or open Gospel. This is the throne of the rite. Christ is the center.

Place the bowl of water to the left of the candle.

Place the salt to the right of the candle.

Place the journal below the candle, nearest to you.

Now arrange the twelve Saint cards in a circle around the central candle, moving clockwise:

Saint John — Aries.
Saint Andrew — Taurus.
Saint Thomas — Gemini.
Saint James the Less— Cancer.
Saint Peter — Leo.
Saint Philip — Virgo.
Saint Bartholomew / Nathanael — Libra.
Saint Jude Thaddaeus — Scorpio.
Saint James the Greater — Sagittarius.
Saint Matthew — Capricorn.
Saint Simon the Zealot — Aquarius.
Saint Matthias — Pisces.

This outer circle is the apostolic temple.

The six angel cards will later be placed between the center candle and the outer circle, forming a six-petaled rose or hexagon around Christ.

If space is limited, the practitioner may place the twelve Saint cards in a straight line or arc instead of a circle. The symbolic meaning remains the same.

**The Full Rite**

**1. Cleansing and Centering**

Wash your hands before beginning. This does not need to be elaborate. The washing is a simple sign that the body is entering prayer.

Stand or sit before the altar.

Take three slow breaths.

Touch the salt lightly and say:

May my intention be purified in truth.

Touch the water lightly and say:

May my heart be softened by grace.

Make the Sign of the Cross and say:

In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.

Sit in silence for a few moments. Let the body arrive. Let the room become a chapel. Let the mind settle into the presence of Christ.

**2. Opening Prayer to Christ**

Pray:

Lord Jesus Christ, Heart of divine love,
open the chapel within me.

Stand at the center of this rite
as Light, Teacher, Savior, and Friend.

Let the holy apostles stand as witnesses.
Let the angels of virtue draw near as ministers of grace.

Awaken what is sleeping.
Heal what is wounded.
Correct what is distorted.
Strengthen what is weak.
Soften what is hard.
Guide what is confused.

Let this rite not end in imagination only,
but in thought, word, action, relationship, and service.

May every card become a door to prayer.
May every prayer become a seed of virtue.
May every virtue become a living act of love.

Amen.

Pray the Our Father.

Our Father, who art in heaven,
hallowed be Thy name.
Thy kingdom come.
Thy will be done
on earth as it is in heaven.
Give us this day our daily bread,
and forgive us our trespasses,
as we forgive those who trespass against us.
And lead us not into temptation,
but deliver us from evil.
Amen.

If it belongs to your practice, pray the Hail Mary.

Hail Mary, full of grace,
the Lord is with thee.
Blessed art thou among women,
and blessed is the fruit of thy womb, Jesus.
Holy Mary, Mother of God,
pray for us sinners,
now and at the hour of our death.
Amen.

Pray the Glory Be.

Glory be to the Father,
and to the Son,
and to the Holy Spirit,
as it was in the beginning,
is now, and ever shall be,
world without end.
Amen.

**3. Opening the Twelve Apostolic Chapels**

Now move clockwise through the twelve Saint cards. As you touch or gesture toward each card, say the invocation.

Saint John, Chapel of Aries,
open the gate of courageous love.

Saint Andrew, Chapel of Taurus,
open the gate of faithful care.

Saint Thomas, Chapel of Gemini,
open the gate of truthful seeking.

Saint James the Less, Chapel of Cancer,
open the gate of humble shelter.

Saint Peter, Chapel of Leo,
open the gate of restored dignity.

Saint Philip, Chapel of Virgo,
open the gate of holy service.

Saint Bartholomew, Nathanael, Chapel of Libra,
open the gate of sincere relationship.

Saint Jude Thaddaeus, Chapel of Scorpio,
open the gate of difficult hope.

Saint James the Greater, Chapel of Sagittarius,
open the gate of holy pilgrimage.

Saint Matthew, Chapel of Capricorn,
open the gate of accountable vocation.

Saint Simon the Zealot, Chapel of Aquarius,
open the gate of purified zeal.

Saint Matthias, Chapel of Pisces,
open the gate of restored belonging.

Pause.

Look at the full circle of cards.

Say:

Holy apostles and saints of Christ,
stand as witnesses around this prayer.

Let these twelve chapels become one temple.
Let this temple open in my heart.

Amen.

**4. Invocation of the Six Virtues**

Place both hands over the heart.

Say:

Kindness, open my heart.
Compassion, move my heart toward suffering.
Mercy, restore what has failed.
Empathy, teach me to see truthfully.
Altruistic Love, teach me to give freely.
Forgiveness, release hatred into God.

Lord Jesus Christ,
make these six virtues one living flame within me.

Amen.

Sit in silence for a few breaths.

**5. Drawing the Six Angel Cards**

Take the shuffled stack of the Angels of Kindness.

Hold it before the candle and say:

Angels of Kindness,
ministers of the gentle action of Christ,
reveal how kindness wishes to live in me now.

Draw one card.

Place it near the center candle.

Take the shuffled stack of the Angels of Compassion.

Say:

Angels of Compassion,
ministers of Christ’s nearness to suffering,
reveal how compassion wishes to move through me now.

Draw one card.

Place it beside the first card, beginning the sixfold pattern.

Take the shuffled stack of the Angels of Mercy.

Say:

Angels of Mercy,
ministers of restoration and holy return,
reveal how mercy wishes to heal, correct, and restore me now.

Draw one card.

Place it in the pattern.

Take the shuffled stack of the Angels of Empathy.

Say:

Angels of Empathy,
ministers of holy perception and reverent understanding,
reveal how empathy wishes to clarify my seeing now.

Draw one card.

Place it in the pattern.

Take the shuffled stack of the Angels of Altruistic Love.

Say:

Angels of Altruistic Love,
ministers of charity and self-giving grace,
reveal how love wishes to pour through me without possession now.

Draw one card.

Place it in the pattern.

Take the shuffled stack of the Angels of Forgiveness.

Say:

Angels of Forgiveness,
ministers of release, truth, and freedom in Christ,
reveal what must be forgiven, released, or returned to God now.

Draw one card.

Place it in the pattern.

You have now formed the Sixfold Heart Mandala.

Pause and look at the six cards.

Do not interpret too quickly. Let the pattern appear. Notice which apostolic chapels have appeared. Notice whether certain signs, saints, elements, or themes repeat. Notice which virtue feels easy to receive and which one creates resistance. Resistance is often part of the revelation.

**6. Reading the Sixfold Heart Mandala**

Read each card slowly using the corresponding chapter in this manual.

Begin with Kindness.

Ask:

How is Christ asking me to practice kindness?
Through which chapel is kindness appearing?
What action does this angel request?

Write one sentence in the journal.

Then read Compassion.

Ask:

Where is suffering asking for my presence?
How can I move toward pain without pride, fear, or collapse?
What does this angel reveal?

Write one sentence.

Then read Mercy.

Ask:

What needs restoration?
Where must I receive mercy?
Where must I show mercy?
What does this angel reveal?

Write one sentence.

Then read Empathy.

Ask:

What am I being asked to see more truthfully?
Where am I projecting, assuming, or refusing to understand?
What does this angel reveal?

Write one sentence.

Then read Altruistic Love.

Ask:

Where is love asking me to give freely?
Where must love be purified of reward, control, or possession?
What does this angel reveal?

Write one sentence.

Then read Forgiveness.

Ask:

What hatred, resentment, debt, or wound must be placed into Christ?
Where is forgiveness possible now?
Where must I begin only with willingness?
What does this angel reveal?

Write one sentence.

When all six readings are complete, read the six journal sentences aloud. These sentences are the first form of your Sixfold Heart Rule.

**7. The Sixfold Invocation**

Now touch or gesture toward each drawn angel card in order.

At the Angel of Kindness, say:

Lord Jesus Christ,
through this Angel of Kindness,
make my presence gentle, courageous, and useful.

Teach me to open the hand,
soften the face,
bless through speech,
and reduce unnecessary harm.

Let kindness become real in my body,
my relationships,
my work,
and my next action.

Amen.

At the Angel of Compassion, say:

Lord Jesus Christ,
through this Angel of Compassion,
move my heart toward suffering without pride or fear.

Teach me to see pain clearly,
to remain grounded,
to serve what is mine to serve,
and to release what belongs only to You.

Let compassion become bread, presence, prayer, protection,
and wise action.

Amen.

At the Angel of Mercy, say:

Lord Jesus Christ,
through this Angel of Mercy,
restore what has failed.

Where I have sinned, lead me to repentance.
Where I have been wounded, lead me to healing.
Where judgment has become despair, open the door of return.

Make me merciful without becoming false.
Make me truthful without becoming cruel.

Amen.

At the Angel of Empathy, say:

Lord Jesus Christ,
through this Angel of Empathy,
teach me to see others without projection, possession, or contempt.

Let me listen before interpreting.
Let me ask before assuming.
Let me honor the mystery of every soul.

Keep me grounded in myself
while making my heart spacious enough to understand.

Amen.

At the Angel of Altruistic Love, say:

Lord Jesus Christ,
through this Angel of Altruistic Love,
teach me to love freely, wisely, and without reward.

Purify my giving of control.
Purify my sacrifice of resentment.
Purify my devotion of vanity.

Let my love seek the good of the other in God,
without losing the life You have entrusted to me.

Amen.

At the Angel of Forgiveness, say:

Lord Jesus Christ,
through this Angel of Forgiveness,
release hatred from my heart and return my life to grace.

Teach me forgiveness without denial,
release without false peace,
truth without vengeance,
and boundaries without bitterness.

Let every wound be placed into Your wounds.
Let every debt be entrusted to Your justice and mercy.

Amen.

Now place both hands over the heart and say:

Kindness.
Compassion.
Mercy.
Empathy.
Altruistic Love.
Forgiveness.

Six virtues.
One heart.
One Christ.

Let the Sixfold Heart awaken in me.
Let it guide my thoughts.
Let it govern my speech.
Let it shape my actions.
Let it heal my relationships.
Let it bless my work.
Let it return me to love.

Amen.

Sit in silence.

Allow the candle to burn quietly.

**8. The Vow of Embodiment**

The rite must become life. Without embodiment, the cards remain beautiful images. With embodiment, they become spiritual practice.

Open the journal.

Write the following six sentences, completing each one as specifically as possible:

Today I will practice Kindness by…

Today I will practice Compassion by…

Today I will practice Mercy by…

Today I will practice Empathy by…

Today I will practice Altruistic Love by…

Today I will practice Forgiveness by…

If the rite is being performed for a longer cycle, write:

This week I will practice…

Or:

This month I will practice…

The actions should be concrete and possible. Do not write vague promises such as “I will be more loving.” Write something the body can actually do.

For example:

Today I will practice Kindness by answering one difficult message without contempt.

Today I will practice Compassion by bringing food to someone who is tired.

Today I will practice Mercy by letting myself begin again after a mistake.

Today I will practice Empathy by listening without interrupting.

Today I will practice Altruistic Love by offering help without needing credit.

Today I will practice Forgiveness by praying for release from one resentment.

Read the six vows aloud.

Then say:

Lord Jesus Christ,
receive these vows not as performance,
but as seeds.

Let them be small enough to live,
clear enough to practice,
and true enough to change me.

Amen.

**9. The Prayer of the Sixfold Heart**

Pray:

Heart of Christ, open in me as Kindness.
Let me become gentle without becoming weak.
Let me become clear without becoming cruel.

Heart of Christ, move in me as Compassion.
Let me come near to suffering without pride.
Let me serve without pretending to be Savior.

Heart of Christ, restore in me as Mercy.
Let failure become return.
Let judgment become healing.
Let shame become repentance and new life.

Heart of Christ, see in me as Empathy.
Let me perceive the other without possession.
Let me understand without invading.
Let me honor what belongs to God alone.

Heart of Christ, give in me as Altruistic Love.
Let my love become free of control.
Let my gifts become clean.
Let my service become joyful, truthful, and wise.

Heart of Christ, release in me as Forgiveness.
Let hatred lose its throne.
Let memory be healed without being erased.
Let justice and mercy meet in Your wounds.

Six flames of one love,
six angels of one heart,
six virtues of one Christ,
live in me now.

Amen.

**10. Sealing the Rite**

Touch the water.

Say:

May grace soften what is hard.

Touch the salt.

Say:

May truth preserve what is holy.

Touch the heart.

Say:

May these virtues become embodied.

Touch the journal.

Say:

May prayer become action.

Look at the central candle.

Say:

Christ at the center.
The apostles around me.
The angels within the work of virtue.
The heart made ready for love.

May all that has opened here
return with more harmony than it left.

Amen.

Pray the Glory Be.

Glory be to the Father,
and to the Son,
and to the Holy Spirit,
as it was in the beginning,
is now, and ever shall be,
world without end.
Amen.

Make the Sign of the Cross.

In the name of the Father,
and of the Son,
and of the Holy Spirit.
Amen.

Extinguish the candle.

Gather the cards slowly. If possible, keep the six drawn angel cards on the altar for the rest of the day, the week, or the month. Let them remain as witnesses to the vows you have made.

**After the Rite**

After the rite is complete, do not rush immediately into distraction. Drink water. Sit quietly. Walk slowly. Let the body return gently.

Within twenty-four hours, perform at least one of the six vows of embodiment. This is important. The rite must cross from prayer into life. Even one small action completes the circuit.

The practitioner may also journal on the following questions:

Which virtue felt easiest to receive?
Which virtue felt most difficult?
Which drawn angel surprised me?
Which apostolic chapel appeared most strongly?
What action is Christ asking of me now?
What must I stop doing in order to live these virtues?
What must I begin doing?
Where did I feel resistance?
Where did I feel consolation?
How can these six cards become a rule of life for the next season?

The six drawn angel cards may be copied into a journal under the heading:

My Sixfold Heart Mandala

Then list:

Kindness appeared through…
Compassion appeared through…
Mercy appeared through…
Empathy appeared through…
Altruistic Love appeared through…
Forgiveness appeared through…

This mandala may become a personal prayer map.

**Interpreting Repeated Chapels**

Sometimes the six drawn cards will include repeated signs or chapels. For example, the practitioner may draw Kindness in Virgo, Compassion in Virgo, and Forgiveness in Virgo. This repetition should be taken seriously. It means that the Chapel of Saint Philip is strongly active in the current season. The virtues are asking to be embodied through humble service, careful repair, practical devotion, and attention to detail.

If several fire chapels appear, the reading may emphasize courage, action, visibility, and movement.

If several earth chapels appear, the reading may emphasize embodiment, responsibility, resources, repair, and material care.

If several air chapels appear, the reading may emphasize speech, understanding, relationship, community, and mental clarity.

If several water chapels appear, the reading may emphasize memory, grief, hidden wounds, forgiveness, spiritual sensitivity, and emotional healing.

Do not make the interpretation overly complicated. Begin with the card entries. Then ask how the virtues are converging. The goal is not to decode an abstract system. The goal is to listen for the shape of grace.

**If a Difficult Card Appears**

Some angel cards may feel confronting. The practitioner may draw Forgiveness in Scorpio and immediately feel resistance. Or Mercy in Capricorn may reveal an accountability the practitioner has been avoiding. Or Empathy in Aquarius may expose a refusal to understand those who are different. This does not mean the rite has gone wrong. It means the rite is working.

The angels of virtue do not flatter the soul. They heal it.

If a difficult card appears, do not reject it. Place a hand over the heart and say:

Lord Jesus Christ,
give me the grace to receive this without fear.

Then read the card slowly. Ask what is being invited, not merely what is being exposed. The angel does not arrive to shame the practitioner. The angel arrives to help the virtue become possible.

**If No Feeling Comes**

Sometimes the practitioner will feel very little during the rite. This is also acceptable. Spiritual practice is not measured by emotional intensity. A quiet rite may be just as powerful as one filled with tears or visions.

If no feeling comes, continue the practice with faithfulness. Read the cards. Write the vows. Perform the actions. The fruit may appear later, through behavior, dreams, conversations, decisions, or subtle changes in the heart.

The rite is not a performance of emotion. It is a structure of devotion.

**The Short Rite of the Sixfold Heart**

When the practitioner does not have time for the full rite, the short version may be used.

Light a candle before Christ.

Make the Sign of the Cross.

Say:

Lord Jesus Christ,
open the Sixfold Heart within me.

Draw one card from each virtue group:

Kindness.
Compassion.
Mercy.
Empathy.
Altruistic Love.
Forgiveness.

Place the six cards in a circle around the candle.

Say:

Kindness, teach me how to open.
Compassion, teach me how to approach suffering.
Mercy, teach me how to restore.
Empathy, teach me how to see.
Altruistic Love, teach me how to give.
Forgiveness, teach me how to release.

Read each card entry.

Write one action for the day.

Pray:

Lord Jesus Christ,
make these six virtues one heart in me.
Let this prayer become action.
Let this action become love.
Amen.

Make the Sign of the Cross.

Extinguish the candle.

This short rite may be completed in ten to twenty minutes.

**The One-Card Daily Practice**

For daily use, the practitioner may draw a single angel card from the full deck or from one virtue group.

If drawing from the full deck, ask:

What virtue is Christ asking me to embody today?

If drawing from a specific virtue group, ask:

How is this virtue asking to live in me today?

After drawing the card, pray:

Lord Jesus Christ,
let this angel teach me one form of love today.

Read the card entry.

Write one action.

At the end of the day, ask:

Did I practice the virtue?
Where did I resist it?
Where did grace help me?
What did I learn?

This one-card practice keeps the deck alive in ordinary life.

**The Forty-Day Sixfold Heart Practice**

The Rite of the Sixfold Heart may also become a forty-day practice. This is especially appropriate during Lent, Advent, a personal retreat, or a season of healing.

On Day One, perform the full rite. Draw the six angel cards and create the Sixfold Heart Mandala.

For the next thirty-eight days, pray with one of the six cards each day, rotating through the virtues:

Day Two: Kindness.
Day Three: Compassion.
Day Four: Mercy.
Day Five: Empathy.
Day Six: Altruistic Love.
Day Seven: Forgiveness.

Then repeat the cycle.

Each day, read the card, pray its prayer, and perform one small action connected to the virtue.

On Day Forty, perform the full rite again. Draw a new Sixfold Heart Mandala. Compare it with the first. Ask:

What has changed?
Which virtue has grown?
Which virtue still resists embodiment?
What has Christ healed?
What is the next season asking?

This forty-day practice turns the oracle into a path of formation. The reader does not merely consult the cards. The reader is trained by them.

**Group Version of the Rite**

The Rite of the Sixfold Heart may be performed in a prayer group, retreat, parish-adjacent contemplative circle, or small spiritual community.

In a group setting, place Christ at the center as usual. Lay out the twelve Saint cards as the outer chapel. Then either draw one Sixfold Heart Mandala for the entire group or allow each participant to draw their own six angel cards.

The group leader may pray:

Lord Jesus Christ,
make this gathering a chapel of virtue.
Let no one here be coerced, exposed, or shamed.
Let each soul receive only what grace permits.
Let the angels of virtue guide us toward love, truth, and embodied goodness.
Amen.

After the cards are drawn, each participant may journal privately. Sharing should be voluntary. No one should interpret another person’s cards without consent.

A simple group sharing format is:

The virtue that feels strongest for me is…
The virtue that challenges me is…
The action I am taking from this rite is…

The group may close by praying the Prayer of the Sixfold Heart together.

**Consecrating the Deck with the Rite**

The Rite of the Sixfold Heart may be used to consecrate the oracle deck before regular use.

Place the full deck before Christ.

Lay the twelve Saint cards in a circle.

Place the seventy-two Angel cards in the center, near the candle.

Pray:

Lord Jesus Christ,
bless this deck as an instrument of contemplative prayer.

Let these cards never be used for vanity, fear, manipulation, or domination.
Let them serve virtue, discernment, healing, and love.

Let the saints stand as chapels of holy witness.
Let the angels appear as messengers of grace.
Let every reading return the soul to You.

May the hands that shuffle be purified.
May the eyes that see be softened.
May the heart that asks be made sincere.
May the life that receives be changed.

In the name of the Father,
and of the Son,
and of the Holy Spirit.
Amen.

Touch the deck lightly to the bowl of water without wetting it, or simply hold your hand over the water and then over the deck.

Say:

May this deck be cleansed for prayer.

Touch the salt or hold your hand over it and then over the deck.

Say:

May this deck be preserved in truth.

Hold the deck over the heart.

Say:

May this deck serve the Heart of Christ.

Then proceed with the full Rite of the Sixfold Heart.

**Ethical Use of the Rite**

Because this rite involves virtue, prayer, and moral discernment, it should be used with humility.

Do not use the rite to control another person.
Do not use the rite to avoid difficult conversations.
Do not use the rite to bypass professional help when such help is needed.
Do not use the rite to declare yourself spiritually superior.
Do not use the rite to force forgiveness from the wounded.
Do not use the rite to excuse harm.
Do not use the rite to seek signs while refusing ordinary responsibility.

The rite is not a substitute for repentance, therapy, pastoral care, medical care, accountability, or justice. It is a contemplative practice that helps the practitioner receive grace and embody virtue.

If the rite reveals that an apology is needed, apologize.

If the rite reveals that a boundary is needed, set the boundary.

If the rite reveals that rest is needed, rest.

If the rite reveals that help is needed, seek help.

If the rite reveals that forgiveness is not yet complete, begin with willingness.

If the rite reveals that love must become action, act.

The truth of the rite is proven in the life that follows it.

**The Six Virtues as a Rule of Life**

After performing the rite several times, the practitioner may wish to form a personal Rule of the Sixfold Heart.

This rule is a simple pattern of daily living based on the six virtues.

A sample rule may be:

I will practice Kindness by reducing unnecessary harm in speech and action.
I will practice Compassion by moving toward suffering with grounded care.
I will practice Mercy by allowing restoration after failure.
I will practice Empathy by listening before assuming.
I will practice Altruistic Love by giving without possession or performance.
I will practice Forgiveness by releasing hatred into Christ and seeking truthful repair.

The practitioner may write a more personal version based on the cards drawn in the rite.

For example:

Because I drew Kindness in Taurus, I will make kindness tangible through food, steadiness, and care for the body.

Because I drew Compassion in Cancer, I will shelter sorrow and honor memory.

Because I drew Mercy in Scorpio, I will bring hidden shame into the difficult hope of Christ.

Because I drew Empathy in Aquarius, I will make room for unfamiliar minds and excluded people.

Because I drew Altruistic Love in Virgo, I will serve through humble details.

Because I drew Forgiveness in Capricorn, I will practice accountability and rebuild trust through time.

This becomes a living rule, not a rigid law. It may guide the practitioner for a day, a week, a month, a season, or a year.

**Closing Teaching**

The Rite of the Sixfold Heart reveals the true purpose of this oracle. The cards are not meant to entertain the anxious mind. They are not meant to replace the conscience. They are not meant to make the practitioner feel special. They are meant to return the soul to the work of love.

The twelve apostolic chapels show that grace comes through many doors. The six angelic virtues show that love has many movements. Christ at the center shows that all virtue must return to its source.

When the practitioner lays out the Saint cards, the soul enters a temple.

When the practitioner draws the Angel cards, the soul receives instruction.

When the practitioner prays the virtues, the heart is softened.

When the practitioner writes the vows, the rite becomes embodied.

When the practitioner performs the actions, the oracle becomes life.

This is the mystery of the Sixfold Heart: the chapel is not only on the table. The chapel is in the heart. The angels are not only on the cards. The angels are in the movements of grace that teach the soul how to love. The saints are not only images. They are witnesses to the life of discipleship. The ritual is not finished when the candle is extinguished. It is finished when kindness is spoken, compassion is practiced, mercy is offered, empathy is honored, altruistic love is given, and forgiveness releases the soul back into God.

Christ is the center.

The apostles form the temple.

The angels carry the virtues.

The practitioner becomes the living altar.

And where the Sixfold Heart awakens, the chapel opens.
