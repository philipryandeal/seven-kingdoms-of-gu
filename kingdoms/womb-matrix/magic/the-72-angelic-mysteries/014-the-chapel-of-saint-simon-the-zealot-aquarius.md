# The Chapel of Saint Simon the Zealot: Aquarius



**Saint Simon the Zealot, Apostle of Purified Conviction**

Saint Simon the Zealot stands at the eleventh gate of the zodiacal cathedral as the apostle of purified conviction, holy reform, and zeal surrendered to Christ. In this chapel, the soul learns that passion for truth must be converted into charity, that the desire to change the world must be placed under the kingdom of God, and that no cause, faction, movement, or ideology can take the place of the Lord.

Simon is one of the most hidden apostles. He is named among the Twelve, but the Gospels do not preserve an individual scene, speech, miracle, or question from him. His apostolic identity reaches us primarily through his name: Simon the Zealot, also called Simon the Cananaean. Because there is little narrative attached to him, his title becomes the doorway into contemplation.

This hiddenness is important. Simon is not remembered because he founded a visible movement around himself. He is remembered because Christ called him into the apostolic company. Whatever zeal marked his life before or during his discipleship, it was gathered into Christ. His conviction was not erased. It was converted.

This is the mystery of the Aquarius chapel. The soul sees systems, movements, communities, futures, injustices, and possibilities. It feels the pressure of what must change. It may burn with reforming fire. This desire can be holy when it becomes service to the kingdom of God. It becomes distorted when it becomes ideology, factionalism, contempt, rebellion for its own sake, or love of the cause more than love of persons.

Saint Simon teaches that zeal must become apostolic. The fire for a new world must pass through Christ. The desire for reform must be purified by humility, charity, obedience, and prayer. The disciple may resist evil, but must not become possessed by anger. The disciple may seek justice, but must not forget mercy. The disciple may labor for the future, but must remain rooted in the living Lord.

In this chapel, Christ asks the soul: What fire has taken hold of you? Does it belong to me? Does it make you more loving, more truthful, more humble, and more willing to serve? Or has zeal become another master?

Saint Simon the Zealot teaches that the cause must bow before Christ.

**Saint Simon the Zealot in Scripture**

Saint Simon is named in all the New Testament lists of the Twelve Apostles. Because there is also Simon Peter, this apostle is distinguished by a title. Matthew and Mark call him Simon the Cananaean or Cananean. Luke and Acts call him Simon the Zealot. These titles are closely related in meaning and point toward zeal, ardor, or passionate devotion.

The meaning of the title must be handled carefully. Later history knows the Zealots as a revolutionary movement connected with resistance to Roman rule, but scholars and Catholic commentators are cautious about whether such an organized party existed in that form during the earthly ministry of Jesus. Therefore, the title should not be treated too simply as proof that Simon belonged to a formal political group as later understood. What can be said more safely is that Simon was remembered as a man marked by zeal.

This matters spiritually. The Gospel does not preserve Simon’s former arguments, loyalties, or struggles. It preserves his place among the Twelve. His defining title remains, but it is subordinated to his apostolic calling. He is not finally Simon the angry man, Simon the factional man, or Simon the ideological man. He is Simon the apostle of Christ.

The lists of the Twelve are themselves significant. Jesus chooses people from different backgrounds, temperaments, and social locations. Simon the Zealot stands in the same apostolic company as Matthew the tax collector. This is a profound sign. Matthew had been associated with the tax system and Roman power. Simon’s title suggests zeal that may have been opposed to such powers. Yet Christ calls them both into the same Twelve.

In the world, these men might have stood on opposite sides of a political or social divide. In Christ, they are gathered into one apostolic body.

This is one of Simon’s greatest scriptural teachings, even though it is hidden in the list itself. The kingdom of God is not built from one faction. Christ calls persons, not categories. He gathers the zealous and the compromised, the fisherman and the tax collector, the thunderous and the practical, the questioning and the hidden. The apostolic communion is stronger than the old division.

Simon is also named in Acts among the apostles gathered after the Ascension. There, the Twelve are shown as a body of witnesses to the life, teaching, death, Resurrection, and Ascension of Christ. Simon is part of this apostolic body waiting in prayer. The zealot becomes one who waits for the Holy Spirit. The man of fire must receive fire from above.

This is important for the Aquarius chapel. Human zeal is not enough. Even righteous passion must wait for Pentecost. The disciple must not confuse personal urgency with the mission of the Spirit. The apostles are not sent simply because they care intensely. They are sent after prayer, communion, and the descent of the Holy Spirit.

Scripture gives us little about Simon as an individual, but what it gives is enough: he is named, chosen, gathered, and sent. His zeal is placed within apostolic communion. His identity is converted from isolated passion into witness.

**Saint Simon the Zealot in Catholic Memory**

Catholic tradition honors Saint Simon the Zealot as Apostle and martyr. His feast is celebrated with Saint Jude Thaddaeus on October 28. The two are often remembered together because they appear near one another in the apostolic lists and because later tradition connects their missionary witness.

Catholic sources are careful to say that little is known with certainty about Simon beyond the New Testament. This does not diminish his sanctity. It teaches humility before the hidden saints. Not every apostle is known through dramatic stories. Some remain veiled, yet their names are written into the foundation of the Church.

Tradition associates Simon with missionary work in various regions, sometimes including Egypt, Mesopotamia, Persia, or other eastern lands. He is often remembered as preaching with Saint Jude and suffering martyrdom with him. The exact details differ among traditions, but Catholic memory receives him as one who carried the Gospel beyond the first circle of Jerusalem and Galilee and gave his life as a witness to Christ.

Simon is often depicted with instruments of martyrdom, such as a saw, because one tradition holds that he was martyred by being sawn. Other traditions give different accounts of his death. The details should be held with care, but the spiritual meaning is clear: Simon’s zeal became faithful witness unto death.

His relics have also been associated in Catholic memory with Saint Peter’s Basilica, where devotion to the apostles is gathered into the heart of the Church. This is fitting. Simon’s zeal, whatever its earlier form, is not remembered as a separate movement outside the Church. It rests within apostolic communion.

Catholic reflection on Simon often emphasizes the breadth of Christ’s call. Pope Benedict XVI notes that Simon and Jude are treated together because so little is known about them, and that Simon’s title, whether Cananaean or Zealot, points to ardent devotion. Benedict also highlights the remarkable variety among the apostles, including Simon the Zealot and Matthew the tax collector. Jesus is interested in persons, not social or religious categories.

This is crucial for understanding Saint Simon. He is not a saint of faction. He is a saint of conversion beyond faction. He reveals that Christ can take a soul marked by powerful conviction and place it into communion with those it might once have opposed.

Saint Simon is therefore an intercessor for activists, reformers, organizers, visionaries, radicals, those with strong convictions, those angered by injustice, and those whose passion needs purification. He does not ask them to become apathetic. He asks them to become holy. He prays that zeal may be converted from rage into witness, from ideology into charity, from faction into communion, and from restless fire into the mission of the Holy Spirit.

**The Mystery of the Aquarius Chapel**

Aquarius is the chapel of vision, reform, collective life, future possibility, social conscience, holy disruption, and the purification of zeal. In this chapel, the soul learns how to care about the world without becoming consumed by the world’s brokenness. It learns how to seek justice without surrendering to hatred, how to resist corruption without becoming contemptuous, and how to work for a new future without making an idol of the future.

Saint Simon the Zealot governs this chapel because he reveals the sanctification of conviction. He is remembered by a name of zeal, but his zeal is gathered into Christ and placed among the Twelve. His old fire becomes apostolic fire. His passion is not allowed to remain isolated. It is joined to communion, prayer, witness, and martyrdom.

The Aquarius soul sees patterns. It sees systems. It sees what is unfair, outdated, oppressive, hypocritical, or in need of renewal. This perception can be a gift. The prophets of God have always seen what others refused to see. The Church herself must continually be purified and renewed by the Holy Spirit. But the gift becomes distorted when vision loses love.

Unconverted Aquarius may become ideological, detached, rebellious, utopian, contemptuous, or addicted to being outside the system. It may love humanity in theory while despising actual persons. It may speak of liberation while creating new forms of control. It may identify so strongly with a cause that it loses the ability to repent, listen, or be corrected.

Saint Simon teaches the mature Aquarius path: let zeal become charity.

The cause must be brought to Christ. The movement must be measured by the Gospel. The future must be surrendered to the kingdom. The reformer must be willing to be reformed. The one who wants to change the world must first allow Christ to purify the heart.

Simon’s presence in the apostolic company with Matthew is one of the great hidden icons of this chapel. The Zealot and the tax collector are both called. This means the kingdom is not identical with any human faction. Christ does not build his Church by confirming our divisions. He gathers persons into a communion that transcends the categories by which the world separates them.

The Aquarius chapel asks each soul: What cause has taken hold of your heart? Does it lead you to Christ or away from him? Does it increase love, courage, humility, and service? Or does it feed contempt, isolation, and rage? Are you willing to let Christ correct your zeal?

Saint Simon answers: keep the fire, but surrender it. Let the Holy Spirit make it apostolic.

**Oracular Meaning**

When the Chapel of Saint Simon the Zealot appears, you are being asked to examine your zeal.

This card often appears when conviction, reform, justice, community, activism, innovation, resistance, or future vision is active in your life. You may feel strongly that something must change. You may be called to speak, organize, protest, build, teach, reform, or refuse cooperation with what is corrupt. Saint Simon does not tell you to become passive. He asks whether your fire belongs to Christ.

The first question of this card is simple: what has your heart?

If the answer is Christ, then your cause must be governed by love. If the answer is anger, identity, faction, superiority, or despair, then your zeal needs purification.

This card can indicate a legitimate call to reform. Something may be unjust, stagnant, harmful, or spiritually dead. You may be seeing clearly what others avoid. But Saint Simon warns that seeing the problem is not the same as becoming holy. The reformer must also be converted.

If you are angry, ask whether the anger is serving charity. Anger can reveal that something is wrong, but it cannot become your lord. If anger is making you less human, less prayerful, less merciful, and less capable of seeing persons, then it has exceeded its place.

If you are part of a movement, community, or collective, this card asks you to examine whether the group serves truth or merely reinforces identity. Aquarius can confuse belonging to the right cause with belonging to Christ. Simon’s presence among the Twelve teaches that apostolic communion is deeper than faction.

This card may also appear when you are being asked to collaborate with someone who comes from a very different background. Remember Simon and Matthew. In Christ, the zealot and the tax collector can become brothers. The kingdom may require communion with those whom your old identity would have rejected.

Saint Simon brings the oracle of converted fire. Do not extinguish zeal. Consecrate it.

**When This Card Appears**

When this card appears, begin by naming the cause, conviction, reform, or future vision that currently carries emotional force in your life.

Write it down plainly. Then ask: how does this serve Christ? How does this serve actual persons? How does this serve truth, mercy, justice, and communion?

If your zeal is holy, it will become more patient, courageous, humble, and loving as it matures. If it is distorted, it will become more contemptuous, rigid, isolated, and unable to receive correction.

If you are working for justice, pray before acting. Do not let urgency replace discernment. The apostles waited in prayer for the Holy Spirit. Human passion alone cannot build the kingdom. Ask for the fire from above.

If you are rebelling against something, ask what you are building. Rebellion may be necessary when evil must be refused, but rebellion by itself is not a vocation. The Christian path is not merely against. It is for Christ, for the kingdom, for mercy, for truth, for the healing of persons and communities.

If you are becoming detached, return to the human face. Aquarius can become abstract. It may speak of systems while forgetting the wounded person in front of it. Saint Simon asks you to let the cause become embodied in acts of charity.

If you are in conflict with someone from a different social, political, religious, or temperamental background, bring the image of Simon and Matthew into prayer. Ask Christ whether he is calling you to a communion larger than your preferred category.

If you feel alone in your convictions, remember that Simon is hidden but not absent. Not every apostle receives dramatic scenes in Scripture. Some are faithful in obscurity. Hidden witness still belongs to the foundation of the Church.

**The Shadow of Saint Simon the Zealot**

The shadow of this card is zeal without love.

This may appear as ideology, factionalism, rebellion for its own sake, contempt, purity politics, spiritualized rage, conspiracy thinking, impatience, or the refusal to be corrected because the cause feels righteous. The shadow believes that intensity proves truth. It does not.

Another shadow is love of humanity without love of actual people. The soul may care deeply about the future, the collective, the movement, or the theory, while becoming cold toward the person directly before it. Saint Simon asks zeal to become incarnate charity.

The Simon shadow can also appear as identity fused with opposition. The soul may no longer know who it is without an enemy. It may confuse being against something with being faithful to Christ. But the Gospel is not merely a refusal. It is a kingdom.

This card also warns against making politics, activism, reform, or innovation into a substitute religion. Even noble causes can become idols when they demand the whole heart. Christ alone is Lord.

Another distortion is despairing detachment. The soul may see the brokenness of the world so clearly that it freezes, withdraws, or begins to believe nothing can change. Saint Simon does not ask for naïve optimism. He asks for apostolic hope: the kind of hope that prays, acts, witnesses, and remains in communion.

The Aquarius shadow wants the future without the Cross, reform without repentance, justice without mercy, community without communion, and fire without the Holy Spirit. Saint Simon stands against this distortion. He teaches that zeal must be purified until it becomes witness.

**Practice of the Chapel**

To work with the Chapel of Saint Simon the Zealot, prepare a simple prayer space with a candle, a crucifix or image of Christ, and, if available, an image of Saint Simon. Place beside the candle a written word or phrase naming a cause, concern, injustice, reform, or future hope that carries weight in your heart.

Begin with the Sign of the Cross.

Say:

“Lord Jesus Christ, purify my zeal.”

Sit quietly and look at the word you have written. Notice what rises in your body. Anger. Grief. Hope. Urgency. Fear. Love. Exhaustion. Desire. Do not judge it immediately. Let it come into the light.

Then pray:

“Saint Simon the Zealot, apostle of converted fire, teach my conviction to serve Christ.”

Ask yourself:

Does this zeal make me more loving?

Does it make me more truthful?

Does it make me more humble?

Does it help me serve actual people?

Does it remain open to correction by Christ?

Now imagine Simon the Zealot standing beside Matthew the tax collector among the Twelve. Let this image challenge every faction in your heart. Ask Christ to gather your convictions into communion.

If you are able, pray for someone you disagree with. Do not pray to defeat them. Pray for their salvation, healing, truth, and good. Then pray the same for yourself.

For the next twenty-four hours, practice one act of purified zeal. Speak truth without contempt. Serve a person rather than an abstraction. Take one constructive step toward justice. Refuse one unnecessary argument. Pray before reacting. Let the fire become useful.

Let this be your offering to Saint Simon.

**Contemplation Questions**

What cause, movement, or conviction has taken hold of my heart?

Has my zeal been surrendered to Christ?

Where has righteous anger become contempt?

Where do I love humanity in theory but neglect actual people?

What reform do I seek, and am I willing to be reformed?

Where has identity become more important than truth?

What future am I trying to build, and does it belong to the kingdom of God?

Where is Christ asking me to collaborate beyond faction?

How can my fire become charity?

What would apostolic zeal look like today?

**Prayer to Saint Simon the Zealot**

Saint Simon the Zealot, Apostle of Christ and witness of purified conviction, pray for us.

You who were called among the Twelve, help us surrender every passion, cause, and conviction to the Lordship of Jesus Christ.

You whose zeal was gathered into apostolic communion, pray that our fire may be purified by charity, humility, and truth.

You who stood in the same apostolic body as Matthew the tax collector, teach us to seek communion beyond faction, category, resentment, and division.

You who are remembered as a hidden apostle, strengthen all who labor faithfully without recognition.

You who carried the Gospel beyond the familiar places, help us work for renewal without losing obedience to Christ.

You who gave your life as a martyr in Catholic memory, pray that our witness may be courageous, steady, and free from hatred.

Saint Simon, apostle of converted fire, intercede for reformers, activists, teachers, organizers, visionaries, and all whose hearts burn for justice.

Protect us from ideology without love, anger without mercy, rebellion without wisdom, and zeal without prayer.

Teach us to resist evil without becoming evil.

Teach us to seek justice without abandoning charity.

Teach us to build the future without worshiping it.

May the Holy Spirit purify our convictions, heal our divisions, and make our passion useful to the kingdom of God.

Through Jesus Christ our Lord. Amen.
