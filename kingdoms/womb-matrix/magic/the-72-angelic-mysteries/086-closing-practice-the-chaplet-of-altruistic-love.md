# Closing Practice: The Chaplet of Altruistic Love



To close this chapter, the reader may practice the Chaplet of Altruistic Love. This devotion is for purifying generosity, strengthening charity, and releasing love from possession, performance, and fear.

Sit before a candle, crucifix, icon, saint card, or angel card. Place both feet on the ground. Let the breath become slow. Say:

“Lord Jesus Christ, teach me to love freely, wisely, and without possession.”

Bring to mind one person, community, creature, ancestor, project, or situation you are called to love. Notice what arises. Is there warmth? Fear? Need? Pride? Resentment? Attachment? Sorrow? Desire to control? Desire to serve? Do not judge too quickly. Hold the whole truth before Christ.

Pray slowly through the twelve chapels:

May altruistic love offer itself bravely.
May altruistic love become generous provision.
May altruistic love communicate truth freely.
May altruistic love nurture without possession.
May altruistic love radiate as blessing.
May altruistic love serve humbly in small things.
May altruistic love honor mutual dignity.
May altruistic love remain faithful in the depths without control.
May altruistic love bless the other’s journey.
May altruistic love become responsible commitment.
May altruistic love widen toward the collective and the stranger.
May altruistic love pour out as mystical charity for all beings.

After each line, breathe once and imagine the corresponding angel placing a small flame of charity in the heart.

When the prayer is complete, ask Christ three questions. First: “Where is my love free?” Second: “Where is my love attached to reward or control?” Third: “What form of love is actually being asked of me now?” Let the answers be simple and practical.

If love is asking for action, act. If love is asking for restraint, refrain. If love is asking for generosity, give cleanly. If love is asking for a boundary, set it without hatred. If love is asking for release, bless the beloved and open your hands. If love is asking for endurance, return to prayer and receive strength from God.

The Angels of Altruistic Love do not ask the reader to disappear. They ask the reader to become free enough to give. They do not ask the reader to possess the beloved. They ask the reader to seek the beloved’s good in God. They do not ask the reader to perform sacrifice. They ask the reader to become a living vessel of charity.

Where altruistic love enters, the ego loosens its grip. Where the ego loosens, generosity becomes clean. Where generosity becomes clean, Christ’s love can move through the soul without distortion. And where love moves freely, the chapel opens.
