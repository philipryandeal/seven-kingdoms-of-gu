# Closing Practice: The Chaplet of Forgiveness



To close this chapter, the reader may practice the Chaplet of Forgiveness. This devotion is for releasing hatred, receiving divine forgiveness, and discerning the difference between forgiveness, reconciliation, justice, and boundaries.

Sit before a candle, crucifix, icon, saint card, or angel card. Place both feet on the ground. Let the breath become slow. Say:

“Lord Jesus Christ, teach me forgiveness without falsehood, and freedom without hatred.”

Bring to mind one wound, debt, regret, resentment, or person connected to forgiveness. This may be someone who harmed you, someone you harmed, a relationship that ended badly, a family memory, a community wound, or a part of yourself you still condemn. Do not force emotion. Do not pretend. Hold the situation before Christ as truthfully as you can.

Pray slowly through the twelve chapels:

May forgiveness interrupt vengeance.
May forgiveness release the body from resentment.
May forgiveness purify the story.
May forgiveness heal memory and family pain.
May forgiveness restore dignity after humiliation.
May forgiveness guide careful repair and amends.
May forgiveness seek truthful reconciliation where possible.
May forgiveness descend into betrayal and deep injury.
May forgiveness open the road beyond the wound.
May forgiveness accept accountability and consequence.
May forgiveness release exile and widen belonging.
May forgiveness surrender sorrow into the mercy of God.

After each line, breathe once and imagine the corresponding angel removing one thorn from the heart and placing it into the wounds of Christ.

When the prayer is complete, ask Christ four questions. First: “What truth must be named?” Second: “What hatred must be released?” Third: “What justice or repair is still needed?” Fourth: “What boundary protects love from becoming false?” These questions keep forgiveness rooted in wisdom.

If the wound is still too raw, do not condemn yourself. Begin with willingness. Pray: “Lord, I am willing to become willing.” If reconciliation is possible, let it be built on truth, repentance, and changed action. If reconciliation is not possible, let forgiveness become the release of hatred and the return of your life to God.

If you are the one who caused harm, do not demand forgiveness from the person you wounded. Bring your repentance to Christ. Make amends where possible. Accept consequences. Let forgiveness become conversion rather than entitlement.

The Angels of Forgiveness do not ask the reader to forget. They ask the reader to become free. They do not ask the wounded to deny pain. They ask that pain be brought into the presence of Christ. They do not ask for false peace. They ask for the peace that can survive truth.

Where forgiveness enters, hatred loses its throne. Where hatred loses its throne, the soul is returned to its own life. Where the soul is returned to life, grace can move again. And where grace moves again, the chapel opens.
