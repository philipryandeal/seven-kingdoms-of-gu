# Creating a Self-Altar

Every altar in your house serves a relationship. Your bóveda serves your relationship with the dead. Your working altar serves your relationship with the spirits you petition and the forces you move. But there is one relationship that most practitioners neglect entirely, and it is the most important one you have: your relationship with yourself.

In the Ifá tradition, this is called the Orí shrine — the altar to your head, your divine consciousness, the part of you that stood before God and chose your destiny before you were born. Your Orí is not your personality. It is not your ego. It is the deepest layer of who you are — the part of you that knows why you came here and what you are supposed to do with this life. In Vedic philosophy, this same principle is called the Ātman — the true Self, the one that doesn't change when your body changes, the one that was there before your name and will be there after it. Different languages, same reality: there is a divine intelligence inside you that is *you*, and it deserves a place in your home where it is honored, tended, and remembered.

That's what a self-altar is. It is the physical house you build for the part of you that is intimately connected to the Divine. And when you tend that altar consistently — when you keep it clean, keep the water fresh, keep the light burning — you strengthen that connection over time. You are telling the deepest part of yourself: *I see you. I remember you. I haven't forgotten why I'm here.*

## Why This Altar Is Private

Your self-altar is not for display. It should be kept out of view from casual visitors, houseguests, and anyone who doesn't need to see it. This is not paranoia. This is protection.

Every other altar in your house faces outward in some sense — it serves a relationship between you and external forces. Your self-altar faces inward. It holds your essence, your aspirations, your vulnerabilities, your image of who you are and who you are becoming. That is intimate information. You don't hang your innermost self on the living room wall for everyone to comment on, project onto, or — whether they mean to or not — affect with their energy. Some people carry envy they aren't even conscious of. Some people look at your shrine to yourself and feel something uncomfortable that they'll never name out loud but that leaves a residue anyway.

Keep it in your bedroom. Better yet, keep it where you can see it from your bed. When you open your eyes in the morning, before the day has a chance to tell you who you are, your self-altar is the first thing that greets you. That matters. That thirty seconds of lying in bed looking at a shrine you built to your own divine consciousness is a grounding moment that sets the tone for everything that follows.

## Setting It Up

Choose a small, dedicated space. A nightstand. A shelf. A small table. Even the top of a dresser if that's what you have. It doesn't need to be large. It needs to be *yours* — a space that no one else uses for anything else.

Clean the physical surface first. Then cleanse it energetically. You can smudge with cedar or tobacco, ring a bell or singing bowl, or simply hold your hands over the surface and speak your intention aloud: "This space is for me. This space holds the best of who I am. Nothing that does not serve my highest good is welcome here." You're not cleansing in a moral sense — you're not trying to be pure. You are clearing the space so that it is vibrant, clean, and ready to receive your energy without interference.

## What Goes On the Altar

This is your altar, so ultimately you decide what belongs on it. But let me give you a framework to start with, and you can build from there.

**Water.** Always. A clear glass of fresh water, just like you would place on your bóveda. But this water is not for your dead — it is for *you*. It represents clarity, flow, coolness of head, emotional balance. Change it every few days. When you pour out the old water and replace it with fresh, you are refreshing your own head. You are saying: I am not stagnant. I am not stale. I am moving, I am flowing, I am alive. If you do nothing else on this altar, keep the water fresh. That alone is a practice.

**A light.** A tea light or a small candle. When you sit at your self-altar, you light it. When you're done, you can let it burn out or extinguish it — your preference. The flame represents your consciousness, your inner fire, the spark of divinity that lives in your Orí. Some days it burns bright. Some days it flickers. You light it anyway.

**A photograph of yourself.** This is the one that makes people uncomfortable, and that discomfort is exactly why it matters. Choose a photograph of yourself that you *like*. Not one where you look impressive or successful — one where you look like yourself. Where you can see your own eyes and feel something warm. If you don't have a photograph like that, take one. And if you can't find a single image of yourself that you feel warmth toward, that is important information about where you are in your relationship with yourself, and this altar is exactly the medicine you need.

You can also include photos of yourself at different stages of life — as a child, as a young person, at moments that mattered. These are not nostalgia. They are acknowledgment. Every version of you that ever existed contributed to who you are now. They deserve a place on your altar.

**Objects that represent your truth.** Not your achievements — your truth. There is a difference. An award you won might belong here if that accomplishment genuinely reflects who you are. But don't load your self-altar with trophies and certificates to impress yourself. This isn't a résumé. This is a mirror. Put things on it that make you feel *recognized* — objects that, when you look at them, make you say "yes, that's me."

Maybe it's a stone you picked up on a walk that felt important for reasons you can't articulate. Maybe it's a piece of jewelry you inherited. Maybe it's a small statue or symbol from your spiritual tradition. Maybe it's something you made with your own hands. Trust your instincts here. If something wants to be on your self-altar, put it there. If something feels wrong or forced, take it off. The altar will tell you what it wants if you listen.

**Elemental balance.** Consider including simple representations of the four elements to bring balance and harmony into your energy field. You already have water and fire if you have your glass and your candle. Add a stone or a small dish of earth for the earth element, and a feather or a stick of incense for air. This is not decoration. These four elements are the building blocks of the material world, and you are a material being living a material life. Keeping them present on your self-altar is a reminder that you are a whole person — not just a spirit trapped in a body, but a spirit *expressed as* a body, and both halves deserve honor.

## The Practice

The practice is simple. Sit at your self-altar. You can do this daily if you want, or weekly, or whenever you feel disconnected from yourself. Light your candle. Look at your photograph. Look at the objects you chose. And ask yourself — honestly, without performance — *how am I?*

Not how are things going. Not what's on the to-do list. How am *I*? Am I taking care of myself? Am I eating well? Am I sleeping? Am I saying yes to things I should be saying no to? Am I neglecting my body? Am I carrying someone else's weight? Am I present in my own life or am I sleepwalking through it?

You don't need to answer out loud. You don't need to journal it unless you want to. Just sit with the question. Your Orí will answer if you get quiet enough to hear it. Sometimes the answer comes as a feeling in your body. Sometimes it comes as a sudden clarity about something you've been avoiding. Sometimes it comes later, in a dream or a moment of knowing that arrives when you're not expecting it.

This is not therapy. This is not self-help. This is a spiritual practice with roots older than any book on any shelf. You are consulting the divine intelligence that lives inside your own head — the one that chose this life, this body, this path. It knows things your conscious mind doesn't. Give it a place to speak.

## The Mirror Principle

Here is the real teaching of the self-altar, and I want you to take this seriously: **the condition of your self-altar is a direct reflection of your relationship with yourself.**

When your altar is clean, your water is fresh, your candle is ready, and everything is arranged with care — that tells you something. It tells you that you are paying attention to yourself. That you consider yourself worthy of tending. That you have not disappeared into your work, your relationships, your obligations, or your service to others at the expense of your own wellbeing.

And when your self-altar starts looking like a mess — dusty, cluttered, water sitting there for two weeks turning cloudy, candle burned down to a stub you haven't replaced — stop. Don't just clean the altar. Ask yourself the harder question: *what am I neglecting?* Because the altar didn't get messy by accident. It got messy because you stopped showing up for yourself. And if you stopped showing up for yourself, something in your life is out of alignment, and your Orí is trying to tell you about it through the only language it has — the physical state of the one space in your home that is dedicated entirely to you.

Clean the altar. Change the water. Light a fresh candle. And then sit down and listen. Your head has something to say.

A brujo who neglects their self-altar will eventually struggle at every other altar in the house. You cannot pour from an empty vessel, and you cannot serve the spirits with integrity if you are not serving yourself first. This is not selfishness. This is foundation. Your Orí is the first shrine. Everything else is built on top of it.

Tend yourself. The rest follows.
