# The Brujo's Herbal

Earlier in this book I told you that the brujo has always been an astrologer, a diviner, and an herbalist — and that the three tools are inseparable. I told you about the Picatrix pipeline from Baghdad to the botánica. I told you about the Olosanyin gathering his herbs before dawn under ritual conditions. I told you about the curandera gathering yerba buena under the waning moon because the plant's power to diminish fever runs with the diminishing light. I told you about the Mansions of the Moon and the nakshatras and the planetary hours, and how all of these systems give the brujo a map of celestial timing that makes his herbal work precise instead of approximate.

Now I need to teach you about the herbs themselves.

Not how to make tinctures and salves — that is the next chapter. Here I need to teach you how to *think* about herbs the way a brujo thinks about them. Because the way a brujo thinks about a plant is not the way a pharmacist thinks about a plant, and it is not the way a New Age wellness influencer thinks about a plant, and it is not the way your abuela thought about a plant — though your abuela was closer to the truth than any of the others.

A brujo thinks about a plant as a *person*. Not metaphorically. The plant is alive. The plant has a character, a temperament, a set of preferences, and a spiritual signature that is as real and as specific as any human personality you have ever encountered. Rue is not "an herb associated with Mars." Rue *is* Martial. Rue *is* combative, protective, hot, sharp, and willing to fight. If you have ever held fresh rue in your hand and felt the way it bites your skin, you already know this. The plant is telling you what it is. Your job as a brujo is to listen.

This is not a modern idea. This is the oldest idea in herbalism, and it runs through every tradition I carry.

## The Plants Are Talking — You Just Need to Learn the Language

The European tradition called it the Doctrine of Signatures. The principle is simple: God — or the cosmos, or the organizing intelligence of nature, whatever language you prefer — marked every plant with signs that indicate its use. A plant with heart-shaped leaves has an affinity for the heart. A plant with red sap has an affinity for the blood. A plant that grows near water has an affinity for the fluid systems of the body. A plant covered in thorns is a protector. A plant that creeps and clings is a binder. A walnut looks like a brain. Eyebright looks like an eye. The plant shows you what it does by showing you what it *is*.

The modern scientific establishment dismisses the Doctrine of Signatures as superstition. They are half right. Taken literally and applied mechanically — "this plant looks like a liver, therefore it cures liver disease" — it is unreliable. But taken as a *method of attention* — a way of training yourself to observe plants closely and notice what they are communicating through their form, their color, their scent, their texture, their habitat, and their behavior — it is one of the most powerful tools an herbalist can develop. The old brujos and curanderos who used this method were not guessing. They were paying attention at a level of detail that most modern people have lost the capacity for, and then they tested what they observed against results, over generations, and kept what worked.

The Yoruba tradition does not use the European language of "signatures," but it does the same thing through a different framework. The Olosanyin — the priest of Osanyin, the one-eyed, one-armed, one-legged Orisha of herbal medicine who can shoot a bow faster than people with two arms — gathers his herbs under specific conditions because the *conditions of gathering* activate the plant's spiritual properties. The when matters as much as the what. An herb gathered in the early morning carries the energy of beginnings. An herb gathered at noon carries solar fire. An herb gathered in the hour of Venus carries the sweetness of attraction. The plant is the same plant regardless of when you pick it. But the spiritual charge it carries — the *work* it can do on your altar — changes with the timing.

The curandero tradition in Mexico and the borderlands operates on the same principle through the framework of hot and cold. Every plant has a temperature — not the physical temperature of the leaf but its energetic quality. Hot plants heat the body and the spirit: they stimulate, activate, protect, and fight. Cold plants cool the body and the spirit: they soothe, calm, diminish, and heal inflammation. This is not metaphor. A curandero diagnosing empacho or susto is reading the patient's energetic temperature and then selecting herbs that restore the balance. Hot illness requires cold herbs. Cold illness requires hot herbs. The system is elegant, practical, and old — it predates the Spanish arrival in the Americas and was reinforced by Spanish humoral medicine, which operated on the same hot-cold logic inherited from Galen and the Greek physicians.

What all of these systems share — European Doctrine of Signatures, Yoruba ritual gathering, Mesoamerican hot-cold energetics — is the understanding that a plant is not just a bundle of chemical compounds. A plant is a *spiritual entity* with a character, a purpose, and a set of relationships to the cosmos that the brujo can learn to read. The chemical compounds are real. The medicinal properties are real. But they are the *body* of the plant. The brujo works with the body *and* the spirit. And the spirit is what makes the difference between an herbal remedy and a magical working.

## Why the Magical Properties Mirror the Medicinal Ones

This is the question that trips up beginners, and it has a simple answer once you understand the framework.

Lavender calms the nervous system when you drink it as a tea. Lavender also calms a room when you burn it as incense in a spiritual cleansing. Why? Because the same quality that makes lavender medicinally sedative — its essential character, its spiritual signature, the *thing that lavender is* — also makes it spiritually calming. The medicinal effect and the magical effect are not two different things. They are two expressions of the same underlying reality. The plant is one thing. It expresses that thing through its chemistry *and* through its spiritual energy. The chemistry is what the pharmacist sees. The spiritual energy is what the brujo sees. Both of them are seeing the plant. Neither of them is seeing the whole plant by themselves.

This is the Hermetic principle at work: as above, so below. As within, so without. The microcosm reflects the macrocosm. A plant that heals inflammation in the body will also cool inflammation in a spiritual situation — a hot argument, a heated enemy, a burning resentment. A plant that stimulates circulation in the body will also get things moving in a stuck spiritual situation — stagnant money, a blocked road, a relationship that has gone cold. A plant that fights infection in the body will also fight spiritual contamination — the evil eye, crossed conditions, the residue left behind by a hex or a hostile spirit.

This is not a belief. This is an observation confirmed by centuries of practice across every tradition I carry. The brujo who understands this principle does not need to memorize correspondence tables. He can *read* the plant. He can hold a sprig of rosemary and know from its sharp scent, its needle-like leaves, its association with fire and the Sun, that this plant protects, clarifies, and drives out what does not belong. He does not need a book to tell him that. The plant is telling him directly.

That said — tables are useful. Especially when you are starting out. So let me give you the frameworks.

## Planetary Correspondences

Every plant carries a planetary signature. This is the single most important correspondence system for a brujo who practices astro-rootwork, because it tells you *when* to work with the plant for maximum effect.

A plant ruled by Mars — rue, garlic, cayenne, nettle, tobacco, dragon's blood — is most potent when gathered or worked in the hour of Mars, on a Tuesday, under a Moon in a combative nakshatra. A plant ruled by Venus — rose, vanilla, jasmine, basil, cinnamon — is most potent in the hour of Venus, on a Friday, under a Moon in a nakshatra that supports love and attraction. The planetary rulership is not arbitrary. It reflects the plant's essential nature: Mars plants are hot, sharp, protective, and combative because Mars *is* hot, sharp, protective, and combative. Venus plants are sweet, beautiful, aromatic, and magnetic because Venus *is* sweet, beautiful, aromatic, and magnetic.

You already have the full planetary correspondence lists in the Planets chapter of this book. Use them. Cross-reference the planet that rules your herb with the nakshatra the Moon is in and the planetary hour you are working in, and you will see your results improve immediately. This is the practical application of everything I taught you in the Astrologer, Diviner, and Herbalist chapter. The theory is there. The timing system is there. Now you need to know which plants belong to which planet, and that information is in your hands.

## Elemental Correspondences

The four classical elements — Fire, Earth, Air, and Water — give you another layer of understanding.

Fire herbs are hot, stimulating, protective, and transformative. They correspond to the South, to the color red, to the energy of will and action. Cinnamon, cayenne, ginger, garlic, dragon's blood, tobacco, bay laurel — these are fire herbs. They heat up your workings. They add force. They burn through blockages and energize what is sluggish.

Earth herbs are grounding, stabilizing, nourishing, and material. They correspond to the North, to the colors green and brown, to the energy of manifestation and physical reality. Patchouli, vetiver, oak, comfrey, Solomon's seal, wheat, corn — these are earth herbs. They anchor your workings in the physical world. They make things real. They hold things in place.

Air herbs are clarifying, communicative, intellectual, and swift. They correspond to the East, to the colors yellow and white, to the energy of thought and communication. Lavender, lemongrass, mint, fennel, anise, sage, eucalyptus — these are air herbs. They clear the mind, open channels of communication, and carry messages where they need to go.

Water herbs are cooling, healing, emotional, and intuitive. They correspond to the West, to the colors blue and silver, to the energy of feeling and the subconscious. Jasmine, chamomile, lotus, willow, mugwort, lemon balm, cucumber — these are water herbs. They soothe, they heal emotional wounds, they open psychic perception, and they connect you to the dream world and the spirits of the dead.

Most herbs carry a primary elemental signature and a secondary one. Rosemary is primarily Fire but carries Air — it is hot and protective but also clarifying and mentally stimulating. Mugwort is primarily Water but carries Fire in its dream-activating capacity — it opens the psychic gates and then lights them up so you can see what is coming through. Learning to read these layers is part of developing your herbal intelligence. Start with the primary element and refine from there as your experience grows.

## The Energetics of Hot and Cold

The curandero tradition gives you a practical classification system that cuts through the complexity of planetary and elemental correspondences and gets you to the essential question: is this plant going to heat things up or cool things down?

Hot herbs stimulate, activate, protect, and fight. They raise energy. They speed things up. They are used when the condition is cold, stagnant, blocked, or sluggish. If the money is not moving, use hot herbs to get it moving. If an enemy is pressing you, use hot herbs to push back. If your body is cold and your energy is low, use hot herbs to bring fire back into your system.

Cold herbs soothe, calm, diminish, and heal. They lower energy. They slow things down. They are used when the condition is hot, inflamed, agitated, or excessive. If a situation is too heated — a conflict, an illness with fever, anxiety, spiritual attack that has you reactive and off-balance — use cold herbs to bring the temperature down. If you need to reduce something — an enemy's influence, a bad habit, an unwanted condition — cold herbs diminish.

This is not a different system from the planetary correspondences. It is the same system seen from a different angle. Mars herbs are hot. Venus herbs tend toward warm. Moon herbs tend toward cool. Saturn herbs are cold. The hot-cold spectrum maps directly onto the planetary spectrum, and when you learn to read both simultaneously, you have a diagnostic tool that covers almost any situation a brujo encounters.

## Working with Crystals and Herbs Together

I want to address this because I know many of you already work with crystals and will want to know how they fit into herbal practice. The short answer is: crystals amplify. They are not herbs. They do not have the same kind of living, vegetable spirit that a plant carries. But they hold energy, they resonate at specific frequencies, and when you place a crystal next to an herb or include a crystal in a working alongside herbal material, the crystal acts as an amplifier — it takes the signal the herb is broadcasting and makes it louder.

Rose quartz with rose petals in a love working. Citrine with cinnamon in a money working. Black tourmaline with rue in a protection working. Amethyst with mugwort for dream work and psychic development. The pairings are intuitive once you understand the correspondences — you are matching energies, pairing a mineral that resonates with Venus alongside a plant that resonates with Venus, and the combined signal is stronger than either one alone.

A few practical notes. First, crystals do not replace herbs. They enhance them. A protection working with black tourmaline alone is weaker than a protection working with rue, garlic, and black tourmaline together. The herbs are the workers. The crystal is the foreman. Second, if you are making a sachet or a mojo bag, the crystal goes in with the herbs — but be aware that some crystals are water-soluble or toxic and should not be placed in baths, elixirs, or anything you will ingest. Malachite is gorgeous and powerfully transformative but it contains copper and is toxic in water. Selenite dissolves. Do your research before you put any crystal in liquid. Third, charge your crystals the same way you charge your herbs — under the appropriate planetary hour, during the appropriate moon phase, with intention and prayer. A crystal that has been sitting on your shelf for six months collecting dust is not amplifying anything except neglect.

## Building Your Herbal Intelligence

I want to close this chapter by telling you how I actually learned herbs, because it was not from a book.

I learned herbs by touching them. I learned rue by rubbing it between my fingers and feeling how it bit my skin and smelling how sharp and green and combative it was before anyone told me it was a Mars herb. I learned lavender by holding it to my nose and feeling my whole body exhale before anyone told me it was a Mercury herb associated with calm and clarity. I learned cinnamon by tasting it and feeling the heat bloom across my tongue before anyone told me it was a Sun herb. I learned mugwort by burning it before bed and dreaming dreams so vivid I woke up convinced I had been somewhere real — before anyone told me it was the classic dreaming herb of every tradition that uses it.

The books came later. The correspondence tables came later. The theoretical frameworks — Doctrine of Signatures, hot-cold energetics, planetary rulership — came later and organized what I already knew from touch and smell and taste and practice. That is the order I recommend to you. Touch the plant first. Smell it. Taste it if it is safe to taste. Burn a little and watch the smoke. Sit with it on your altar overnight and see what you dream. *Then* go to the tables and see whether your experience matches the tradition. Nine times out of ten it will. And on the tenth time, when your experience diverges from the book — trust your experience. You are building a relationship with that plant, and no book on earth knows your relationship better than you do.

Start with ten herbs. Not thirty. Not a hundred. Ten herbs that you can acquire easily, that are relevant to the workings you actually do, and that you can afford to keep in stock. Learn those ten deeply — learn their planetary rulership, their element, their hot-cold quality, their scent, their taste, their behavior in a bath, in a candle dressing, in a floor wash, in a sachet. Track them in your grimoire. Note which planetary hours and which nakshatras make them hit hardest. After a year of working with ten herbs you will know those ten plants better than most practitioners know a hundred, and your workings will reflect it.

Then add ten more. Then ten more. In three years you will have a working pharmacopoeia of thirty herbs that you know intimately — not from a table but from your altar, your hands, your grimoire, and your results. That is how a brujo learns herbalism. Not from a lecture. From the plants themselves, tested against the sky, verified by results.

The plants are talking. Learn to listen. The rest follows.
