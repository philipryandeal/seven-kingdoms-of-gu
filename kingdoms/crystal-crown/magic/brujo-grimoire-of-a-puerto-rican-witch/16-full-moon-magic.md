# Full Moon Magic

Twenty years of practice has taught me one thing about the Full Moon: she does not need your belief to do her work. She does not require your altar, your candles, your correspondences, or your invocations. She is going to rise full every twenty-nine and a half days whether you notice her or not, and the tides will move, and the night will be brighter, and the strange feelings you have been having all week will reach their crescendo on the night she peaks.

Witchcraft is not the act of making the Moon do something. Witchcraft is the act of *showing up* to what she is already doing — and bringing fire to meet her.

## What the Full Moon Is, Astronomically

A Full Moon happens when the Earth sits between the Sun and the Moon, and the Moon's face is fully illuminated by the Sun's light from across our planet. She is not making her own light. She is a mirror, and what she shows you is the Sun's fire reflected through her cool body, traveling roughly a quarter of a million miles to land on your skin while you stand in your yard at midnight.

This matters because it tells you what the Full Moon actually *is*: she is the Sun's fire, softened. She is solar energy you can look at directly. She is the day made bearable, the truth made gentle, the fierce thing made viewable by being reflected. Everything you feel under her — the heightened emotion, the strange clarity, the inability to sleep, the sudden honesty — is solar fire arriving by lunar mail.

She is also always exactly opposite the Sun in the zodiac. When she is full in Aries, the Sun is in Libra. When she is full in Cancer, the Sun is in Capricorn. This opposition is not an accident or a technicality — it is the entire architecture of Full Moon magic, and I will return to it in a moment.

## What the Full Moon Is, Energetically

In my tradition — which is mine, not received, built over twenty years of doing this work — the Full Moon is the night when *what has been growing in the dark becomes visible*. Whatever you have been incubating since the New Moon, whether you knew you were incubating it or not, reaches its peak of expression on this night. The Moon does not bring the lesson. The Moon *reveals* the lesson that has been quietly forming inside you all month.

This is why people get strange on Full Moons. This is why emergency rooms fill up. This is why old fights resurface, secret feelings get said out loud, and people make decisions they have been postponing for weeks. The Full Moon does not cause these things. She *exposes* them. She turns the lights on.

The work of Full Moon magic is to meet that exposure with intention. To say to the Moon: *I see what you are showing me, and I will not flinch from it, and I will do something with it before this light fades.*

## Why Fire

Most Full Moon traditions you will read about reach for water. Charge water under the Moon, bathe in moonlit water, leave crystals in water, drink the moon water. Water is the Moon's element — receptive, cool, mirroring, holding.

I work fire instead.

Here is why. The Moon is already water. She is already cool and reflective. To meet her with more water is to add nothing — it is to deepen the trance she has already put you in. I have seen too many witches dissolve into their Full Moon practice and come out softer but not changed. Fire is what the Moon is *made of* underneath. The light she reflects is the Sun's fire. To work fire under the Full Moon is to honor what she actually is — solar energy in lunar dress — and to do the harder work of *acting* on what she shows you rather than only feeling it. Fire transforms. Fire eats. Fire converts one thing into another and sends the smoke up to wherever smoke goes. Water holds. Fire moves.

This is not a criticism of water work. It is simply my answer, after twenty years, to the question of which element actually carries the work forward. I have chosen fire. The Vedic tradition that taught me much of what I know about fire ceremony — Homa, Agnihotra, the householder's hearth — agrees. The fire is the mouth of the gods. What you give the fire, the fire carries. So, on Full Moon nights, I light a cauldron.

## The Cauldron as Agni's Mouth

Before we get to the technical instructions for building a fire cauldron, I want you to understand what the cauldron *is*. She is not equipment. She is not a magical accessory. She is not a prop. The cauldron is Agni's mouth — the place where you feed offerings to the sacred fire, and where the fire takes them and carries them where they need to go. Agni is the Vedic name for the fire-spirit, the eldest among the gods in many tellings, the one who eats every offering and ferries it across the threshold between the seen and the unseen. You do not have to call him Agni. You can call him by another name, or by no name at all. But you must understand that the fire is a *being*, not a chemical reaction.

When you light your cauldron, you are not starting a fire. You are *receiving a guest*. Treat her accordingly. Keep her clean. Do not use her for non-sacred purposes once she has been consecrated. Speak to her when you light her and when you extinguish her. Thank her every time. A consecrated cauldron will start to feel different over the years. Mine has burned hundreds of times now, and when I light her, she is not the same object she was the first night. She remembers. So will yours.

## The Polarity Principle

The last thing I want you to understand before you begin: every Full Moon is a *polarity*, not a single sign.

When the Moon is full in a sign, the Sun is in the sign exactly across the wheel. This means every Full Moon ritual you do is implicitly held by two energies — the lunar sign the Moon is illuminating, and the solar sign quietly underneath. They are not at war. They are in *conversation*, and the conversation is the actual teaching of that month's moon.

Aries Moon is lit by Libra Sun: the warrior held by the partner. Cancer Moon is lit by Capricorn Sun: the inner home held by the outer structure. Scorpio Moon is lit by Taurus Sun: the descent held by the body. And so on through the wheel.

In each of the twelve rituals that follows, I will name the polarity at work. You do not have to do anything special with the solar sign. Just know that it is there, underneath, holding the other side of the lesson. When you reach the moment in the fire ceremony where the flame begins to die, that is often when the solar sign speaks. Listen.

## A Last Word Before You Begin

The rituals in this chapter are templates. Not commandments. I have given you colors, herbs, incenses, stones, and words — and you should feel free to use exactly what I have written, or to substitute, or to throw out my suggestions entirely and use what your own practice has taught you. The bones of the work — the cauldron, the fire, the offering, the silence — those are what matter. The dressing is yours.

The Full Moon is going to rise tonight or this month or next month whether or not you light a fire. The question is whether you will meet her. Light the cauldron. She is waiting.

## Casting A Spell with a Fire Cauldron

Casting a spell using herbs in a cauldron can be a meaningful and symbolic ritual. The process often varies depending on the intention behind the spell. To safely create a fire cauldron using Epsom salt and alcohol, you need to be cautious and follow these steps. I recommend getting on YouTube and watching a few videos of itches making a cauldron fire before attempting it yourself. This activity should be conducted in a well-ventilated outdoor area, away from any flammable materials. Always prioritize safety and have a fire extinguisher or bucket of sand nearby. Here’s how you might perform a simple spell using herbs:

Materials Needed: A cast iron cauldron with lid, Epsom salt (magnesium sulfate), Isopropyl alcohol (rubbing alcohol) with a concentration of 70% or higher, herbs associated with your intention (e.g., rosemary for protection, lavender for peace, basil for prosperity), long matches, and a small piece of paper and a pen.

Set Your Intention: Before you begin, clearly define your intention. What do you want to achieve with this spell? Take a moment to focus your mind and visualize the outcome. On a small piece of paper, write down your intention or a simple phrase that represents your goal. Find a quiet, undisturbed area to perform your spell. You might want to cleanse the space by burning cedar or another method of your choice.

Choose Your Herbs: Select herbs that correspond to your intention. Research their symbolic meanings if you’re unsure. For example:

Rosemary for protection

Lavender for peace and tranquility

Basil for prosperity and success

Place Herbs in the Cauldron: As you place each herb into the cauldron, focus on your intention. Visualize the energy of the herbs amplifying your goal. If using, add a few drops of essential oil or place crystals around the cauldron to further enhance your spell.

Setting Up the Cauldron: Place the cauldron on a stable, non-flammable surface. Make sure the area is clear of any flammable materials and that you have a fire extinguisher nearby. Sprinkle a thin layer of Epsom salt at the bottom of the cauldron. The Epsom salt will add a subtle color effect to the flames. Carefully pour isopropyl alcohol into the cauldron.

Ratio of Salt and Alcohol: The ratio for the epsom salt and alcohol should be 1 to 1, equal parts. Use a measuring cup to put the salt then the alcohol in the cauldron. You do not need much. You should have a medium size cauldron, a half a cup of salt and alcohol will be a big flame, but it will stay contained in the cauldron. If you feel scared you can put the lid on the cauldron and the fire will stop within seconds.

Lighting the Fire: Using a lighter or a long match, carefully ignite the alcohol. Stand back as you do this to avoid any sudden flare-ups. The alcohol should catch fire and burn with a blue flame. The Epsom salt may cause slight variations in the color of the flame.

Meditate: Enjoy the flame from a safe distance. Do not leave the fire unattended and keep it away from any flammable objects. The fire will burn as long as there is alcohol left. Once the alcohol is consumed, the fire will naturally extinguish. This could take about 10-15 minutes.

Burn the Paper: Carefully light the piece of paper with the candle flame and place it into the cauldron to burn. As it burns, imagine your intention being sent out into the universe. Spend a few moments in meditation, focusing on your intention and the desired outcome. Feel gratitude as though your goal has already been achieved. When you’re ready, extinguish the candle safely and give thanks for the energy and assistance you’ve received.

Extinguishing the Fire: If you need to put out the fire before the alcohol is consumed, you can cover the cauldron with a fireproof lid to suffocate the flame. Do not use water, as it can cause the alcohol to splash. Do not add more alcohol to the cauldron while the fire is burning or still hot. Keep children and pets at a safe distance. Ensure proper ventilation if performed indoors, although outdoor use is strongly recommended.

By following these steps, you can safely create a controlled fire in a cauldron using Epsom salt and alcohol. Always prioritize safety and exercise caution throughout the process. Remember, the effectiveness of a spell is often tied to the focus and intention you bring to the process. Trust in your intuition and personal connection to the ritual, adapting these steps as needed to fit your practice and beliefs.

## Full Moon in Aries

*The Sky*

When the Moon is full in Aries, she stands opposite the Sun in Libra. This is fire reflected through the mirror of relationship — the warrior seen by the partner, the self lit by the other. Aries is cardinal fire, ruled by Mars: the first sign, the spark that says *I am, I begin, I move.* Libra answers from across the sky: *and never alone, even when you stand at the front.*

Wherever Aries lives in your chart is where the universe is asking you to start something. The Libran sun underneath asks whether you are starting from courage or from a wound.

*The Theme*

This moon is for the courage to begin again — to light the first fire of a thing you have been hesitating to start, or to relight a fire that has guttered low. Customize freely. This moon belongs to whatever in your life is asking to be born.

*The Altar*

Red and white. A red candle for Mars, a white candle for the Moon, set on either side of the cauldron. Dragon's blood incense on a charcoal disc. Carnelian or bloodstone at the cauldron's base if you work with stones.

*The Burn Kit*

Dragon's blood resin

Dried ginger root

Black peppercorns

Cayenne

Garlic skins

Juniper berries

Cedar shavings

*The Opening*

Light the red candle: *Mars, fire of will, be here.* Light the white candle: *Moon in Aries, full and reflecting, be here.* Light the cauldron. Watch the blue flame rise.

*Agni, sacred fire, mouth of the gods,* *Receive** what I give. Burn what is ready to burn.* *Make me brave again.*

*The Fire Ceremony*

Sit. Music low — Vedic drone, tabla, bowls, whatever does not compete with thought. Breathe with the flame.

When something rises — a hesitation, a name, an old grudge, a half-formed beginning — take a pinch from the burn kit and offer it. *This, into the fire. This beginning, into the fire so it may live.* Don't try to fill the time. Let things rise on their own.

As the flame begins to die, stop offering. Sit with the embers. This is where the Libra-Sun speaks — the quiet after effort, where you see what you actually did.

*The Seal*

*Agni, you have eaten what I gave. Carry it where it needs to go. Svaha.*

Let the candles burn down or snuff them gently. Drink water. Eat something. The ritual is complete.

## Full Moon in Aquarius

*The Sky*

The Moon is full in Aquarius, opposite the Sun in Leo. Air lit by fire — the future illuminated by the heart, the collective vision warmed by the individual flame. Aquarius is fixed air, ruled by Saturn in older tradition and Uranus in modern: the sign of the future, the collective, the strange and the necessary. Leo across the sky makes sure the visionary does not lose the heart that makes vision matter.

*The Theme*

This moon is for the future, the collective, the strange invitation, the new shape. It is also for releasing the old self-image so the next version can arrive. Customize freely.

*The Altar*

Electric blue and silver. A blue candle for the strange and electric, a silver candle for the Moon, with a touch of violet if you have it. Storax and lavender incense. Aquamarine, amethyst, or labradorite at the cauldron.

*The Burn Kit*

Lavender

Eucalyptus

Storax (or substitute frankincense)

Peppermint

Juniper berries

Lemongrass

Blue lotus (or substitute lotus seeds)

A few mint leaves fresh

*The Opening*

Light the blue candle: *Strange one, future-bringer, be here.* Light the silver candle: *Moon in Aquarius, full and electric, be here.* Light the cauldron.

*Agni, lightning fire, fire that arrives from nowhere,* *Receive** what I give. Burn the old shape so the new can arrive.*

*The Fire Ceremony*

Sit. Music strange — electronic, ambient, glitch, anything that does not sound like the past. Breathe and let your mind go somewhere it doesn't usually go.

When something rises — an old identity you've outgrown, a future-self trying to break through, a way of belonging that has stopped fitting, a vision of the collective you want to be part of — offer it. *This old shape, into the fire. This new arrival, into the fire so it may be welcomed.* Aquarius allows the strange offerings. Things you can't even name.

As the flame dies, feel the air around you. The Leo-Sun underneath reminds you: even the most futurist vision is for the warming of actual hearts.

*The Seal*

*Agni, you have eaten what I gave. Let the new arrive when it arrives. Svaha.*

Let the candles burn or snuff. The ritual is complete.

## Full Moon in Pisces

*The Sky*

The Moon is full in Pisces, opposite the Sun in Virgo. Water lit by earth — the ocean illuminated by the careful hand on the shore, the mystic kept whole by the tender. Pisces is mutable water, ruled by Jupiter in older tradition and Neptune in modern: the sign of dissolution, mystical union, dreamwork, the great sea where all rivers go. Virgo across the sky holds the shoreline so the mystic does not dissolve entirely.

*The Theme*

This moon is for dissolution, mystical union, dreamwork, the return to the great waters. It is also for releasing what you have been carrying that is not yours to carry. Customize freely.

*The Altar*

Sea-green and lavender. A blue-green candle for the deep waters, a lavender candle for the dreaming, white for the Moon. Jasmine and myrrh incense, or kyphi if you can find it. Amethyst, moonstone, aquamarine, or fluorite at the cauldron.

*The Burn Kit*

Jasmine flowers

Lotus seeds (or water lily petals)

Willow bark

Mugwort

Myrrh or kyphi blend

A pinch of kelp or seaweed

Lily petals

Blue lotus if available (otherwise more jasmine)

*The Opening*

Light the green candle: *Great waters, be here.* Light the lavender candle: *Moon in Pisces, full and dreaming, be here.* Light the cauldron.

*Agni, fire on water, fire that does not fight the sea,* *Receive** what I give. Take me into the great **fire-water**.*

*The Fire Ceremony*

Sit. Music oceanic — Vedic chant, deep chorale, anything that feels like dissolving without drowning. Breathe slow and let the edges of yourself soften.

When something rises — a sorrow that isn't yours, a piece of collective grief you have absorbed, a dream you can't quite remember, a longing for something you don't have words for, a piece of you that wants to return to source — offer it. *This burden, into the fire. This longing, into the fire so it may be carried.* You may weep. That is correct. Tears and fire are not enemies tonight.

As the flame dies, sit with the embers and the silence and the dream. The Virgo-Sun underneath holds the practical thread that brings you back. You do not have to dissolve all the way. You only have to remember you are made of water.

*The Seal*

*Agni, you have eaten what I gave. Let what is not mine return to its source. Let what **is** mine remain. Svaha.*

Let the candles burn down. Drink water immediately — Pisces moons can leave you ungrounded. The ritual is complete.
