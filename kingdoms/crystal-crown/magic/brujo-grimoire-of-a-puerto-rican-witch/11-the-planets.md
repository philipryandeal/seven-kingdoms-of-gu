# The Planets

Every brujo I have ever respected has had a relationship with the planets. Not an intellectual understanding — a *relationship*. The way you have a relationship with a person you see every week, whose moods you have learned to read, whose gifts you know how to receive, and whose bad days you know how to navigate. The planets are not symbols on a chart. They are forces that move through your body, your altar, your workings, and your life every single day, whether you are paying attention to them or not. The difference between a brujo who pays attention and one who does not is the difference between a sailor who reads the wind and one who rows against it and wonders why he is tired.

What follows is not a textbook. This is what I have learned about each planet through twenty years of practice — through tracking their hours on my altar, timing my workings to their rhythms, watching what happens when I honor them and what happens when I ignore them. I am going to tell you what each planet *is* in my experience, how it moves, what it wants, and how to work with it. After that I will give you the correspondences — the colors, herbs, stones, metals, and timing that tradition assigns to each one. Those lists are tools. What I am telling you first is how to hold the tools.

One more thing before we begin. In my tradition, the planets are not separate from the Orisha. Orunmila is the Sun. Eshu is Mercury. Oshun is Venus. Ogun is Mars. Obatala is Jupiter. Obaluaye is Saturn. Yemoja is the Moon. These are not metaphors. The Orisha *are* planetary forces expressed as spiritual persons, and the planets *are* Orisha expressed as celestial bodies. When I light a candle in the hour of Venus, I am lighting a candle for Oshun whether I say her name or not. When I work in the hour of Mars, Ogun is in the room. You do not have to use the Yoruba names. But you should know that the tradition I carry understands these forces as alive, as persons, as beings who will respond to you if you approach them with respect and consistency. That changes how you work.

## The Sun

The Sun is the boss. Every other planet in this chapter borrows its light, orbits its gravity, or defines itself in relationship to it. When you understand the Sun, you understand the center that holds everything else in place.

In Ifá, the Sun is Orunmila — the witness of creation, the eleri ipin, the one who was present when every soul chose its destiny. That is not a decorative title. It tells you what solar energy actually *does*: it witnesses, it illuminates, it makes things visible that would otherwise remain hidden. The Sun does not create your destiny. It reveals the destiny you already chose. That is why solar workings are workings of clarity, authority, and self-knowledge. You are not asking the Sun to give you something you do not have. You are asking it to show you what you already carry.

I work with the Sun on Sundays, in the hour of the Sun, and I will tell you what twenty years of that practice has taught me: the Sun does not negotiate. The Sun does not meet you halfway. The Sun is what it is at the volume it is, and your job is to rise to that volume or step out of the light. Solar workings that fail almost always fail because the practitioner was not ready to see what the Sun showed them. You ask for clarity and then the clarity arrives and it is uncomfortable — you see that the relationship is over, that the job is wrong, that you have been lying to yourself about something important. That is the Sun doing exactly what you asked. Do not blame the Sun for answering your prayer.

The best solar work I have ever done has been simple. A yellow candle lit at dawn on a Sunday. A glass of water charged in direct sunlight for an hour and then drunk slowly, with intention. A prayer spoken facing east at sunrise, asking Orunmila to show me what I need to see today — not what I want to see, what I *need* to see. Those three things, done consistently, will teach you more about solar magic than any elaborate ritual.

**Day:** Sunday

**Hours:** Use a planetary hours calculator — the Sun rules the first hour after sunrise on Sunday, then cycles through the Chaldean order throughout the week.

**Colors:** Gold, yellow, orange, bright white

**Herbs:** St. John's Wort, calendula, sunflower, cinnamon, frankincense, bay laurel, saffron, orange peel, rosemary

**Stones:** Citrine, sunstone, amber, tiger's eye, yellow topaz

**Metal:** Gold

**Incense:** Frankincense, copal, cinnamon

**Orisha:** Orunmila

**Guna:** Sattvic — the Sun is clarity, truth, consciousness itself

**Best workings:** Clarity, authority, leadership, self-knowledge, vitality, health, success, visibility, oaths, consecrations, anything that needs to be *seen*

## The Moon

If the Sun is the boss, the Moon is the one who actually runs the house.

I cannot overstate how important the Moon is to a brujo's practice. She governs your timing. She governs your herbs — when you gather them matters as much as what you gather. She governs your emotional weather, your dream life, your mediumship, and the receptivity of every working you perform. A brujo who does not track the Moon is a brujo who is guessing, and guessing is not a method.

In Ifá, the Moon is Yemoja — the mother of waters, the guardian of children, the one whose domain is the ocean and the womb. Yemoja does not ask you to be strong. She asks you to be *open*. Lunar energy is receptive energy. It is the energy of receiving messages, receiving healing, receiving the spirits when they come to mount you or speak through you. Every medium I have ever known has had strong lunar energy in their chart, because mediumship is, at its root, a lunar capacity — the ability to become a mirror, to reflect what the spirits pour into you without distorting it with your own noise.

I track the Moon every single day. I know which nakshatra she is in before I plan any working. I know whether she is waxing or waning before I decide whether to build or diminish. I know when she is full and when she is dark, and I work differently on those nights because the energy is different and pretending otherwise is foolish. This is not obsessive. This is basic. A farmer knows what season it is before he plants. A brujo knows what the Moon is doing before he works. Same principle.

Practically, here is what I have learned: the waxing Moon builds. Anything you want to grow — money, love, health, influence, spiritual power — you work it during the waxing phase. The full Moon reveals and charges. Whatever has been growing reaches its peak expression, and anything you leave in her light absorbs her energy. The waning Moon diminishes. Anything you want to reduce — illness, debt, an enemy's power, a bad habit, a spiritual blockage — you work it during the waning phase. The dark Moon is for rest, divination, and listening. She is not absent. She is *listening*, and so should you.

The nakshatras — the twenty-seven lunar mansions — give you even more precision. The Moon spends roughly one day in each mansion as she travels the zodiac, and each mansion has a different quality. I have given you the full nakshatra table elsewhere in this book. Use it. Track the Moon's mansion for a year alongside your workings and you will see patterns that no book can teach you.

**Herbs:** Jasmine, mugwort, chamomile, willow, lemon balm, lotus, white sandalwood, cucumber, moonflower

**Stones:** Moonstone, pearl, selenite, opal, clear quartz

**Metal:** Silver

**Incense:** Jasmine, sandalwood, camphor

**Orisha:** Yemoja

**Guna:** Sattvic

**Best workings:** Emotional healing, fertility, dream work, mediumship development, divination, charging tools and waters, ancestor communication, protection of children and families, anything involving receptivity, intuition, or the subconscious

## Mercury

Mercury is the fastest planet, and he will teach you something about speed if you let him — specifically, that speed without direction is just chaos with good shoes on.

In Ifá, Mercury is Eshu — the messenger, the opener of roads, the trickster, the one who stands at every crossroads and decides what gets through and what does not. Eshu is the first Orisha honored in every ceremony because without him, no message reaches its destination. No prayer lands. No offering arrives. Eshu is the postal service of the cosmos, and if you do not pay him first, your mail does not get delivered.

That is exactly what Mercury does in your workings. Mercury governs communication — whether your petition gets heard, whether your spell's intention arrives clearly at its destination, whether the deal closes, whether the conversation goes the way you need it to go. I have seen beautifully constructed workings fail because the practitioner did not pay attention to Mercury. The timing was wrong. The intention was muddled. The words were sloppy. Mercury does not care how much frankincense you burn if your petition is vague. He delivers exactly what you send, so you had better send something clear.

I work with Mercury on Wednesdays, and I will tell you what his hour feels like on my altar: quick, electric, slightly amused. Mercury is not solemn. He is not heavy. He moves fast and he expects you to keep up. The best Mercury workings I have done have been short, sharp, and precise — a single candle, a clearly written petition, a specific request for a specific communication to land in a specific way. Mercury does not do sprawling ritual. He does a clean strike.

Mercury retrograde is real, and I do not care how many skeptics roll their eyes at it. Three to four times a year, Mercury appears to move backward through the zodiac, and during those periods communication gets tangled, technology misbehaves, old conversations resurface, and plans made during the retrograde have a tendency to need revision later. I do not stop working during Mercury retrograde. I adjust. I double-check my words. I revisit rather than initiate. I use retrograde as a review period — go back through your grimoire, revisit old workings, tie up loose ends. That is Mercury asking you to slow down and look at what you missed the first time.

**Day:** Wednesday

**Colors:** Orange, mixed and iridescent colors, yellow-green

**Herbs:** Lavender, fern, fennel, dill, lemongrass, parsley, marjoram, caraway, peppermint

**Stones:** Agate, citrine, fluorite, aventurine, peridot

**Metal:** Mercury (symbolic — do not handle the metal, it is toxic) / quicksilver representations, mixed metals

**Incense:** Lavender, mastic, storax, sandalwood

**Orisha:** Eshu

**Guna:** Rajasic — Mercury is movement, exchange, restless intelligence

**Best workings:** Communication, business deals, contracts, travel, study, writing, eloquence, divination, opening roads, closing deals, sending messages (spiritual and mundane), technology, networking, anything involving words, speed, or information

## Venus

Venus is the planet I underestimated the longest, and she made me pay for it.

I came up in traditions that emphasize warrior energy — Ogun, Mars, iron, fire, the nganga, the combative side of the work. That is real and that is necessary and I do not apologize for any of it. But for years I neglected Venus, and what I got for that neglect was a life that was powerful but not sweet. Effective but not beautiful. Defended but not nourished. Venus taught me — slowly, because she is patient in a way Mars is not — that a brujo who can only fight is a brujo who is only half alive.

In Ifá, Venus is Oshun — the river goddess, the keeper of sweetness, fertility, beauty, love, diplomacy, and honey. Oshun is not soft. That is the mistake people make. Oshun went to war. Oshun negotiated with the Orisha when the world was out of balance and the other gods had failed. Oshun wields her beauty and her sweetness as instruments of power, and if you confuse sweetness with weakness, you will learn the difference the hard way. Venus is the same. She is the planet of attraction, and attraction is a force — not a decoration.

I work with Venus on Fridays, and her hour feels like honey on the altar. Warm, slow, golden. Venus workings take their time. They are not instant. Love work, beauty work, money work rooted in attraction rather than force — these are Venus workings, and they unfold over days and weeks, drawing what you need toward you rather than pushing you toward it. The difference matters. Mars gets what it wants by going out and taking it. Venus gets what it wants by becoming so magnetic that what she needs walks through her door on its own.

The best Venus work I know is the simplest: a green or pink candle dressed with honey and cinnamon, lit on a Friday in the hour of Venus, with a prayer to Oshun or to the principle of beauty and sweetness in your life. Ask to receive. Ask to be nourished. Ask for the thing you need to come to you gently. Then let it come.

**Colors:** Green, pink, rose, copper, soft gold

**Herbs:** Rose, vanilla, jasmine, apple, basil (sweet basil), cinnamon, cardamom, hibiscus, damiana, yarrow, myrtle

**Stones:** Rose quartz, emerald, malachite, jade, copper-based stones, rhodonite

**Metal:** Copper

**Incense:** Rose, benzoin, vanilla, sandalwood, jasmine

**Orisha:** Oshun

**Guna:** Rajasic — Venus is desire, attraction, the pull toward beauty and union

**Best workings:** Love, attraction, beauty, fertility, art, music, diplomacy, reconciliation, sweetening jars and honey work, self-worth, financial abundance through attraction, anything involving pleasure, harmony, or the magnetic drawing of what you desire

## Mars

Mars is the planet I did not have to learn to love. Mars loved me first.

I am a Palero. I work with iron, with the nganga, with the dead who fight on my behalf. I am a child of traditions that understand that spiritual combat is real, that enemies exist, and that a brujo who cannot protect himself and his people is a brujo who will be eaten alive. Mars is the planet of all of that — the fighter, the protector, the one who does not flinch, the iron in your spine.

In Ifá, Mars is Ogun — the lord of iron, the opener of roads by force, the blacksmith, the warrior, the one who clears the path with a machete when diplomacy has failed. Ogun is the patron of soldiers, surgeons, and anyone who works with metal and fire. He is not evil. He is not cruel. He is *necessary*. There are situations in your life — and in your spiritual work — where the only answer is force. Where the enemy will not stop unless you stop them. Where the blockage will not move unless you break it. Where the protection your family needs is not a prayer but a wall. That is Mars. That is Ogun. That is the iron.

I work with Mars on Tuesdays, and his hour feels like a blade being sharpened. The air tightens. The altar heats up. Mars does not do gentle. If you light a candle in the hour of Mars for a protection working, you will feel the difference immediately — there is a hardness in the room, a readiness, a sense that something is standing between you and whatever is coming. That is the energy you are harnessing.

But here is what Mars has also taught me: the brujo who stays in Mars all the time burns out and burns others. Mars is a tool, not a lifestyle. You pick it up when you need it and you set it down when the work is done. A man who carries a machete everywhere eventually cuts someone who did not need cutting. I have watched practitioners get addicted to combative work — always fighting, always defending, always looking for the next enemy — and it hollowed them out. Mars without Venus is a blade without a hand. You need both.

**Day:** Tuesday

**Herbs:** Rue, garlic, black pepper, cayenne, ginger, dragon's blood, nettle, tobacco, basil (hot basil / holy basil), thistle, pine, mustard seed

**Stones:** Carnelian, bloodstone, red jasper, garnet, iron-bearing stones, hematite

**Metal:** Iron, steel

**Incense:** Dragon's blood, tobacco, pine, cedar, camphor

**Orisha:** Ogun

**Guna:** Tamasic — Mars is raw force, destruction that clears the ground for something new

**Best workings:** Protection, defense, spiritual warfare, exorcism, banishing, court cases (when you need to fight, not negotiate), breaking blockages by force, physical strength and vitality, courage, competition, cutting ties, anything involving conflict, boundaries, or the assertion of will

## Jupiter

Jupiter is the planet of abundance, and abundance is not what most people think it is.

Most people think abundance means more money. Jupiter does not care about your bank account — not specifically. Jupiter is the principle of *expansion itself*. Whatever Jupiter touches, it makes bigger. If your life is good, Jupiter makes it better. If your life is a mess, Jupiter makes it a bigger mess. Jupiter does not judge. Jupiter amplifies. That is why the old astrologers called Jupiter the "greater benefic" but also warned against its excesses — Jupiter in the wrong place at the wrong time produces overreach, overconfidence, and the particular kind of failure that comes from biting off more than you can chew while feeling absolutely certain you can handle it.

In Ifá, Jupiter is Obatala — the king of the white cloth, the father of the Orisha, the one who shapes human beings from clay in the womb. Obatala is wisdom, patience, moral authority, and the long view. He is the elder at the head of the table who does not need to raise his voice because everyone already knows he is in charge. That is Jupiterian energy at its best: expansive authority grounded in wisdom, generosity that flows from genuine abundance rather than performance, the kind of success that builds over years rather than days.

I work with Jupiter on Thursdays, and his hour feels like a door opening. Not a dramatic door — a quiet one. A subtle widening of possibility. The best Jupiter workings I have done have been workings for long-term growth, for wisdom, for the expansion of my teaching and my reach. Jupiter is not fast money. Jupiter is the working you do in January that bears fruit in September. Jupiter is the prayer for wisdom that changes the trajectory of your whole year. Jupiter rewards patience and punishes haste.

If you are doing a working for genuine abundance — not a quick money spell but a foundational shift toward prosperity — Jupiter on a Thursday in the hour of Jupiter is your best celestial ally. Dress a blue candle with sage and a touch of honey. Ask Obatala or the principle of wise expansion to open the road ahead of you — not to flood you with more than you can carry, but to grow what you already have until it is enough and then some.

**Day:** Thursday

## Saturn

Saturn is the planet nobody wants to talk about, and that is exactly why you need to talk about him.

Every other planet in this chapter will give you something if you ask nicely. Saturn will give you something whether you ask or not, and what he gives you is the truth about what you have built and whether it will hold. Saturn is the building inspector of the cosmos. He does not care about your intentions. He does not care about your feelings. He cares about your structure — your discipline, your foundations, your follow-through, the quality of your actual work as opposed to the work you told yourself you were going to do. Saturn will knock down anything that is not built right, and he will do it on his own schedule, not yours.

In Ifá, Saturn is Obaluaye — the lord of disease and healing, the one who governs the earth itself, the old man wrapped in straw who carries both the plague and the cure. Obaluaye is not cruel. He is honest, and honesty at the scale he operates on looks like cruelty to people who are not ready for it. When Obaluaye takes something from your life — a relationship, a job, a belief, a foundation you were standing on — he is showing you that what he took was already rotten. He did not destroy it. He revealed that it was already destroyed. You just had not looked yet.

I work with Saturn on Saturdays, and I will tell you the truth: his hour is heavy. It feels like weight on the shoulders. Like gravity increased slightly. Like the room got more serious. Saturn does not play. When I do Saturn workings, I do them with respect and with sobriety and with the understanding that I am working with the slowest, most patient, and most unforgiving force in the planetary system. Saturn rewards discipline over decades. Saturn punishes shortcuts immediately.

The best Saturn workings are workings for structure, endurance, protection through strength, and the kind of hard self-honesty that nobody enjoys but everybody needs. If you are building something that needs to last — a business, a spiritual practice, a body of work, a relationship that will survive pressure — work with Saturn. Light a black candle on a Saturday in the hour of Saturn. Offer myrrh. Ask for the strength to see clearly and build well. Do not ask Saturn to be kind. Ask Saturn to be *accurate*. That is his gift, and it is worth more than kindness.

**Day:** Saturday

**Herbs:** Myrrh, comfrey, mullein, horsetail, patchouli, cypress, Solomon's seal, boneset, valerian, vetiver

**Stones:** Obsidian, onyx, jet, hematite, black tourmaline, smoky quartz

**Incense:** Myrrh, patchouli, cypress, vetiver

## Uranus

Here is where we cross a line in the tradition.

Uranus, Neptune, and Pluto are not part of traditional Vedic astrology. The Vedic system works with seven visible planets — Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn — plus the two lunar nodes, Rahu and Ketu. The outer planets were not visible to the naked eye and were not discovered until the modern era. Many Vedic astrologers do not use them at all.

I use them. I use them because I have watched them work in charts, I have felt them in practice, and I am not interested in pretending that a force does not exist because it was discovered after the tradition was codified. The tradition is alive. It grows. It absorbs what is true and discards what is not. The outer planets are true, and any brujo who has lived through a Pluto transit knows it in his bones.

That said, I do not have Orisha correspondences for the outer planets. The traditional Yoruba-to-planet mapping stops at Saturn. I am not going to fabricate correspondences that my tradition does not carry. What I can tell you is how these forces move and what they do to your life and your practice.

Uranus is the planet of sudden change. He is the lightning bolt, the revolution, the day the thing you thought was permanent turns out to be temporary. Uranus does not warn you. Uranus does not prepare you. Uranus shows up and rearranges the furniture while you are still sitting in the chair.

In my experience, Uranus governs the moments in a brujo's life when the spirits break his practice open and force him into something new. The initiation you did not plan for. The revelation that came sideways. The moment your whole cosmology shifted because you saw something you could not unsee. Every practitioner I know who has gone through a major spiritual transformation had Uranus active in their chart when it happened.

You cannot schedule Uranus workings the way you can schedule a Jupiter or Venus working. Uranus is not tame. What you can do is stay awake to his transits — know when Uranus is moving through a sensitive part of your chart and prepare yourself for disruption. Not bad disruption necessarily. Sometimes the lightning bolt illuminates a road you could not see in the dark. But disruption.

**Colors:** Electric blue, ultraviolet, silver-white, iridescent

**Herbs:** Ginseng, gotu kola, coffee, star anise, clove (in its electric aspect), eucalyptus

**Stones:** Labradorite, aquamarine, clear quartz (in its flash aspect), moldavite, rainbow obsidian

**Incense:** Eucalyptus, camphor, any sharp aromatic that clears the sinuses and wakes you up

**Best workings:** Breaking patterns, innovation, liberation from stagnant situations, sudden insight, awakening, anything involving radical change or the courage to be different. Work with Uranus when you need to *shatter* something, not when you need to build.

## Neptune

Neptune is the planet of the mystic, and the mystic's greatest danger is drowning.

Neptune governs everything that dissolves boundaries — dreams, visions, psychic perception, mystical union, artistic inspiration, addiction, delusion, and the particular kind of spiritual grandiosity that makes a practitioner believe they are more advanced than they are. Neptune is the deepest water in the planetary system, and deep water can drown you as easily as it can baptize you. The difference is whether you can swim.

Every brujo works with Neptune whether he names it or not. When you sit at your bóveda and the boundary between you and the dead thins and you feel them *there*, pressing close, speaking in impressions and images and sudden emotions that are not yours — that is Neptune. When you enter a dream and the dream is more real than waking life and you come back with information you could not have known — that is Neptune. When you lose yourself in ceremony, when the lwa or the Orisha mount you and your individual identity dissolves into something larger — that is Neptune. Mediumship is a Neptunian capacity. So is prophecy. So is art. So is madness.

The danger with Neptune is losing the shore. I have seen practitioners dissolve into their mystical experiences and not come back intact. They lose their grounding. They start believing every vision is a message from God. They stop eating properly, stop sleeping properly, stop taking care of the material world because the immaterial world is so much more interesting. That is Neptune without Saturn — the ocean without a shore. You need both. Every mystic needs a discipline. Every dreamer needs a practice. Every visionary needs a grimoire where they write down what they saw so they can read it back with sober eyes the next morning and decide what was real.

I do not schedule regular Neptune workings the way I do with the inner planets. Neptune is always present when I do mediumship work, when I do dream work, when I sit at the bóveda in the dark and open myself to whatever comes. My relationship with Neptune is less about conscious ritual and more about maintaining the practice of *boundaries within the boundless*. Ground yourself before you open up. Ground yourself after you close down. Keep your body fed, your water fresh, your self-altar tended, your feet on the earth. Then go as deep as Neptune takes you.

**Colors:** Sea green, lavender, soft violet, ocean blue, translucent

**Herbs:** Mugwort, lotus, seaweed, blue lotus, passionflower, kava, jasmine, poppy (symbolic — do not ingest), water lily

**Stones:** Amethyst, larimar, aquamarine, pearl, lepidolite, blue lace agate

**Incense:** Jasmine, frankincense (in its mystical aspect), sandalwood, lotus, mugwort

**Best workings:** Mediumship development, dream work, psychic protection, artistic inspiration, spiritual surrender, deep emotional healing, any working that requires opening the boundary between the seen and unseen worlds. Always ground before and after. Neptune without grounding is a shipwreck waiting to happen.

## Pluto

Pluto is the planet of the underworld, and every brujo who works with the dead already knows him.

The astronomers demoted Pluto to a dwarf planet in 2006. The cosmos did not receive the memo. Pluto still transits. Pluto still transforms. Pluto still tears down what is false and builds cathedrals out of the rubble, and anyone who has lived through a hard Pluto transit — Pluto conjunct your Moon, Pluto square your Sun — knows that whatever the International Astronomical Union says about classification, Pluto is as real as a car wreck and twice as transformative.

Pluto is death and rebirth. Not metaphorical death — *actual* encounter with the forces of destruction and regeneration that run underneath everything you think is permanent. Pluto governs the nganga. Pluto governs the cemetery. Pluto governs the moment you stand at a grave at midnight and negotiate with the dead for power and you understand, in your body and not just your mind, that everything you have will be taken from you eventually and the only question is what you do with it while it is yours.

I do not work with Pluto lightly. Pluto workings are the heavy artillery. They are for the moments when something in your life needs to die — not be adjusted, not be improved, not be gently released, but *killed* so that something else can be born in its place. The end of a chapter. The destruction of a pattern that has been running your life for years. The confrontation with a shadow that you have been pretending does not exist. Pluto does not do half measures. When you invite Pluto to the altar, you are inviting the force that strips the flesh from the bone and shows you what the skeleton looks like. That is terrifying. It is also, sometimes, exactly what you need.

The Palero understands Pluto instinctively. The nganga *is* Pluto — a vessel packed with death, with bones and grave dirt and the spirits of the dead, worked by a man who has looked at mortality and decided to make it his ally rather than his enemy. Every time I feed the nganga, I am working with Plutonian energy. Every time I sit with the Nfumbe and listen to what the dead want, I am in Pluto's house.

If you are going to do a Pluto working — and you should only do it when you genuinely need transformation at the root level — do it on a Saturday night, in the darkest hours. Light a black candle. Burn dragon's blood and myrrh. Sit with whatever you need to kill in your life and name it honestly. Give it to the fire. Let it go. And understand that what comes after will not look like what came before. That is the point. That is Pluto's only gift: he makes sure you cannot go back.

**Colors:** Black, deep maroon, dark crimson, the color of dried blood

**Herbs:** Dragon's blood, wormwood, belladonna (symbolic — extremely toxic, do not ingest or handle), mandrake, patchouli, myrrh, black cohosh, pomegranate

**Stones:** Obsidian, black tourmaline, garnet, bloodstone, smoky quartz, moldavite, malachite (in its transformative aspect)

**Incense:** Dragon's blood, myrrh, patchouli, copal (in its funerary aspect)

**Best workings:** Deep transformation, shadow work, ancestral healing at the root level, breaking generational patterns, confronting death, working with the dead (especially the powerful and ancient dead), ending things that refuse to end, rebirth after destruction. Handle with respect. Pluto does not negotiate.

## A Final Word on the Planets

You now have ten forces mapped out in front of you. Seven traditional planets plus three outer planets. Each one is an energy, a tool, a relationship you can develop over time. Do not try to work with all of them at once. That is like trying to play ten instruments simultaneously — you will make noise, not music.

Start with the one that calls you. Start with the one whose description made you feel something in your chest. Start with the one whose day feels right, whose hour you can carve out of your week, whose herbs you already have in your kitchen. Light one candle on one day in one hour and sit with that planet for a month. Track what happens in your grimoire. Then add another. Then another.

Over time you will develop a planetary practice that is as unique to you as your fingerprint. You will discover that you run hot with certain planets and cold with others. You will find that your Mars workings hit like a hammer but your Saturn workings need more patience. You will learn your own sky. And a brujo who knows his own sky — who has mapped the territory through practice rather than theory — is a brujo who can time any working to any celestial window and trust that the wind is at his back.

The planets are always moving. Learn to move with them.
