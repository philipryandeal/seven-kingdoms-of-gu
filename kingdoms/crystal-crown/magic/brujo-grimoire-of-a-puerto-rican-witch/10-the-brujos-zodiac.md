# The Brujo's Zodiac

Before you can time a working, you need to understand the clock.

The zodiac is the clock. It is the belt of sky through which the Sun, the Moon, and the planets travel as seen from Earth — a circle of three hundred and sixty degrees divided into twelve signs of thirty degrees each. Every celestial body you will work with in this book is, at any given moment, somewhere in that circle. Knowing where they are — which sign, which degree, which nakshatra — is what allows you to time your workings to the sky instead of guessing.

This chapter gives you the orientation you need before we move into the planets, the Moon, and the practical system of putting it all together. Think of it as learning to read the map before you walk the territory.

## Two Zodiacs, One Sky

There are two zodiacs in use today, and you need to understand the difference because it affects everything in this book.

**The Tropical Zodiac** is the system used by most Western astrologers. It anchors the zodiac to the seasons — specifically, to the vernal equinox. The first day of spring (in the Northern Hemisphere) is defined as zero degrees Aries, and the rest of the signs follow from there in thirty-degree increments. This system made perfect sense two thousand years ago when it was codified, because at that time the vernal equinox actually occurred in the constellation Aries. The map and the territory matched.

They no longer match. Due to the precession of the equinoxes — the slow wobble of the Earth's axis — the vernal equinox has drifted backward through the constellations at a rate of about one degree every seventy-two years. Today, when a Western astrologer says the Sun is in Aries, the Sun is actually in the constellation Pisces. The tropical zodiac is measuring the seasons. It is no longer measuring the stars.

**The Sidereal Zodiac** is the system used in Vedic (Jyotish) astrology and the system I use in my practice. It anchors the zodiac to the actual positions of the fixed stars. When I say the Moon is in Taurus, the Moon is actually in front of the stars of Taurus. The map matches the territory.

The difference between the two systems is called the **ayanamsa** — the angular gap between where the tropical zodiac says a planet is and where it actually is against the stellar background. As of right now, that gap is roughly twenty-four degrees, which means your sidereal sign is approximately one sign back from your tropical sign. If you have always known yourself as a Gemini Sun in Western astrology, you may be a Taurus Sun in sidereal. This surprises people. It should not. The sky is what it is. The question is whether your system is looking at the sky or at the calendar.

## My Ayanamsa

Not all sidereal systems agree on exactly where the zodiac begins. Different traditions anchor to different stars, producing slightly different starting points. The Lahiri ayanamsa, used by most Indian astrologers, anchors to the star Spica. The Fagan-Bradley ayanamsa, used by some Western siderealists, anchors to a different stellar reference.

I use the **Dhruva ****ayanamsa**, which anchors the zodiac to the Galactic Center — Sagittarius A*, the supermassive black hole at the center of our galaxy. Four million solar masses. The gravitational anchor around which every star you can see orbits, including our Sun.

The Dhruva ayanamsa places the middle of the Vedic nakshatra Mula — the "root," the foundation — at the precise position of Sagittarius A*. I explained why this matters in the Astrologer, Diviner, and Herbalist chapter earlier in this book. The short version: the Maya pointed at this same spot and called it the root of the World Tree, the beginning of the Road to the Underworld. The Vedic tradition placed the nakshatra of the Root there. Two civilizations on opposite sides of the planet, looking at the same point in the sky, and calling it the same thing. When I anchor my zodiac to the Galactic Center, I am anchoring to the point that both traditions already identified as the origin.

If you want to practice as I do, you will need an astrology app that supports sidereal calculations. I recommend **Astro Gold** for detailed chart work and **Deluxe Moon** for tracking lunar phases and transits. In Astro Gold, you must manually change the settings from Tropical to Sidereal and select the appropriate ayanamsa. I use the **Usha Shashi** ayanamsa setting, which corresponds to the Galactic Center Middle of Mula calculation.

If you prefer to use the Western Tropical zodiac, that is your right and this book will still work for you. The correspondences — which herbs belong to which planet, which workings belong to which Moon phase — are based on the nature of the energies, not on which calculation method you use to find them. A Mars herb is a Mars herb whether you are reading a tropical or sidereal chart. But for the timing — for the precision work of catching the right nakshatra, the right planetary hour, the right celestial window — I encourage you to try sidereal. Let the sky be your sky.

## The Twelve Signs

Whether you use the tropical or sidereal zodiac, the twelve signs carry the same essential natures. What changes is *when* the Sun passes through each one. Here are the signs, their elements, and their core qualities — the energetic vocabulary you need to read the clock.

**Aries — Fire.** The initiator. Cardinal fire — the spark that says *I begin*. Aries energy is courageous, assertive, direct, impatient, and willing to go first. When the Moon or a planet transits Aries, the energy favors bold starts, competition, and decisive action. Not the time for subtlety.

**Taurus — Earth.** The builder. Fixed earth — the ground that holds. Taurus energy is patient, sensual, stubborn, and rooted in material reality. Transits through Taurus favor building wealth, planting seeds (literal and metaphorical), slow steady work, and anything that needs to last.

**Gemini — Air.** The messenger. Mutable air — the wind that carries information. Gemini energy is curious, quick, communicative, scattered, and hungry for variety. Transits through Gemini favor communication, study, networking, writing, and short-distance travel.

**Cancer — Water.** The nurturer. Cardinal water — the wave that pulls you home. Cancer energy is emotional, protective, intuitive, and deeply connected to family and ancestry. The Moon rules Cancer, and transits here are powerful for ancestor work, emotional healing, and tending the inner home.

**Leo — Fire.** The sovereign. Fixed fire — the flame that holds its ground. Leo energy is generous, creative, proud, dramatic, and oriented toward self-expression. Transits through Leo favor creative work, visibility, leadership, and claiming your authority.

**Virgo — Earth.** The tender. Mutable earth — the hand that refines. Virgo energy is analytical, meticulous, humble, service-oriented, and devoted to getting it right. Transits through Virgo favor healing work, purification, detailed craft, and the sacred ordinary.

**Libra — Air.** The partner. Cardinal air — the breath that seeks balance. Libra energy is diplomatic, aesthetic, relational, and oriented toward harmony and justice. Transits through Libra favor partnerships, negotiations, artistic work, and legal matters where fairness is the goal.

**Scorpio — Water.** The transformer. Fixed water — the deep that does not move until it moves everything. Scorpio energy is intense, penetrating, secretive, regenerative, and unafraid of death. Transits through Scorpio favor deep transformation, shadow work, sexual magic, ancestor work with the heavy dead, and anything that requires going beneath the surface.

**Sagittarius — Fire.** The seeker. Mutable fire — the flame that travels. Sagittarius energy is optimistic, philosophical, adventurous, and hungry for meaning. The Galactic Center lives in Sagittarius. Transits here favor spiritual expansion, long-distance travel, publishing, teaching, and the pursuit of truth.

**Capricorn — Earth.** The builder of mountains. Cardinal earth — the stone that climbs. Capricorn energy is ambitious, disciplined, patient, and willing to work for decades to build something that endures. Transits through Capricorn favor long-term planning, structure, authority, and the kind of work that pays off slowly but permanently.

**Aquarius — Air.** The revolutionary. Fixed air — the idea that will not bend. Aquarius energy is independent, innovative, humanitarian, eccentric, and committed to the future. Transits through Aquarius favor innovation, community work, breaking patterns, and anything that serves the collective rather than the individual.

**Pisces — Water.** The mystic. Mutable water — the ocean that dissolves all boundaries. Pisces energy is compassionate, intuitive, artistic, dreamy, and prone to dissolving the line between self and other. Transits through Pisces favor mediumship, dream work, artistic creation, spiritual surrender, and any working that requires you to let go of control and trust the current.

## The Three Gunas

Vedic philosophy classifies all energy — all matter, all thought, all action, everything that exists — into three fundamental qualities called the Gunas. Understanding the Gunas helps you read the character of any planet, any nakshatra, any herb, any working you perform. They are the adjectives of the cosmos.

**Sattva** is the quality of clarity, truth, harmony, and light. Sattvic energy is calm, aware, balanced, and oriented toward wisdom. The Sun and Moon are Sattvic planets. Jupiter is Sattvic. Sattvic workings are workings of illumination, healing, blessing, and spiritual development. When you sit at your bóveda in peace and your dead speak clearly and the guidance you receive is calm and true — that is Sattva.

**Rajas** is the quality of action, desire, passion, and movement. Rajasic energy is dynamic, ambitious, creative, and restless. Venus and Mercury are Rajasic planets. Rajasic workings are workings of attraction, creation, communication, and the pursuit of desire. When you light a candle for love and your heart is burning with want and the working moves fast and hot — that is Rajas.

**Tamas** is the quality of inertia, darkness, density, and raw material force. Tamasic energy is heavy, grounding, destructive in the sense of clearing ground, and associated with the physical world in its most solid form. Mars and Saturn are Tamasic planets. Tamasic workings are workings of protection, banishing, binding, destruction of obstacles, and confrontation with shadow. When you work the nganga and the room gets heavy and the Nfumbe speaks from the bottom of something old and dark — that is Tamas.

None of these qualities is good or bad. All three are necessary. A world without Tamas would have no physical reality. A world without Rajas would have no movement. A world without Sattva would have no awareness. The brujo works with all three, choosing the quality that matches the working. You would not bring Sattvic calm to a spiritual warfare operation any more than you would bring Tamasic heaviness to a healing blessing. Read the Guna. Match the energy. That is the skill.

## The Days and Their Planets

The seven-day week is a planetary inheritance. Each day belongs to a planet, and that association is not arbitrary — it is embedded in the languages themselves, carried from Hellenistic Egypt through Rome and into every European language that followed.

**Sunday — the Sun.** Solar energy. Clarity, authority, vitality, success. The best day for solar workings, for charging objects in sunlight, for oaths and declarations that need to be witnessed by the brightest light available.

**Monday — the Moon.** Lunar energy. Emotion, intuition, dreams, ancestors, the subconscious. The best day for bóveda work, for spiritual baths, for dream incubation, for any working that requires receptivity and listening.

**Tuesday — Mars.** Martial energy. Courage, protection, conflict, physical strength. The best day for protection workings, for spiritual warfare, for court cases where you need to fight, for breaking blockages by force.

**Wednesday — Mercury.** Mercurial energy. Communication, intellect, travel, commerce. The best day for workings involving contracts, negotiations, writing, study, sending messages, and opening channels of communication with spirits.

**Thursday — Jupiter.** Jovian energy. Abundance, wisdom, expansion, justice. The best day for workings of prosperity, long-term growth, legal matters where justice is on your side, spiritual development, and teaching.

**Friday — Venus.** Venusian energy. Love, beauty, attraction, pleasure, harmony. The best day for love workings, sweetening jars, beauty magic, artistic creation, and any working that draws through magnetism rather than force.

**Saturday — Saturn.** Saturnian energy. Structure, discipline, endurance, limitation, karmic justice. The best day for workings of binding, banishing, long-term protection, building foundations, and honest self-assessment. Also a powerful day for working with the old dead — the ancestors who are furthest back in your line.

When you combine the day with the planetary hour — the specific segment of that day ruled by the planet you want to work with — you double the planetary signal. A Venus working done on Friday in the hour of Venus is operating on the strongest possible Venusian frequency. The planetary hours chapter earlier in this book and the apps I recommended will give you the exact timing. Use them.

Now you have the map. The twelve signs tell you what kind of energy is moving through the sky. The three Gunas tell you the quality of that energy. The seven days tell you which planetary frequency is loudest. The ayanamsa tells you how to read the actual sky instead of an outdated calendar.
