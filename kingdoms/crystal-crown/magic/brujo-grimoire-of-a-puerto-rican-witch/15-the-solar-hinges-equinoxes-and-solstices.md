# The Solar Hinges: Equinoxes and Solstices

Four times a year, the sun does something specific. It crosses a line, or it stops and turns around. These are the solar hinges — two equinoxes when light and dark are even, two solstices when one of them runs to its extreme and then breaks. They are not religious holidays. They are astronomical facts. The earth tilts, the sun moves, and the spirits respond. This is older than any tradition that names them. It is older than us. A Brujo who knows how to read these four days knows how to ride the year instead of getting dragged behind it.

Here is what you need to understand: the dead notice. The muertos move differently at the hinges. So does the land. So does your own body, if you have been paying attention. The equinoxes have an exchange energy — balance tipping one way or the other, things crossing over. The solstices have a concentration energy — one force running to its limit and then collapsing into its opposite. Different engines. Different work. You do not have to celebrate these days. You do not have to throw a ritual every quarter like a clock striking. But if you are building a magical life and you ignore them, you are leaving free power on the table four times a year. Pick the ones that speak to you. Build the others in slowly. Over a few years you will have your own cycle, written in your grimoire, marked in your body, witnessed by your dead.

## Spring Equinox: The Planting

Light and dark are even, but the momentum is moving toward light. This is the up-tilt. Whatever you put into the ground now — literal soil or the soil of your life — has the whole bright half of the year to grow. Magically, this is when you plant. Not when you reap, not when you charge, not when you release. You plant. The energy is forward, green, hungry. It wants something to feed on. Good work for this hinge: new ventures, new relationships, new spiritual commitments, anything you want to grow over six months. Bad work for this hinge: endings, banishing, deep ancestral grief work. The current is going the wrong way.

Planting Working

1. Light your bóveda. A clear glass of fresh water for the muertos. Tell them what you are doing.

2. Prepare a small pot of soil, or a patch of garden you can return to.

3. Write your intention on a small piece of paper. One thing. Specific. Fold it small.

4. Bury the paper in the soil with a seed on top of it. Any seed — herbs are traditional, but anything that will grow.

5. Water it with water from the bóveda glass, or fresh water you have set briefly on the bóveda.

6. Speak the intention out loud, once, over the pot. Then thank the muertos.

7. Tend the plant through the season. The working lives as long as the plant lives. If it dies, do not panic — read what the dying is telling you and start again next year.

## Summer Solstice: The Charging

The longest day. The sun running to its limit. After tomorrow it begins to lose ground, but today it is at full power and that power is available to anyone awake enough to use it. Magically, this is when you charge and seal. Anything that needs solar force — tools, talismans, resguardos, working waters you will draw from through the dark half — gets laid out today and given the year's strongest light. Long-term workings get sealed now because the sun is loud enough to lock them in. Good work for this hinge: charging, consecrating, sealing, oaths, declarations, anything that needs a witness as bright as possible. Bad work for this hinge: introspection, grief work, anything that needs quiet. There is no quiet today.

Charging Working

1. Light your bóveda before dawn or early morning. Tell the muertos you are doing solar work today and you ask them to hold the house. They will. This is their job.

2. Gather everything that needs charging: tools, jewelry, your resguardo, jars of water, a bottle of Florida water, whatever you use.

3. Lay them out in direct sun — windowsill, porch, yard. From late morning until just before sundown if you can.

4. Around midday, go out to them. Speak over each item what it is for and what year it is being charged into.

5. Pass Florida water or clear rum over each item to seal. A small sip for yourself if you want.

6. Bring everything in before sundown. Do not leave them out overnight — the working is solar, not lunar.

7. Thank the muertos. Snuff the bóveda candle at your normal time, not before.

## Autumn Equinox: The Release

Light and dark are even again, but now the momentum has reversed. The down-tilt. From here the year falls toward darkness, and the muertos start walking closer. You can feel it in the air a week before the date if you know what you are listening for. Magically, this is when you release. Not banish — release. There is a difference. Banishing is for things attacking you. Releasing is for things you have been carrying that it is time to put down. Habits, griefs, debts of energy, identities you have outgrown. The earth is hungry for them. The composting half of the year is starting and the soil will digest whatever you give it. Good work for this hinge: release, completion, harvest accounting, anything you want the dark half to finish for you. Bad work for this hinge: starting new long projects. You are working with the wrong current.

Release Working

1. Light your bóveda. Add a black candle alongside your usual white one — this is one of the few times in the year I would recommend a black candle on the bóveda, and only because the muertos themselves are leaning closer at this hinge.

2. Take two pieces of paper. On the first, write what you are releasing. Be honest. The muertos can tell when you are lying.

3. On the second, write what you are carrying with you into the dark half. Things you are not done with yet. Keep this paper.

4. Burn the first paper, or bury it in the earth. Either works — fire is faster, earth is slower and gentler. Choose by what you are releasing.

5. Place the second paper under the bóveda glass. It will sit there through the dark half.

6. Sit with the muertos for a while. Do not ask for anything. Just sit. They will tell you things if you are quiet.

7. Thank them and close the working.

## Winter Solstice: The Vigil

The longest night. The sun at its weakest. After tomorrow it begins to return, but tonight it has fallen as far as it falls. This is the hinge where the dead are closest to the living all year. Not Halloween. Not the Day of the Dead. The winter solstice. The other dates are folk calendars layered over what the actual sky is doing. The sky is doing this. Magically, this is when you sit with your dead. Petitions made tonight, in the long dark, carry into the returning light. Whatever you ask the ancestors to walk you toward, they will begin to walk you toward at dawn. Good work for this hinge: ancestral communion, vigils, deep petitions for the year ahead, soul-level rest. Bad work for this hinge: anything fast. Anything performative. Anything that does not require silence.

Solstice Vigil

1. Dress your bóveda fully. Fresh white cloth, multiple glasses of clean water, photos of your dead if you have them, anything they liked in life — coffee, rum, a cigarette, a piece of bread.

2. Light candles at dusk. As many as you can keep an eye on. White, plus one for each named ancestor if you can manage it.

3. Sit. Name them out loud, one by one. Tell them about the year. Tell them what you are afraid of. Tell them what you want.

4. Keep the vigil as long as you can — full night if you are able, a few hours if you are not. Sleep next to the bóveda if you fall asleep.

5. Make one specific petition for the returning year. What do you want them to walk you toward? Say it clearly. Once is enough.

6. At dawn, or when you wake, thank them by name. Snuff the candles. Leave fresh water.

7. The bóveda stays heavy for a few days after. Do not dismantle it quickly. Let the visit linger.

## Keeping the Year

Mark these four dates in your grimoire. After each working, write down what you did, what you noticed, what the muertos said or did not say, what the weather was, what your body felt like. Year after year these entries become a personal cosmology — your own private astronomy, witnessed by your dead and recorded in your own hand. That is what a Brujo's grimoire is for. Not theory. Not other people's correspondences copied off the internet. Your observations, on your land, with your dead, across your years. The solar hinges are four of the best places to start. Plant in the spring. Charge in the summer. Release in the autumn. Sit with your dead in the winter. Do that for a decade and you will not need anyone to tell you what a witch is.

You will be one.
