# The Brujo's Grimoire

Let me be direct with you: if you are not keeping a written record of your practice, you are flying blind. I don't care how gifted you are, how strong your mediumship is, or how many years you've been working. Memory lies. Memory rearranges. Memory drops the details that turn out to matter most six months later. Your grimoire is not a diary. It is not a gratitude journal. It is the working record of a practicing brujo — a lab notebook for someone who does real things in the spirit world and needs to track what happened. What you did, what the spirits said, what worked, what failed, and what you don't understand yet.

I have kept a grimoire for over twenty years. The earliest ones are embarrassing. That's fine. The point was never to write something beautiful. The point was to write something *true*, and to write it down before my mind had a chance to edit, rationalize, or forget.

## Getting Set Up

Keep it simple. You need a notebook — physical or digital, I don't care which. I use bullet journaling as my method. If you don't know what that is, go to YouTube and search "bullet journal" and you'll find a hundred tutorials. The short version: it's a system of rapid notation using short entries, symbols, and an index that lets you find things later. It's fast, it's flexible, and it doesn't require you to write three paragraphs when three lines will do.

If you go physical, pick a notebook you actually like holding. You're going to be reaching for this thing at five in the morning with a dream dissolving out of your head, so it needs to live next to your bed and feel natural in your hand. Number your pages if they aren't pre-numbered. Keep a running index in the front so you can find entries by date and type.

If you go digital, I use the Day One app personally. It timestamps everything, it's searchable, and it syncs across devices. Use whatever works for you. The best grimoire is the one you actually write in.

Now — here's where I'm going to diverge from every other journaling guide you've ever read. I don't want you tracking your mood. I don't want gratitude lists. I want you tracking three things that actually make you a better brujo: your stars, your dead, and your dreams.

## Tracking Your Stars

If you've read the rest of this book, you know that I practice astro-rootwork — a system of herbal magic anchored to the lunar mansions, the planetary hours, and the galactic calendar. Your grimoire is where that system comes alive, because this is where you stop taking correspondences on faith and start building a record of what actually works *for you*.

Every tradition hands you tables. Moon in this mansion, use this herb, perform this kind of working. Fine. That's the starting point. But those tables were built by other practitioners based on their results, their spirits, and their geography. Your spirits may have different preferences. Your hands may run hotter with certain herbs than others. The only way to find out is to do the work, write it down, and read it back.

Here's the tracking format I use. One line per entry. Keep it tight.

## Date | Lunar Mansion | Planetary Hour | Herb(s) Used | Working Performed | Result

That's it. Six columns. You can do it in thirty seconds after a working. Here's what a real entry looks like:

*March 7 — Rohini — Hour of Venus — basil, cinnamon — money drawing bath before job interview — callback received within 48 hours, offered position March 12. Strong hit.*

*April 22 — Ashlesha — Hour of Saturn — rue, camphor — protection floor wash, front door and windows — felt immediate shift in house energy, but dog was agitated for two days. Effective but harsh. Try softer combination next time under same mansion.*

*May 15 — Punarvasu — Hour of Jupiter — lavender, chamomile — healing candle for my mother's knee surgery — surgery went well, recovery ahead of schedule. Doctor surprised. Keep this combination on file.*

Do this for a year. Three hundred and sixty-five entries. Then sit down and read the whole thing back to front. You will see patterns that no book can teach you. You'll see which lunar mansions consistently produce results for you and which ones fall flat. You'll see which herbs your spirits favor. You'll see which planetary hours your altar runs hot and which ones feel dead. You are building your own almanac — not inherited, not borrowed, *earned* through practice and verified by results. This is how folk magic has always worked. Someone did the thing, watched what happened, wrote it down, and passed it on. You are that someone now.

## Recording Your Dead

Every brujo works with the dead. If you have a bóveda — a spiritual altar for your ancestors and spirit guides — then you already have a place where communication happens. What most practitioners don't do is *write it down*. And that's where they lose the thread.

Here's what I've learned after two decades of ancestor work: the muertos are teaching you a curriculum. They are not giving you random messages. They are building toward something — a revelation, a correction, a new direction in your life — and they are doing it slowly, over weeks and months, because that's how they work. One Tuesday they give you a feeling. The next week, a name. The week after that, a dream. If you don't write each piece down as it comes, you will never see the pattern. You'll sit at your bóveda and feel like nothing is happening, when in fact something enormous is happening and you're just not tracking it.

Your grimoire entry after every bóveda session should include:

**Date and time you sat down.** The spirits keep schedules. You'll notice over time that certain days and times produce stronger communication.

**What you said.** Your prayers, your questions, your petitions. Write them down verbatim or as close as you can get. You need to know what you asked so you can recognize the answer when it comes.

**What you received.** This is the hard part and the important part. What did you hear? What did you feel? What images came? What emotions moved through you that weren't yours? Don't interpret. Don't analyze. Just record. "Felt heavy pressure on my shoulders." "Heard the name Maria." "Saw a road with palm trees." "Felt sudden grief that lifted after two minutes." Write it ugly. Write it confused. Write it honestly.

**What happened in the days after.** This is the part everyone forgets. Go back to your entry three or four days later and add a line about what followed. Did a sign appear? Did someone call you out of nowhere? Did something shift in your circumstances? The muertos often answer *after* the session, not during it, and if you're not circling back to your entries, you're missing the responses.

Here's a sample entry:

*June 3, 9:15pm — Sat at **bóveda** with a white candle and a glass of water. Asked my grandmother's spirit for guidance about whether to move forward with the new job. Prayed the Our Father and the Hail Mary, then sat in silence for twenty minutes. Felt warmth on the left side of my face. Heard nothing specific but had a strong impression of **the color green**. Restless afterward — couldn't sleep until 1am.*

*June 6 follow-up — Ran into an old coworker at the grocery store. She mentioned a position opening at her company. Green logo. Sent my resume that afternoon.*

Read six months of entries like this back to back. I promise you — the curriculum will be visible. Your dead are not silent. You just need a record long enough to hear the full sentence they're speaking one word at a time.

And track your signs. That hawk that appeared after you asked a question at the bóveda. That song on the radio that answered what you were thinking about in the car. The stranger who said exactly the right thing at exactly the right moment. Written down in your grimoire, these become evidence — a paper trail of spiritual communication that you can review, verify, and trust. Left unrecorded, they fade, and you start doubting your own mediumship. Don't let that happen. The grimoire is your witness.

## Mapping Your Dreams

This is the section most witchcraft books skip entirely, and it's one of the most important tools a brujo has. Dreaming is technology. It is one of the oldest technologies in every tradition I'm initiated in. In Vodou, the lwa speak in dreams. In Palo, the nfumbe communicate through sleep. In Espiritismo, the dream is the primary channel for spirit contact. In the witchcraft traditions of Europe and Latin America, dreaming is where the witch flies — not metaphorically, but practically. If you are not paying attention to your dreams, you are ignoring a direct line to the spirit world that is open every single night.

Your grimoire needs a dream log. Here's how it works.

Keep your notebook or your phone next to your bed. When you wake up — *before you check your phone, before you talk to anyone, before you get out of bed if you can help it* — write down the dream. All of it. Every detail you can capture. Dreams evaporate in minutes. If you wait until after breakfast, it's gone. Write fast, write messy, don't worry about making sense of it. You can analyze later.

Your dream entry should include:

**Date and time you woke up.** You'll notice that dreams at different hours of the night carry different weight. The ones that wake you at 3am are not the same as the ones you have just before dawn.

**The dream itself.** As much detail as you can capture. People, places, actions, dialogue, objects, colors, animals, weather. Everything.

**Feeling tone.** This is separate from the content. After you write down what happened, write one line about how it *felt*. Not what you think it means — how it felt in your body and your emotions while it was happening. "Felt peaceful." "Felt terrified but not in danger." "Felt like I was being watched by something ancient." The feeling tone is often the real message. The imagery is the delivery system.

Here's a sample entry:

*June 10, 4:22am — I was in a house I've never seen but it felt like mine. There was a long hallway with blue tile floors. At the end of the hallway, an old woman was sitting in a wooden chair. She was wearing a white headwrap and she didn't look at me but she **said** "you already have what you need." There was a smell of cigar smoke. A dog was sleeping at her feet.*

*Feeling tone: Calm. Authoritative. Like being called into the principal's office but knowing you're not in trouble.*

Over months of entries, you'll start recognizing patterns that no dream dictionary can give you. Recurring locations — these are places in the astral that you're being taken to repeatedly, and they matter. Recurring figures — these may be spirits introducing themselves to you. They often show up several times before they identify themselves by name. Recurring symbols — these become your *personal* dream vocabulary, a symbolic language between you and your spirits that is unique to your relationship with them.

For those of you who want to go further: lucid dreaming. This is the practice of becoming conscious inside the dream — knowing that you are dreaming while the dream is still happening. For a brujo, this is extraordinarily useful because it allows you to ask questions directly, to navigate deliberately, to interact with spirits with your full awareness rather than being a passive passenger.

There are many methods for developing lucidity. Here are two I recommend starting with:

**Reality checks.** Several times per day, in waking life, stop and genuinely ask yourself: *Am I dreaming right now?* Look at your hands. Read a sign, look away, and read it again. In waking life, the sign says the same thing. In a dream, it changes. If you build this habit while awake, you'll eventually do it inside a dream — and that's the moment you become lucid.

**Pre-sleep intention.** Before you fall asleep, hold a clear statement in your mind: "Tonight I will recognize that I am dreaming." Say it like you mean it. Some practitioners write it in their grimoire as the last entry of the day, right before they close their eyes. The act of writing it anchors the intention.

Track your lucidity rate in your grimoire: how many nights per month are you becoming conscious in the dream? That number should go up over time. If it isn't, adjust your practice. Try different induction methods. Some practitioners find that certain herbs taken as tea before bed — mugwort is the classic one — increase dream vividness and recall. Experiment, record, review.

## The Quarterly Review

Every three months, sit down with your grimoire and read the whole quarter back. Not to feel good about yourself. Not for "personal growth." You're reading for *patterns in your practice* that tell you where your power is concentrating and where your spirits are directing you.

What lunar mansions produced the strongest results this quarter? Which herbs came up again and again? What's the curriculum your ancestors have been teaching — what's the sentence they've been building one word at a time? What's happening in your dream life — are you visiting the same places, meeting the same figures, receiving the same symbols?

Your grimoire is a mirror. Not of who you are, but of *how you work*. And a brujo who understands how they work — who knows their strong nights and their dead hours, who knows which spirits come easy and which ones demand patience, who knows the herbs their hands run hot with and the mansions that light up their altar — that brujo is dangerous. In the best possible way.

Write it down. All of it. Every working, every whisper from the dead, every dream you wake up clutching like a letter you haven't opened yet. Your grimoire is the most honest document you'll ever produce. Treat it that way.
