# The Moon: Phases and Mansions

I gave the Moon her own section in the Planets chapter because she deserves to be understood as a planetary force — as Yemoja, as the mirror of the Sun, as the engine of receptivity and emotional intelligence. But the Moon does something no other planet does: she gives you a *daily* working calendar. The Sun moves one degree per day. Saturn takes nearly thirty years to complete the zodiac. The Moon crosses the entire sky in twenty-nine and a half days, changing signs every two and a half days, changing nakshatras every day. She is the fastest hand on the clock, and she is the one the brujo watches most closely.

This chapter gives you both layers of the Moon's timing system — her phases and her mansions — so you can use them together.

## The Eight Phases

The Moon's cycle from new to full and back to new takes approximately 29.5 days. Within that cycle, there are eight distinct phases, and each one carries a different quality of energy for your workings. You do not need to memorize this. You need a Moon phase app on your phone — Deluxe Moon or any equivalent — and you need the habit of checking it before you plan any working. After a few months it becomes second nature. After a year you will feel the phase in your body before you look at the screen.

**New Moon.** The Moon is between the Earth and the Sun, and her face is dark. You cannot see her. This is the phase of beginnings, of planting seeds in the dark, of setting intentions that will grow over the coming cycle. New Moon energy is quiet, inward, and potent in the way that a seed is potent — everything is compressed into potential. Best workings: setting intentions for the month ahead, beginning new projects, divination about what is coming, quiet altar work. Not the time for big, loud, outward-facing magic. The energy is going inward, and you should go with it.

**Waxing Crescent.** A thin sliver of light appears on the Moon's right side just after sunset. The seed has cracked open. The intention you set at the New Moon is beginning to take root. Best workings: early-stage nurturing of new projects, building momentum, prayer for growth.

**First Quarter (Half Moon, waxing).** Half the Moon's face is lit. The growth hits its first challenge — this is the phase where obstacles appear and decisions need to be made. Push through or pull back. Best workings: overcoming obstacles, making decisions, workings that require force to move past a block.

**Waxing Gibbous.** More than half the Moon is lit and growing toward full. The energy is building, accelerating, getting louder. Whatever you have been growing is becoming visible. Best workings: refinement, adjustment, doubling down on what is working, preparation for the Full Moon.

**Full Moon.** The Moon is opposite the Sun, fully illuminated. Peak energy. Maximum visibility. Whatever has been growing reaches its climax — for better or worse. The Full Moon *reveals*. She does not create the situation. She turns the lights on so you can see what is already there. Best workings: culmination, manifestation, charging tools and waters, divination, ceremony, fire cauldron work (see the Full Moon chapter later in this book), gratitude, celebration. The Full Moon is also the night when the dead walk closest — not the only night, but a powerful one. Bóveda work on the Full Moon carries extra weight.

**Waning Gibbous.** The light is receding. The peak has passed. The energy turns from building to distributing — sharing what you gathered, teaching what you learned, beginning the process of release. Best workings: gratitude, sharing knowledge, distributing abundance, beginning to let go of what no longer serves.

**Third Quarter (Half Moon, waning).** Half the Moon's face is lit, but the light is shrinking. This is the mirror of the First Quarter — another decision point, but this time the decision is about what to release. Best workings: cutting ties, ending what is finished, honest assessment of what did and did not work this cycle.

**Waning Crescent.** The last thin sliver of light before the Moon goes dark again. The energy is at its lowest, quietest, most inward point. Best workings: banishing, diminishing an enemy's power, reducing illness or pain, deep rest, surrender, preparation for the next New Moon. Some practitioners do no magic at all during this phase and simply rest. That is valid. The Moon is resting too.

**The core principle is simple:** Waxing Moon builds. Full Moon peaks. Waning Moon diminishes. New Moon rests and plants. Everything else is refinement. If you only remember that framework, you are already ahead of most practitioners who never look up.

## The Twenty-Seven Mansions

The nakshatras are the brujo's secret weapon.

Most practitioners who work with the Moon stop at the four phases — new, waxing, full, waning. That gives you four settings on your dial. The nakshatras give you twenty-seven. Instead of asking "is the Moon waxing or waning?" you can ask "is the Moon in Rohini tonight, which favors building finances and love? Or is she in Bharani, which favors destructive magic and exorcism? Or is she in Pushya, which some consider the most auspicious nakshatra of all and which favors every nourishing working except marriage?"

Twenty-seven distinct lunar energies. One per day, roughly. Each with its own ruling planet, its own Guna, and its own list of workings it supports best. This is precision timing, and it is the difference between "I did a money working on a waxing Moon" and "I did a money working on a waxing Moon with the Moon in Rohini in the hour of Jupiter on a Thursday." The second practitioner is catching a wave. The first is paddling in open water.

The nakshatras are lunar mansions in Vedic astrology — twenty-seven divisions of the ecliptic, each spanning 13 degrees and 20 minutes. The Moon passes through one nakshatra approximately every day as she travels the zodiac. Each nakshatra is ruled by one of nine planets in a repeating sequence: Ketu, Venus, Sun, Moon, Mars, Rahu, Jupiter, Saturn, Mercury. This sequence repeats three times to cover all twenty-seven.

Each nakshatra also carries a Guna — Sattvic, Rajasic, or Tamasic — which tells you the *quality* of the energy, not just the type. A Sattvic nakshatra supports clear, harmonious, spiritually elevated work. A Rajasic nakshatra supports dynamic, desire-driven, creative work. A Tamasic nakshatra supports heavy, grounding, combative, or materially focused work. None is better or worse. Each is right for different purposes.

## Using Phases and Mansions Together

The phases give you the broad strokes. The mansions give you the fine detail. Use them together.

A money-drawing working done on a waxing Moon in the mansion of Rohini (which favors building finances) on a Thursday (Jupiter's day) in the hour of Jupiter — that working is catching a wave with every current running in your favor. A protection working done on a waning Moon in the mansion of Bharani (which favors destructive magic and spiritual warfare) on a Tuesday (Mars's day) in the hour of Mars — that working is hitting with the full weight of the sky behind it.

You will not always get perfect alignment. Life does not wait for the ideal celestial window. Sometimes the money is needed *now* and the Moon is in the wrong mansion and it is Wednesday instead of Thursday. Do the working anyway. Imperfect timing with strong intention beats perfect timing with weak intention every time. But when you *can* time it — when the situation allows you to wait a day or two for the right mansion, the right hour, the right day — do it. The difference in results will speak for itself.

Track everything in your grimoire. Date, phase, mansion, planetary hour, what you did, what happened. After a year of this, you will have your own almanac — not inherited, not borrowed, earned through practice and verified by results. That almanac is the most valuable magical tool you will ever own.
