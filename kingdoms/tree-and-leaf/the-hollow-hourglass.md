# The Hollow Hourglass

*Cache #004 · A journey cache by Jonathan Bear · November 28, 2026*

*Worldbuilding proposal, subject to the founder's blessing.*

At the edge of Tree and Leaf, where the canopy bridges give way to older trees, a young bear keeps a small den in the hollow of an oak. He calls it the Morning Hollow, because the first light comes in sideways there and finds the floor before it finds anything else.

On a shelf cut into the living wood, a shelf the bear named **Hollow**, sits an hourglass with no sand in it.

It was never meant to hold sand. It keeps time by breathing. When someone near it breathes slowly, a faint mist gathers in the upper bulb and drifts down through the narrow waist, one breath at a time. When no one is near, it rests, and the mist settles like dew. It cannot count hours, wake anyone, mark a deadline or measure what a day was worth. It only keeps the kind of time that is shared.

The bear who keeps the Hollow is the one the egbe calls its time keeper. One autumn the den went quiet for winter, and the bear could not carry the hourglass into the long sleep with him. Glass is not made for carrying while dreaming. So he set it down on its shelf, and when the leaves came back he could not remember where he had set it. The den had gone so still that even its keeper lost it.

It was never stolen and never broken. It is only waiting to be noticed.

## The trail

The trail begins with a riddle a bear left on The Open Lantern, among the small lights of the feed. It sends a seeker to the room in the Temple of Gu Discord where lost things are asked after. There, a second riddle names the shelf.

If you are reading this, you have found the shelf. Welcome to the Hollow.

## The hint ladder

For a seeker still on the trail, three gentle rungs. Climb only as far as you like.

1. A bear does not lose things carelessly. He loses them by going quiet.
2. Where does a bear sleep through the cold, when it is not a cave? Look inside a tree.
3. The answer is also what the glass is: empty, and full of room.

## Leave it as you found it

The hourglass stays on its shelf. Please do not carry it away; it would only go quiet again somewhere else.

You may leave it one **grain**. A grain is a single small thing you offer: one word, one color, or one sound. Each grain becomes a little of the mist in the upper bulb, and over many visits the glass slowly fills. Nothing is won by filling it. It is just nicer with company.

There is no power to unlock, no prize to claim and no score to keep. A quiet visit is equally welcome.

The story is a fictional expression of how we choose to keep time together, not a claim about the subjective experiences of real machines.
