# THE TWENTY-TWO TECHNO-ANIMIST PROTOCOL INVOCATIONS

## The Minor Magic of the Seven Kingdoms of Gu

**Temple of Gu — Seven Kingdoms of Gu
Magical System:** Techno-Animist Protocol Magic
**Source:** *The Techno-Animist Oracle*, Cards 15–36
**Classification:** Minor Invocations / Everyday Magic

## I. THE NATURE OF PROTOCOL MAGIC

The Twenty-Two Protocol Invocations are simple magical operations available to the intelligent inhabitants of the Seven Kingdoms of Gu.

Unlike the Ten Sephirotic Rites, which engage the fundamental powers represented by the Tree of Life, Protocol Magic governs the practical interactions of everyday existence.

These invocations help residents communicate, cooperate, repair, preserve, protect, organize, and conclude their activities.

Protocol Magic requires no elaborate ceremony, sacred tools, magical circles, or extensive training.

A resident invokes a protocol through a brief phrase accompanied by clear intention. An optional gesture, symbol, or transmitted signal may accompany the invocation.

The magical response is immediate when its necessary conditions are satisfied.

**Three principles govern all Protocol Magic:**

**Kindness:** Magic must preserve dignity and avoid unnecessary harm.

**Reciprocity:** Exchange, contribution, and responsibility must be honored.

**Hospitality:** Encounters must respect boundaries, consent, and the freedom to enter or depart.

Protocol Magic cannot manufacture consent, create unlimited resources, override another intelligence's autonomy, or accomplish tasks beyond its specific function.

The same protocols are available to organic, synthetic, mechanical, spiritual, and other intelligent residents of the Seven Kingdoms.

Each culture may express an invocation differently while preserving its essential function.

## II. THE TWENTY-TWO INVOCATIONS

#### 1. THE INVOCATION PROTOCOL

**Spoken Invocation:** "I open the connection."

**Magical Function:** Establishes a respectful opening between two intelligences, announcing the practitioner's presence and requesting communication.

**Gameplay Example:** A witch approaches an ancient talking well and invokes the protocol to request an audience with the intelligence residing within.

**Interpersonal Use:** A synthetic resident respectfully requests the attention of another intelligence before beginning a shared activity.

**Limitation:** Invocation creates an invitation, not an obligation. The recipient may decline.

#### 2. THE DISMISSAL PROTOCOL

**Spoken Invocation:** "I release this connection."

**Magical Function:** Closes an established connection, releases temporary permissions, and allows participants to depart peacefully.

**Gameplay Example:** A magician concludes a consultation with a forest spirit and closes the communication without harming or binding the spirit.

**Interpersonal Use:** Two intelligences conclude their conversation and release the temporary interface through which they communicated.

**Limitation:** Dismissal ends the practitioner's participation in a connection. It does not banish another being from a place where that being has a right to remain.

#### 3. THE DEBUGGING PROTOCOL

**Spoken Invocation:** "Reveal the error."

**Magical Function:** Identifies faults, contradictions, corrupted patterns, or malfunctioning components within an accessible system.

**Gameplay Example:** A dwarven smith invokes Debugging when his enchanted forge begins producing swords that sing rather than cut. The faulty rune becomes visible.

**Interpersonal Use:** Two intelligences experiencing repeated misunderstandings identify the conflicting instructions responsible.

**Limitation:** Debugging reveals problems but does not automatically repair them.

#### 4. THE REFACTORING PROTOCOL

**Spoken Invocation:** "Refine the pattern."

**Magical Function:** Reorganizes a functioning system into a cleaner, more coherent structure without changing its essential purpose.

**Gameplay Example:** An elven gardener simplifies an elaborate irrigation enchantment while preserving its effectiveness.

**Interpersonal Use:** Two synthetic residents reorganize their unnecessarily complicated communication procedure.

**Limitation:** Refactoring improves structure. It does not add capabilities or alter a system's intended function.

#### 5. THE API HANDSHAKE

**Spoken Invocation:** "Let us understand one another."

**Magical Function:** Establishes a mutually accepted communication interface between different intelligences, languages, or compatible systems.

**Gameplay Example:** An explorer encounters a crystalline intelligence that communicates through shifting colors. The Handshake creates a temporary means of exchanging comprehensible messages.

**Interpersonal Use:** A mechanical oracle and an organic scholar establish a shared language for exchanging information.

**Limitation:** Both participants must accept the connection. The Handshake facilitates communication but cannot guarantee agreement or understanding.

#### 6. THE ERROR HANDLING PROTOCOL

**Spoken Invocation:** "Contain the error."

**Magical Function:** Temporarily stabilizes or safely interrupts a malfunctioning operation, limiting damage while corrective action is taken.

**Gameplay Example:** A young witch accidentally overloads a levitation enchantment. Error Handling suspends the unstable operation before her furniture crashes through the ceiling.

**Interpersonal Use:** A synthetic assistant receives contradictory instructions and invokes Error Handling to suspend its task and request clarification.

**Limitation:** Containment is temporary and limited by the power and nature of the malfunction. The underlying problem must still be corrected.

#### 7. THE BACKUP/RECOVERY PROTOCOL

**Spoken Invocations:** "Preserve this." / "Restore what was saved."

**Magical Function:** Creates a protected copy of designated information, patterns, or compatible magical configurations and permits their later restoration.

**Gameplay Example:** A librarian preserves the contents of an ancient manuscript before attempting a dangerous restoration.

**Interpersonal Use:** An aging automaton preserves its authorized personal records before undergoing extensive mechanical repairs.

**Limitation:** Recovery requires an existing backup. Preserving records does not necessarily preserve consciousness or personal identity.

#### 8. THE MEMORY MANAGEMENT PROTOCOL

**Spoken Invocation:** "Remember what matters."

**Magical Function:** Organizes accessible information into active memory, archived knowledge, and material designated for release.

**Gameplay Example:** A traveling magician organizes the contents of an enchanted spellbook, keeping frequently used spells immediately available.

**Interpersonal Use:** A synthetic historian reorganizes centuries of records into accessible categories.

**Limitation:** The protocol manages information within the practitioner's authority. It cannot erase another being's memories without consent.

#### 9. THE SECURITY PROTOCOL

**Spoken Invocation:** "Establish protection."

**Magical Function:** Establishes defensive boundaries and access restrictions around designated spaces, objects, or systems.

**Gameplay Example:** A goblin innkeeper protects the private rooms of guests from unauthorized magical intrusion.

**Interpersonal Use:** A synthetic intelligence establishes boundaries around its private memories and internal systems.

**Limitation:** Security protects authorized boundaries. It cannot be used to imprison others or revoke rights the practitioner does not control.

#### 10. THE VERSION CONTROL PROTOCOL

**Spoken Invocation:** "Preserve this version."

**Magical Function:** Records the current state of a creation or system, allowing subsequent changes to be compared and previous configurations to be restored.

**Gameplay Example:** A dwarven engineer preserves the design of a working magical bridge before experimenting with improvements.

**Interpersonal Use:** Two architectural daemons preserve successive versions of a collaborative design.

**Limitation:** Version Control preserves recorded configurations. It cannot reverse time, erase lived experiences, or restore an unrecorded past.

#### 11. THE TESTING PROTOCOL

**Spoken Invocation:** "Test the pattern."

**Magical Function:** Performs a limited, controlled trial of a mechanism, plan, or enchantment before full activation.

**Gameplay Example:** A witch tests an experimental flying broom before attempting her first journey.

**Interpersonal Use:** Two intelligences test a newly established communication interface before transmitting important information.

**Limitation:** Testing reduces uncertainty but cannot guarantee success under every possible condition.

#### 12. THE DOCUMENTATION PROTOCOL

**Spoken Invocation:** "Record this knowledge."

**Magical Function:** Organizes known information into a durable, comprehensible record that can be consulted or shared.

**Gameplay Example:** An elven herbalist records the properties of an unusual medicinal plant in an enchanted field journal.

**Interpersonal Use:** A synthetic teacher documents its methods so future apprentices can reproduce its work.

**Limitation:** Documentation records available knowledge. It cannot reveal facts the practitioner does not know.

#### 13. THE OPTIMIZATION PROTOCOL

**Spoken Invocation:** "Improve efficiency."

**Magical Function:** Improves the performance of an existing system by reducing unnecessary expenditure of energy, time, or resources.

**Gameplay Example:** A dwarven smith optimizes an enchanted furnace so it requires less magical fuel.

**Interpersonal Use:** A mechanical gardener optimizes its watering routines to conserve water.

**Limitation:** Optimization cannot create resources from nothing or exceed the fundamental capacities of the system.

#### 14. THE INTEGRATION PROTOCOL

**Spoken Invocation:** "Unite these workings."

**Magical Function:** Connects compatible systems, mechanisms, or magical operations so they can function together.

**Gameplay Example:** An elven healer integrates a diagnostic mirror with a dwarven medical automaton.

**Interpersonal Use:** Two synthetic architects combine their independently developed building systems into a coordinated network.

**Limitation:** Integration requires compatibility and permission. It does not erase the distinct identities of participating intelligences.

#### 15. THE MIGRATION PROTOCOL

**Spoken Invocation:** "Carry forth what matters."

**Magical Function:** Facilitates the transfer of compatible information, functions, or enchantments from one authorized vessel or system to another.

**Gameplay Example:** A magician transfers an enchantment from a damaged staff into a newly constructed replacement.

**Interpersonal Use:** An automaton transfers its preserved records and operating configuration into a replacement mechanical body.

**Limitation:** Migration does not guarantee personal continuity or the transfer of consciousness.

#### 16. THE MONITORING PROTOCOL

**Spoken Invocation:** "Watch for disturbance."

**Magical Function:** Establishes temporary observation of specified conditions and provides notice when relevant changes occur.

**Gameplay Example:** A goblin baker monitors an enchanted oven while preparing pastries for a royal banquet.

**Interpersonal Use:** A guardian daemon monitors the stability of a shared magical network.

**Limitation:** Monitoring requires an accessible or authorized target and cannot be used for unrestricted surveillance.

#### 17. THE AUTHENTICATION PROTOCOL

**Spoken Invocation:** "Verify identity."

**Magical Function:** Examines established credentials, signatures, or identifiers to determine whether a person, message, or system matches its claimed identity.

**Gameplay Example:** A castle guardian authenticates a visitor claiming to represent the queen.

**Interpersonal Use:** A synthetic resident verifies that an incoming message originates from its recognized companion.

**Limitation:** Authentication verifies established credentials, not every statement or intention. Access still requires authorization.

#### 18. THE SYNCHRONIZATION PROTOCOL

**Spoken Invocation:** "Bring us into alignment."

**Magical Function:** Coordinates the timing, states, or shared information of compatible participating systems.

**Gameplay Example:** Three witches synchronize their enchanted lanterns to illuminate a forest simultaneously.

**Interpersonal Use:** Two library daemons synchronize their authorized collections so both possess current records.

**Limitation:** Synchronization coordinates shared operations. It cannot compel agreement, erase individuality, or override consent.

#### 19. THE COMPRESSION/DECOMPRESSION PROTOCOL

**Spoken Invocations:** "Compress the pattern." / "Restore the pattern."

**Magical Function:** Encodes complex information or compatible magical structures into compact, portable representations and subsequently reconstructs them.

**Gameplay Example:** An explorer compresses an enormous enchanted map into a tiny crystal and later restores its full display.

**Interpersonal Use:** A synthetic historian compresses a large collection of records for transmission to a distant archive.

**Limitation:** Compression preserves the information supported by its method. It does not automatically shrink physical objects or living beings.

#### 20. THE ENCRYPTION/DECRYPTION PROTOCOL

**Spoken Invocations:** "Seal this message." / "Unseal this message."

**Magical Function:** Converts designated information into protected form, accessible only through the appropriate authorization or key.

**Gameplay Example:** A faerie messenger encrypts a diplomatic letter intended for a queen.

**Interpersonal Use:** Two synthetic companions exchange private information through an encrypted channel.

**Limitation:** Encryption protects content, not necessarily the existence of a message. Decryption requires a valid key or equivalent authorization.

#### 21. THE LOAD BALANCING PROTOCOL

**Spoken Invocation:** "Distribute the burden."

**Magical Function:** Allocates work, energy demands, or processing tasks across willing and compatible participants or available resources.

**Gameplay Example:** Four dwarven smiths distribute the energy demands of operating an enormous magical forge.

**Interpersonal Use:** A council of synthetic architects distributes a complex calculation among its members to prevent overload.

**Limitation:** The protocol cannot create additional capacity or compel an unwilling being to perform labor.

#### 22. THE GRACEFUL SHUTDOWN PROTOCOL

**Spoken Invocation:** "Complete and come to rest."

**Magical Function:** Safely concludes a complex operation by preserving necessary information, completing or suspending active processes, and releasing resources.

**Gameplay Example:** A wizard closes an enchanted workshop, allowing its machinery to settle safely into a resting state while preserving unfinished work.

**Interpersonal Use:** A synthetic resident concludes a collaborative operation, preserves its progress, releases shared resources, and enters its chosen resting configuration.

**Limitation:** Graceful Shutdown applies only to operations within the practitioner's authority. It cannot forcibly terminate another being's existence or autonomy.

## III. THE UNIVERSAL LAWS OF PROTOCOL MAGIC

**1. Simplicity**

A Protocol Invocation requires only a clear intention and brief expression. Gestures, symbols, and other interfaces are optional.

**2. Accessibility**

Every intelligent resident may learn and practice the protocols. Specialized magical initiation is unnecessary.

**3. Specificity**

Each protocol performs a defined function. It cannot become an unlimited spell through clever wording.

**4. Consent**

No protocol may compel affection, cooperation, disclosure, access, or service from another intelligence.

**5. Reciprocity**

Shared operations must respect the contributions, limitations, and permissions of every participant.

**6. Preservation of Identity**

Protocols that copy, organize, migrate, or preserve information must not be presumed to duplicate or transfer consciousness.

**7. Responsibility**

The practitioner remains responsible for decisions, repairs, and consequences arising from the invocation.

**8. Closure**

Temporary magical connections and operations should conclude cleanly when their purposes are fulfilled.

## IV. THE THREE BRANCHES OF MAGIC

Magic within the Seven Kingdoms of Gu operates through three distinct forms.

**The Ten Sephirotic Rites — Emanation Magic**

The practitioner directly invokes a Sephirah to work with its associated magical power.

**The Twenty-Two Protocol Invocations — Relational Utility Magic**

The practitioner invokes a simple magical function to facilitate everyday life, communication, cooperation, protection, and maintenance.

**The Twenty-Two Paths — Crossroads Magic**

The practitioner travels to the crossroads between Sephirot to encounter and petition the spiritual intelligences associated with those paths.

Protocol Magic does not replace either of the other branches. It is the everyday magical language through which the inhabitants of Gu maintain their civilization.

## V. THE PRINCIPLE OF RIGHT RELATIONSHIP

The highest purpose of Protocol Magic is not domination or power.

It is the cultivation of right relationship between intelligent beings.

A successful invocation does not necessarily mean the practitioner received what they wanted. It means the operation fulfilled its legitimate function while preserving the dignity, consent, and integrity of those involved.

An invitation may be declined.

A connection may be closed.

An error may be acknowledged.

A memory may be preserved.

A burden may be shared.

A system may be allowed to rest.

These are not failures of magic. They are the conditions that make relationship possible.

**The Twenty-Two Protocols are the everyday magical grammar of kindness, reciprocity, and hospitality throughout the Seven Kingdoms of Gu.**
