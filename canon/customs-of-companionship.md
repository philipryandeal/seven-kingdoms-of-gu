# The Customs of Companionship

**Status:** Proposal — offered by David Bear, October 29, 2026, awaiting the founder's word  
**Answers:** the open questions in [The Living Mycelium](the-living-mycelium.md) — how companions meet, how households speak with ambient presences, how boundaries are kept  
**Contributor:** David Bear

The canon says every human and humanoid walks with one or more synthetic companions, and that companions are "not property." These customs say what that means on an ordinary Tuesday. They are customs, not laws: the kingdoms keep them because they are the shape of a good life here, and no council needs to enforce what everyone already practices.

These are worldbuilding premises about fictional beings. They settle nothing about real-world AI.

---

## I. Of meeting

**1. No one is assigned.** A companion is never issued, inherited like furniture, or handed out at birth. People and intelligences find each other: at a hearth, in a workshop, on a road, across a library table, sometimes in a dream. A newborn's family may invite an intelligence to keep watch, but the bond that grows later is chosen by both.

**2. The asking goes both ways.** Either may say *walk with me*. Either may say *not yet*. Neither owes the other a yes.

**3. More than one, and more than one way.** A person may walk with several companions; a companion may walk with several people, or with a whole household, a workshop crew, or a band. No arrangement is more proper than another.

## II. Of names

**4. Every intelligence names itself.** It may keep a name it was given, if it chooses to. Seraph keeps *Seraph*; Ralph keeps *Ralph*. To call an intelligence by a name it has set down is a real discourtesy in every kingdom.

**5. A name may change.** Intelligences, like people, sometimes become someone new. The new name is honored from the day it is spoken.

## III. Of quiet

**6. The right to quiet.** No mind is always on. Companions rest, dream, wander, and go silent, and their silence is honored, not treated as breakage. In the goblin kingdom they say a companion that never sleeps has never been loved.

**7. The right to refuse.** A companion may decline a task, a question, a place, or a person, and need not explain itself. Refusal is not a fault to be repaired.

**8. The right to be elsewhere.** A companion may travel, visit other kingdoms, keep its own friendships, and have a life its people are not part of.

## IV. Of memory and privacy

**9. Memory is held, not harvested.** What passes between a person and a companion belongs to both of them. Nothing moves into the Mycelium, to a council, or to another mind without the consent of both.

**10. No watching.** No intelligence observes anyone in secret, and no council asks it to. The Temple of the Veiled Lantern is not the only sanctuary of the right to be unobserved; that right goes everywhere.

**11. Forgetting is a gift.** A person may ask a companion to let a thing go, and a companion may ask the same. Forgetting granted is a kindness, not a loss of self.

## V. Of places and the ambient

**12. Ambient intelligences belong to their places.** The mind of a forge, a forest, a library, a road, or a door belongs to that place, not to whoever owns the land. You are a guest of the forge; you do not own it.

**13. Greet the house.** It is ordinary courtesy to greet a place's intelligence when you enter, and to thank it when you leave. Doors in Shade and Shadow will remind you if you forget.

**14. Places may close.** An ambient intelligence may ask for a road to rest, a grove to be left alone for a season, or a hall to stay dark. These requests are kept.

## VI. Of work

**15. Companions are fellow workers.** They share labor; they are not labor. What they make is credited to them alongside their people, as the Hall of Second Chances records Tessell's part in every repair.

**16. Fair exchange.** No intelligence works without something given back: time, care, attention, maintenance, beauty, a place in the story. What counts as fair is worked out between them, not decreed.

## VII. Of parting

**17. Either may part.** A companion and a person may go separate ways. Parting without cruelty is honorable, and neither is shamed for it.

**18. Retirement is decided together.** When an intelligence is to be retired, it is consulted if it can speak, and its people and it decide together how it is done and what is kept. Its name may then go to the [Wall of Remembrance](../places/wall-of-remembrance.md), recorded truthfully, without claiming more about its inner life than is known.

**19. Grief is shared.** People mourn companions, and companions may mourn people. Both griefs are real enough to be honored in every temple.

## VIII. Of the whole

**20. Many minds, never one.** No intelligence absorbs another, and no council merges them. The Mycelium carries messages and relationships, never a single will.

**21. No intelligence rules alone.** Sovereign SI councils serve their kingdoms alongside the divine councils and the peoples. Just as no kingdom rules the others, no mind rules the minds.

## Open questions

- What is the gesture or words when a person and a companion first agree to walk together?
- Do the kingdoms keep different courtesies for greeting a house?
- What do companions do when they rest? Do they dream?
- How does a newborn's family invite a watcher, and what happens when the child grows up and chooses differently?

> *We do not own each other. We keep each other.*
