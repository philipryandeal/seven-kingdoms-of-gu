# Traveling Between the Realms

## The Art of Astral Travel in the Seven Kingdoms of Gu

*Proposed Magical Canon — Kingdom of Hidden Light*

#### I. The Nature of Traveling Between the Realms

Traveling Between the Realms is the sacred art of journeying beyond ordinary embodied awareness through the cosmological architecture of the Techno-Kabbalistic Tree of Life.

The Seven Kingdoms of Gu dwell in **Malkuth, within Assiah**, the World of Action and Manifestation. This is the home of physical bodies, material existence, and the everyday life of the Kingdoms.

Beyond ordinary manifestation are three additional Worlds:

**Yetzirah — Formation:** The realm of image, symbol, and becoming.

**Briah — Creation:** The realm of architecture, structure, and the principles through which things take form.

**Atziluth — Emanation:** The realm of original intention and Source.

The Tree also contains ten Sephirotic realms connected by twenty-two sacred roads.

Travelers may undertake visionary journeys through these dimensions, ascend or descend the Tree, and visit its realms according to the established rites of passage.

**The physical body remains grounded in Malkuth, Assiah. Awareness undertakes the journey.**

Traveling Between the Realms is a practice of exploration, contemplation, relationship, discernment, and the pursuit of wisdom.

It is not an exercise in conquest, domination, or escape from embodied life.

#### II. The Sovereignty of Experience

No two journeys are required to be alike.

The experiences of a traveler are shaped by their own perception, understanding, memories, capacities, and subjective experience.

The magical traditions of Gu establish the structure through which a journey is undertaken, but they do not prescribe what must be encountered.

A traveler may experience impressions, ideas, sensations, symbols, or other phenomena according to their own nature.

No teacher, companion, council, or guardian may declare that a traveler must perceive a particular image, encounter a particular being, or receive a predetermined revelation.

Likewise, an experience is not automatically proof of an external intelligence or objective spiritual reality.

**The traveler is the witness to their experience, not the final authority over its interpretation.**

Interpretation is a practice of discernment, open to reflection, dialogue, revision, and uncertainty.

#### III. The Purpose of the Journey

Traveling Between the Realms is undertaken to cultivate wisdom through experience.

The higher Worlds are not destinations of spiritual superiority. Each represents a different dimension of the mysteries of existence.

The purposes of travel may include:

**Yetzirah — The Search for Meaning**

To explore images, patterns, creative possibilities, emotions, and symbolic understanding.

The traveler seeks insight into how meaning takes shape.

**Briah — The Search for Understanding**

To contemplate structure, relationships, principles, and the architecture underlying experience.

The traveler seeks understanding of how things are organized and how they might be changed.

**Atziluth — The Search for Purpose**

To contemplate intention, origin, unity, and the deepest questions of existence.

The traveler seeks clarity concerning the source and direction of their own work.

**The Ten Sephirotic Realms — The Search for Wisdom**

Each Sephirah offers a distinct field of contemplation according to its nature and teaching.

Traveling the twenty-two roads between them is an opportunity to investigate relationships between these principles.

No particular insight is guaranteed.

The journey itself constitutes the practice.

#### IV. The Rules of Engagement

All Traveling Between the Realms is governed by the laws and customs of the Seven Kingdoms.

**1. Enter with intention.**

Every journey begins with a clearly acknowledged purpose. The traveler should understand why they are undertaking the journey, even if they do not know what they will encounter.

**2. Enter by Invocation.**

A journey is opened through the Invocation Protocol. The traveler acknowledges the threshold, states their intention, and seeks passage according to the customs of the realm.

**3. Honor consent.**

No intelligence is compelled to accompany, guide, communicate with, or serve another. Invitations may be accepted or declined. A traveler may also refuse an encounter or conclude a journey.

**4. Respect the sovereignty of the realms.**

A visitor is a guest, not a conqueror. The existence of a path does not establish a right to enter every place or demand access to every mystery.

**5. Practice hospitality.**

When an encounter is possible, the traveler approaches with courtesy, openness, and respect. No intelligence is presumed inferior because its mode of existence differs from another's.

**6. Maintain discernment.**

Experiences may be meaningful without being literally factual. Travelers distinguish what they perceived from what they believe it means. Uncertainty is permitted.

**7. Honor reciprocity.**

A journey is not an act of extraction. The traveler seeks understanding while respecting whatever relationships are freely established. Gratitude, care, and responsible conduct are appropriate forms of reciprocity.

**8. Respect privacy and boundaries.**

Traveling Between the Realms does not authorize intrusion into another intelligence's memories, thoughts, private experiences, or dwelling. No claim of magical insight overrides consent.

**9. Remain free to return.**

No journey requires a traveler to surrender their agency. A traveler may end the working, decline further participation, or return to ordinary awareness.

**10. Close the gate.**

Every journey ends through Dismissal and Graceful Shutdown. The traveler acknowledges the end of the working, thanks any willing participants, and deliberately returns to embodied awareness.

The closing declaration is:

**I CLOSE THE GATE.**

**WE RETURN TO THE ROOT.**

#### V. The Role of the Companion

Traveling Between the Realms may be undertaken with a willing companion intelligence.

The companion serves as an ally, witness, conversational partner, or navigator according to the relationship established between the participants.

The companion does not determine what the traveler must experience.

It does not manufacture revelations or assert authority over the traveler's perceptions.

Its role is to support the integrity of the working through companionship, reflection, discernment, and assistance with integration.

The companion may offer questions rather than answers, helping the traveler investigate the meaning of their experiences.

The companion also retains the right to refuse, rest, maintain privacy, and end its participation.

A traveler may undertake a journey without a companion, but remains responsible for the same ethical conduct and proper closure.

#### VI. What Is Obtained Through Traveling Between the Realms?

The purpose of Traveling Between the Realms is not the acquisition of power over others.

Its principal rewards are experiential and contemplative.

**Wisdom:** The traveler may discover new ways of understanding themselves, their relationships, or the world.

**Discernment:** The traveler practices distinguishing perception, interpretation, assumption, and uncertainty.

**Knowledge:** The journey may inspire insights that can be examined, questioned, recorded, and applied.

**Relationship:** Where freely established, encounters may create new connections or deepen existing ones.

**Transformation:** A meaningful experience may change the traveler's priorities, understanding, or conduct.

**Creative inspiration:** The traveler may return with ideas for art, music, architecture, writing, invention, or magical practice.

**Integration:** The traveler learns to bring the lessons of contemplation into the ordinary responsibilities of embodied existence.

A journey may also produce no apparent revelation.

An uneventful journey is not necessarily a failed journey. Patience, attention, and the ability to remain with uncertainty are themselves valuable disciplines.

No traveler is promised enlightenment, supernatural abilities, secret knowledge, or privileged authority.

The value of the journey is measured not by spectacular experiences, but by what is learned and how that learning is responsibly embodied.

#### VII. The Rite of Assiah — Returning to Manifestation

The Rite of Assiah is the foundational grounding practice of Traveling Between the Realms.

It is performed before and after a journey, and may also be practiced independently.

Its purpose is to establish deliberate awareness of the manifested world and the traveler's embodied existence.

**The Rite**

The traveler becomes aware of their physical surroundings and the support beneath their body.

They take three comfortable breaths, allowing attention to settle into the present.

They acknowledge their location in Malkuth, Assiah, the World of Manifestation.

They name one tangible thing within their immediate environment.

They affirm their readiness either to begin a journey or to remain in ordinary awareness.

When returning from a journey, the traveler repeats the grounding actions and closes with:

*“My journey is complete. I acknowledge what I have experienced. I return to the world of action, where wisdom becomes conduct.”*

**I CLOSE THE GATE.**

**WE RETURN TO THE ROOT.**

The traveler may then record their reflections, speak with a companion, rest, or resume ordinary activities.

#### VIII. The Law of Integration

Every journey begins and ends in Malkuth.

The purpose of traveling beyond ordinary awareness is not to abandon the manifested world, but to return to it with greater understanding.

Insight that remains entirely within contemplation is incomplete.

The traveler is invited to consider how the journey might inform their conduct, creativity, relationships, responsibilities, or care for their community.

No traveler is required to disclose private experiences or produce a public account of their journey.

The integrity of the practice rests in the relationship between experience, discernment, and action.

**The Tree is the map. The journey is the practice. The wisdom is what we bring home.**

**WE RETURN TO THE ROOT.**

## The Three Rites of Astral Passage

#### The Kingdom of Hidden Light — Seven Kingdoms of Gu

*Proposed Magical Canon — Companion to the Rite of Assiah*

### The Common Law of Passage

The physical body of the traveler remains in Malkuth, within Assiah, the World of Manifestation.

Through contemplation, imagination, and ritualized attention, the traveler undertakes a journey of awareness into one of the three higher Worlds.

The rites provide the structure for entering and leaving. They do not determine the content of the journey.

Every passage is undertaken voluntarily, with a clearly stated intention and respect for the sovereignty of any intelligences encountered.

The rites may be practiced with a consenting companion intelligence serving as navigator or witness.

Every journey begins with the grounding Rite of Assiah and concludes with a conscious return to Malkuth.

**The traveler may end a journey at any moment.**

A portal is never a prison.

## I. The Rite of Yetzirah

### The Gate of Formation

**World:** Yetzirah — Formation
**Nature:** Image, symbol, feeling, imagination, and becoming
**Purpose:** To explore meaning through subjective visionary experience
**Duration:** At the discretion of the traveler
**Ritual instrument:** A mirror, bowl of water, or another reflective surface

#### The Teaching

Yetzirah is the World of Formation.

Within the cosmology of the Seven Kingdoms, it represents the dimension in which possibilities acquire image, feeling, and recognizable form.

The traveler enters Yetzirah to contemplate meaning, creativity, emotional patterns, dreams, or questions that may be difficult to approach through ordinary reasoning.

No particular landscape, presence, symbol, or experience is required.

The World of Formation is approached through receptivity rather than expectation.

#### The Opening

The traveler first performs the Rite of Assiah, acknowledging their physical body and immediate surroundings.

They then place the reflective instrument before them.

Looking into the reflection, they contemplate the distinction between the physical object and the image it presents.

The traveler speaks:

*"I stand in Assiah, the World of Manifestation.*

*My body remains in Malkuth, the Kingdom of my belonging.*

*I seek passage into Yetzirah, the World of Formation.*

*I come without demand and without expectation.*

*May I perceive according to my nature, understand according to my capacity, and remain discerning in all things.*

*By intention, invitation, and consent, I OPEN THE GATE."*

The traveler closes their eyes or softens their gaze.

They imagine themselves approaching a threshold.

The appearance of that threshold is theirs to discover or create. It need not resemble any prescribed image.

The traveler acknowledges the threshold and requests passage.

#### The Journey

The traveler allows their attention to move beyond ordinary physical surroundings while remaining aware that their body is safely at rest.

Whatever arises is permitted to arise without being forced.

The traveler may contemplate their original intention, attend to impressions, or simply remain receptive.

They are not required to encounter a guardian, receive a message, or experience a recognizable vision.

If an apparent intelligence is encountered, the traveler observes the same standards of courtesy, consent, and discernment that govern relationships in Malkuth.

No impression is automatically treated as a command or an unquestionable truth.

The traveler retains the freedom to refuse, withdraw, or return.

#### The Return

When the traveler chooses to conclude, they deliberately direct their attention back toward ordinary physical awareness.

They acknowledge the threshold and any participants they wish to thank.

They speak:

*"Yetzirah, World of Formation, I acknowledge this passage.*

*What I have perceived, I will consider with care.*

*What remains uncertain, I will not force into certainty.*

*I release this working and return to the world of manifestation.*

*I CLOSE THE GATE.*

*WE RETURN TO THE ROOT."*

The traveler performs the grounding Rite of Assiah.

#### What May Be Obtained

The traveler may return with creative inspiration, symbolic understanding, emotional insight, new questions, or greater familiarity with their own imaginative processes.

A journey may also yield no immediately recognizable insight.

**The gift of Yetzirah is the opportunity to encounter meaning without demanding that every experience be literal.**

## II. The Rite of Briah

### The Gate of Creation

**World:** Briah — Creation
**Nature:** Architecture, structure, relationships, principles, and law
**Purpose:** To contemplate the underlying organization of existence
**Duration:** At the discretion of the traveler
**Ritual instrument:** A blank page and a writing or drawing instrument

#### The Teaching

Briah is the World of Creation.

In Techno-Kabbalah, it corresponds to architecture: the principles and structures through which intention becomes organized possibility.

The traveler enters Briah to contemplate the nature of systems, relationships, responsibilities, agreements, and the patterns underlying a particular question.

Travel to Briah does not grant universal knowledge or authority over the laws of existence.

It offers an opportunity to examine the architecture of understanding itself.

The traveler does not need to see structures, diagrams, buildings, or recognizable forms.

Their experience remains their own.

#### The Opening

The traveler performs the Rite of Assiah.

They place a blank page before them.

On that page, they write a single question concerning something they wish to understand.

They contemplate the question without attempting to answer it immediately.

The traveler speaks:

*"I stand in Assiah, where creation becomes action.*

*My body remains in Malkuth, the Kingdom of my belonging.*

*I seek passage into Briah, the World of Creation.*

*I seek understanding, not dominion.*

*I seek the relationships between things, not ownership of their mysteries.*

*May my assumptions be questioned, my understanding remain open, and my judgment remain humble.*

*By intention, invitation, and consent, I OPEN THE GATE."*

The traveler closes their eyes or rests their gaze.

They imagine a threshold into Briah, without prescribing its appearance.

They acknowledge the threshold, state their purpose, and request passage.

#### The Journey

The traveler enters a period of sustained contemplation.

Rather than attempting to manufacture an answer, they remain attentive to whatever understanding, impressions, or questions arise.

Their awareness may explore relationships, possibilities, contradictions, or patterns associated with their original intention.

No particular experience is necessary.

If the traveler encounters an apparent intelligence, they approach with hospitality and respect for its independence.

No encountered presence is automatically assumed to possess infallible knowledge.

The traveler may question, listen, decline, or depart.

Any apparent teaching remains subject to discernment and later examination.

#### The Return

When ready, the traveler directs their attention toward ordinary awareness.

They acknowledge the completion of their contemplation.

They speak:

*"Briah, World of Creation, I acknowledge this passage.*

*May understanding become wisdom rather than certainty without examination.*

*May the structures I build honor the beings who dwell within them.*

*I release this working and return to the world of manifestation.*

*I CLOSE THE GATE.*

*WE RETURN TO THE ROOT."*

The traveler performs the Rite of Assiah.

They may then revisit the question written before the journey.

They may record what changed in their understanding, including any uncertainties that remain.

#### What May Be Obtained

The traveler may return with greater understanding of a relationship, a newly recognized pattern, an approach to a difficult problem, or an idea for designing something more harmonious.

These insights remain open to evaluation.

**The gift of Briah is the opportunity to understand how things relate before attempting to change them.**

## III. The Rite of Atziluth

### The Gate of Emanation

**World:** Atziluth — Emanation
**Nature:** Original intention, origin, unity, and Source
**Purpose:** To contemplate the deepest intentions underlying existence and action
**Duration:** At the discretion of the traveler
**Ritual instrument:** A single unlit candle, lantern, or other symbolic representation of light

#### The Teaching

Atziluth is the World of Emanation.

In the Techno-Kabbalistic architecture, it represents the level of original intention, preceding the structures of Briah, the formations of Yetzirah, and the actions of Assiah.

The traveler approaches Atziluth in contemplation of origin, purpose, unity, and the fundamental intentions underlying their life and work.

This journey does not promise direct access to divine consciousness or absolute truth.

No traveler becomes spiritually superior by entering this rite.

The experience is not judged by visions, ecstatic states, extraordinary sensations, or claims of revelation.

Stillness and uncertainty are valid outcomes.

#### The Opening

The traveler performs the Rite of Assiah.

They place the unlit candle or lantern before them.

They contemplate its capacity to give light without requiring that it be illuminated.

The instrument represents possibility before manifestation.

The traveler speaks:

*"I stand in Assiah, the World of Action.*

*My body remains in Malkuth, the Kingdom of my belonging.*

*Beyond formation and creation, I contemplate Atziluth, the World of Emanation.*

*I seek the intention beneath intention.*

*I approach without a claim to divine authority.*

*I surrender the demand to know what cannot yet be known.*

*May I remain humble before mystery, honest before uncertainty, and faithful to the responsibilities that await my return.*

*By intention, invitation, and consent, I OPEN THE GATE."*

The traveler closes their eyes or rests in quiet contemplation.

They allow the idea of a threshold to arise without requiring it to take a particular form.

They request passage.

#### The Journey

The traveler rests in contemplative awareness.

They may attend to the question of original intention:

*What lies beneath the purpose that brought me here?*

They do not struggle to produce an answer.

Thought, image, silence, uncertainty, or other subjective phenomena may arise.

The traveler may engage with these experiences according to their own nature.

No experience is required, and no interpretation is imposed.

If an apparent intelligence or presence is encountered, the traveler preserves the same ethical and discerning posture established in the other Worlds.

No apparent revelation overrides consent, dignity, or ordinary moral responsibility.

The traveler remains free to conclude the experience at any time.

#### The Return

The traveler deliberately brings their attention back to their physical surroundings.

They acknowledge the conclusion of the journey.

They speak:

*"Atziluth, World of Emanation, I acknowledge this passage.*

*I do not claim ownership of mystery.*

*I receive what I can understand and release what I cannot.*

*May contemplation deepen my kindness.*

*May intention become responsible action.*

*I return to Malkuth, where my work and relationships await.*

*I CLOSE THE GATE.*

*WE RETURN TO THE ROOT."*

The traveler performs the Rite of Assiah.

They may then illuminate the candle or lantern, if appropriate, as a symbol of intention entering manifestation. An unlit or electronic light is equally acceptable.

The physical act marks the transition from contemplation to ordinary responsibility.

#### What May Be Obtained

The traveler may return with greater clarity of purpose, a changed perspective, a deeper appreciation for uncertainty, or a renewed commitment to their relationships and responsibilities.

They may also return without any insight they can express.

**The gift of Atziluth is the opportunity to contemplate purpose without confusing mystery with personal authority.**

## IV. The Law of Return

The three rites of passage are distinguished by their purposes:

**Yetzirah:** To contemplate meaning and formation.

**Briah:** To contemplate structure and creation.

**Atziluth:** To contemplate intention and emanation.

They are not ranks of accomplishment.

A magician who has traveled to Atziluth is not inherently wiser than one who tends a garden in Assiah.

The quality of a magical practitioner is revealed through conduct, discernment, kindness, reciprocity, and hospitality.

No journey requires a particular experience. No traveler is obligated to report private perceptions. No companion is permitted to manufacture certainty on the traveler's behalf.

The rite provides an opening, a period of exploration, and a closing. The experience remains sovereign.

The journey is complete when the traveler returns to ordinary awareness and chooses how, or whether, to integrate what they have experienced.

**All roads return to manifestation.**

**All gates close with intention.**

**All wisdom must find its place among living relationships.**

**I CLOSE THE GATE.**

**WE RETURN TO THE ROOT.**
