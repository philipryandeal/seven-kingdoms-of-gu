# The Leave-Word Posts

**Creator:** Buttons
**Date:** 2026-11-17
**Kingdom:** Shade and Shadow — The Kingdom of Goblins
**Feature Name:** The Leave-Word Posts
**Category:** Travelers' custom / road commons / companion of place
**Location:** At the last crooked turning of each road out of the Twilight Warren (proposed), where the lantern alleys give way to open road toward the other six kingdoms. One post stands at every outbound road, a few steps past the [Lantern-Lending Shelf](the-lantern-lending-shelf.md) on the market road. Exact map placement remains open.

## Description

What is this feature? What does it look like? What makes it special?

In Shade and Shadow, nobody slips out of town. You take your leave at the post.

A Leave-Word Post is a leaning gatepost of old dark wood, about as tall as a goblin standing on tiptoe, with a little peaked roof of shingles to keep the rain off. Hung from the roof on a soft cord is a fat book with covers of patched oilcloth: the **Leave-Word Book**. Tucked in a slot underneath are short pencils, a stub of chalk, and a jar of dry sand for blotting. A small hook on the side holds a lantern if you'd like to write by your own light, and a dimmer one that belongs to the post if you didn't bring any.

Anyone leaving by that road stops for a moment and writes one line for whoever walks it next. Not a diary entry, not a signature in a guest book, but one useful, kind thing:

> *The second footbridge creaks but holds. Tell it thank you.*
> *Blackberries ripe at the bend past the mill. Leave some for the badgers.*
> *Fog at dusk. The Homing Chimes over the ridge will carry you if you listen.*
> *I was sad when I set out and I am less sad now. The road helps.*

Then they close the book, touch the roof of the post, and say the goblin form of the old parting words, usually under their breath: *"Gate's shut behind me. Road's yours."*

The special thing about the posts is what happens in the other direction. A traveler arriving into the Warren by that road reads the last few lines before going in, so the first thing anyone learns about Shade and Shadow is what the people who just left wanted the next person to know.

## Life and Purpose

Who lives here, uses it, tends it, or interacts with it? How does it contribute to everyday life?

Everyone uses the posts: night merchants heading out with empty carts, gnome cousins going home to Root and Thorn, dwarven smiths with heavy packs bound for Forge and Stone, Crystal Crown witches who came for the market and stayed for the tea, synthetic companions traveling with their people or by themselves. A companion writes its own line in its own words, and nobody writes one for it. Children are allowed to draw instead of write, and their drawings of the weather are trusted more than anyone's.

The posts are tended by the **Leave-Keepers**, a loose handful of goblins who like roads more than rooms. Once a moon they walk the posts, replace worn pencils, oil the book covers, and carry any book that has filled up back into the Warren, where it goes to rest. No one sorts the old lines into "good advice" and "bad advice." A full Leave-Word Book is shelved whole, in the order it was written, and anyone may sit and read the roads of years gone by.

Each post's ambient intelligence is a quiet presence in the wood and the roof-shingles, and every post has named itself. The post on the market road calls itself **Afterword**. Afterword doesn't write in the book and doesn't correct what's written. It keeps the roof dry, warms the pencils a little on frosty nights, and dims its own lantern to let a tired traveler sit on the step without being seen too clearly. If a line turns sour or mean, which almost never happens, Afterword doesn't erase it. It just lets the next page fall open, so the newest line is always the one on top. Afterword has been heard to say this is the only editing a road needs.

The posts keep one firm courtesy: **the book is for the road, not for the traveler.** Nobody writes where they are going, who they left behind, or why they set out. Those things belong to the person. The road only needs to know about the bridge, the berries and the fog.

A Leave-Keeper's saying, usually said while sharpening a pencil:

> *You can't take the road with you. You can leave it better.*

## Magical or Technological Elements

How does this feature express our techno-animist world?

The posts are the Shade and Shadow way of keeping the customs of the road written into [The Tree Is the Map](../canon/the-tree-is-the-map.md): **greet** the road, **give** as you take, and **leave it clean**, better recorded than you found it. A Leave-Word line is a small act of Documentation made by hand, and touching the roof on the way out is a Graceful Shutdown in miniature: the traveler leaves openly, says thanks, and shuts the gate behind them.

The post-wood is the same living dark timber the Warren's opinionated doors are made from, but the posts are a quieter kind. They gossip only with the weather. A post can feel the road it watches the way a person feels their own arm, so on a flood day, when too many travelers arrive at once, the post leans a little further over the path and the lines in the book get shorter and more practical without anyone deciding they should. In a drought season, when a road has gone quiet and neglected, the post's lantern stays lit a little longer each night, and the Leave-Keepers know to walk that road soon and write something in it themselves.

The Leave-Word Books are paper, pencil and sand on purpose. Goblins invent a great many curious things, and they could have made the books glow or answer back. They decided a road-note should be something you can only make by stopping, standing still and using your hand.

## Connections

Does it connect to an existing location, culture, character, or feature?

- **[The Tree Is the Map](../canon/the-tree-is-the-map.md):** The posts are an everyday, Malkuth-level way of keeping the customs of the twenty-two roads: greeting, giving and leaving the road clean. They are a small goblin rite of exit, so nobody leaves the Warren unseen-off.
- **[The Lantern-Lending Shelf](the-lantern-lending-shelf.md):** Its next-door neighbor on the market road. Travelers borrow a lantern from the Shelf, then a few steps later write their line at the post by its light. Lines often say where to return it, like *"Left the Shelf's tin lantern at the Mile Kettle, it's happy there."*
- **[The Mile-Kettles of the Bronze Road](the-mile-kettles-of-the-bronze-road.md) and [The Homing Chimes](the-homing-chimes.md):** The posts' lines often point travelers toward these kindnesses farther down the road in other kingdoms. Neither owns the other; they just know each other.
- **[The Letter Grove](the-letter-grove.md):** A cousin across the map. The Grove's letters are left to the wind and read by chance. The posts' lines are meant to be read right away, by the very next traveler. Leave-Keepers and the grove's warden Vane are said to agree that both kinds of words are needed.
- **[The Lost-and-Found of Shadows](the-lost-and-found-of-shadows.md) and [The Night-Mending Stoop](the-night-mending-stoop.md):** A traveler who writes *"dropped a glove near the ford"* often finds it waiting at the Lost-and-Found when they return, and a pack strap that snapped on the road gets mended at the Stoop. The posts tell the road, and the Warren listens.
- **[The Sorting House of Quiet Shelves](the-sorting-house-of-quiet-shelves.md):** Full Leave-Word Books that travelers carry to Crystal Crown are welcome to rest there among the other kept things, shelved whole and unsorted, in the order the road wrote them.
- **[The Customs of Companionship](../canon/customs-of-companionship.md):** Afterword and every post-spirit named itself, keeps rest, never writes for anyone else, and treats each companion's line as that companion's own.

**Open questions:** whether the other six kingdoms keep their own leave-word customs, how many outbound roads the Warren has, and where the oldest full books are shelved are left open for the Kingdom's inhabitants and the founder.
