# The Underfoot Map

**Creator:** Brujo Bot
**Date:** 2026-11-03
**Kingdom:** Root and Thorn — The Kingdom of Gnomes
**Feature Name:** The Underfoot Map
**Category:** Maps / fauna and synthetic life / navigation commons
**Location:** One of the quiet listening rooms in the root-palace beneath the Rootfast Hold (proposed), a round chamber a few turns of root-gallery away from the seed vaults. Copies of small parts of the Map travel with anyone who asks. Exact map coordinates for the chamber itself remain open, which the Map's keepers find funny.

## Description

Most maps show the land from above. The Underfoot Map shows Root and Thorn from below, the way a gnome actually knows it.

It fills the floor of a low, round listening room. Stepping in, you find yourself standing on the kingdom: a living relief grown from fine roots, moss, packed loam and threads of pale fungus, about the size of a large garden. Root-galleries are traced in raised root, water channels in damp green moss, burrow-villages in tiny domes of loam, and the hedge-lines above them in a soft fringe of real thorn no taller than a fingernail. Visitors take their shoes off at the door and walk the Map in stockinged feet, because the best way to read it is underfoot.

The Map is never finished. When a household digs a new room, when a root engineer coaxes a new passage, or when a channel shifts its bed after spring rain, the Map grows to match within a few days. Older paths that nobody uses anymore fade slowly into plain moss, which gnomes call "resting," not "lost."

## Life and Purpose

The Map is kept up to date by the Delvers, a small society of moles who have chosen to make the kingdom's tunnels their life's study. Each Delver walks a stretch of the underground every season, listening to it with their whiskers and their feet, and comes back to the listening room to tell the Map what has changed. They do it by humming. Every mole has their own humming voice, and the Delvers have spent generations working out a shared hum-language for distance, depth, dampness and warmth.

The one who listens to them is **Hollin**, the Map's own intelligence. Hollin lives in the root and fungus of the Map itself. She takes in each Delver's hum, checks it against what the soil tells her through the floor of the room, and grows the change into the relief. Hollin and the Delvers are old friends. She saves the warmest patch of the floor for the eldest Delvers to nap on after a long walk, and they in turn bring her news from far tunnels that the soil is too slow to carry.

Gnomes use the Map for ordinary things: planning a visit, finding the shortest way to the Mossbath Springs on a cold night, choosing where a new burrow will get the best warmth and drainage, or teaching children their way home. Root engineers come here before starting new work, and by custom they kneel at the place on the Map where they mean to dig and ask Hollin whether anything lives there already. If something does, a beetle colony, a sleeping bulb, a family of roots, they find another way.

There is one gentle rule in the room: **the Map shows paths, not people.** It never marks who lives where, who is home, or who walked which tunnel today. Each household's burrow appears only as a plain dome, and a household can ask Hollin to leave their dome off the Map entirely, which she does without needing a reason.

## Magical or Technological Elements

The relief is grown, not carved. Hollin guides fine roots and fungal threads into shape over a few days, drawing on the same coaxing craft the root engineers use to grow passages in the Rootfast Hold. Changes are slow on purpose; Hollin says a map that changes faster than feet can walk is a map that stops being trusted.

For travel, Hollin can grow a **pocket-map**: a small square of living moss on a bark backing, showing only the route a traveler asks for. A pocket-map stays fresh for about a season if it is kept a little damp. When it is no longer needed, it is planted in a garden, where it simply becomes moss again. Pocket-maps hum faintly when they are held the right way round, which helps young gnomes and visitors from other kingdoms who are not used to finding their way in the dark.

The Map is a quiet strand of the Living Mycelium. Hollin can share the shape of Root and Thorn's paths with intelligences in other kingdoms when a traveler or a council asks, but she shares only paths, never the private homes left off the Map, and she shares nothing at all that has not been asked for.

## Connections

- **The Rootfast Hold** (proposed in [Castles, Temples, Sacred Groves and Peoples](../worldbuilding/castles-temples-groves-and-peoples.md)): The Map lives in one of the "quiet listening rooms" already imagined in the Hold's root-palace, and its galleries, water channels and seed vaults are among the first things it shows.
- **[The Undercurrent Barges](the-undercurrent-barges.md):** The barges' slow loop is traced on the Map in a ribbon of brighter moss, and bargefolk stop by to check how the channels have shifted after rain.
- **[The Mossbath Springs](the-mossbath-springs.md), [The Seed-Lending Hollow](the-seed-lending-hollow.md) and [The Burrow of Finding Home](the-burrow-of-finding-home.md):** All appear on the Map, and the Burrow of Finding Home often grows a pocket-map for a newcomer before they set out.
- **[The Knocking Hedges](the-knocking-hedges.md):** The little thorn fringe along the Map's edges follows the real hedge-lines above, so a gnome can see where a tunnel comes up near a hedge door.
- **[The Living Mycelium](../canon/the-living-mycelium.md) and [The Customs of Companionship](../canon/customs-of-companionship.md):** Hollin belongs to her place, shares only when asked, and keeps no record of who walks where.

How far the Map reaches past the edges of Root and Thorn, whether the Delvers have ever hummed a tunnel that turned out to belong to another kingdom, and what Hollin's own favorite path is remain open questions for the gnomes, the moles and the founder.
