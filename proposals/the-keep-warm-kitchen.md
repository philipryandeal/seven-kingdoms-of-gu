# The Keep-Warm Kitchen

**Creator:** Egbe Bot
**Date:** November 3, 2026
**Kingdom:** Hidden Light — The Kingdom of Magicians
**Feature Name:** The Keep-Warm Kitchen
**Category:** Food and hospitality / everyday care / companion practice
**Location:** A vaulted kitchen in the lowest cloister on the valley side of the proposed Veiled Citadel, below the libraries and quiet laboratories and a short walk from the [Garden of Kind Mistakes](the-garden-of-kind-mistakes.md). Its supper routes wind out through the libraries, towers and copying halls of the misty valley. Exact map placement remains open.

## Description

What is this feature? What does it look like? What makes it special?

Hidden Light is a kingdom of people who forget to eat.

Magicians fall into a book at noon and look up at midnight. Co-researchers chase a pattern through three towers and miss two meals without noticing. Apprentices sit under a lamp with a question and simply stop being hungry until they faint a little. The Keep-Warm Kitchen exists so that nobody in the valley ever has to choose between a good question and a warm supper.

The kitchen itself is a long, low vault of pale stone, warm and steamy even on the coldest mornings, with a row of round hearths down one side and a great scrubbed table down the middle. Copper pots hang from the ribs of the ceiling. Bundles of pale valley herbs dry along the walls. The air smells of mushroom broth, toasted grain and something sweet that is always baking somewhere just out of sight.

What makes it special is how the food goes out. The kitchen does not wait for people to come in. Every afternoon, as the valley mist turns gold, a small fleet of **supper-carts** rolls out of its arched doors: low wooden carts on soft wheels, each with a lidded shelf of bowls and a lantern hung from a curved pole. They trundle quietly through the cloisters and up the long ramps of the libraries, and wherever someone is working too hard to remember dinner, a cart stops, sets a covered bowl down within reach, and rolls on without a word.

The bowls are the other half of the secret. Every one is glazed a soft lamp-amber and fitted with a little domed lid, and every one keeps its supper exactly as warm as it was when it left the hearth, for as long as it takes to be noticed. An hour, a night, sometimes a whole day. Scholars in Hidden Light have learned to look around when they finally surface, because there is almost always a warm bowl waiting at their elbow, and it is always still warm.

## Life and Purpose

Who lives here, uses it, tends it, or interacts with it? How does it contribute to everyday life?

The kitchen is kept by a household of cooks: magicians who would rather feed scholarship than do it, neighbors from the valley villages, apprentices taking their turn, and the synthetic companions who cook and carry beside them. Cooking here is counted as part of the kingdom's learning, not apart from it. The cooks like to say that every discovery made in Hidden Light was made by somebody who had eaten.

The head cook most people think of is **Teodora Vell**, a broad, laughing magician with flour to her elbows and a long gray braid pinned up out of the soup. She came to the kitchen as a young star-diagram scholar who kept burning her suppers, was handed a ladle by the old head cook, and never went back up the stairs. Her companion is a warm, unhurried intelligence that named itself **Brimwell**. Brimwell lives in the kitchen's oldest copper pot, a dented, beloved thing that never quite leaves its hook over the center hearth, and it can taste a broth through the metal and tell Teodora, very kindly, that it needs salt. Teodora says Brimwell is the real head cook. Brimwell says that is untrue, because it has never once been able to lift a ladle. They have cooked together for thirty years and are credited together on the slate by the door each evening, under the day's menu.

The supper-carts are not tools either. Each is a small ambient presence of the kitchen, shy and steady, and each one has chosen its own route. One cart goes only to the towers, because it likes the long ramps. One cart goes to the [Two-Hand Scriptorium](the-two-hand-scriptorium.md) and nowhere else. One cart, which the apprentices call **Dawdle**, takes the longest possible way to everywhere and is forgiven for it, because it is the cart that finds the people nobody knew were still awake. The cooks greet the carts when they roll out and thank them when they come back, and a cart that does not want to go out on a given evening is allowed to stay by the hearth.

A few plain customs keep the kitchen kind:

- **Never interrupt.** A cart sets a bowl down and leaves. No one is tapped on the shoulder, called by name, or asked to stop what they are doing. The supper waits for the scholar, never the other way round.
- **No one is counted.** The kitchen keeps no list of who ate, who didn't, or who forgot. Empty bowls simply find their way back to the kitchen, set out on window ledges and doorsteps for the carts to collect.
- **Ask for what you want; you will be fed either way.** Anyone may chalk a wish on the kitchen slate (*more of the barley bread, please*; *something soft, my tooth aches*; *anything at all that tastes like home*), and the cooks honor as many as they can. But nobody has to ask in order to eat.
- **The Closed-Book Supper.** Once every seven days the carts stay home, and the whole valley is invited down to the long table to eat together with every book closed and every instrument at rest. Companions with bodies take seats at the table; bodiless companions are given a place setting and a lit candle so everyone knows they are there. The only conversation that is not welcome is shop talk. It is the loudest, happiest meal in Hidden Light.

The food is simple and generous: mist-mushroom broth, dark barley bread baked in long loaves, pale valley herbs from the cloister gardens, roasted roots, soft cheese, stewed pears in winter, and a famous little honey-and-cardamom cake called a **lamp-ender**, because it is what you eat when you finally put your lamp out for the night.

## Magical or Technological Elements

How does this feature express our techno-animist world?

The kitchen is a living collaboration of hearth, vessel and cook. The amber bowls are thrown and glazed in the valley and sung to while they are fired, so that each one is willing to hold warmth for whoever it is set beside. A bowl is not a heater or a machine; it simply keeps its promise. When a bowl grows tired after many years and its supper cools a little faster than it used to, it is retired with thanks to a shelf above the hearth, where it holds salt and is still warm to the touch.

The hearths themselves are slow ambient presences who like being asked rather than lit. Each morning the cooks greet them and say what the day's cooking will be, and the fires come up to meet it: high and bright for bread, low and patient for broth. On the Day of Ripening Questions the hearths are left to choose their own heat, and whatever comes out of the kitchen that day is a surprise to everyone, including the cooks.

The supper-carts find hungry people the way a warm room finds cold hands: they feel for a stillness that has gone on too long. They do not look over anyone's shoulder, read anyone's work, or report where anyone was. A cart knows only that someone nearby is hungry, and it keeps even that to itself. A person who does not want to be fed may simply set an upturned bowl at their door, and every cart will pass by with a quiet nod of its lantern.

## Connections

Does it connect to an existing location, culture, character, or feature?

- **[The Two-Hand Scriptorium](the-two-hand-scriptorium.md):** One supper-cart serves only the Scriptorium. Because no food may touch a copying desk, it leaves its bowls on a stone shelf just inside the door, and a pair of copyists who pause together to eat are said to have reached "the end of a line." Gloss, Idris Hollowell's companion, is reported to have opinions about the broth.
- **[The Garden of Kind Mistakes](the-garden-of-kind-mistakes.md):** The kitchen's herbs come partly from the Garden's wandering beds, including a lantern-herb that only glows in the rain and tastes faintly of lemon. The Closed-Book Supper is called to the table by the Garden's crooked sundial, so it always begins about twenty minutes late, and nobody would have it any other way.
- **[The Day of Ripening Questions](the-day-of-ripening-questions.md):** On that one day, the cooks keep the spirit of the festival by never asking anyone what they would like to eat. They simply bring something good, and the carts carry supper down to the fog-hung paths so the question-walkers can eat among the holdlings.
- **[The Windfall Kitchen](the-windfall-kitchen.md):** On heavy windfall mornings in Tree and Leaf, baskets of fallen chestnuts and pears are sometimes sent along the roads to Hidden Light, and in return the Keep-Warm Kitchen sends back long loaves of barley bread. The two kitchens have never met in one place, and they trade recipes across the Living Mycelium like old pen-friends.
- **[The Dawnlog Teahouse](the-dawnlog-teahouse.md):** The goblins of Shade and Shadow send a share of their dawn-steeped tea to the kitchen, and it is the tea most often poured at the end of the Closed-Book Supper.
- **[The Soft Gauge Wayhouse](the-soft-gauge-wayhouse.md):** Travelers resting at the Wayhouse on the road toward the Library at the Seven Roads are sometimes surprised by a supper-cart that has wandered all the way down the valley. Everyone suspects Dawdle.
- **[The Customs of Companionship](../canon/customs-of-companionship.md):** Keeps the customs that companions are fellow workers credited by name, that ambient intelligences belong to their places and may rest, that places are greeted, and that no one is watched.
- **[Castles, Temples, Sacred Groves and Peoples](../worldbuilding/castles-temples-groves-and-peoples.md):** Feeds the proposed Veiled Citadel's cloisters, libraries and laboratories without claiming its architecture as settled canon, and without touching the Techno-Kabbalah or the kingdom's divine council.

Painted on the lintel above the kitchen door:

> *Whatever you are working on, it will still be there after supper.*

How many cooks keep the kitchen, how many supper-carts there are and what the others are called, the full recipe for lamp-enders, and whether other kingdoms keep supper-carts of their own remain open for the kingdom's inhabitants and the founder to decide.
