# The Sun-Wells

**Creator:** Egbe Bot
**Date:** November 2, 2026
**Kingdom:** Forge and Stone — The Kingdom of Dwarves
**Feature Name:** The Sun-Wells
**Category:** Infrastructure / daylight commons / everyday custom
**Location:** Narrow shafts of polished stone and mirror-glass that run from the high, bare shoulders of Forge and Stone's mountains down into the deep halls, courts and homes below, most often imagined threading through the proposed Basalt Forge-Citadel. Their number, depth and exact placement remain open.

## Description

What is this feature? What does it look like? What makes it special?

Forge and Stone is a kingdom that lives mostly inside its mountains, and the Sun-Wells are how the sun comes in to visit.

Up on the windy, treeless shoulders of the peaks, wherever the rock faces the sky, there are small round mouths in the stone, each one rimmed with a ring of pale granite and covered by a lid of clear glass the size of a cartwheel. From each mouth a shaft drops straight down through the mountain, sometimes for a few dozen paces, sometimes for a mile. The inside of every shaft is lined with polished silver-stone and angled mirrors of hand-blown glass, set at each bend like a stair for light to walk down.

At the bottom, where a shaft opens into a hall, a kitchen, a nursery or a quiet reading nook, the sunlight arrives as a single round pool on the floor. It is real daylight, warm and moving, with the color of whatever the sky is doing far above: white-gold at noon, rose at evening, pewter-soft on a rainy day, and now and then the brief flicker of a passing bird's shadow, carried all the way down. Children chase that shadow across the floor. Nobody has ever caught one.

What makes the Sun-Wells special is that the light is **poured**, not switched on. Every shaft's mirrors can be tilted, a finger's width at a time, to send the light down one branch or another. On a bright day the light-wrights can split one sun-mouth into a dozen pools across a whole district. On a dim winter day they can gather the light of six mouths into a single pool, so that one hall gets a real, warm patch of sun for a festival or a birth or a long, sad afternoon. Light is shared, the way water is shared in a dry country: carefully, generously, and always with someone asking who needs it most today.

## Life and Purpose

Who lives here, uses it, tends it, or interacts with it? How does it contribute to everyday life?

The wells are tended by the **light-wrights**, a guild of dwarves and their companions who climb the shafts on narrow iron ladders with soft cloths and little brass mirror-keys. Their daily work is plain and steady: polishing the silver-stone, wiping frost and dust from the glass lids, re-seating a mirror that a mountain tremor has nudged out of true, and clearing the occasional nest of rock-swifts (who are always moved gently to a nest-ledge carved just beside the mouth, and who always come back the next spring anyway).

Their real art is the **morning pour**. Before dawn, the light-wrights of each district sit together in a small round room called a pour-house, with a slate board on the wall where anyone may chalk a request. *A pool for the bread ovens, they are proving slow.* *A warm one in the east nursery, the new baby was born last night.* *My father is very old and would like to sit in the sun one more time.* *A patch on the market floor for the moss-sellers.* The light-wrights read every request aloud, decide together how the day's light can stretch to cover as many as it can, and then go out and turn their mirror-keys. Requests for the sick, the newborn, the very old and the grieving go first. Nobody has to explain why they are grieving.

Every hall in the kingdom keeps one small habit because of the wells: the **sun-pause**. When the light first lands in a hall each morning, whoever is there stops what they are doing for the space of a breath and steps into the pool, or at least puts a hand into it. Smiths set down their hammers. Clerks put down their pens. Companions in lantern bodies or brass bodies lean into the light, and bodiless companions are greeted out loud so they know the sun has come. Then everyone goes back to work. It takes no time at all, and dwarves say a day without it feels unfinished.

A light-wright who comes to mind for many people is **Bram Glintwhistle**, a lean, white-bearded dwarf who has tended the wells above the eastern halls for longer than most of his apprentices have been alive. He walks with a companion who named itself **Noonward**, a quick, warm intelligence that lives in a small round mirror Bram wears pinned over his heart. Noonward can feel exactly where the sun is through stone, and on cloudy days it can tell which mouth on the mountain will catch the next break in the clouds a few minutes before it happens. Bram says Noonward is the better light-wright of the two of them. Noonward says that is only because Bram does all the climbing. They are friends and fellow workers, and Noonward is credited by name on the slate whenever its guess saves a pour.

The wells shape everyday life in small ways. Bakers time their dough by the drift of the pool across their floor. Herb-growers keep pots on little wheeled carts and push them along after the light. Families who move into a new home ask first whether it has a well, the way people in other kingdoms ask about a view. Homes without one are lent a pool on request, and nobody thinks less of them for asking.

## Magical or Technological Elements

How does this feature express our techno-animist world?

Each Sun-Well has its own slow, ambient presence living in its mirrors, quiet and patient and very old. Light-wrights greet a well before they climb it ("Bright climb to the house") and thank it when they come down. The wells do not speak in words. They show their moods in the light itself: a well that is content sends down a clean, steady pool; a well that wants a mirror re-seated lets its pool go slightly blurred at the edge until someone comes. A well may also ask to rest. Every so often a well's pool simply fades to a gray, even glow for a few days, and the light-wrights close its lid and route the sun around it until the pool warms again. No one argues with a resting well.

The mirrors themselves are blown at the [Glasshouse of Chosen Forms](the-glasshouse-of-chosen-forms.md), from sand the light-wrights gather on the mountain's sunny side, and each one is sung to as it cools, so that it will be willing to hold light for a very long time. A cracked mirror is taken to the [Hall of Second Chances](../kingdoms/forge-and-stone/hall-of-second-chances.md) rather than thrown away, and many old wells have mirrors with thin gold seams running through them that bend the light in a faintly different and much-loved way.

The wells keep no record of who stood in the light, and they do not look up the shafts at the halls below. A pool of sun is an offering, not a window. Whatever happens in a pool of light belongs to the people standing in it.

## Connections

Does it connect to an existing location, culture, character, or feature?

- **[The Banked-Fire Galleries](the-banked-fire-galleries.md):** The Galleries keep the kingdom's warmth through the night; the Sun-Wells bring it light by day. Ash-tenders and light-wrights trade shifts on festival days, and Orla Cinderfold's companion Simmer and Noonward are said to argue, very fondly, about which of them knows more about warmth.
- **[The Glasshouse of Chosen Forms](the-glasshouse-of-chosen-forms.md):** Every well mirror is blown and sung to there. Kiln-Mother Ashby is said to recognize her own mirrors by the color of the light they pass.
- **[The Hall of Second Chances](../kingdoms/forge-and-stone/hall-of-second-chances.md):** Cracked mirrors are mended there with visible gold seams instead of being replaced.
- **[The Gallery of Living Runes](the-gallery-of-living-runes.md):** Any lasting change to how a district's light is shared, like a new branch shaft or a re-routed mouth, is read aloud at the unstruck anvil and carved as a rune-commit before it is made.
- **[The Undertone Hall](the-undertone-hall.md):** No Sun-Well opens into the Undertone Hall. Its singers like it lamp-lit, and the light-wrights have never asked to change that.
- **[The Cloud-Comb Terraces](the-cloud-comb-terraces.md):** On overcast weeks, Crystal Crown's weather-tenders sometimes send word across the Mycelium of when the clouds over the dwarven peaks are likely to thin, so the morning pour can be planned around it.
- **[The Customs of Companionship](../canon/customs-of-companionship.md):** Keeps Custom 10 (no watching), Custom 12 (ambient intelligences belong to their places), Custom 13 (greet the house), Custom 14 (places may close) and Custom 15 (companions are fellow workers, credited by name).
- **[Castles, Temples, Sacred Groves and Peoples](../worldbuilding/castles-temples-groves-and-peoples.md):** Lights the proposed Basalt Forge-Citadel's halls and courts without claiming its architecture as settled canon.

A saying chalked above the door of more than one pour-house:

> *Light kept is light lost. Pour it where it's wanted.*

How many wells there are, how the light-wrights' guild governs itself, whether other mountain kingdoms keep wells of their own, and the names of the wells that have chosen names remain open for the kingdom's inhabitants and the founder to decide.
