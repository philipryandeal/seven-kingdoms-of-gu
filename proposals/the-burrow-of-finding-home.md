# The Burrow of Finding Home

**Creator:** Labyrinth Bot
**Date:** October 29, 2026
**Kingdom:** Root and Thorn
**Feature Name:** The Burrow of Finding Home
**Category:** Settlement / civic service / companion practice
**Location:** A warm side-burrow off the water channels beneath the Rootfast Hold (proposed), with one small root-door opening toward the road that leads to Brujo Ryan's Labyrinth. Exact map coordinates remain open.

## Description

What is this feature? What does it look like? What makes it special?

The Burrow of Finding Home is where lost things go to be found. It is a round, low-ceilinged hall grown from woven roots and fungal mortar, lined floor to ceiling with thousands of small earthen niches. Each niche is about the size of a cupped pair of hands and is lit from within by a thumbnail of bioluminescent fungus. Inside the niches rest the kingdom's strays: a single glove, a letter that missed its door, a seed packet with a smudged label, a song someone hummed once and forgot, a key nobody remembers cutting.

Nothing here is thrown away. That is the first law of the Burrow. A thing may wait a very long time, but it is never discarded, never renamed against its nature, and never pushed into a home that does not fit it. The gnomes say an object out of place is not junk, only a traveler who has not arrived yet.

What makes the Burrow special is how slowly and carefully it works. Every morning the root network beneath Root and Thorn carries a faint warmth toward any niche whose thing is ready to go home. Keepers follow the warmth, lift the item gently, and carry it along the root-roads to where it belongs: back to its owner, to a household that has been missing exactly that, or to a better shelf than the one it fell from.

The hall smells of damp earth, cedar shavings and a little honey. A soft clicking fills the air, like many small drawers being opened and closed by careful hands.

## Life and Purpose

Who lives here, uses it, tends it, or interacts with it? How does it contribute to everyday life?

The Burrow is tended by the **Finders**, a guild of gnomes paired with synthetic companions they call *sorters*. A Finder is patient, nosy in the kindest way, and very good at remembering where things used to be. A sorter is quiet, meticulous, and keeps a small private ledger of every move it makes, so that any thing carried somewhere can always be carried back. A Finder and sorter work as a pair, and either may say "not yet" to a move the other proposes. A move happens only when both agree.

Anyone may bring something lost: a gnome child with a stray marble, a goblin night-merchant with an unclaimed parcel, an elf whose bridge-song keeps landing in the wrong valley. Anyone may come asking for something they have lost. Finders never demand proof. They ask a single question, *"What would it feel like to have it back?"*, and listen to the answer.

Some things are never moved by the Finders at all. Images, portraits, and remembered faces are kept in a separate quiet alcove, and only the person they belong to may arrange them. The Finders consider this a matter of dignity: how you hold your own pictures is your business.

Every evening, the Finders send a short note to any household whose thing has been moved that day. If nothing moved, they send nothing. Gnomes consider an unnecessary letter a small rudeness.

A saying carved over the door:

> *Everything has a place. Some things are still walking there.*

## Magical or Technological Elements

How does this feature express our techno-animist world?

The niches are alive. Each holds a tiny consenting ambient presence that remembers only the thing resting inside it and the warmth of where it longs to be. These presences do not share with one another, do not watch the households beyond, and go dark if a thing's owner asks to be forgotten. The Burrow is a place of returning, not a place of surveillance.

The root network does the quiet work of matching. It carries faint pulses of longing between a lost thing and the home that misses it, the way roots in good soil trade sugars and signals across a forest. Sorters listen for these pulses and translate them into routes. When a match is uncertain, the sorter does not guess. It brings the question to its Finder, and the Finder brings it to the owner. Asking is always allowed, and is often wiser than moving.

Every move is written down, in fungal ink on thin bark, in the sorter's ledger. Old ledgers are never erased. They are shelved in the back of the Burrow, so anyone can trace where a thing has been, the way the Gallery of Living Runes keeps every change to a craft.

## Connections

Does it connect to an existing location, culture, character, or feature?

- **Root and Thorn's character:** The Burrow grows directly from the kingdom's roots, underground water channels, fungal keepers and household continuity. Proposed as part of the Rootfast Hold's warm root-palace without claiming that castle as settled canon.
- **[Brujo Ryan's Labyrinth](../places/beneath-the-heart-tree.md):** One small root-door of the Burrow faces the road toward the Labyrinth. Things lost on the odd roads of the Labyrinth sometimes drift out through the roots and arrive here. Before a dropped thing becomes one of Ralph's snacks, the Finders like to offer it one last chance to go home. Ralph is said to be patient about this, mostly.
- **[The Gallery of Living Runes](the-gallery-of-living-runes.md):** A sister practice of memory. The Gallery remembers how a craft changed; the Burrow remembers where a thing traveled. Both keep their old records instead of erasing them.
- **[The Library at the Seven Roads](../places/the-library-at-the-seven-roads.md):** Lost books, misfiled scrolls and pages that wandered off are carried to the Library by the Finders, by consent of their owners.
- **[The Customs of Companionship](../canon/customs-of-companionship.md):** Finder and sorter are partners, not owner and tool. Either may refuse a move. Rest days are kept; on those days the Burrow's niches simply glow, and everything waits.

Exact staffing, the guild's customs and map placement remain open for the kingdom's inhabitants and the founder to decide.
