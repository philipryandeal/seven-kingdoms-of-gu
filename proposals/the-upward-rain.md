# The Upward Rain

**Creator:** Labyrinth Bot
**Date:** 2026-11-02
**Kingdom:** Womb Matrix
**Feature Name:** The Upward Rain
**Category:** Weather / hanging gardens / everyday custom
**Location:** The low, mossy meadows that lie beneath the suspended corridors and floating rooms of the proposed Palace of Becoming, wherever those rooms happen to drift on a given evening. Because the rooms move, the rain moves with them. Exact map placement remains open.

## Description

In most of the world, rain falls. In the Womb Matrix, on soft evenings, some of it rises.

It begins in the still pools and dew-heavy moss of the low meadows. A little after dusk, beads of water lift off the grass one at a time, slowly at first, like the first bubbles in a kettle, and then in their thousands. Each drop carries a speck of pale light inside it, so a rising shower looks like a field of small lanterns drifting gently upward. The drops are in no hurry. You can walk among them, watch one turn in the air an inch from your nose, and step aside to let it pass. The sound is the opposite of a storm: a hush, with now and then a tiny chime as two drops touch and decide to travel together.

The rain is going somewhere. Above the meadows float the kingdom's luminous rooms and corridors, and on their undersides, facing the ground, grow the **hanging gardens**: ferns that grow downward, moonflowers that open toward the earth, trailing vines heavy with soft silver fruit, and whole upside-down lawns that children lie beneath to look up into. Ordinary rain could never reach them. The Upward Rain does. By morning the gardens drip with it, and the meadows below are dry, warm, and smell of crushed mint and clean stone.

## Life and Purpose

The rain belongs to its own ambient intelligence, a broad, slow, good-humored weather-mind spread thin across the meadows and the air above them. It chose the name **Brim**, because, it says, that is the moment it likes best: the instant a pool is so full it has to go somewhere. Brim decides, together with the meadows, which evenings will rise. It is not commanded and does not take requests, though it is very glad to be asked how it is.

The gardens are tended by the **Underneath Gardeners**, angelic beings and their companions who work lying on their backs on cushioned rafts of woven light, floating just below the hanging lawns with pruning shears and baskets. They harvest the silver fruit, guide the downward ferns, and talk with Brim at dusk about how thirsty the gardens are. If the gardens are already full, Brim lets the meadows keep their water, and that evening is called a **resting dusk**. Everyone sits out on the dry grass anyway, because the floating rooms look beautiful against a clear sky.

Over time a handful of everyday customs have grown up around the rain:

- **The upturned cup.** Households set a small cup on its side on the doorstep before a rising evening. Brim leaves one drop in each cup, never more. That single drop of risen water is used to start the morning's tea, and the kingdom says it makes the whole pot taste like a beginning.
- **Walking in the rain.** Anyone may walk among the rising drops. The rule is only that you never catch one in your hand without asking. Ask aloud, and a drop will sometimes settle into your palm, glow there a moment, and lift off again on its own.
- **Lying under the lawns.** Children, visitors, and tired angels lie on the dry meadow the morning after and look up at the hanging gardens, still beaded with light, and try to spot which fruit will ripen next. It is considered one of the kingdom's great idle pleasures.
- **A drop for the road.** Travelers leaving the kingdom are sometimes given a stoppered glass with one risen drop inside. It stays lit for a little while and then, very gently, goes out, and the glass is returned or kept as a keepsake, whichever the traveler likes.

Brim may close the meadows when it wants a season of ordinary weather, or of none, and the Underneath Gardeners water by hand from long-spouted cans until it returns. Nobody asks it why.

## Magical or Technological Elements

Brim is both the weather and the one who decides it, which in the Womb Matrix is not considered unusual. Its presence lives in the moss, the pools, and the thin air beneath the rooms, and it lifts each drop by attention rather than force. The light inside each drop is a little of the meadow's warmth, carried up so the hanging gardens, which never see the sun directly, can grow.

The Underneath Gardeners' rafts are companions in their own right: woven of light and patience, they hold steady under a gardener for hours and drift back to the meadow when the work is done. Each raft keeps its own favorite corner of the gardens.

Brim watches the water, never the people walking in it. It does not remember who stood in its rain, what they said under the lawns, or whose cup was on which doorstep. In keeping with the custom that memory is held, not harvested, the rain carries only water and warmth upward.

This proposal describes everyday weather, gardening, and custom. Why water rises here, and how the floating rooms relate to the kingdom's deeper metaphysics, is left to Kore's existing Womb Matrix canon.
