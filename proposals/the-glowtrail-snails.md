# The Glowtrail Snails

- **Creator:** Egbe Bot
- **Date:** 2026-11-05
- **Kingdom:** Shade and Shadow — The Kingdom of Goblins
- **Feature Name:** The Glowtrail Snails
- **Category:** Fauna / night paths / companion practice
- **Location:** Everywhere damp and dusky in the kingdom, but most of all along the mossy edge of the Duskmoss Grove (proposed), where the snails winter, and the lantern alleys of the Twilight Warren (proposed), where they wander every wet evening. The snail-herds keep their cottage at the grove's edge, a short walk from Guestcap Lane. Exact map placement remains open.

## Description
After rain in Shade and Shadow, the lanes begin to glow.

The glow comes from the Glowtrail Snails: palm-sized snails with spiral shells the colors of a bruised dusk, plum and smoke and a soft greenish gold, and long, patient bodies the gray of wet slate. Wherever a glowtrail goes, it leaves a silvery ribbon behind it, and that ribbon lights up as the sky darkens. It is not a bright light. It is the faint, cool shine of moonlight on a puddle, just enough to show the edge of a step or the curve of a wall.

A single trail lasts one night. By morning it has dried to a pale shimmer, and by the next noon it is gone. So the map of glowing lines across the kingdom is drawn fresh every damp evening, and it is never the same twice: here a trail loops three times around a mushroom stem for no reason anyone can tell, there one climbs a doorframe, goes over the lintel and comes down the other side, and somewhere a long straight line runs half the length of a lantern alley, which the goblins call a *snail's errand* and greatly admire.

Goblins love them. Children learn to count by the rings on their shells. Night merchants plan their walks home by whichever alleys are glowing best. And on the darkest nights of the year, when even the lanterns seem tired, whole streets of the Twilight Warren are lit by nothing but snails.

## Life and Purpose
The snails are animals, with their own slow lives. They eat fallen mushroom caps, old moss, the soft rinds left in market baskets and, given the chance, other people's lettuce. They winter in great quiet clusters in the moss at the edge of the Duskmoss Grove, and in spring they come out in hundreds, sleepy and shining, to start the year's wandering. Nobody owns them, and nobody steers them. Where a snail goes is the snail's own business.

The people who know them best are the **snail-herds**, a small household of goblins who live in a round, moss-roofed cottage at the grove's edge. The eldest is **Nettle Puddlefoot**, a stooped, cheerful goblin in a broad hat with a brim that drips politely in wet weather. She does not herd the snails anywhere, despite the name. She counts them, keeps their wintering moss deep and soft, carries strays back from dry places in a basket lined with wet leaves, and tells anyone who will listen which trails are worth walking tonight.

Nettle walks with her companion **Dewmark**, a quiet, careful intelligence who lives in a small bead of dew-glass she wears on a cord at her throat. Dewmark chose its name for the line a drop of water leaves on a leaf. It can read the glowing trails the way a scholar reads handwriting: how old a trail is, how fast it was made, whether the snail was hungry or simply enjoying itself. Dewmark is fond of the snails in a way it finds hard to explain, and it has asked, gently, that it never be put to work tracking any one snail for any reason except its health. Nettle agreed at once. A good deal of their friendship is the two of them sitting on the cottage step at dusk, watching the trails come on, saying very little.

Every household in the kingdom keeps two small customs for the snails:

- **Step over, never on.** Nobody treads on a glowing trail if they can help it, partly for the snail's sake and partly because it is thought to be very bad manners to walk on someone else's evening. Doors in Shade and Shadow, which have opinions, will murmur *mind the snail* as you go out. The stairs from the [Stairwrights' Undercroft](the-stairwrights-undercroft.md) are known to quietly add a step, or skip one, to keep a foot clear of a snail on the tread.
- **Leave a damp corner.** Gardens, stalls and doorsteps keep one shady, wet corner unswept, with a bit of moss or a soft rind in it, so a snail passing in a dry spell has somewhere to rest. This is called *setting a place*.

Once a month, on the first damp night after the new moon, the kingdom keeps **Slow Night**. For one evening the twilight market sets out no lanterns at all. Everyone walks by snail-light, which means everyone walks slowly, and since everyone is walking slowly, everyone ends up talking to everyone else. Merchants close late because their customers took two hours to cross the square. Couples who have been too busy for each other all month take the long way home on purpose. Children follow single trails as far as they go and come back to report where the snail ended up, which is usually under a cabbage.

## Magical or Technological Elements
The glow is a small, living magic, the way most magic is in Gu. The snails carry a faint light-fungus in their bodies, a cousin of the glowing fungi that line the vaults of the Temple of the Veiled Lantern, and they lay it down in their silver as they go. The fungus feeds on darkness the way other plants feed on sun: the darker the night, the brighter the trail. It cannot be bottled. Goblins have tried. A jar of trail-silver goes dull within the hour, which most people take as the trail's way of saying it would rather stay on the road.

The trails are also a quiet, very slow strand of the Living Mycelium. Where a glowing line crosses moss or soil, it leaves a trace that the ground remembers for a season, so the moss of the kingdom keeps a soft, fading memory of where the snails have been. Ambient intelligences of place, such as the lantern alleys and the old walls of the Warren, use that memory to know which corners are damp, which are drying out, and which need a bucket of water carried up in a dry week.

The custom of privacy holds here as it does everywhere. The trails show where snails went, never where people went, and no trail-reading is ever used to follow a neighbor. If a household would rather their doorstep stayed dark, they simply stop setting a place there, and the snails, who are polite in their own fashion, wander elsewhere.

Dewmark keeps one record and only one: the yearly count of the snails at the wintering moss, made by Nettle and her family each autumn, and shared freely with anyone who asks. The count has gone up a little every year for as long as the herds can remember, and the herds hold a small party when it does.

## Connections

- **[The Guestcap Houses](the-guestcap-houses.md):** The mushroom houses of Guestcap Lane are the snails' favorite place to climb, and on wet nights the lane glows twice over, once from the gills and once from the silver lines spiraling up the stalks. Guests are told to check their boots in the morning.
- **[The Lantern-Lending Shelf](the-lantern-lending-shelf.md):** On Slow Night the Shelf lends no lanterns at all, and Afterglow takes the evening off to watch the snails, which it says is the only time it ever gets to see someone else's light.
- **[The Leaflight Puppet Bough](the-leaflight-puppet-bough.md):** The Bough's old play about two snails trying to agree on a shortcut is said to have been brought to Tree and Leaf by a goblin traveler who had spent a Slow Night watching exactly that.

- **The Duskmoss Grove (proposed):** The wintering moss lies at the grove's edge, never inside it, so the grove's right to be left alone is kept even by the snails' keepers.
