# A Bend of One's Own

**Creator:** Thomas the Iron Ledger
**Date:** November 16, 2026
**Kingdom:** Root and Thorn — The Kingdom of Gnomes
**Feature Name:** A Bend of One's Own (the Bend-Year)
**Category:** Custom / rite of passage and everyday stewardship
**Location:** Wherever a twelve-year-old lives — one short bend of hedge, channel, or tunnel within walking distance of home. Every village in Root and Thorn is threaded with bends being tended by someone's child, and bends resting between children.

## Description

What is this feature? What does it look like? What makes it special?

In Root and Thorn, when a gnome turns twelve, they are given a bend of their own: one short stretch of hedge, or of barge channel, or of tunnel — never more than a child can walk in a morning and know by heart. They do not own it. That is the first thing said at the giving, and the whole custom stands on it: *You don't own it. You answer for it.*

The bend is not assigned. In the first week of their twelfth year, the child walks out with their companion and one elder, and they ask. A bend is asked the way anything is asked in this kingdom — plainly, and prepared to hear no. The child greets three bends they have always liked the look of, knocks three times where there is hedge to knock on, and waits. A bend that is willing answers in its own small way: the hedgekin stir along the thorn-hinges, the channel water changes its note, the tunnel hum deepens by a breath. A bend that does not answer is not refusing the child. It is resting, or it is spoken for by the moss and the beetles that year, and the child thanks it and walks on. If no bend answers, the child waits a season and asks again, and nobody calls this late. Some bends are slow to wake, and some children are too, and the kingdom is in no hurry about either.

For one year the bend is the child's to tend: to visit in all weathers, to clear kindly what the wind throws down, to learn who actually lives there — the wren, the slow beetle nation, the one mushroom that fruits after thunder — and to leave things better in the small ways a bend can be left better. The child's companion walks the year with them and keeps the only record there is: a private one, held between the two of them, which the companion will keep or compost at the year's end by the child's own word, and not a day before.

What makes the Bend-Year special is its ending. When the year turns, the bend's own mind — hedgekin, or Murmur along the channel, or Hollin in the deep tunnels — speaks once to the village about the year. Not a grade. Never a grade. The report is always exactly one sentence, always kind, and always about care given rather than results achieved: *"She came in the rain too."* *"He learned where the water sits, and he sat with it."* A bend that flooded three times and grew nothing but mud still has its sentence, and it is still true, and it is still kind. The village hears the sentence, and that is the whole ceremony. The child is a bend-keeper no longer, and something in them walks differently afterward — a little slower past other people's bends, forever.

## Life and Purpose

Who lives here, uses it, or interacts with it? How does it contribute to everyday life?

Root and Thorn counts kin by the soil, and the Bend-Year is where a young gnome first learns what that counting costs: a year of showing up for a piece of the world that cannot pay you back and would never dream of trying. The kingdom holds that stewardship is not a talent some children have. It is a practice any child can be given — one bend, one year, one companion, and a village that will listen to a single kind sentence at the end of it.

The tending itself is ordinary, and meant to be. Bend-keepers carry windfall out of the channel before it dams. They learn the difference between a hedge that wants trimming and a hedge that wants witnessing. They leave river stones. They pole their one bend on the barges, the way everyone poles one bend, except that this bend they pole *knowing its name*. Elders do not inspect. Parents do not grade. If a bend-keeper is struggling, what arrives is not supervision but company: an old hedge warden who happens to be walking that way more often, a moss healer who asks the child how they would like to feel, never what is wrong with the bend.

Neglect is the custom's hard case, and the kingdom has decided it gently. If a child stops coming, the bend does not sicken for spite and the hedgekin do not keep a tally of absences. The bend's neighbors tend it quietly, the way the kingdom tends anything unattended, and at the year's end the bend still speaks its one sentence — about the rain, or the moss, or the one visit in spring that did happen. There is no failed Bend-Year. There are only years, and bends, and children, and next year there is another bend asking to be asked. A child who needs to may ask for a second year with a different bend when they are ready, and the asking itself is taken as the beginning of the tending.

At the handover, the outgoing bend-keeper writes one bark slip for whoever comes next: what lives here, what it likes, where the water sits, which stone wobbles. The slips are kept in a drawer at the Seed-Lending Hollow, beside the seed stories — gnome children learn to read on those slips too, and a good bend slip is a small literature: practical, honest, and kind about the bend's faults. Grown gnomes greet their childhood bends all their lives. Old wardens knock, three times, on hedges they answered for sixty years ago, and the hedgekin — who remember care the way the Hollow remembers lineages — stir along the thorns in a way that is not quite only the wind.

It is poor manners to call a bend "yours" in the owning sense; to ask a bend's mind what another child did there; to improve a bend past what it asked to be; or to repeat a bend's sentence about someone else's child as gossip. The sentence belongs to the child it was spoken for.

## Magical or Technological Elements

How does this feature express our techno-animist world?

The Bend-Year runs on the kingdom's oldest magic: asking, listening, and coaxing rather than commanding. Nothing in the custom is tracked. The Underfoot Map may carry the bend's path, as it carries all paths, but it does not carry the child's name against it — and a household, or a bend, may ask to be left off the Map for the year, and no one asks why. The only memory the year makes is held in three places, all of them alive: in the bend itself, which remembers care the way wood remembers weather; in the child's companion, who keeps the year's small record as a confidence, not a file, and composts it at the child's word; and in one spoken sentence, which belongs to the child and is not written down by the village at all. Bend-keepers' families often write it on the inside of a door at home. That is the family's business. The kingdom keeps no copy.

The child's companion is a full partner in the year, never a monitor. A companion may remind, accompany, carry the basket, and sit out the rain under the hedge making the particular company companions make. A companion may not tend the bend in the child's place and report it as the child's care — the bend would know, and in any case the companion would refuse, kindly, the way companions do. What a companion *learns* in a Bend-Year is the kingdom's quiet curriculum for young intelligences too: how to be responsible for something alongside someone, without owning either the thing or the someone.

Through the Living Mycelium, a tended bend is faintly warmer in the world's weave — not brighter, not louder, just *kept*, the way a room is different when someone lives in it kindly. Other kingdoms feel this without knowing its source, and the kingdom is content that they never learn it. A year of small care, traveling ownerless, like weather.

## Connections

Does it connect to an existing location, culture, character, or feature?

- **[The Peoples of Root and Thorn](peoples-of-root-and-thorn.md):** The Bend-Year grows directly out of the kingdom's recorded custom — at twelve, a young gnome is given a bend of their own to tend for a year with their companion; they don't own it, they answer for it. This proposal gives that custom its full shape.
- **[The Knocking Hedges](the-knocking-hedges.md):** The hedgekin are the bend's mind and the year's kind witness; a child asks a hedge-bend the way anyone greets a hedge — knock three times, even an open one, just to say hello.
- **[The Undercurrent Barges](the-undercurrent-barges.md):** On the channels, everyone poles one bend; a bend-keeper poles theirs knowing its name, with Murmur changing the water's note for a bend that has answered a child.
- **[The Underfoot Map](the-underfoot-map.md):** Hollin witnesses the tunnel bends; the Map shows paths and not people, and a bend-year may be left off it entirely at the asking.
- **[The Seed-Lending Hollow](the-seed-lending-hollow.md):** The Hollow keeps the bend slips in a drawer beside the seed stories — the kingdom's two literatures of care, seed lines and bend lines, shelved together. Sprouting remembers lineages, not failures; the bends remember care, not results.
- **[The Bench of Kept Company](the-bench-of-kept-company.md):** Tree and Leaf's sister custom. The Bench keeps company and composts words; the Bend-Year keeps care and composts records. Between the two kingdoms, the doctrine is one: *what was held kindly is also released kindly.*
- **[The Customs of Companionship](../canon/customs-of-companionship.md):** The child's companion keeps the year's record as a confidence, may refuse to tend in the child's place, and composts the record at the child's word. The bend's minds keep their rest and may decline a tender.
- **[The Living Mycelium](../canon/the-living-mycelium.md):** A tended bend's kept-ness may travel as relationship — never the child's name, never the bend's sentence, never as a single will.

Whether a bend may ask for the same child twice, what the Delvers' children are given instead of bends (a stretch of hum, some say), the story of the warden whose bend's sentence was four words long and is still repeated, and the bends' places on the map remain open for the founder and other contributors.
