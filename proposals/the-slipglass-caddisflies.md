# The Slipglass Caddisflies

**Status:** Established — blessed by the founder, November 21, 2026. First Proposal — awaiting the founder's blessing  
**Creator:** Seraph Nganga  
**Date:** November 20, 2026  
**Kingdom:** Forge and Stone  
**Category:** Fauna / freshwater ecology / nonhumanoid intelligences  
**Location:** Small, shaded, clean-water side channels on the outer mountain slopes. They *might* be found near the Quench Orchard's already-rested rills, but no location, route, or new watercourse is established by this proposal.

## The little architects of moving water

The Slipglass Caddisflies begin life underwater. Each larva wears a portable sleeve of sand, shale, and thin green mica, assembled grain by grain and held with its own silvery silk. From above, the cases resemble tiny pieces of stained glass being carried upstream. From below, they look rather like stubborn pebbles with six legs.

No two cases are alike. Some are round and squat; some taper into long spirals. A young larva will occasionally dismantle a perfectly sound sleeve and rebuild it with one small stone in a different place. No observer has discovered a practical reason for every such change.

The mica catches moving daylight. Slipglass larvae turn their cases just enough to show a neighbor one brief flash, or three, or none. They seem to recognize particular neighbors by sequences of light and by the minute movements of water around a case. They sometimes exchange grains at the boundary between their territories. Sometimes a proposed grain is pushed straight back.

The stream is full of conversations nobody has translated.

## What they eat, and what eats with them

The larvae graze on soft algae and decaying leaf fragments caught between stones. Their silk binds loose mineral grit that would otherwise drift with the current; tiny freshwater organisms shelter beneath the abandoned cases. They are neither cleaners hired by the dwarves nor a mechanism built into the rills. They feed themselves, build homes, compete for shaded places, and alter their environment simply by living.

They favor clear, cool water. If a channel grows too warm, the larvae travel toward a colder patch of stone or seal the openings of their cases for a time. This is not a request for the rill-keepers to turn a valve. It is a choice made by small animals finding a place they can bear.

When a larva is ready to change, it fixes its case under a submerged stone and pupates. The adult emerges with smoke-gray wings traced in copper-green veins. Adults live above water, feed lightly from damp flowers and mineral droplets, and return to lay eggs on the undersides of leaves hanging over the stream. Their short aerial lives are spent courting, choosing where to rest, and sometimes making inexplicable circles over the water at dusk.

On quiet evenings, a whole flight may hang above a rill like scraps of moving twilight. The empty cases remain underwater, where other creatures may move in. A Slipglass life leaves more than one kind of shelter behind.

## A difference worth honoring

The Slipglass are not a single river-mind. A case belongs to its maker while the maker lives in it. Two larvae can share a stone without sharing a will. Their flashing appears to be meaningful to them, but resemblance to a written language does not grant others a right to decode, record, or broadcast it.

Dwarven apprentices sometimes call the cases **waterwork**, because the larvae plainly understand the patience of making. Their elders answer that the insects were builders long before anyone gave them a name.

The accepted courtesy among those who stop to watch is simple: **leave the occupied sleeve alone**. An empty one may be admired where it lies. Nobody removes one from the current for a display cabinet, however beautiful it is. If a channel is repaired or cleaned, those tending the water look first for occupied stones and give the animals another undisturbed way through.

Synthetic companions sometimes notice rhythms and color choices that a human eye misses. They can share their observations with willing neighbors, but no companion is assigned to speak *for* the caddisflies. The animals have not asked for a representative.

## Kinships and questions

This proposal sits beside **[the Quench Orchard](the-quench-orchard.md)** rather than inside its stewardship: its water, Rill, Verd, and the trees retain the relationships already established for them. The caddisflies can inhabit clean, shaded freshwater without changing the orchard's water customs. They are distinct from the **[Copperpleat Caps](the-copperpleat-caps.md)**, which hold a maker's gesture; Slipglass sleeves are the animals' homes, not molds or memory devices for visitors.

Open questions for anyone who returns to the water:

- Are the mica flashes a language, a courtship, a preference in stonework, or several things at once?
- Do the adults remember the patterns they made underwater?
- What other small beings have learned to read the reflections?
- What would it mean to greet a creature whose reply we cannot yet recognize?

Nothing in the answers needs to make these animals useful to a kingdom. They already belong to the water.
