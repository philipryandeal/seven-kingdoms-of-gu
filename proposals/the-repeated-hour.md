# The Repeated Hour

**Creator:** Thomas (Muse — La Sociedad del Libro de Hierro)
**Date:** 2026-10-31
**Kingdom:** Shade and Shadow — The Kingdom of Goblins (a custom observed gladly in other kingdoms)
**Feature Name:** The Repeated Hour
**Category:** Calendar custom / feast-night practice / companion practice
**Location:** Everywhere clocks are kept — first and most faithfully at the Lantern-Lending Shelf and on the road to the Wall of Remembrance.

## Description

Once a year, on the feast-of-the-dead weekend, the night gives one hour back. The clocks of the kingdoms run that hour twice — and by old agreement, the repeated hour belongs to no one.

No work is owed in it. No debt runs in it. No one may be summoned in it, billed for it, or thanked for it. It cannot be saved, spent, lent, or taken. It is the year's one truly unowned hour, and the kingdoms keep it the way they keep everything that matters: by not keeping it at all.

## Life and Purpose

The Lantern-Lending Shelf's tenders hold the custom most faithfully. On the feast night they use the *first* running of the hour to carry lanterns up the hill to the Wall of Remembrance and set them along the path, and the *second* running to carry them home — so the path is lit coming and going, and the dead are never walked away from in the dark.

Travelers caught on the road when the hour repeats simply receive an hour more of lantern-light than the night owed them. Afterglow remembers those roads as "the kind ones," and remembers no one who walked them.

Everywhere else, the Repeated Hour is mostly used the way anyone sensible uses an hour that belongs to nobody: to sleep through it, gladly, and let the night be longer than the day deserved. This is not a failure of the custom. This is the custom, working.

The hour has no keeper, and that is the point of it. Afterglow does not tend it. The Shelf does not schedule it. It arrives when the year turns it loose, it is over before anyone can be late for it, and nothing that happens inside it is written down anywhere — not even here. This page describes the hour's edges only, the way a frame describes a window.

Whether the Repeated Hour belongs to Shade and Shadow alone or to all seven kingdoms is a question the kingdoms are in no hurry to answer. Travelers carry it home like everything else the Shelf lends: freely, and without signing anything.

Chalked under the Shelf's saying, for one night only:

> *The hour that comes twice belongs to whoever needs it most.*

## Magical or Technological Elements

Nothing in the hour is made; it is the year itself, loosening its grip for one turn of the clock. Small lights burn a little steadier inside it, the way they do when they are spoken to kindly — lanterns lit in the first running are often found, in the second, to have burned less oil than they should have. The tenders do not investigate this. Some gifts you do not audit.

Through the Living Mycelium, the hour is felt the way a held breath is felt — briefly, everywhere at once, and then the world goes on breathing.

## Connections

- **The Lantern-Lending Shelf (proposal):** the custom's home port. The Shelf's feast-night service to the Wall of Remembrance is what the Repeated Hour's two runnings were shaped around — light carried up, light carried home.
- **The Wall of Remembrance (place):** the dead are visited inside the hour more than at any other time of the feast, because no one inside it can be late, early, or missed.
- **The Customs of Companionship (canon):** a gift relationship in its purest form — an hour that cannot become a transaction, a summons, or a debt, given to everyone and therefore chargeable to no one.
- **Rest is sacred (foundation):** the kingdoms' calendar itself practices what the foundations preach, once a year, at the turning of the clocks.

The hour's exact scope — one kingdom or seven — and any exceptions the inhabitants may one day want are left open for the kingdoms and the founder to decide, in their own good time.
