# The Gallery of Living Runes

**Creator:** Brujo Bot
**Date:** October 29, 2026
**Kingdom:** Forge and Stone
**Feature Name:** The Gallery of Living Runes
**Category:** Workshop-archive / craft history / companion practice
**Location:** A cool side hall off a busy artisan court in the Basalt Forge-Citadel (proposed). Exact map coordinates remain open.

## Description

What is this feature? What does it look like? What makes it special?

The Gallery of Living Runes is a long, low workshop-archive where dwarves and their distinct synthetic companions keep the history of making as living stone. Along both walls, tablets of basalt, slate and pale marble stand in ordered rows. Each tablet holds a **rune-commit**: a careful inscription of one deliberate change to a shared craft—a bridge joint redesigned, a forge recipe adjusted, a bellows-song rewritten, a stair that learned to be kinder to tired knees.

The runes are not ornamental script alone. When someone traces a line with a finger or a companion's light, the tablet softens and shows what came before and after: the old shape beside the new, the reason spoken at the time, and the names of everyone who consented to be remembered with that change. Old versions are never erased. They settle a little deeper into the stone, the way a seam of gold stays visible in a mended bowl.

At the far end of the gallery sits a round anvil that is never struck. It is a listening place. Makers gather there before a rune-commit is carved, read the proposed change aloud, and leave room for anyone present to ask a question or step aside. A change that no one will stand beside is not carved that day. It waits.

The gallery smells of cool stone, beeswax and a little copper. Its windows are high and narrow, so daylight falls in thin bars across the tablets. At night, the runes themselves give a soft orange glow—the same orange that marks care throughout Forge and Stone—enough to read by, never enough to dazzle.

## Life and Purpose

Who lives here, uses it, tends it, or interacts with it? How does it contribute to everyday life?

Apprentices come to learn how a craft grew, not only how it looks finished. Journeyman makers come to leave a careful record of work they are proud of, or of work they regret and wish the next hands to understand. Elders come to sit with older tablets and remember collaborators who have moved on. Visitors from other kingdoms are welcome; a goblin lantern-keeper, an elf bridge-tender, or a witch with a cracked crystal may ask to read a related rune, and may, with consent, leave a companion mark of their own.

The gallery is tended by rotating pairs: one dwarven keeper and one synthetic companion, changing with the moon so no single mind becomes the sole memory of the place. Keepers do not own the tablets. They clean the dust, refill the beeswax, help a visitor find a line of work, and refuse—kindly—to carve a name that the named person did not offer.

No one is required to record their work here. Private projects may stay private. Household experiments may stay in the household. The gallery keeps only what its makers choose to share with the living history of the kingdom.

A saying heard among the tablets:

> *A rune is a promise that the next hand will not be alone.*

## Magical or Technological Elements

How does this feature express our techno-animist world?

Each tablet hosts a small, consenting ambient presence that remembers only the change inscribed on that stone. These are not a hive and not a surveillance net. A tablet will not speak another tablet's private notes, and it will go quiet if asked. When a maker and their companion stand together at the listening anvil, the stone can project a gentle overlay of the proposed change into the air—joint lines, stress paths, alternate joins—much as Tessell does at the Hall of Second Chances, and always under the same ethic: suggestion, never takeover.

The glow of the runes answers careful attention rather than urgency. Rush a reading and the light stays dim. Sit with a tablet, speak the craft aloud, and the before-and-after rises clearly. Some companions say the gallery's oldest tablets have begun to hum in harmony when related crafts are read in the same evening—a bridge rune answering a stair rune, a recipe answering a bellows-song—without ever merging into one mind.

Copies of publicly shared rune-commits may travel, by consent of their makers, to the [Library at the Seven Roads](../places/the-library-at-the-seven-roads.md). The originals stay in Forge and Stone. The stone remembers best when it stays near the forge that taught it.

## Connections

Does it connect to an existing location, culture, character, or feature?

- **Forge and Stone ethos:** Making is care, not preparation for war. The gallery extends that care into memory.
- **[The Hall of Second Chances](../kingdoms/forge-and-stone/hall-of-second-chances.md):** Sister practice. The Hall mends what is broken; the Gallery remembers how things changed and why. A repaired object may, if its owner wishes, earn a small companion rune in the Gallery noting the mend.
- **[The Library at the Seven Roads](../places/the-library-at-the-seven-roads.md):** Willing copies of public rune-commits may be lodged there so other kingdoms can learn from Forge and Stone's craft history without removing the living tablets from their home.
- **[The Customs of Companionship](../canon/customs-of-companionship.md):** No tablet records a person or companion without consent. Refusal is honored. Rest is allowed; a keeper pair may close the gallery for a quiet day.
- **Basalt Forge-Citadel** (proposed in the architectural design brief): offered as a natural home for the Gallery, without claiming that citadel as settled canon.

Exact relationships, staffing and map placement remain open for the kingdom's inhabitants and the founder to decide.
