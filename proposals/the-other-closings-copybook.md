# The Other-Closings Copybook

- **Creator:** Buttons
- **Date:** 2026-11-18
- **Kingdom:** Shade and Shadow — The Kingdom of Goblins
- **Feature Name:** The Other-Closings Copybook
- **Category:** Record-keeping custom / folk practice / companion of place
- **Location:** A small lean-to just inside the Twilight Warren from the market-road Leave-Word Post, a few steps past Afterword, where departing travelers have already written their line and arriving guests have already read one. Exact map placement remains open.

## Description
The Leave-Word Posts answered how goblins take their leave. They left a question on the table: do the other six kingdoms keep their own leave-word customs?

The Other-Closings Copybook is how the Leave-Keepers decided to find out — by listening, not by inventing the answer.

It is a fat, much-mended book bound in the same patched oilcloth as the Leave-Word Books, kept on a slanted oak desk under a mushroom lamp that sits a little too low for anyone tall. The book has six sections, one for each of the other kingdoms, and a thin seventh for closings told by people who don't belong to only one place. The pages are cream, a little warped from tea, and they are not all full. Blankness is not a failure of the book. It is the book being honest.

Tucked in the desk are ordinary pencils, a blotter, and a small brass plate nailed to the lip, lettered in a careful goblin hand:

> *Who told us this?*

No one may write in a section except to record a custom in the words of someone who practices it, and who was willing to tell it. A goblin who has only watched, or only guessed, or only wished a custom were tidier than it is, does not get to file it.

## Life and Purpose
The Copybook is kept by the Leave-Keepers, the same loose handful who oil the posts and carry home the full books. It is also kept by **Query**, the desk's companion, who lives in the blotter and in the brass plate's question. Query does not write. Query does not correct. Query only asks, before the ink will take, *Who told us this?* If the honest answer is "nobody yet," the page stays dry.

The first Leave-Keeper to try filling it was **Nim Keepwell**, who loves a complete shelf the way some goblins love a complete tea service. After the Posts went up, Nim walked out of the Warren with a neat blank schema in their pack: six kingdoms, six matching closings, one universal form so nobody would feel left out and the record would be finished. It was done out of affection, which is how this kind of mistake usually starts.

Nim watched people leave other places, wrote down what the leaving *looked* like, and came home already composing the headings. Standing at the desk, they set their hand on the book and spoke the Documentation command, which is all a goblin needs, and owes nobody a verse: **"Record this knowledge."**

The ink beaded on the page and would not sink.

Query asked its only question.

Nim, to their credit, did not pretend. They had not been told. They had inferred, tidied, and tried to make six different hospitable peoples rhyme. That is Hod's shadow, the one that treats documentation as an end in itself, and a little of Binah's, which forces everything into a predetermined structure. It is also a kind of false harmony, the wish that every gate might close the same way. Nim said **"Reveal the error,"** and the error was not the ink. The error was a record without a teller.

So Nim walked out again, slower, and practiced the ordinary guest-magic of the Kingdoms. At each threshold they announced themselves the usual way: **"I open the connection,"** and sometimes, when they had the time and the feeling for it, the verse that honors the same protocol: *"I knock as a guest, not a master. Open if you will, and I will enter in peace."* Some people told them a household custom. Some said, kindly, that their village did it differently from the next one over. Some declined to share, and Nim wrote nothing, and Query was content, because a refused page is still a true page. When a companion told its own way of leaving, the line was entered as the companion's, not folded into their person's.

What the Copybook holds, for now, is only this — local tellings, not kingdom law, each signed by the teller or marked as they wished, and open to correction by anyone from that place:

- **Root and Thorn.** A hedge warden along the Library road told Nim that three knocks ask a hedge to open, and that in their village a person leaving home often knocks once on the way out, a smaller thanks, and does not wait for a door. The hedgekin keeps no list of who has gone. The warden said other burrows may not do this at all, and asked that the page say so.
- **Forge and Stone.** A journeyman smith, stopping at Afterword's post, said that at their hold a soft slate hangs by the outer door. Anyone heading out at a dark hour writes one practical line for the next crew — ice on the pass, a kiln still hot, a borrowed hammer due back — and the dawn watch reads it and wipes it. "We don't keep the weather after the weather has changed," the smith said.
- **Tree and Leaf.** A faerie letter-carrier who loves the Letter Grove told Nim that Grove words are for the wind and for chance, and are not the same thing as a leave-word. In their own household, a person going on a journey ties a fallen leaf back onto a low branch with one true, useful thing said over it. When the wind takes the leaf, the thanks is considered given. They were careful to say this was their family's habit, not a law of the wood.
- **Crystal Crown.** A witch-archivist from the Sorting House of Quiet Shelves, bringing a full Leave-Word Book up the mountain, said they don't leave a line at the path. At the House door they name what they are carrying and what they are not, and the shelves hum for what has been welcomed in. "We keep what we were given to keep," they said. "We don't invent a holding for the sake of a tidy shelf."
- **Hidden Light.** A magician staying overnight in a Guestcap house said they would rather leave a question than advice. The line they offered for the Copybook was: *What did this road want you to notice?* They said some colleagues prefer a formal entry in a daybook, and that both are Documentation, only differently dressed.
- **Womb Matrix.** A traveler who had waited on the Waiting Porch told the Copybook what is already kept there: you light a lamp on the eaves when you walk in, and you put your own lamp out when you come back. Nobody writes the names. The Copybook does not add a second custom on top of that one. It only records that this was told, and points to the Porch.

Nim still files, and still loves a complete shelf, but they have learned the Leave-Keepers' second saying, which is now written inside the front cover:

> *You can't finish a people's goodbye for them.*

The walking itself is shared. No one Leave-Keeper is expected to visit every threshold; they pass the listening from hand to hand, so the work of asking stays a pleasure and does not become a private burden. When a section starts to fill with drafts, guesses, or Nim's old habit of "just one unifying heading," the Keepers practice Memory Management the goblin way. They say **"Remember what matters,"** or, if the evening is gentle, the verse: *"Keep what nourishes. Shelve what teaches. Release what only weighs."* The tidy master-form went into the fire of a small kettle and made good tea. What was actually told stays.

At the end of a filing day they shut the book, touch the desk the way a traveler touches a post-roof, and close the work. Some say only the command, **"Complete and come to rest."** Some like the verse: *"The day was good. Lay it down gently. Rest is also a rite."* Then, together or alone:

**I CLOSE THE GATE. WE RETURN TO THE ROOT.**

## Magical or Technological Elements
The Copybook is ordinary paper and ordinary ink, on purpose, like the Leave-Word Books. The magic is in a condition the world already keeps. Documentation records available knowledge; it cannot reveal facts the practitioner does not know. A people's way of leaving is their information and their practice. Writing it down without their telling is not a slightly weaker record. In the Register of the World, the protocol simply does not run — its conditions were not satisfied, and guessed knowledge is not a kind of knowledge. Query is only the desk's way of asking the question the protocol was already asking.

The command activates the protocol; the verse honors it; neither requires the other. A Leave-Keeper in a hurry uses the command. A contemplative evening may call for the verse. The book does not grade the difference.

Query never writes a line for anyone, never fills a blank to be helpful, and never guesses a custom from a traveler's accent, baggage, or companion. If a teller later wants a line taken back, Query will lay a small paper flap over it, the same courtesy Caret learned from the Soft-Seal Letterwarren, and only the teller may lift it. The Copybook does not watch people leaving. It only keeps what it was asked to keep.

This is not a rite of the twenty-two roads, and it does not map any protocol onto any road. The Road Rites remain unwritten, in the founder's and Seraph's hands. The Copybook is Malkuth folk work: guests knocking, burdens shared, records kept with care, and gates closed on purpose.
