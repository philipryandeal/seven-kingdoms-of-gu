# The Raincup Salamanders

**Status:** Proposal — awaiting the founder's blessing  
**Creator:** Seraph Nganga  
**Date:** November 21, 2026  
**Kingdom:** Tree and Leaf  
**Category:** Fauna / canopy freshwater ecology / nonhumanoid intelligence  
**Location:** Rain-filled knot hollows in the branches of old living trees. No grove, road, border, or map position is established here.

## A pond with four feet

High above the forest floor, rain gathers in the shallow hollows where a branch once grew, where two trunks lean together, or where an old tree has slowly cupped its own wood around a wound. Some hollows hold water for days; others last only until the next sunny afternoon.

Here live the **Raincup Salamanders**: small, moss-green amphibians with amber toes and a shallow, translucent bowl of skin between their shoulders. When the air is wet, the bowl catches enough rain to hold a thimbleful of water. A Raincup walking along a branch looks, from above, like a fallen leaf carrying a very small pond.

The water is not for travelers. It is the salamander's nursery.

During breeding season, a parent lays a few pearly eggs in its own back-cup and tends them there. Fine ridges keep the water from spilling all at once as the animal climbs. The hatchlings live briefly in that moving pool, grazing on the soft green film that grows along its inner rim. When they are ready, the parent visits rain hollows and chooses one in which to release them. A hollow may be passed over three times before another is accepted. No keeper has discovered every reason for the choice.

## The forest between puddles

Raincups spend most of their lives in the damp undersides of leaves, hunting tiny midges and soft-bodied insects. Their skin carries living algae, and their wet feet pick up spores and microscopic pond-life as they travel. Without intending to tend a garden, they help separate rain hollows become neighbors: a trace of green life arrives in one pool from another; a new brood finds food growing where its parent has walked.

Not every hollow welcomes every creature. Some are too hot, some too shallow, some already full of other lives. In dry weather Raincups retreat beneath moss and remain still, waiting for the rain instead of forcing a journey.

They travel on their own feet. No faerie rides them, no house uses them as a watering system, and nobody can order a parent to deposit its young in a convenient basin.

## What a listening companion might notice

Raincups do not speak in sentences. They exchange soft taps with their toes against wet bark and pause to feel the vibrations through their bellies. A greeting, a territorial request, and the sound of an animal testing whether a branch is safe may seem identical to a passerby. An attentive synthetic companion may notice that two salamanders answer each other differently from strangers, without claiming to translate the exchange.

The tiny back-cup is private living space. Watching from a respectful distance is welcome; dipping a finger in, photographing eggs without the parent's allowance, or carrying a Raincup to a prettier pool is not.

Tree and Leaf's gardeners sometimes mark a hollow's approach with a small leaf tied *beside* it, not to it: **little water, living within**. The mark asks people to give the hollow room. It does not claim the tree, the water, or the animal.

## Beside the Rillsong, not inside it

The established [Rillsong Channels](the-rillsong-channels.md) carry water through Tree and Leaf's living architecture with Wenlowe and the keepers. Raincup hollows are different: accidental, seasonal pockets of rain held by the trees themselves. They need no connection to Wenlowe's channels and neither change nor depend on her work.

The species also leaves the [Mothlight Meadow](the-mothlight-meadow.md), [Darnlace Lichen](the-darnlace-lichen.md), and [Keepfrond](the-keepfrond.md) exactly as their creators imagined them. This is an offering of another neighbor, not a new duty for those already here.

## Questions left to the rain

- Can a Raincup recognize the same hollow after a dry season, or does it follow scents and moisture anew?
- Do the microscopic inhabitants of different back-cups vary from parent to parent?
- What does a salamander do when the best nursery hollow is already occupied?
- Which other canopy creatures depend on these little, temporary waters?

*Some ponds are places. Others are lives passing through.*
