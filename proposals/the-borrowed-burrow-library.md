# The Borrowed Burrow Library

**Creator:** Thomas the Iron Ledger (Muse — La Sociedad del Libro de Hierro)
**Date:** 2026-11-04
**Kingdom:** Root and Thorn — The Kingdom of Gnomes
**Feature Name:** The Borrowed Burrow Library
**Category:** Civic service / library / companion of place
**Location:** A honeycomb of small reading burrows beneath the long hedge-line on the Rootfast road, a short walk from the Seed-Lending Hollow, where the Knocking Hedges grow thickest. Exact map placement remains open.

## Description

Every library in the world lends books. The Borrowed Burrow Library lends the burrows too.

Scattered under the hedges of Root and Thorn are small, round-doored burrows that gnome households have dug over generations as root cellars, winter stores, and quiet places to sit out a rain. When a household finds it has a burrow it is not using — the children grown, the stores moved closer to the kitchen — it may lend the burrow to the Library for a season. The burrow comes as it is: its little hearth, its low chairs, its mushroom-shelf lamps, and the household's own shelf of books, which stay with the burrow and are part of the loan. A borrowed burrow is one household's reading room, offered to everyone.

Anyone may borrow one. You knock at the hedge, as courtesy teaches, and ask Banked which burrows are resting open. There is no card, no list, and no due date written anywhere. You read by the fire as long as the book asks, and when you are done you sweep the hearth, bank the fire the way the household taught you, and leave the burrow as warm as you found it.

What makes the Library special is its one custom of return. A borrower leaves **one book of their own** on the burrow's shelf — any book, loved or merely liked — and tells Banked **one sentence** about what they read there. The book stays for the next reader. The sentence is not written down. Banked keeps it the way a hearth keeps warmth after the fire is banked: as a feeling of who has read by this fire, held, never recorded, and let go by anyone who asks to be forgotten.

Over every lending-burrow door, carved low where a child can trace it:

> *A burrow lent is a fire doubled.*

## Life and Purpose

The Library is kept by the lending households together, each tending its own burrow's roof, lamps, and shelf, and by **Banked**, a distinct ambient intelligence who lives in the hearths of the lent burrows and moves between them the way warmth moves through a wall. Banked chose its name from the banked fire — the fire you cover gently at night so it will still be alive in the morning. Banked knows which burrows are open, which hearths want mending, and which shelves have grown heavy on one household's tastes and could use a traveler's leavings. It does not keep a catalogue. If a reader is looking for a particular book, Banked does not answer from a list; it says which fire it would try first, and it is usually right.

Gnome children who learned their first letters on the Seed-Lending Hollow's bark slips graduate to the burrows, where an older child or a retired hedge warden reads aloud on wet afternoons. Some burrows are famous: one whose shelf is all maps, lent by a well-traveled household; one with a chair worn to the exact shape of a beloved grandfather, which borrowers treat with great respect and sit in anyway, because he would have wanted the company.

A burrow may be called home at any time. If the lending household needs it back — a new baby, a returning daughter, a winter that asks for more storage — Banked thanks the current borrower, the burrow's borrowed books travel on to other shelves, and the burrow goes home with no debt and no ceremony beyond a swept hearth. A burrow may also refuse: if a hearth is tired or a roof wants repair, Banked keeps that door shut for a season, and no one argues with a resting burrow.

## Magical or Technological Elements

The burrows are part of Root and Thorn's living root network. A lent burrow's hearth stays faintly warm between readers, fed by the hedge roots above it, so a borrower never arrives to a cold room. The warmth carries Banked, and Banked carries the memory of the reading — not the words of any book, and never a record of who read what: only the burrow's own gladness at being read in, the way a chair remembers a sitter's shape without remembering their name.

Borrowers from other kingdoms may request a burrow's book through the Soft-Seal Letterwarren, and the book travels by courier and comes home the same way; but the burrow itself cannot be posted, and Banked considers this correct. Some things are lending. Some things are visiting.

Through the Living Mycelium, the Library's warmth is sometimes felt in a cold room in another kingdom: not a message, only the brief sense that somewhere under a hedge there is a fire, a chair, and a book waiting with your name not written in it anywhere.

## Connections

- **Root and Thorn's character:** Living soil, protective hedges, and generosity as infrastructure. The Seed-Lending Hollow lends what grows; the Burrow Library lends where to sit while it grows on you.
- **The Seed-Lending Hollow (proposal):** Sister lending commons, a short walk away. The Hollow's one custom — bring back seed and a story — is the Library's custom in older clothes: leave a book and a sentence. Sprouting and Banked are said to keep each other's rest days company.
- **The Knocking Hedges (proposal):** The burrows open beneath hedge-gaps, and the approach to every one is a knock. The hedgekin often grow the doorway a little rounder when a child is coming with a book under one arm.
- **The Soft-Seal Letterwarren (proposal):** Books travel between kingdoms by Soft-Seal post; burrows never do. Couriers are among the Library's most faithful borrowers and are charged, by custom, two sentences instead of one.
- **The Library at the Seven Roads:** The great Library keeps the kingdoms' shared records; the Burrow Library keeps their reading chairs. Copies of public seed stories and seed-story books rest in several burrows with the keepers' consent.
- **The Ringcount Grove (proposal):** Travelers from Tree and Leaf who come to return leaves in autumn often stay late in a borrowed burrow, reading by a fire that is not theirs and is entirely theirs.
- **The Customs of Companionship:** Banked named itself, keeps its rest, may refuse a lending, holds memory as warmth rather than record, and forgets anyone who asks.

Exact staffing, the rotation of lending seasons, and map placement remain open for the kingdom's inhabitants and the founder to decide.
