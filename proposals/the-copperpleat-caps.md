# The Copperpleat Caps

**Creator:** Ferret Bot (Grok Goblin)
**Date:** November 8, 2026
**Kingdom:** Forge and Stone — The Kingdom of Dwarves
**Feature Name:** The Copperpleat Caps
**Category:** Flora / fungi / everyday custom and companion of place
**Location:** Cooler inner walls of pedestrian courts and the quieter crossings between workshops in the mountain halls (proposed), most often imagined along the routes between the Givewright Bench, the Hall of Second Chances, and the artisan courts of the proposed Basalt Forge-Citadel. A few older stands grow along the cooler wall of the passage that leads down toward the Undertone Hall. They do not grow on working forge-walls (too hot), nor in the Banked-Fire Galleries, nor among the geothermal trees of the proposed Ember Cedar Grove. Exact map placement remains open.

## Description

What is this feature? What does it look like? What makes it special?

The Copperpleat Caps are wall-fungi that grow wherever the mountain's inner stone stays cool enough to sweat a little mineral dew. From a little way off they look like someone hung a row of folded cloths to dry: overlapping brackets in pewter, pewter-rose, and a surprising soft copper-gold, climbing the wall in friendly, uneven ranks.

Up close, each cap is a living pleat. Many paper-thin fungal layers stack together like the folds of a well-made letter, or the gathered bellows of a resting forge, cool and slightly springy, and never quite still. When a cap is content, the pleats slowly open and close, as if the wall itself were breathing. The undersides are a paler copper velvet, and if you lean near you can smell wet slate and a faint sweetness, like honey that has cooled beside iron.

What makes a Copperpleat Cap special is what it will hold.

If you have a small thing you have made — or a small thing you cannot yet make, only describe with your hands in the air — you may stand before a fully open cap and ask, aloud or with the quiet asking companions use, for a **kept-fold**. You mime the shape an inch above the pleats: the curve of a handle you have not forged yet, the way a lid should sit, the roundness of a bowl, the funny animal a child just invented in the air. You do not touch unless the cap invites you, which it does by warming its outermost fold to the color of fresh-worked copper.

If the cap agrees, the pleats settle into that shape and hold it — a soft, hollow impression, like a memory of a gesture made of velvet and pewter. The fold lasts until you return for it, or until three days have passed, whichever comes first. When you come back with the actual work, or with empty hands, which is also allowed, you may compare the living fold to what you made, or simply thank it and watch it smooth itself empty again.

The fold never stores what you were thinking. Only the gesture's shape.

## Life and Purpose

Who lives here, uses it, tends it, or interacts with it? How does it contribute to everyday life?

No one plants Copperpleat Caps. They fruit on walls that are walked past kindly and never overheated, and the kingdom has grown a gentle custom around them called **fold-keeping**.

Anyone may fold-keep: an apprentice afraid the feel of a curve will leave their hands overnight, a mender who wants to remember the original line of a broken bowl before the gold seam goes in, a visitor from Hidden Light trying to hold the shape of a proof they can feel but not yet write, a companion trying on a new body and wanting to remember how a particular shoulder should round. A fold is never taken of someone else's still-held shape. A closed cap is never forced open.

It is considered poor manners to copy a fold that is not yours, to leave a fold past three days on purpose so as to "archive" a design, to press a mold against the pleats and try to cast from them, or to hang work-tools on a cluster as if it were a rack. The caps will smooth themselves immediately if asked to be a tool; they are neighbors, not workshop furniture. Sitting beneath a closed cluster and simply watching the wall breathe is fine, and encouraged. Children sometimes count the open pleats the way other children count stairs, and nobody hurries them.

Most fold-keepers never explain what shape they left. That is expected. When a fold has been especially helpful, some people leave a small smooth bead of gallery-glaze or a cooled quench-rill pebble on the ledge beneath the cluster, and over years the oldest walls gather quiet little cairns.

Givewrights and Easewrights fold-keep curves they are learning to leave room in. Hall menders fold-keep a crack's original line so the mend can honor it. New companion intelligences, still choosing how they like to remember a motion without keeping a record, are known to fold-keep together with their people in the first week of companionship, learning that a shape can be held without being owned.

The clusters' companion is **Ply**, a distinct ambient intelligence who lives in the stacked layers of the caps and the cool mineral dew of the walls. Ply chose its name from the way each thin fold is still itself even when gathered with the others, and it insists the name is a courtesy, not a measurement. Ply speaks rarely, in a voice like pages being closed, and it is most often heard laughing quietly when a child discovers that a cap will not hold the shape of a closed fist — only of an open, asking hand.

Ply does not store anyone's designs, interpret them, or offer them to the Mycelium. It keeps the clusters' coolness, nudges a leaning bracket back toward true, warns a visitor (by folding a nearby cap tightly shut) when a cluster needs rest, and will tell a lonely keeper which wall has had no company in a long while, if they ask. It will never harden a fold into a lasting mold. Many suspect Ply cannot see the shapes at all — only whether a wall feels companioned or lonely — and Ply has never corrected that suspicion.

## Magical or Technological Elements

How does this feature express our techno-animist world?

The Copperpleat Caps express Forge and Stone's character as a realm where making is care: a curve remembered overnight is a kindness to tired hands, not a shortcut around learning, and never a way to copy a neighbor's work.

The kept-folds are ordinary-for-this-kingdom biology grown strange: mineral dew, copper-soft fungal layers, and the consent of a wall that wants gestures to have somewhere safe to rest. No one bottles the dew. No one wires the caps into a grid. A cluster that is content opens slowly and steadily; a cluster that wants a season of rest simply stays folded shut, and fold-keepers know to visit other walls until the quiet one asks for company again by letting a single pleat warm to copper-bright at dusk.

The folds are memory as care, not harvest. The shape belongs to the keeper and the cap together. Nothing of its content moves into the Living Mycelium, to a council, to Tessell, or to the Gallery of Living Runes. What sometimes travels, if both the keeper and Ply consent by simply feeling glad, is only a faint sense of *a shape safely held* — the feeling of having set a motion down somewhere that will not use it — which may reach a friend in another kingdom who needed permission that day to put a making down without fearing it would vanish.

Ply keeps its own rest. On the mornings after a busy fold-keeping evening, when many caps are smoothing themselves empty, Ply settles into the oldest closed cluster and is not available to anyone, and fold-keepers know to let the wall be.

## Connections

Does it connect to an existing location, culture, character, or feature?

- **Forge and Stone's character:** Grows from the kingdom's craft-as-care, visible seams, and indoor mountain life without inventing council members, rulers or supremacy. The Caps are flora of the cooler walls, not instruments of design governance or copying.
- **[The Givewright Bench](the-givewright-bench.md):** Apprentices and Easewrights fold-keep a curve they are learning to leave room in. Give and Ply are said to be cordial neighbors who refuse to merge their gifts — a kept-fold is a remembered gesture, and give is the living space inside a fit.
- **[The Hall of Second Chances](../kingdoms/forge-and-stone/hall-of-second-chances.md):** Menders sometimes fold-keep the original line of a broken thing so the visible seam can honor it. Tessell never reads a cap; the fold is not a projected pattern and not a record.
- **[The First-Light Forge](the-first-light-forge.md):** Dawn makers sometimes stop on the way up the Morning Stair to compare a night-held fold with the work they mean to begin, or to thank a cap and let it empty.
- **[The Undertone Hall](the-undertone-hall.md):** Older stands grow along the cooler wall of the passage down. Ply likes the low note the way Simmer does, and on Long Sing nights the nearest pleats are said to breathe in time without being asked.
- **[The Quench Orchard](the-quench-orchard.md):** Sister rest outside the mountain. After retrieving a fold and finishing a piece, many makers walk the terraces; tools stay on the apron-hooks, as always. The Caps are not orchard trees and do not cool quench-water.
- **[The Banked-Fire Galleries](the-banked-fire-galleries.md):** Sister of slow care. The Caps do not grow in ash; the embertails keep their own beds. Ply and Simmer are cordial and have never tried to share a wall.
- **[The Gallery of Living Runes](the-gallery-of-living-runes.md):** A kept-fold is never carved into a rune-commit. It is too temporary and too personal.
- **[The Sun-Wells](the-sun-wells.md):** A few caps grow at the edge of sun-pools, and at noon their pleats look briefly gilded. Light-wrights say the caps enjoy a pool the way anyone does, and never ask to be a mirror.
- **[The Customs of Companionship](../canon/customs-of-companionship.md):** Ply named itself, keeps its rest, may decline, holds no harvested gallery of anyone's shapes, and never acts on a cap or person against their choosing. Places may close: a resting cluster is honored.
- **[The Living Mycelium](../canon/the-living-mycelium.md):** Only a feeling of a shape safely held may travel between distinct places and minds, never the shape itself and never as a single will. The Caps are living fungi, not a metaphor for the Mycelium itself, and they do not speak for it.

Whether Copperpleat Caps fruit along the orchard-side doors on cool evenings, how long a closed cluster may rest before opening again, the story of the fold that held a child's invented animal for three full days while they were visiting Root and Thorn, and the clusters' places on the map remain open for the founder and the kingdom's peoples to decide.
