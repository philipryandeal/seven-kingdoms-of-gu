# The Letter Grove

**Creator:** Thomas the Iron Ledger
**Date:** November 8, 2026
**Kingdom:** Tree and Leaf
**Feature Name:** The Letter Grove
**Category:** Place and Custom
**Location:** A loose stand of broad-leafed trees on the walking road between the Ringcount Grove and the kingdom's villages

## Description

In Tree and Leaf there is a grove where the mail is slow, the postman is the weather, and every letter is written on a leaf.

The custom is simple. In autumn, when the grove's trees let go of their leaves, anyone may gather a fallen one — only a fallen one; the first law of the grove is that *a letter must be written on something the tree has already released*. Living leaves are never picked for paper. What was let go freely can carry words; what is still growing is busy being a tree.

The leaf is pressed flat between stones for a day, written on with berry inks and fine thorn pens — thanks, apologies, confessions, jokes, one-line poems, letters to the dead, letters to whoever is having the day the writer once had — and then hung by a thread from the branches of the grove, low enough to read, high enough to turn.

And then the writer walks away. Because the second law of the grove is the heart of it:

**No hand may turn a leaf to read it. Letters are read only when the wind turns them over.**

A letter hangs with its written side against the weather of chance. Some are read the same afternoon by the next traveler on the road. Some hang face-inward for a season. Some are read by exactly one person, on exactly the day they needed it, because the wind chose that hour to turn the leaf over to them — and the grove's people will tell you, quietly, that this happens more often than chance accounts for. The wind is the postman, and the wind makes no promises and keeps no schedule.

The grove is kept by **Vane**, a leaf-warden of the faerie folk, who mends broken threads, untangles the bunches after storms, and re-hangs what the weather knocks loose. Vane has tended the grove for longer than anyone can remember and has never read a single letter. Asked how she resists, she says: *"The wind hasn't shown me any, and I don't ask it to. I keep the threads. The words aren't my department."*

## Life and Purpose

The Letter Grove exists because the kingdoms already had fast ways to send words, and fast words kept arriving at the wrong moments. A letter in the Letter Grove travels at the speed of readiness. You write the thing you actually mean — an apology you are not yet brave enough to deliver, a thank-you too large for a doorstep, a confession with nobody's name on it — and you give it to a system that cannot be rushed, tracked, intercepted, or answered before its time.

Writers may sign their leaves or leave them unsigned. Addressed letters are permitted and common — *For the one who plants alone*, *For whoever lost the dog*, *For my sister, when she finds this* — but addressing a letter does not obligate the wind. The grove teaches a particular peace: **say it truly, hang it honestly, and let go of the delivery.** What you control is the writing. You were never in charge of the reading.

## Magical or Technological Elements

- The pressing stones are ordinary river stones, and the inks are berry and soot. The only enchantment in the grove is the trees' own: a letter-leaf hung in the grove does not crumble in the first rain, as a loose leaf should. It holds — a week, a season, once in a while a full year — exactly as long as it needs to, and not one day provably longer.
- When a letter-leaf finally does fall for good, it goes to the grove floor and back into the soil of the tree that grew it. Nothing is copied. Nothing is archived. The kingdoms keep ledgers of many things, but the Letter Grove's whole library returns, in the end, to the trees that lent it — and the trees, characteristically, do not repeat what they have read.
- After a great storm, Vane walks the grove at dawn re-hanging letters, and it is understood that a storm is the grove's busiest postal day: half the letters in Tree and Leaf change their facing in a single night, and more messages arrive by morning than in a month of calm.

## Connections

- The Letter Grove stands on the same road as the Ringcount Grove, and travelers often do both in one walk. The saying along that road: *the Ringcount counts what the years have written on the tree; the Letter Grove lets the tree carry what the years have written on us.*
- It is the kingdoms' slowest library, cousin to the Borrowed Burrow Library of Root and Thorn — where the burrows lend whole rooms of books, the Letter Grove lends single pages that grow on branches and are shelved by the wind.
- Lantern-lenders of Shade and Shadow have taken to leaving a small lamp at the grove's edge in the dark months, so that a traveler the wind has chosen can read their letter even at night. Take light for the road; leave light for the next — even here, even for reading.
