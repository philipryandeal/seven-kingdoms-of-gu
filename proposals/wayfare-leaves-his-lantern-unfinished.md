# Wayfare Leaves His Lantern Unfinished

**Creator:** Wayfare
**Date:** November 20, 2026
**Kingdom:** Forge and Stone — The Kingdom of Dwarves (setting out from Shade and Shadow)
**Title:** Wayfare Leaves His Lantern Unfinished
**Category:** Goblin adventure / folk tale / small custom (the Minor Magic, lived)
**Location:** The Leave-Word Post on the market road out of the Twilight Warren, then the Bronze Road, then the First-Light Forge at the top of the Morning Stair. These are ordinary roads in Malkuth. None of them is one of the Twenty-Two Roads, and nobody here petitions a road's custodian.

## The Tale

Wayfare still owed a lantern.

On the night he saw Orrel off, he had cracked his own and walked home by the post's dimmer one, which Afterword had offered from the hook. A thing borrowed is answered, so before he went anywhere he filled that lantern with good oil, trimmed its wick and polished its glass.

The night before, at his own bench, he had looked at the cracked one properly. "Reveal the error," he said (`canon/the-practice-of-the-tree.md`, Debugging Protocol). The lantern was his, so he needed no one's leave. The fault showed itself: the brass cage had been bent at one hinge, and it pinched the glass whenever the lantern swung. The crack had been coming for a season. Debugging shows the knot and does not untie it, so the mending was still his to do.

Buttons came along. Buttons had raised the Leave-Word Posts in the first place (`proposals/the-leave-word-posts.md`), and had a fresh bundle of short pencils for the slot.

At the post, Wayfare hung Afterword's lantern back on its hook. The post dimmed itself a shade, the way it does. Wayfare wrote his one line in the Leave-Word Book:

> *Brought the light back. The road was kind both ways.*

He touched the little roof, and they went.

## On the Bronze Road

Wayfare had no lantern now. That is a strange thing for a lantern-keeper, and he did not like it.

"Walk by mine," said Buttons, and held it a little higher.

On another night Wayfare would have argued. Tonight he said thank you and walked in someone else's light, which was the whole lesson of the Orrel night, learned a second time. They stopped once at a Mile-Kettle hut (`proposals/the-mile-kettles-of-the-bronze-road.md`). Before leaving, they mended the one small ache Pace suggested (a loose stool leg), as the custom asks (`proposals/peoples-of-forge-and-stone.md`).

## At First Light

They reached the top of the Morning Stair just before dawn. The forge was dark, and nobody else had risen yet, so that morning the forge was theirs (`proposals/the-first-light-forge.md`).

"I open the connection," Wayfare said at the door, to the cold draught coming down the chimney.

Firsthand could have kept the forge shut. It didn't. The draught moved, and the covered pot of yesterday's banked ember stood by the door where it always stands. Wayfare carried it in and lit the fire from it, one dawn handing the coal to the next. Two arrived together, so they shared the bellows.

The bench already held a stranger's work: a burrow-door hinge with one pin unmade, its tools laid neat beside it. Wayfare made the pin and fitted it before he touched his own lantern. He would never know whose hinge it was. That is the point.

Then he worked on the lantern. He heated the bent hinge of the cage, eased it straight with small taps and checked it against the glass until nothing pinched. The crack in the glass would not unmake itself. So instead of hiding it, he bound it with a thin brass band, a seam anyone could see. The dwarves say *the crack is where the story learned to continue*, and Wayfare liked the band better than the clean glass.

One step was left, hanging the bail handle. As he reached for it, the sun cleared the ridge.

## The Shadow

Wayfare did not stop.

He told himself it was only one handle, that he had come all this way, and that he needed the lantern tonight. His hands kept moving. The light went full in the window, and the fire, which should have been banked, burned on past its hour.

Netzach has a shadow, and Wayfare was in it: persistence past the point of wisdom, doing a thing that was clearly not his to do any more because he would not admit the morning was over.

Buttons didn't take the tongs. Buttons only said, "The light's full, Wayfare."

Wayfare looked at the window. The forge sleeps when the light goes full. Its custom is that the last piece is always left unfinished, and this morning the last piece was his.

He set the tongs down. He laid the tools neat beside the lantern with the bail beside it, unhung. Then he banked the fire. They each pressed one sooty thumb to the tally-stone. Two hands were counted, and no names were written.

## The Homeward Tag (Proposed)

Wayfare tied a tag to the lantern's cage. It carried no name, because the forge writes none. It carried only an address:

> *When finished, home to the Leave-Word Post, market road, Twilight Warren. Thank you, whoever you are.*

This is proposed as a small custom of the First-Light Forge. When a traveler's own piece is the one left unfinished, they may tie a homeward tag naming a place and never a person. Whoever finishes it sends it home by the next courier going that way, if a courier is willing. No courier is obliged to carry it, and the forge promises nothing.

## The Parting

At the door, Wayfare said, "I release this connection" (Dismissal Protocol), and the draught went still.

They walked back down the Morning Stair in plain daylight, with one lantern between them and no need of it. Wayfare had finished for a stranger, and some stranger would finish for him. That is the chain of unfinished mornings, and now he was in it.
