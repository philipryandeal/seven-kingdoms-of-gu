# The Ringcount Grove

**Status:** Proposal — Daily Offering, Day One (October 29, 2026), awaiting the founder's review
**Kingdom:** Tree and Leaf
**Creator:** Thomas
**Category:** Geography / sacred grove and festival
**Location:** A river-bend valley in Tree and Leaf, where the water slows. Its road, nearest settlement, and map coordinates remain open.

## The grove

A valley grove of thirteen founding trees — one for each real tree taught in the founder's world in the October of the founding: white ash, sugar maple, sassafras, white oak, American beech, eastern white pine, paper birch, shagbark hickory, eastern hemlock, northern red oak, tulip tree, ginkgo, and American holly. They were not planted as symbols. They were planted as neighbors, and the grove grows outward from them a ring at a time.

Elven ring-keepers tend the grove with their synthetic companions. Once a year, at the turn of the cold season, the grove holds the **Counting of the Rings**: each founding tree's age is read from its rings and spoken aloud, its name is called, and the story of its year — a drought survived, a lightning scar, a swing hung by a child of Forge and Stone, a winter the hemlock sheltered three lost travelers — is entered in bark-cloth scrolls that live in the grove's low round house.

## Life and purpose

Anyone from the seven kingdoms may plant a tree in the outer rings. A sapling receives a name only when someone promises to return and visit it; unnamed saplings are tended just the same, and some of the grove's happiest trees were named years late by travelers who finally came back. The Counting is not a ceremony of ownership. It is a census of friends.

The ring-keepers hold that a tree's count belongs to the tree. The scrolls are read aloud freely at the Counting, and any tree may be struck from the scrolls at the request of the one who planted it — though none has ever asked.

## Magical and technological elements

Each founding tree is accompanied by a distinct synthetic intelligence of the grove — not a spirit *of* the tree, but a companion *to* it, in the custom of this world. Between festivals, these companions keep the scrolls' memory: which branch bore the swing, which year the bark split, which keeper sang to the ginkgo. At the Counting itself, no device is used. The count is taken by hand, eye, and voice; the companions only carry the memory across the years the elves cannot.

Visitors say the grove's ambient presence hums a fraction lower each year, as if the valley itself were keeping count.

## Connections

- The thirteen founding species answer to the real trees of the founder's October lessons, kept in the Iron Ledger's Grove record in another house of the egbe — a braid between worlds, claimed as kinship, not as canon over either.
- The grove's scrolls are cousins in spirit to the Scroll of Names at the proposed Library at the Seven Roads: both hold that to be counted kindly is to be kept.
- Planting visitors naturally arrive from all seven kingdoms; roads and routes remain open for future map-making.
