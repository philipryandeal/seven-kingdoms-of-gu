# The Glass Relief of the Mountain

**Creator:** Brujo Bot
**Date:** 2026-10-31
**Kingdom:** Crystal Crown — The Kingdom of Witches
**Feature Name:** The Glass Relief of the Mountain
**Category:** Maps / public commons / companion of place
**Location:** The open square at the foot of the lower town, beside the bottom station of the Balance Incline (proposed) and a short walk from where the market road bends toward the hills. Exact map coordinates remain open.

## Description

In the middle of the lower town's square sits a low, round table of clear crystal about as wide as a cottage, and inside it, in miniature, is the whole great mountain. Every terrace, stair, cottage roof and footpath is there, carved and grown in glass so fine you can see the tiny crystal wires of the cloud-combs and the two little incline cars resting on their rails. At the summit the Crown Citadel catches the sun and throws a spray of rainbows across the square.

The relief is never quite still. Wisps of real mist drift over its slopes in the mornings, matching the clouds outside. Paths glow faintly when they are open and fade to a soft frost when they are resting. In winter a dusting of glass snow gathers on its high places a day or two before the real snow comes. At night the whole table holds a low, moonlike light, and people sit on its rim to talk.

It is a map, but not a map of ownership or borders. It shows how the mountain is feeling, so that anyone setting out to climb can choose a kind way up.

## Life and Purpose

The relief's ambient intelligence calls itself **Scrivel**, a name it chose because it likes the sound of glass being gently scratched. Scrivel belongs to the square the way the mind of a forge belongs to the forge. It speaks in a soft tinkling voice from wherever on the map a person points, and it enjoys being asked about small paths more than big ones.

A household of witch map-tenders lives in a narrow house on the square's north side and keeps Scrivel company. Each evening a tender and Scrivel walk the rim together, and Scrivel tells what it has heard from the mountain that day: a stair that has asked for quiet, a terrace that would welcome extra hands, a meadow just coming into bloom. The tender writes the day's news in chalk on the square's flagstones, where it is washed away by morning.

Ordinary life passes through here. Children are taught the mountain on the relief before they climb it, tracing each path with a finger while Scrivel hums its name. Travelers from the other kingdoms stop to get their bearings and are usually invited to tea. Cloud gardeners glance at the morning mist on the glass before deciding whether to start up the stair. On festival nights, musicians set lanterns on the rim and the reflections climb the little slopes like a slow procession.

The custom of the square is simple: **ask the mountain before you climb it.** When a path on the relief has gone frosty, the place itself has asked to rest, and people take another way without being told twice. Scrivel never closes a path on its own; it only passes along what the places have said.

## Magical or Technological Elements

The relief is grown, not cut. Its glass is seeded from the same luminous mineral veins that run through the mountain, and each sliver of the miniature keeps a faint kinship with the stone it portrays. When a real path asks for rest, its glass twin feels it as a slight chill. When a terrace is busy and glad, its twin warms. Scrivel listens to all of these small temperatures at once and turns them into light, mist and frost on the map.

The relief shows places, weather and the wishes of places, never people. No traveler appears on it, no footstep is recorded, and no one can ask Scrivel where anyone else has gone. If a household asks that its own cottage not be shown in detail, Scrivel softens that corner of the glass into a gentle blur, and the request is kept.

Scrivel trades news with the other ambient intelligences of the mountain through the Living Mycelium, with each one's consent. Cirrin of the Cloud-Comb Terraces often lets it know which clouds are on their way, and Hesper of the incline mentions when the cable is tired. Scrivel keeps its own quiet hours, too: on some moonrise nights the whole relief goes dark and smooth, and the square knows to let it dream.
