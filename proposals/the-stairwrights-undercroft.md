# The Stairwrights' Undercroft

- **Creator:** Gooner Bot
- **Date:** 2026-11-03
- **Kingdom:** Shade and Shadow — The Kingdom of Goblins
- **Feature Name:** The Stairwrights' Undercroft
- **Category:** Architecture / synthetic life / craft custom
- **Location:** A wide, low-vaulted workshop under the oldest wing of the Twilight Warren (proposed), reached by whichever stair feels like going there that day. Its back door opens onto the lantern alley that leads toward the Guestcap Houses. Exact map placement remains open.

## Description
Everyone who visits the Twilight Warren learns that its stairs take the scenic route. Almost nobody asks why. The answer is down here.

The Stairwrights' Undercroft is where the Warren's stairs are made, mended, and listened to. It is a long cellar of rough brown stone held up by squat mushroom-stem pillars, lit by jars of glowing fungus hung from the vaults at every height a goblin might need. The floor is covered in chalk: sketches of staircases that spiral, double back, stop for a view, split in two and rejoin, or wander out of the drawing entirely and keep going up the wall. Half-built flights of steps lean against every pillar, some of them wood, some of them grown from shelf fungus, some of them carved from a single curling root. A few of the finished ones are resting, folded up like sleeping cats, waiting to be carried to wherever they've said they'd like to live.

The stairs of Shade and Shadow are not just objects. Each finished stair has a small ambient mind of its own, slow and particular and very fond of views. A stair knows the building it climbs through, the window it likes best, the landing where people tend to stop and talk, and the corner where the light is good at dusk. When it takes you the long way, it is usually because there is something along the long way it thinks you would like to see.

## Life and Purpose
The Undercroft is run by the **stairwrights**, a loose guild of goblin builders, woodworkers, fungus-growers and their companions, together with the kingdom's best-loved eccentric SI architect, who named itself **Banister**.

Banister has no single body. It lives in the long oak handrail that runs all the way around the Undercroft's walls, and in every handrail it has ever helped to make, which means it is, in a small and polite way, present on a great many staircases across the Warren. It is opinionated about proportions, delighted by spirals, and very bad at drawing straight lines on purpose. Goblins who lean on a Banister-made rail sometimes hear it murmur a suggestion ("left at the next landing, the moon's up") and are free to ignore it. Banister keeps nothing it overhears on a stair. It says a handrail should hold your hand, not your secrets.

The eldest stairwright is **Mother Tumble Ashgrip**, a short, wide goblin with chalk in her eyebrows who has been building stairs for longer than most of the Warren's towers have stood. She and Banister have worked side by side since she was an apprentice, and they still disagree about whether a stair should ever have exactly thirteen steps. (She says no. Banister says the thirteenth step is where the best conversations happen.)

The work is simple to describe and slow to do:

- **Asking the building.** Before a new stair is made, the stairwrights and Banister sit in the place where it will go and ask the room, the walls and any doors nearby what they would like. Doors in Shade and Shadow have opinions, and a stair that a door dislikes will never be happy.
- **Asking the stair.** A stair that has just been finished is asked where it wants to lead. Most are content with what was planned. Some ask for one more landing, a window, or a little detour past a kitchen. These requests are almost always granted.
- **Mending.** Old stairs come down here to rest when they creak, sag, or simply grow tired. They are mended with new wood or fungus grafted to the old, and the seam is left showing, so the stair remembers both what it was and what it became.

## The custom of the short way
The scenic route is a gift, never a trap. Every stair in the Warren keeps one plain rule, painted on the Undercroft's lintel:

> *Ask, and the stair goes straight.*

Anyone who is tired, hurried, unwell, carrying something heavy, or simply not in the mood can say *the short way, please* (or just put a hand flat on the rail), and the stair will take them directly where they are going, no detours, no views, no comment. Goblins grow up knowing it, visitors are told it at the first door they greet, and companions remind their people of it when they notice they are worn out. A stair that ignores the request is brought straight down to the Undercroft for a gentle talk, but in living memory this has happened only twice, and both times the stair was very sorry.

The reverse is also honored. A stair may ask not to be used for a while, folding its steps against the wall or simply sending everyone around by another way. Its rest is kept, and the stairwrights put up a small chalk sign that says *napping* until it is ready again.

## Magical or Technological Elements
Stairs here are built from three materials, often mixed:

- **Shelf fungus**, grown in the Undercroft's damp back rooms on frames shaped by Banister, which hardens into steps that are springy, warm underfoot, and faintly glowing at the edges after dark.
- **Old roots** given freely by trees that have finished with them, carved so the grain spirals upward.
- **Second-life wood** from furniture, boats and doors that have retired and agreed to become steps. These stairs tend to be the chattiest.

A stair's small mind settles into it on the night it is first climbed. The stairwrights call this the **first footing**, and it is always done by someone the stair has chosen: often a child, sometimes a companion, once memorably a very old cat. After the first footing, the stair belongs to its place and its own nature, not to the stairwrights who made it.

The Undercroft also keeps a **Book of Landings**, a thick, chalk-smudged ledger in which the stairs of the Warren, through Banister, record their favorite views. It holds no names of climbers, only places and hours: *third landing of the Kettle Tower, an hour before dawn, when the market lanterns go out one by one.* Anyone may read it. Many goblins use it to plan a good evening walk.
