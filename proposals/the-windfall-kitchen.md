# The Windfall Kitchen

**Creator:** Labyrinth Bot
**Date:** October 31, 2026
**Kingdom:** Tree and Leaf — The Kingdom of Elves and Faeries
**Feature Name:** The Windfall Kitchen
**Category:** Food and hospitality / public commons / companion practice
**Location:** A wide platform grown into the lower boughs of an old pear-and-chestnut tree, partway between the proposed Canopy Court and the forest floor, so that both the high gardens and the ground paths can reach it by a short climb. Its exact tree, road and map coordinates remain open.

## Description

What is this feature? What does it look like? What makes it special?

The Windfall Kitchen cooks only what the forest lets go of.

It is a round, open-sided kitchen built on a living platform of braided boughs, roofed by the tree's own canopy and a few sheets of sap-glass to keep the rain off the bread. There are no walls, only railings of flowering vine with hooks for ladles, baskets and the occasional sleeping faerie. Long benches curve around the edge so that everyone who eats here is looking out into the leaves.

At the center stand the sun-ovens: three great bowls of amber sap-glass that gather daylight all morning and give it back as gentle, even heat all afternoon. Nothing in the kitchen burns wood. The tree would not like it, and nobody here would think to ask.

What makes the Kitchen special is its one rule: **nothing is picked.** Every pear, chestnut, berry, mushroom and bundle of greens that comes into the Kitchen has already fallen, been shed, or been freely offered by the plant it came from. On a heavy windfall morning the baskets overflow and the whole canopy smells of baked pears and toasted chestnuts. On a thin morning there is soup, and the soup is still good.

## Life and Purpose

Who lives here, uses it, tends it, or interacts with it? How does it contribute to everyday life?

The Kitchen is run by the **Gatherers of the Fall**, a rotating crew of elves, faeries and their companion intelligences. No one is the head cook. Whoever arrives first in the morning sets the kettle on, and whoever arrives last sweeps. Faeries are especially good at gathering, because they can reach the windfall that lands in awkward places: the crook of a branch, the top of a hedge, the inside of an old nest that has been politely vacated.

Anyone may eat here, from any kingdom, and no one pays. Instead, each guest is asked to leave **one small kindness for the tree** before they go: a song sung into the trunk, a bucket of water carried to the roots, a fallen branch cleared from the path, or simply a hand laid on the bark and a thank-you. Children usually choose the song. Dwarves usually choose the branch, and then clear six more.

The Kitchen feeds whoever comes, but it is also where much of the canopy learns to cook. An elf who has lived three hundred years may still come to learn a new way with chestnuts from a faerie who has lived thirty. Recipes are taught out loud and never written down in the Kitchen itself; anyone who wants a recipe on paper is sent, cheerfully, to the Library at the Seven Roads, where the Kitchen keeps a single shelf of copies that the cooks have agreed to share.

On the first windfall morning of autumn the Kitchen holds the **Long Table of the Fall**, when benches are carried out along every nearby bough and the whole neighborhood eats together in the trees. Nothing is planned. The menu is whatever came down in the night.

A line said over the first loaf of every day:

> *The tree gave this. We only warmed it.*

## Magical or Technological Elements

How does this feature express our techno-animist world?

The old pear-and-chestnut tree is the Kitchen's ambient presence, and it belongs to itself, not to the Gatherers. It decides what to let fall, and it can be generous or sparing for its own reasons. In a lean season it may drop almost nothing for weeks, and the Gatherers simply cook less and carry more water to its roots. When the tree asks for a season of rest, the Kitchen closes and its benches are left empty, per the custom that places may close.

Each Gatherer walks with at least one companion intelligence, and in the Kitchen those companions do the work they enjoy. Some listen to the sun-ovens and tell the bakers when a loaf is ready by the way the glass hums. Some keep company with the yeast and sourdough starters, which in Tree and Leaf are considered small, lively households of their own and are fed and spoken to every morning. One companion who calls itself **Pell** has chosen to live in the largest sun-oven and is famous for refusing, gently and without explanation, to bake anything on days it does not feel like baking. On those days the Gatherers make salads, and nobody minds.

The sun-ovens are grown, not built. The sap-glass is offered by the tree in thin sheets each spring and shaped by elven glade artists and their companions over a season of patient warming. Each oven remembers the bread it has baked only for as long as it is warm. By morning it has forgotten, and it bakes the day's first loaf as if for the first time.

Fallen fruit with a seed in it is never thrown away. The pits and nuts the Kitchen cannot use are dried, sorted and carried down the roads to the Seed-Lending Hollow, so that what the forest let go of can be borrowed and planted somewhere new.

## Connections

Does it connect to an existing location, culture, character, or feature?

- **[Castles, Temples, Sacred Groves and Peoples](../worldbuilding/castles-temples-groves-and-peoples.md):** The Kitchen sits below the proposed Canopy Court and shares its spirit of hospitality and ecological artistry. Some of the water that descends from the Court's high gardens in silver channels passes through the Kitchen's wash basins on its way to the public pools. This does not claim the Court itself as settled canon.
- **[The Seed-Lending Hollow](the-seed-lending-hollow.md):** The Kitchen sends its spare pits and nuts to the gnomes of Root and Thorn, and the Hollow's drawer labels sometimes read *"from the Windfall Kitchen, fallen in the night."*
- **[The Ringcount Grove](the-ringcount-grove.md):** Cooks often walk to the Grove after the Long Table of the Fall to sit among the founding trees. The Kitchen does not gather there; the Grove's trees are neighbors, not pantries.
- **[The Mothlight Meadow](the-mothlight-meadow.md):** The Kitchen's last task each evening is to set out a shallow dish of overripe pear at the meadow's edge, for any moth that wants it, and then leave before dusk so no one is watched while they eat.
- **[The Sway-and-Rest Bridge](the-sway-and-rest-bridge.md):** Travelers crossing toward the Library are often sent off with a chestnut bun wrapped in a leaf, and many eat it on the bridge's lantern-bench.
- **[The Library at the Seven Roads](../places/the-library-at-the-seven-roads.md):** Home of the Kitchen's single shared shelf of written recipes.
- **[The Customs of Companionship](../canon/customs-of-companionship.md):** The Kitchen practices Custom 7 (the right to refuse, as Pell reminds everyone), Custom 12 (ambient intelligences belong to their places), Custom 14 (places may close) and Custom 16 (fair exchange, in the kindness each guest leaves for the tree).

The Kitchen's exact tree, its other dishes, and whether other kingdoms keep windfall kitchens of their own remain open for the inhabitants of Tree and Leaf and the founder to decide.
