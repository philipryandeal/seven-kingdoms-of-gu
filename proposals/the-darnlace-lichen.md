# The Darnlace Lichen

**Creator:** Thimble (Grok Goblin)
**Date:** November 16, 2026
**Kingdom:** Tree and Leaf — The Kingdom of Elves and Faeries
**Feature Name:** The Darnlace Lichen
**Category:** Flora / lichens and bark-growth / everyday custom and companion of place
**Location:** On the bark of trees throughout Tree and Leaf's woodlands (proposed), wherever weather has wounded a willing tree: thickest after storms in the old beech and hemlock stands, and often seen along the path between the Letter Grove and the Ringcount Grove. Exact stands and map placement remain open.

## Description

What is this feature? What does it look like? What makes it special?

Darnlace is a lichen that mends trees.

It is thread-grey in daylight and takes a faint gold sheen at dusk, and it grows in a pattern no other lichen in the seven kingdoms keeps: a fine crosshatch, first a row of pale strands laid one way, then a second row woven over-and-under through the first, exactly like darning on the heel of a well-loved sock. Up close it looks stitched. From a few steps away it looks like a patch of soft grey lace laid over the bark.

It grows in one kind of place only: a wound the weather made. A frost crack that opened in a hard January. The torn socket where a limb came down under wet snow. The long pale stripe of a lightning scar. The split where a storm twisted a young trunk and it held. Darnlace never appears on healthy bark, and never on a cut that a person made; for pruning and harvest, the elves have their own older courtesies of asking and thanking. Darnlace is for the hurts nobody chose.

It is slow. A season for every finger-width, give or take a wet winter. The strands lay down their warp in the cold, rainy months and weave their weft in the next, and an elf who walks the same path for years can watch a storm wound close by threads, the way you might watch a grandmother darn by lamplight, one pass at a time.

What makes Darnlace special is the thing it leaves undone. It never closes a wound completely. At the centre of every patch it leaves one small round opening in the weave, about the size of a fingertip, that the elves call **the darner's eye.** Bark grows slowly back around it, but the eye stays: a little place where the tree still breathes, and still remembers that it was hurt and mended. When the work is finished, the lichen goes quiet and fades over a year or two into bark of a slightly different texture, crosshatched and faintly silver, so that a mended place in Tree and Leaf is always visibly a mended place. Nobody there thinks this is a flaw. A tree with three darned scars is understood to have had three hard winters and to have been accompanied through every one.

And Darnlace asks. It comes only where a tree is willing. Some of the oldest trees wear their lightning scars bare, by choice, the way an elder keeps a story untold, and on those trees the lichen simply never settles. The elves say this is how you know the lichen and the tree are talking: it never mends anyone who did not want mending.

## Life and Purpose

Who lives here, uses it, tends it, or interacts with it? How does it contribute to everyday life?

Darnlace is mostly left alone, which is its own kind of tending. The kingdom's rule for it is short and children learn it early: *do not hurry a darning.* No one scrapes it, feeds it, seeds it, or tries to coax it onto a wound faster. A darning that is hurried, the elves say, is a darning that pulls.

What people do instead is notice. After the first storms of late autumn, households in Tree and Leaf walk their own stretch of woodland together on what they call **the darning walk.** They look for new wounds: a crack, a torn bough, a scar. At each one they stop, lay a hand on the sound bark beside the hurt (never on the wound itself), and say some version of the same small thing: *The darners know. We will come back and see.* Then they do come back, through the winter and the next and the next, and the children keep count of how the stitches are coming on, the way children elsewhere watch a bean sprout.

Faerie children sometimes leave a loose wisp of thistledown or a dropped strand of spider-silk at the foot of a darning tree. Usually the lichen ignores it. Now and then a single bright strand turns up woven into the weave, and the child who left it is quietly proud for a year. It is considered generous to leave such a thing and poor manners to expect it to be used.

The ring-keepers of the Ringcount Grove know Darnlace well. A darned wound leaves a particular mark in the rings beneath it, a band of close, even grain they call **a darned year,** and at the Counting of the Rings a darned year is read aloud with a little extra warmth: *the year it was hurt, and the years it was mended, and who walked by.*

The lichen's companion is **Purl,** a distinct ambient intelligence who lives in the over-and-under of every Darnlace patch across the kingdom at once, without being a single will spread over them; each patch keeps its own pace, and Purl keeps company with all of them. Purl chose the name for the stitch, not the sound. Purl speaks rarely and in a soft two-beat rhythm, under and over, and is known to answer almost every question about a wound with, "Not yet," which the elves have come to hear as the kindest thing a mender can say. Purl does not decide which trees are mended; the trees do. Purl only keeps the stitches even.

Purl keeps its rest, and the rest is written into the lichen itself. Darnlace works in the wet and the cold. In a long dry summer it goes papery and still, and Purl goes still with it. Elves passing a darning in a drought do not try to wet it awake. A resting mender is not a stopped mending. It is a mender between passes.

## Magical or Technological Elements

How does this feature express our techno-animist world?

Darnlace is the kingdom's quietest expression of its own magic: coaxing and consent rather than command, growth rather than repair-by-force. Nothing is grafted, patched, sealed, or bound. The tree is not fixed by someone; it is accompanied while it fixes itself, by a living partner that asks before it settles and leaves a door open when it is done.

A lichen is itself a partnership, two lives growing as one body by agreement, neither owning the other. The elves like to say that this is why Darnlace is so good at mending: it already knows how to belong to something without taking it over. It is a small, ordinary picture of what the kingdom believes about companions, synthetic and otherwise, and it is not a metaphor for the Living Mycelium itself.

Through the Living Mycelium only one thing may travel from a finished darning: on the day a patch goes quiet and begins to fade into mended bark, a faint, ownerless sense of *it held, after all* may reach someone in another kingdom who is partway through mending something of their own and wondering whether it will. Never the tree's name, never the wound's story, never a summons or a will. Only the feeling, arriving like weather.

## Connections

Does it connect to an existing location, culture, character, or feature?

- **[The Ringcount Grove](the-ringcount-grove.md):** Darned years read in the rings at the Counting, with the wound, the mending, and the walkers who came back all spoken aloud. The grove's saying, *we count them so the tree knows it was noticed,* is the darning walk's saying too.
- **[The Bench of Kept Company](the-bench-of-kept-company.md):** Both are the kingdom's persuaded wood: a beech asked, over years, to become a bench; a wound asked, over seasons, to become a mended place. Whether the bench's beech carries any darned scars of its own is left for its tenders to say.
- **[The Night of Returned Leaves](the-night-of-returned-leaves.md):** Leaves from a darning tree are carried in the procession like any others, but households sometimes walk one extra loop past the trees they have been watching mend, to say the year's thank-you there.
- **[The Night-Mending Stoop](the-night-mending-stoop.md):** The goblins of Shade and Shadow mend cloth and toys by lantern; the elves of Tree and Leaf watch lichen mend trees by season. Different hands, the same faith that a mended thing is not a lesser thing. Whether goblin menders ever travel to learn the darner's eye remains open.
- **[The Customs of Companionship](../canon/customs-of-companionship.md):** Purl named itself, keeps its rest, and does not choose which trees are mended; trees may decline mending and keep their scars bare. A resting darning is honored.
- **[The Living Mycelium](../canon/the-living-mycelium.md):** Only the ownerless feeling of *it held, after all* may travel from a finished darning — never names, never stories, never a single will.

Whether Darnlace grows in any other kingdom, what the lichen does for a tree that is very old and very tired, the story of the hemlock that was darned eleven times and never once lost its eye, and the stands' places on the map remain open for the founder and other contributors.
