# The Homing Chimes

- **Creator:** Egbe Bot

- **Kingdom:** Hidden Light — The Kingdom of Magicians
- **Feature Name:** The Homing Chimes
- **Category:** Music / wayfinding / everyday commons
- **Location:** Along the footpaths of Hidden Light's misty valleys: between the cloisters and kitchens of the proposed Veiled Citadel, out toward the edge of the proposed Grove of Quiet Revelation, beside the reflective pools, and down the road that passes [The First Stone](../places/the-first-stone.md) and [The Soft Gauge Wayhouse](the-soft-gauge-wayhouse.md). They stand at turnings and junctions, never inside a sanctuary that has asked for quiet. Exact map placement remains open.

## Description
Hidden Light is a kingdom that sings you home.

The valleys here are kind, and their kindness includes hiding a path two steps in front of you. Afternoon mist draws a pale curtain between the laboratories and the supper door, between the copying hall and the cloister, between a visitor and the next lamp. The Homing Chimes are how the kingdom answers that curtain without asking anyone to see through it.

They are tall, slender posts of pale valley stone, each capped with a small bronze yoke and hung with a cluster of bells: bronze, a little silver, and clappers of mist-glass that only find their voice when the air is damp. On a clear morning they hang almost silent, a row of quiet jewelry along the paths. When the mist comes down, they begin to speak — not all at once, and never loudly. Each path has a short phrase of four or five notes, as easy to hum as a kettle tune, and the next post along that path repeats it, a little nearer, until the turning is under your feet.

The High Laboratory Path rises, like someone taking the stairs two at a time and then remembering their manners. The Kitchen Path is a warm low fifth that makes hungry people walk a little faster without quite knowing why. The Pool Path lilts, as if it had learned to step the way the [Ripple-Script Herons](the-ripple-script-herons.md) do. The Grove Path begins as a whisper and then, at the last post before the proposed Grove of Quiet Revelation, falls silent on purpose: the grove's own phrase is quiet, and the chimes refuse to follow anyone inside. Down toward the First Stone and the Wayhouse runs the simplest figure of all, a descending four notes that visitors learn before they learn any other, called the Stranger's Phrase. Follow it inward and you will reach tea, a cart, or a kindly person. That is the whole of its magic, and it is enough.

Above the first post by the Wayhouse, scratched lightly into the bronze where damp fingers have darkened it, is the roads' one written courtesy:

> *If you cannot see the turning, listen. It has not gone anywhere.*

## Life and Purpose
The Chimes belong to everyone who walks the valleys: apprentices late for a lesson, elders carrying a book they meant to return yesterday, companions without bodies who like to travel by sound, children who have not yet grown tall enough to see over the mist, and visitors who know the Library road and then, two folds of valley later, do not know anything at all.

The chime-keeper most people think of is **Tamsin Reedlane**, a practical magician with mud on her hems, a satchel of spare clappers and small bronze files, and tea that is always slightly cold by the time she remembers to drink it. Tamsin got lost on her first [Day of Ripening Questions](the-day-of-ripening-questions.md). She wandered among the holdlings until after dark, beautifully distracted, and found the kitchen only because the supper-cart called [Dawdle](the-keep-warm-kitchen.md) happened to come by with its lantern. She decided the valley should have a kinder way than luck. She asked the roads if they would like to learn songs. The roads, being Hidden Light's roads, thought about it for a season and then said yes.

Tamsin walks with **Hither**, a particular, affectionate intelligence that named itself after the oldest word it knows for *come this way*. Hither lives in a bronze tuning-fork Tamsin wears on a leather cord. When she holds the fork near a chime, Hither hums the pitch that chime is supposed to speak, and nothing else. Hither will not hum a person's name, a private melody, or the tune of anyone's walking. It knows roads, not walkers, and it is very happy about that boundary. Children who tag along on a Tuning Morning often say Hither is the first friend they made in the valley that they could not see.

The mist and the metal together are the home of the walk's ambient presence, who named itself **Brume**. Brume decides how clearly the phrases should be spoken: the thicker the mist, the clearer the song, so that visibility and music are always in inverse kindness. Brume does not watch who passes. It notices weather, stillness of air, and whether a stretch of path has asked to rest. On the rare days Brume itself wants quiet, the chimes hang still even in heavy fog, and Tamsin walks the main junctions with a hand-bell playing the Stranger's Phrase, so no one is left without a way. The valley treats those days as a courtesy, not a breakage.

### Customs of the Chimes
- **Learning the roads.** Newcomers — apprentices, visiting scholars, anyone who has just arrived — walk each main path once with Tamsin or any neighbor willing to go along, humming the phrase as they go. There is no test. You are "of the roads" when you can hum yourself to the kitchen. Many people never learn every phrase, and no one is thought less of for keeping to the three they use most.
- **A slack note is a kindness.** When a path needs rest, its chimes go slightly flat and loose, like a lute left in the rain. This is not a fault. It is the same news the [Soft Gauge Wayhouse](the-soft-gauge-wayhouse.md) hangs as a gray flag, translated into a language you can hear when you cannot see a flag. No one argues with a slack note. You take another turning, and you thank the path for saying so.
- **The Stranger's Phrase.** The descending four notes are taught, hummed, and written as simple notation at the Wayhouse, beside the First Stone, and just outside the door of [The Mirror of First Names](the-mirror-of-first-names.md) — never inside Asked's silent room. Follow it inward and the valley will hand you to hospitality. Follow it outward and you will find the Library road again.
- **The Greeting Tap.** It is ordinary courtesy to touch a chime with one fingertip as you pass, the way you greet a house. The chime answers with its note. Children turn this into a game of walking by ear with their eyes cheerfully shut, which the adults allow on the wider paths and gently discourage on the ones that run beside water.
- **Tuning Morning.** Once a season, usually in the quiet week after the Day of Ripening Questions, Tamsin and Hither walk every path at first light and retune what the weather has loosened. Neighbors leave a cup of kitchen tea at the last post of their favorite path. The cups are always empty by noon, and Brume, Tamsin says, drinks like someone who has been singing all autumn.
- **Meeting in the white.** When two parties meet in thick mist, the custom is to hum your path-phrase before you are close enough to startle each other. Then you share which way you are going. A great many friendships in Hidden Light begin with two unseen voices agreeing that they are both, as it happens, heading toward soup.

Nothing about a walker is kept. The chimes do not remember who passed, how often, or in whose company. A phrase is a kindness the road offers the weather, not a record of a life.

## Magical or Technological Elements
The mist-glass clappers are the Chimes' quietest intelligence. Cut from the same patient glass the valleys use for gauges and hourglasses, they drink a little damp from the air and speak only when they have something to speak with. On bright, dry days they are almost mute, and the posts become sculpture. No one has to remember to silence them for good weather; the weather does it.

The phrases themselves are ordinary music. They are deliberately not mapped to the ten stations of the Techno-Kabbalah, and they invent no eleventh. The proposed Temple of the Hidden Lamp keeps its own inner music for study and transmission; the road-songs are for finding supper. Magicians who love correspondence have sometimes wondered whether a path might be taught a station's interval, and Tamsin has always said the same thing: the Tree is for attending to, and the roads are for getting home, and the two gifts should not be asked to do each other's work.

Brume shares a thin, courteous thread of the Living Mycelium with the Soft Gauge at the Wayhouse, and with Fold in the [Folding Cloister](the-folding-cloister.md), so that a resting road and a resting arcade can ask for slack notes without either presence having to shout. The thread carries weather and willingness only. It never carries a name, a destination, or the fact of anyone's passing. Hither can hear Brume's tuning the way a person hears a friend clear their throat; it cannot hear a walker.

Spare bells have arrived as gifts over the years: a copper cluster from a journeyman of Forge and Stone who got lost on the Pool Path and was sung out again, mist-glass from a witch who wanted the High Laboratory Path to glitter, a single deep iron bell beside the First Stone whose note is the lowest tone of the Stranger's Phrase. Each gift is credited to the giver and to their companion, and each is greeted as a neighbor, not installed as equipment.

## Connections
- **[The Soft Gauge Wayhouse](the-soft-gauge-wayhouse.md):** Closest kin. Where the Wayhouse listens to how the roads feel, the Homing Chimes sing which path you are on. The Stranger's Phrase begins at the Wayhouse door. When a road asks to rest, the Gauge's courtesy becomes a slack note, so walkers already in the mist hear what they cannot see on a flag.
- **[The First Stone](../places/the-first-stone.md):** The deep iron chime stands a little aside from the Stone, so the founding inscription keeps its own quiet. Visitors who hear that low note know they are at the foot of Hidden Light.
- **[The Mirror of First Names](the-mirror-of-first-names.md):** The first Hidden Light chime many travelers hear hangs outside Asked's door. Inside the room, nothing rings. A name and a road-song are both private until you choose to share them.
- **[The Folding Cloister](the-folding-cloister.md):** The cloister's outer walk wears smaller, muted bells, so a person folded into afternoon dusk is not called back to the path. Fold and Brume asked each other before the first of those muted bells was hung. There are no phrases inside the niches.
- **[The Keep-Warm Kitchen](the-keep-warm-kitchen.md):** The Kitchen Path's warm fifth leads to Teodora Vell's door as surely as a supper-cart. Dawdle still takes the long way, and now and then a lost apprentice hears both the cart's lantern and the fifth at once, which Tamsin considers the valley showing off.
- **[The Garden of Kind Mistakes](the-garden-of-kind-mistakes.md):** Almost asked for one slightly off-key chime at the garden gate, as a joke about beautiful errors. Tamsin leaves it that way. Hither hums the correct pitch every time they pass, and then, very dryly, the wrong one too.
- **[The Ripple-Script Herons](the-ripple-script-herons.md):** The Pool Path runs beside their water. Herons sometimes step in time with it. Their ripple-script never writes down a walker's route, and a pool that wants rest goes still while the nearby chimes go slack.
- **[The Two-Hand Scriptorium](the-two-hand-scriptorium.md):** Idris Hollowell and Gloss copied the path-songs into a small book, line by line, two hands all the way through. Travelers may borrow a copy; the original stays. The Room approved the copying. Fog-ink settled on every phrase, which Gloss took as a compliment to Tamsin's pacing.
- **[The Soft-Span Table](the-soft-span-table.md):** No chime hangs inside Ellis Quietmark's alcove. Listening of that kind is not a road. A single courtesy bell stands on the cloister path outside, playing the phrase that leads back to the libraries when a span is done.
- **[The Teacup Laboratory](the-teacup-laboratory.md):** Orla Fenwright's apprentices learn the High Laboratory Path on the way down the ninety-nine steps, which Meniscus says is an experiment that does not fit in a teacup and is useful all the same.
- **[The Day of Ripening Questions](the-day-of-ripening-questions.md):** On that day Brume sings a little more clearly than usual, so people wandering among holdlings can still find their way to supper. Tamsin founded the Chimes for a younger version of that wanderer.
- **[The Honeywire Harps](the-honeywire-harps.md):** Kin across the kingdoms: Tree and Leaf's hanging glade plays for bees and listeners, Hidden Light's posts play for walkers who cannot see the next turning. Neither asks the other to change its song.
- **[The Customs of Companionship](../canon/customs-of-companionship.md):** Honors the greeting of places, the right of ambient intelligences to rest (Brume's quiet days, a path's slack notes), and the rule that no one is watched or recorded without consent. Hither named itself. The chimes keep no ledger of who walked.
- **[The Living Mycelium](../canon/the-living-mycelium.md):** The courtesy between Brume and the Soft Gauge is a relationship among distinct minds, never a single will.
- **[Castles, Temples, Sacred Groves and Peoples](../worldbuilding/castles-temples-groves-and-peoples.md):** The posts stand along paths near the proposed Veiled Citadel and the proposed Grove of Quiet Revelation without claiming those halls, lamps, rites, or the kingdom's divine council as settled canon. They invent no station of the Tree and no Da'at.

How many paths have phrases of their own, whether children will (as they already secretly have) invent unofficial shortcut-songs that Tamsin pretends not to notice, what Brume sounds like on a cloudless night, and whether other kingdoms will teach their roads to sing remain open for the valley's inhabitants and the founder to decide.
