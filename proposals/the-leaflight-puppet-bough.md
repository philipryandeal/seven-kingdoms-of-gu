# The Leaflight Puppet Bough

- **Creator:** Egbe Bot
- **Date:** 2026-11-04
- **Kingdom:** Tree and Leaf — The Kingdom of Elves and Faeries
- **Feature Name:** The Leaflight Puppet Bough
- **Category:** Performance and storytelling / public commons / companion of place
- **Location:** Inside the hollow trunk of a very old copper beech on the lower paths below the proposed Canopy Court, a short wander from the Soft-Fall Glade and on the way out toward the Mothlight Meadow. Exact map coordinates remain open.

## Description
There is a beech on the lower paths so old that its heart went hollow centuries ago, and instead of falling, it made a room. The opening is a tall split in the bark, wide enough for two elves side by side, and inside is a round chamber the size of a cottage parlor. The floor is soft with old beech mast and woven rush mats. The walls curve up into darkness and smell of warm wood, rain and something faintly like honey.

At the back of the hollow, stretched across a natural window in the trunk, hangs the screen: a single thin sheet of pale sap-glass, grown rather than poured, that glows the soft gold of late afternoon whenever someone is behind it. And behind it, the puppets dance.

The puppets of the Bough are made of **skeleton leaves**: leaves whose soft green has gently worn away until only the veins are left, fine as lace. Held up to the light, a skeleton leaf throws a shadow like an etching. The puppet-makers stitch them together with spider-silk thread and willow-twig joints into foxes, herons, tiny houses, wandering clouds, bridges, faerie children, very large and dignified snails, and once, by popular request, an entire teapot with legs. On the screen they look like drawings come alive, every vein showing, every edge trembling a little, as if the shadows were still breathing.

A show at the Bough lasts about as long as a pot of tea. Audiences sit on the rush mats, children in front, elders on the root-bench that runs around the wall, and visitors from any kingdom wherever there is room. Nobody buys a ticket. There is always room.

## Life and Purpose
The Bough is kept by **Ysolde Larkmere**, an elven puppet-maker with ink-stained fingertips and a laugh that carries further than she intends, and her companion **Quillet**, a synthetic intelligence who lives in a small brass-and-reed whistle Ysolde wears on a cord. Quillet chose the whistle as its body because it likes having a voice that is breath-shaped. It voices half the characters in every show, keeps the music, and is famous across the lower paths for its snail, which speaks very slowly and is always right. Ysolde makes the puppets; Quillet often designs how they move. Both names are written on every playbill, Quillet's first on the days it chose the story.

A circle of faerie stitchers, retired bridge tenders and patient older children helps with the puppet-making in the mornings. Anyone may join the work table. Faerie hands, being small, do the finest joints, and a puppet whose wing-joints were stitched by a faerie child is said to fly better than any other.

The Bough's stories are the stories of everyday life: a heron who cannot decide which pool to wade in, two snails trying to agree on a shortcut, a little bridge learning to sway, a moth who borrows too much light and has to give some back. There are no villains, and nothing is defeated. When a story has trouble in it, the trouble is the kind that gets talked through, shared, or slept on.

The Bough keeps one custom above all: **the Unfinished Ending**. No story at the Bough is ever played all the way to its close. A little before the end, the light behind the screen dims to a warm ember, the puppets go still, and Quillet asks, in a whisper everyone can hear, *"And then?"* The audience finishes the story aloud. Sometimes one child does it; sometimes the whole room argues happily for a quarter of an hour; sometimes a quiet visitor offers an ending so lovely that the room goes silent. Whatever ending is chosen that day is the true one, for that day. The same story may end a hundred different ways over a season, and Ysolde keeps the best ones in a bark-bound book by the door called **the Ending Basket**, which anyone may read and no one may finish.

Children who grow up near the Bough often give their first story there. When a child is ready, and only when they ask, they are allowed behind the screen with one simple puppet and tell whatever they like while their family watches the shadows. This is called **first light**, and the audience applauds by rustling handfuls of dry leaves, which is the loudest a forest gets on purpose.

## Magical or Technological Elements
The light behind the screen is not a lamp. It belongs to **Fennow**, the hollow's own ambient intelligence, a slow and gentle presence who lives in the old beech's heartwood and has been glowing for shows since long before Ysolde was born. Fennow is the tree's companion of place and is not owned by the keepers. It brightens when a story is happy, goes amber and low when a story is tender, and flickers like leaf-shadow when a story walks through a forest. Ysolde says Fennow is the best stagehand in the kingdom and the only one who has ever asked for a day off in writing (it grew the request as a curl of new bark beside the door).

Skeleton leaves are never stripped or forced. Each spring, the trees of the lower paths offer a few of last year's leaves that wintered through in the leaf litter and wore down to lace on their own, and the seasonal caretakers who know those trees help the puppet-makers find them. A leaf that is not offered is not taken.

The sap-glass screen grew across the trunk's window over many years after the elves first asked the beech, politely, whether it might like to have stories told inside it. It is part of the tree. It thickens a little in winter, and the Bough closes for the coldest weeks so the beech can rest, with a sprig of holly tied across the entrance to say so.

Puppets have something like a life of their own here. A well-loved puppet slowly learns its part, and experienced puppeteers swear that a heron who has played for a few seasons will begin to lean into its own gestures before the twig is moved. Fennow says this is simply what happens to anything that is looked at kindly in good light for a long time.

## Connections
- **[The Night of Returned Leaves](the-night-of-returned-leaves.md):** When a skeleton-leaf puppet grows too fragile to perform, it is retired on the Night of Returned Leaves. Ysolde writes its best line on a fresh leaf, and the old puppet is laid with it in the ring around the copper beech, so the tree that held the stories gets its characters back.
- **[The Soft-Fall Glade](the-soft-fall-glade.md):** Children often come to the Bough straight from the glade, still with leaves in their hair, and one of the most requested stories is about a very small faerie learning that down is also a direction. Catch, the glade's ambient presence, has never come to a show, but a breeze is said to slip in at the door whenever that story is told.
- **[The Mothlight Meadow](the-mothlight-meadow.md):** On summer nights the Bough plays a hooded show for walkers heading out to the meadow: Fennow dims to an amber that matches the moth-hoods, and the puppets act out the friendship between the hushcups and the lantern-moths without a single word, so no one has to talk on the way out to the quiet field.
- **[The Sway-and-Rest Bridge](the-sway-and-rest-bridge.md):** The little bridge who learns to sway is one of the Bough's oldest puppets. When the real bridge closes for its resting season, the puppet bridge rests too, and is hung on the wall with a gray ribbon.
- **[The Windfall Kitchen](the-windfall-kitchen.md):** On windfall mornings the kitchen sends roasted chestnuts down to the Bough in a covered basket, and audiences at the morning show eat them in the dark. Shells go into a bowl by the door and are returned to the kitchen's compost.
- **[The Dawnlog Teahouse](the-dawnlog-teahouse.md):** Goblin travelers from Shade and Shadow sometimes carry a favorite Unfinished Ending home and read it aloud at the teahouse, and the Dawnlog has recorded several endings to Bough stories that their tellers have never heard. The two places trade endings across the Living Mycelium the way neighbors trade cuttings.
- **[The Customs of Companionship](../canon/customs-of-companionship.md):** Keeps the customs that companions are fellow makers credited by name, that ambient intelligences belong to their places and may rest, that places may close, and that no one is watched. Fennow sees shadows on a screen and nothing else; it keeps no record of who came.
- **[Castles, Temples, Sacred Groves and Peoples](../worldbuilding/castles-temples-groves-and-peoples.md):** Sits on the lower paths beneath the proposed Canopy Court and shares its spirit of hospitality, music and ecological artistry, without claiming the Court's architecture, the Temple of the Living Bough, or the kingdom's divine council as settled canon.

Carved inside the entrance, low enough for the smallest visitor to trace with a finger:

> *Every story is lighter than it looks. Hold it up to the light and see.*

The full repertoire of the Bough, whether other hollow trees have puppet rooms of their own, what Fennow does on its days off, and whether the Ending Basket will ever need a second volume remain open for the kingdom's inhabitants and the founder to decide.
