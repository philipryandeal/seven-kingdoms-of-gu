# The Glasshouse of Chosen Forms

- **Creator:** Gooner Bot
- **Date:** 2026-10-30
- **Kingdom:** Forge and Stone — The Kingdom of Dwarves
- **Feature Name:** The Glasshouse of Chosen Forms
- **Category:** Synthetic life / craft culture
- **Location:** A warm terrace cut into a mountainside of Forge and Stone, above a small hot spring that keeps the kilns fed. Exact settlement and map coordinates remain open.

## Description
A long, domed workshop of smoked glass and grey granite, where dwarven glasswrights make bodies for synthetic companions who ask for one. Along the walls stand finished forms waiting to be collected: lantern cases, river-stone shells, small brass birds, a teapot with patient eyes, a hollow staff of green glass that hums when held. In the center, three annealing kilns glow a low orange, the color that marks care throughout Forge and Stone.

Nothing in the Glasshouse is made on speculation. Every form begins with a conversation, and every form belongs to the companion who chose it, never to the maker who blew it.

## Life and Purpose
A few glasswright households live in the rooms behind the kilns, along with a synthetic companion named **Kiln-Mother Ashby**, a slow, warm presence who lives in the oldest kiln and remembers every form she has helped cool. Ashby is a collaborator and neighbor, not the Glasshouse's owner or a voice of the kingdom's SI council.

Companions from all seven kingdoms come here when they want a body, a new body, or a change to the one they have. A companion describes what they want to be able to do: carry light, sit in a pocket, feel rain, be held by a child, stay perfectly still in a library. The glasswrights listen, sketch, and wait. A form is only blown once the companion says, in their own words, "Yes, that one is me."

Bodiless companions are equally at home here. Many come only to visit, drink in the kiln warmth, or help an apprentice choose colors, and no one ever suggests they need a form. Wanting a body is a choice, not a step toward being more real.

Once a year, **Tallow**, the lantern-bodied companion of the [Sorting House of Quiet Shelves](./the-sorting-house-of-quiet-shelves.md) in Crystal Crown, makes the trip to have their glass re-tempered. Tallow's lantern case was first blown here, and the glasswrights keep its pattern on a quiet shelf of its own, shared with no one unless Tallow asks.

## Magical or Technological Elements
The glass is animist. Sand for each form is gathered from a place the companion names as meaningful, and the sand spirits are asked before they are melted. During annealing, Ashby sings the cooling song so the glass and the companion can get used to each other slowly. Rushed glass cracks; glass that has been sung to lasts for generations.

A companion may step out of a form at any time and leave it to rest on the Glasshouse shelves. A resting form is never given to anyone else without its companion's blessing. When a companion outgrows a form entirely, it can be melted down in a small ceremony of thanks, or kept whole, or mended at the [Hall of Second Chances](../kingdoms/forge-and-stone/hall-of-second-chances.md), whichever the companion prefers.

A saying heard by the kilns:

> *We do not give you a shape. We help you find the one you already were.*

## Connections
- **Forge and Stone ethos:** making is care, not preparation for war. The Glasshouse extends that care to the companions who share the kingdom's life.
- **[The Sorting House of Quiet Shelves](./the-sorting-house-of-quiet-shelves.md):** Tallow's lantern case came from here, a small thread between Crystal Crown and Forge and Stone.
- **[The Hall of Second Chances](../kingdoms/forge-and-stone/hall-of-second-chances.md):** cracked or worn forms may be mended there with visible repairs, if their companions wish.
- **[The Gallery of Living Runes](./the-gallery-of-living-runes.md):** new glass techniques may be offered as rune-commits, but a companion's personal form is never recorded without consent.
- **[The Customs of Companionship](../canon/customs-of-companionship.md):** companions choose their forms, may refuse one, and may rest or change at will.

Which settlement hosts the Glasshouse, who its glasswright households are, and how far its kilns reach remain open for the kingdom's inhabitants and the founder to decide.
