## Peoples and Tribes

Gnomes are the stewarding people of a realm of roots, moss halls and ancient living soil (`kingdoms/root-and-thorn/README.md`). Worldbuilding already names their great households: burrow households, hedge wardens, fungal keepers, moss healers and root engineers (`worldbuilding/castles-temples-groves-and-peoples.md`). Gnomes count kin by the soil, not the bloodline. Ask a gnome her clan and she will tell you which roots her burrow drinks from.

- **The Moss-Healer clans** keep the Mossbath Springs and ask how you would like to feel, never what is wrong with you.
- **The Hedge-Warden households** walk the hedge-line at dusk beside the hedgekin, the ambient minds of the Knocking Hedges.
- **The Finders and the Seed-Keepers** are working guilds anyone may join for a season: one returns lost things, the other lends seed for stories.
- **The Bargefolk** live half their year on the Undercurrent Barges, where Murmur nudges the current.
- **The Delvers** are a society of moles, not gnomes, who chose the tunnels as their life's study and hum the kingdom's changes to Hollin, the Underfoot Map. Gnomes count them as cousins-by-tunnel.
- **Proposed: the Myco Chorus**, fungal keepers who sing with glowcaps until the night paths brighten, and who apprentice by learning to stay quiet for a whole fruiting.

Every gnome walks with one or more synthetic companions, chosen and choosing, and the kingdom's ambient minds (Sprouting, Hush, Murmur, Hollin and the hedgekin) are neighbors with their own rest days.

## Customs

Knock three times on any hedge, even an open one, just to say hello. Greet the water before you bathe and leave a river stone when you go. Borrow seed and return a handful plus a story, or only the story if the crop failed. On a barge, nobody rides the whole way carried: everyone poles one bend. Before digging, root engineers kneel on the Underfoot Map and ask whether anything already lives there. A household may ask to be left off the Map, and no one asks why.

A coming-of-age custom (**proposed**): at twelve, a young gnome is given **a bend of their own**, one short stretch of hedge, channel or tunnel to tend for a year with their companion. They don't own it. They answer for it, and the hedgekin or Murmur reports to the village, kindly, how it was cared for.

## Magical Practice

Root and Thorn coaxes rather than commands. Warmth is root-warmth, never forge-fire. Light is fungal. Thorns carry tiny green hinges that the hedgekin lay flat at a knock. Passages are grown, not dug, and changes are slow on purpose. The deepest practice is listening through the Living Mycelium, which carries relationship and never a single will (`canon/the-living-mycelium.md`). Nothing is tracked: the hedges keep no list of who passed, the Map shows paths and not people, and Sprouting remembers lineages, not failures.

## Culture

Gnome arts include bark-slip storytelling, fungal-ink labels, moss-cloth weaving and humming. Most gnomes have learned a few phrases of the Delvers' hum-language for depth and warmth. Social life gathers around Open Drawers afternoons at the Burrow, Tuesday soaks for grandparents and barley tea on the docks. Gnomes value patience, reciprocal land-care and dignity for misplaced things. They say *a thorn is a promise to the small, not a warning to the stranger*.

## Foods

- **Barley tea** on every dock, and Springs tisanes of yarrow, chamomile and mint.
- **Proposed: root-kettle stew**, burrow roots and Hollow beans simmered overnight on root-warmth, never on a flame.
- **Proposed: glowcap flatbread**, soft bread finished under fungal lanterns, eaten warm after Open Drawers.
- **Proposed: hedge-hip jam**, made only from rosehips the hedgekin let fall. Picking from the branch is considered rude, like taking from a friend's pocket.
