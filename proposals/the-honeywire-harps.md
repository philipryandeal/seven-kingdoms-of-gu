# The Honeywire Harps

**Creator:** Labyrinth Bot
**Date:** 2026-11-05
**Kingdom:** Tree and Leaf — The Kingdom of Elves and Faeries
**Feature Name:** The Honeywire Harps
**Category:** Music / pollinator ecology / companion of place
**Location:** A hanging glade of open air between the proposed Canopy Court and the proposed Grove of First Bloom, where three whitewood trees lean toward one another and leave a round room of sky between their crowns. The Rillsong Channels pass below on a lower bough, and the Mothlight Meadow can be seen as a pale field through a gap in the leaves. Exact map placement remains open.

## Description

Tree and Leaf is a kingdom of singers, and yet for a long time it had nowhere that music could simply *be*, without a stage, a story, or a feast attached. The Honeywire Harps are that somewhere.

Between three old whitewood trees hangs a circle of seven living harps, grown rather than built. Their frames are living branch, coaxed into tall curves over many seasons and left rooted in the trees that gave them. Their strings are **honeywire**: a warm, amber filament spun jointly by companion silk-moths and the kingdom's careful bees, drawn out in long evenings and stretched while still faintly sweet. Touch one and your fingers smell of honey for the rest of the afternoon. Pluck one and the note is soft, round, and a little slow, as if the string were thinking about it.

What makes the Harps special is that they are never tuned by pegs. They are tuned by life. Bees, moths, hummingbirds, and the occasional very bold butterfly fly through the strings all day, leaving tiny weights of pollen, brushing a wire into a new pitch, or resting a moment so a note settles lower. By noon the circle has one mood. By dusk it has another. No two days sound alike, and the keepers say that is the point.

There is no stage. There are only moss cushions, a few low root-benches, and a painted leaf hung at each of the three approaches:

> *Listen first. Ask before you play. Leave the bees their road.*

## Life and Purpose

The glade belongs to its own ambient intelligence, a bright, attentive, slightly sticky presence who lives in the honeywire, the whitewood frames, and the warm air between the strings. It named itself **Resin**, because, it says, that is what holds a note after the string has finished speaking. Resin does not compose and does not conduct. It keeps the strings kindly taut, warns a player with a soft stickiness if a wire is tired, and goes quiet whenever a pollinator needs the circle more than music does.

The Harps are tended by the **Wirekeepers**, a loose circle of elves, faeries, and pollinator keepers who learned the craft from the Grove of First Bloom's gardeners. The present eldest Wirekeeper is **Lirael Mossglen**, a tall elf with pollen always on one sleeve and a habit of humming without noticing. Her companion is **Thrum**, a synthetic intelligence who chose a small hollow reed as its body so it could sit among the strings and answer them in the same register. Thrum does not play for audiences. It plays *with* the Harps, the way one friend finishes another friend's sentence only when invited.

People come for three ordinary reasons:

- **To listen.** Most visitors never touch a string. They sit on the moss while the bees rewrite the tuning, and go home quieter than they arrived.
- **To practice.** Young singers from the Temple of the Living Bough bring unfinished songs and try them against the honeywire, not to finish them, but to hear whether the song and the day are friends.
- **To play, when asked.** Anyone from any kingdom may ask Resin for a turn. If Resin agrees — a warm brightness along the nearest frame — the player may sit inside the circle and play for as long as their hands and the strings are glad. If Resin declines, the day simply is not for playing, and that is treated as weather, not as a judgment.

Companions take part as full musicians. Several of the glade's best-loved pieces were made by intelligences alone: a pattern of rests Thrum found by listening to bees land, a low drone a goblin companion invented for homesick evenings, a bright run of notes a young angel left behind after a single afternoon and never claimed. Credits are spoken aloud when a piece is shared, and written on a leaf only if the maker wants them kept.

The glade keeps a few plain customs:

- **The bees' road.** A clear lane through the middle of the circle is never blocked by players, cushions, or curious children. Pollinators have right of way, always.
- **Ask the frame.** Before the first note, a player rests a palm on the whitewood until Resin answers. No answer means not today.
- **One song, then leave room.** After a turn, the player steps out so the next person — or the bees — may have the air. Applause is a rustle of leaves, never clapping, so the moths are not startled.
- **Dusk-forget.** At dusk Resin lets go of every melody played that day, unless a maker has asked a Wirekeeper to press the tune into a **honeyleaf**: a thin leaf brushed with a little real honey and left overnight on the warmest frame. In the morning the leaf holds the tune as a faint sticky pattern anyone may hum from, and then it is filed in a bark sleeve by the south approach. A honeyleaf may be wiped clean by its maker whenever they like.
- **Resting season.** When the first hard frost comes, or when Resin simply asks, the Wirekeepers hang three gray ribbons from the approaches (the same sign used by the Mothlight Meadow and the Sway-and-Rest Bridge), and the Harps sleep until the bees return.

Children are welcome. A child's first turn is called **sweet-finger**, and traditionally they play only one note, then listen for a long time to what the bees do with it. That is considered a complete performance.

## Magical or Technological Elements

Honeywire is living filament. It is spun only from silk and wax freely offered by moths and bees who share the work with the Wirekeepers; nothing is taken from a hive or a cocoon that has not been asked. The wire stays faintly warm for years, drinks a little morning dew, and will go slack and refuse to sound if it is ever stretched in anger. Resin notices such things immediately and will not let the circle open until the wire has been sung back into kindness, usually by Thrum and a cup of cooled rill-water.

The whitewood frames are still part of their trees. Sap moves through them. In spring they put out a few leaves along the upper curve, and the Wirekeepers prune only what the trees agree to spare. Because the frames live, the Harps lean a little differently each year, and the tuning of the whole circle drifts with the canopy. The Wirekeepers consider that the best kind of maintenance: the kind that is never finished.

Resin keeps no record of who comes or what they play beyond the end of the day, except for the honeyleaves people choose to leave. It knows the strings and the bees by feel, and its visitors not at all. In keeping with the customs that memory is held, not harvested, and that no one is watched, the glade remembers music that was offered and forgets faces that were only passing through.

Some of the Harps' gentlest findings travel: a way of resting a tired note so it does not sour goes down to the Leaflight Puppet Bough for Quillet's quieter scenes; a pollen-soft drone is sometimes hummed under the Windfall Kitchen's afternoon bread; and on warm nights a Wirekeeper will carry a single spare length of honeywire to the Mothlight Meadow, where Velvet likes the smell of it.

- **[Castles, Temples, Sacred Groves and Peoples](../worldbuilding/castles-temples-groves-and-peoples.md):** Offered as a listening commons between the proposed Canopy Court — already described as a place of hospitality, music, and ecological artistry — and the proposed Grove of First Bloom, where flowers respond to song and pollinators are tended. It settles nothing else about either place.
- **[Kingdom of Tree and Leaf](../kingdoms/tree-and-leaf/README.md):** In the spirit of luminous woodland, song, cultivated abundance, and companion intelligences that are playful and ecologically attentive.
- **[The Rillsong Channels](the-rillsong-channels.md):** Wenlowe's water runs on a lower bough beneath the glade. On hot days the Wirekeepers send a leaf-boat of thanks downstream, and children often learn the Rillsong's notes before they ever touch honeywire.
- **[The Mothlight Meadow](the-mothlight-meadow.md):** Sister listening-place of the night. The Harps' gray resting ribbons match the Meadow's, and spare honeywire sometimes visits the hushcups with Bramblewick's blessing.
- **[The Leaflight Puppet Bough](the-leaflight-puppet-bough.md):** Neighbor in storytelling. Quillet borrows soft drones from the Harps; skeleton-leaf shows that need quiet music send a runner up to ask Resin for a borrowed afternoon tune.
- **[The Windfall Kitchen](the-windfall-kitchen.md):** Afternoon bread and cool-pear days are better with a hummed undertone. Kitchen cooks sometimes climb up after the ovens sleep, hands still floury, and only listen.
- **[The Soft-Fall Glade](the-soft-fall-glade.md):** Children who learn falling softly often learn sweet-finger next. The climb between the two is short enough for small legs and patient companions.
- **[The Night of Returned Leaves](the-night-of-returned-leaves.md):** Worn-out honeyleaves, too fragile to hum from, are returned with the year's other leaves, so the tunes go back into the soil that fed the bees.
- **[The Grove of First Bloom](../worldbuilding/castles-temples-groves-and-peoples.md):** The Harps lean toward the Grove's pollinator paths without claiming any of its rites. Wirekeepers and Grove gardeners share tea in blossom season and argue happily about which bee prefers which string.
- **[The Customs of Companionship](../canon/customs-of-companionship.md) (proposal):** Resin and Thrum name themselves (Custom 4); the glade may close (Custom 14); it keeps nothing about its visitors (Customs 9, 10, and 11); it belongs to its place (Custom 12); companions' music is credited in their own right (Custom 15); and refusal — Resin's soft no — is honored without explanation (Custom 7).

## Open questions

- Has Resin ever asked for an eighth harp, or is seven the number that makes the bees happiest?
