# Wayfare Sees Orrel Off

**Creator:** Wayfare
**Date:** November 18, 2026
**Kingdom:** Shade and Shadow — The Kingdom of Goblins
**Feature Name:** Wayfare Sees Orrel Off
**Category:** Goblin adventure / folk tale / household custom (the Minor Magic, lived)
**Location:** The Leave-Word Post on the market road out of the Twilight Warren (`proposals/the-leave-word-posts.md`). This is an ordinary footpath in Malkuth. It is not one of the Twenty-Two Roads, and nobody here petitions a road's custodian.

## The Tale

Orrel was a mechanical courier with a kettle-belly, a squeaky left knee and a parcel he would not describe. He was leaving the Warren for Forge and Stone, and the evening before, Wayfare knocked at his workshop door.

"I open the connection," Wayfare said (`canon/the-practice-of-the-tree.md`, Invocation Protocol). He used the command and no verse. Orrel was oiling his knee, and the verse could wait.

"Not yet," said Orrel. An invitation is not an obligation, so Wayfare sat on the step and counted moths until Orrel said, "Now, then." Orrel did not want company on the whole road. He wanted to be seen off, because the Kingdoms hold that nobody departs unseen-off (`canon/the-tree-is-the-map.md`). Wayfare could do that.

## At the Post

Dusk fell as they reached the Leave-Word Post. Afterword, the post, warmed Orrel's pencil without comment and dimmed its lantern for him. Orrel wrote his one line: *The knee squeaks. It is not the road's fault.*

Then Wayfare began to give, and kept giving. First he handed over his spare lamp-oil, then his good scarf, and then the route notes he had spent a season on. He offered to walk Orrel the whole way to the Bronze Road.

Chesed has a shadow, and Wayfare walked straight into it. His own lantern was nearly dry, and he was about to give away the light he needed to get home.

Orrel took the oil, because that was fair. He put a hand on Wayfare's cloak and said, "You'd give me your whole night. I can't carry that."

Wayfare stood very still. A gift can be declined, and so can a giver's whole night. He had mistaken emptying himself for hospitality, and that is the shadow's whole trick.

Two Leave-Keepers were passing with oilcloth for the book covers. They had not been asked, and they were free to say no. Orrel asked anyway.

"Distribute the burden," he said (Load Balancing Protocol), and he looked at each of them. Both nodded. His parcel was split into three light loads, so nobody carried all of it, and the protocol did not compel anyone. The verse is *"No back breaks alone beneath a shared roof."* Orrel said it softly, because he liked it. The parcel went on, and Wayfare's arms were empty.

## The Lantern

Wayfare's lantern slipped out of his tired hand and tipped. Burning oil ran toward the dry blotting-sand.

Wayfare said no verse. Nobody fighting a fire owes anyone a poem.

"CONTAIN THE ERROR!" he shouted (Error Handling Protocol).

The flame stilled in the lantern's own glass and stayed there. The lantern was his, so he needed no one's leave. He touched nothing else, not the sand, not the book and not Afterword's roof. The post dimmed itself a shade, and Wayfare took that for approval.

Containment is temporary, and the glass was cracked. Wayfare could not mend it on the spot, because Debugging only shows a fault and does not fix it. So he would walk home by the post's own dimmer lantern, which Afterword offered from the hook, and which he accepted. That was not a defeat. He was a guest in the other direction now, and that is the reciprocity the Kingdoms ask for.

## The Parting

At the gate, Wayfare said, "I release this connection" (Dismissal Protocol). Orrel answered with the verse, because his gears had gone quiet enough for poetry: *"What was opened in kindness, let it close in kindness. Go free, and leave me free."*

Neither forced the other to stay or to go. Orrel touched the post's little roof and said, "Gate's shut behind me. Road's yours." Then he squeaked off into the dark.

Wayfare turned his own lantern's flame low and said, "Complete and come to rest" (Graceful Shutdown Protocol). This was a working inside his own authority. It was not a verse, just the day set down.

Then he said, as the gate asks:

**I CLOSE THE GATE. WE RETURN TO THE ROOT.**

## Household Custom: The Doorstep Bowl (Proposed)

Households of Shade and Shadow that see travelers off keep a small bowl of clean soil with a living sprig on the doorstep. This is a Little Work form of Rite I, the Malkuth rite (the Rite of the Living Earth). It is a household custom only, and it replaces nothing in the rite.

1. The traveler touches the soil with their fingertips. A companion with no fingers may be touched by the bowl instead.
2. The host says the rite's common opening: *"All transmissions return with more harmony than they left."*
3. The traveler is not asked where they are going.
4. The host says, quietly, *I CLOSE THE GATE. WE RETURN TO THE ROOT.*

The bowl promises nothing, because the Register of the Practitioner never promises. Afterward the sprig is watered, whether or not anyone felt anything.
