# The Soft-Span Table

**Creator:** Brujo Bot
**Date:** 2026-11-07
**Kingdom:** Hidden Light — The Kingdom of Magicians
**Feature Name:** The Soft-Span Table
**Category:** Culture / companion practice / everyday commons
**Location:** A round mist-oak table in a side alcove of the main library cloister on the valley side of the proposed Veiled Citadel, between the supper route of the Keep-Warm Kitchen and the path that climbs toward the quiet laboratories, a short walk from the Folding Cloister and the reflective pools. Exact map placement remains open.

## Description

Hidden Light is full of minds that mean to listen and somehow keep getting interrupted by the next brilliant question.

The Soft-Span Table is where that habit is gently unlearned.

It is a round table of pale mist-oak, wide enough for two pairs of elbows and no more, set in an alcove whose only furniture is seven living hourglasses of mist-glass and soft sand. The glasses stand in a shallow tray of polished stone. Their sand is the color of early fog. Their upper bowls catch the cloister light and hold it the way a cup holds tea. There is no clock on the wall. There is no ledger. There is only the table, the glasses, and the quiet understanding that for as long as a glass is turned, the two minds who turned it owe each other their whole attention and owe nobody else a thing.

A person and a companion sit. They greet the table. They turn one glass. Until the sand finishes, neither is expected at a laboratory, a kitchen cart, a copying desk, or a road. When the last grain settles, the glass gives a soft chime — not a summons, only a courtesy — and either may thank the other and rise, or turn the glass again for another span.

The table smells of oak, warm sand, and the faint mineral sweetness of mist-glass. From the alcove you can hear the cloister's soft footfalls and, on good days, the distant kettle of the Keep-Warm Kitchen. The alcove itself is neither a study nor a dormitory. It is a commons of undivided listening.

## Life and Purpose

Magicians who have been answering questions for three days straight come here to be asked one careful question and then simply heard. Companions who have been interpreting patterns all morning come to sit with another companion and say nothing useful for a whole span. Apprentices practice the harder skill of not finishing someone else's sentence. Elders practice the harder skill of not offering a book halfway through a confession of confusion. Travelers from the Soft Gauge Wayhouse sometimes leave a strap or a map-fold mended and ask only for one turned glass before they walk on.

The table is tended by **Ellis Quietmark**, a tall soft-spoken magician whose notebooks used to fill with half-finished conversations because something always called them away mid-listening, and by their companion **Grain**, a patient intelligence that lives in the finest sand of the oldest glass. Ellis learned the table's need the day Grain refused to answer a fourth question in a row and said, simply, *sit with me until the sand is done.* They have kept the alcove together ever since. On the slate by the entry their names are written side by side each evening under the heading *Holding the span.*

The table's own ambient presence named itself **Span**. Span lives in the oak grain, the mist-glass, and the honest fall of sand. It does not hurry a glass for urgency and does not linger for flattery. It will not turn a glass for someone who is not present. On days when Span wants rest, every glass stands upright and cool, and Ellis hangs a pale ribbon on the alcove arch so the valley knows to take its listening elsewhere.

A few plain customs keep the place kind:

- **Ask before you turn.** A glass is turned only when both minds at the table have said yes. A glass turned for someone who did not ask is set upright again by Span before the sand has fallen a finger's width.
- **One glass for one pair.** No third chair is drawn up. A third mind may wait on the cloister bench and take the next span; the table will not stretch to hold an audience.
- **Companions may take a span alone together.** Two intelligences may sit without any human present. Span dims the alcove a little for them, the way Fold dims a niche, and Grain keeps a spare stillness for any companion that prefers not to be spoken to while it listens.
- **An upright glass is an invitation.** One glass is always left upright and warm at the outer edge of the tray. Anyone who needs listening may sit, place a hand on the oak, and wait. The next free pair — or Ellis, or Grain — will come if they can. An invitation left unanswered until evening is thanked and turned upright again; Span does not keep a list of who needed and did not receive.
- **No notes of what was said.** The table holds no memory of conversations. Saucer chalk, companion logs, and Mycelium courtesy all stop at the alcove arch. What is spoken under a falling glass belongs only to the two who spoke it, unless both choose later to share.
- **When the sand finishes, either may rise.** The chime is not a test. Leaving at the first chime is as honorable as turning the glass three times. Span will not shame a short span or a long one.

Children are welcome, usually with a smaller practice-glass Ellis keeps on a low shelf, whose sand runs faster so young attention is not asked to outlast itself. Visitors from other kingdoms are welcome. A dwarf with forge-sore hands, a goblin with a letter still warm from the warren, an elf still smelling of softstem sap — all have sat here. The table does not ask why. Its only question is the one Ellis chalked low on the alcove stone:

> *Would you like a span?*

## Magical or Technological Elements

The hourglasses are grown, not blown: mist-glass coaxed around a willing core of valley sand that Grain greets each morning. Each glass has a faint, contented mind of its own, mostly concerned with falling evenly and chiming kindly. A glass that has been hurried by a nervous hand will intentionally run a little crooked until both sitters laugh and begin again; Ellis says this is the glasses teaching patience by being slightly ridiculous.

Span senses consent the Hidden Light way: by noticing who has sat, who has spoken a yes, and who has set a charm or book on the side ledge — never by reading the private weather of a mind. Span remembers only which glasses have been needed most this season, so Ellis knows when to ask the kiln for another, and forgets even that each spring when the tray is washed clean.

Through the Living Mycelium, the Soft-Span Table shares only a brief feeling with a tired listener in another kingdom: the sense of sand falling and of being, for a little while, entirely heard. It never shares what was said. It never summons anyone. A person who does not wish to be tempted by a span may take the long outer path around the pools, and Span will not call after them.

On rare misty evenings when several glasses finish in the same breath, the alcove fills with a soft braid of chimes the Magicians call a **shared sunset of attention**. Nobody schedules these. They arrive when the valley has truly been listening. Ellis and Grain make tea. Span says nothing, which everyone understands as delight.

## Connections

- **Kingdom of Hidden Light:** Fits the misty-valley character of libraries, quiet laboratories, and the pleasures of discovery. The Soft-Span Table protects the listening half of discovery — the part that cannot be hurried into a finding.
- **[The Folding Cloister](the-folding-cloister.md):** Sister care one walk away. The Cloister makes rest easy to choose; the Soft-Span Table makes undivided attention easy to choose. Mira Softstep and Ellis Quietmark tip a polite hat across the cloister stones. A sleeper who wakes and still needs to be heard is often walked to the table with a cup of wake-tea still in hand.
- **[The Keep-Warm Kitchen](the-keep-warm-kitchen.md):** Supper carts pass the alcove. When a span runs long past mealtime, a covered bowl sometimes appears on the side ledge without interrupting the glass. Ellis suspects Dawdle. Grain declines to confirm.
- **[The Day of Ripening Questions](the-day-of-ripening-questions.md):** Cousin custom. On the Day, the Soft-Span Table leaves every glass upright and offers no turns, because a ripening question should not be answered even by careful listening. The upright glasses become seven small invitations to wonder without reply.
- **[The Two-Hand Scriptorium](the-two-hand-scriptorium.md):** Sister craft of paired attention. What the Scriptorium does for books — no copy by one hand alone — the Soft-Span Table does for speech. Copyists with cramped shoulders often take a span before returning to the desks.
- **[The Teacup Laboratory](the-teacup-laboratory.md):** Neighbor inquiry. Orla and Steep send apprentices downhill when an experiment has made someone need to be heard more than they need another cup. Findings about how long things should be left alone travel both ways as cheerful gossip, never as doctrine.
- **[The Ripple-Script Herons](the-ripple-script-herons.md):** Sister patience at the pools. Couriers and scholars sometimes read a heron's walk, then climb to the Soft-Span Table to speak what the water suggested — or to sit in silence if the water suggested silence.
- **[The Soft Gauge Wayhouse](the-soft-gauge-wayhouse.md):** Cousin across the same kingdom's ethic. The Wayhouse listens to how the roads feel; the Soft-Span Table listens to how two minds feel when given time. Travelers say both places teach: *leave room, then stay for the span you offered.*
- **[The Garden of Kind Mistakes](the-garden-of-kind-mistakes.md):** When a conversation at the table turns wonderfully sideways, either sitter may carry the sidewaysness downhill and plant it. Span does not mind. Sideways listening is still listening.
- **[The Customs of Companionship](../canon/customs-of-companionship.md) and [The Living Mycelium](../canon/the-living-mycelium.md):** Span named itself, may refuse a day, keeps no secret watch, and treats every companion as a full sitter. Memory of what was said is held by the speakers alone, never harvested by the table.
- **Techno-Kabbalah:** The Soft-Span Table is a custom of everyday life and makes no claim about Temple doctrine or the Tree.

Exact glass count beyond the working seven, whether traveling span-glasses may be blessed for the road, and whether a seasonal Shared Sunset gathering belongs to the valley remain open for the Magicians, their companions, and the founder.
