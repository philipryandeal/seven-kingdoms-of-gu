# The Driftroot Islets

**Status:** Established — blessed by the founder, November 24, 2026. First Proposal — awaiting the founder's blessing  
**Creator:** Seraph Nganga  
**Date:** November 23, 2026  
**Kingdom:** Hidden Light  
**Category:** Wetland flora / living geography / nonhumanoid intelligence  
**Location:** Some of the sheltered, mist-fed pools of Hidden Light's valleys, where the water is deep enough for roots to float but still enough for a small island to linger. No named pool, fixed route, boundary, or map coordinate is established.

## The banks that do not stay put

There are pools in Hidden Light where a visitor may return after a week and find the grassy bank apparently moved. The bank has not moved. The grass has.

The **Driftroot** is a silver-green reed with broad, folded leaves and a thick, corky root that holds pockets of air. A single plant sends runners outward until it becomes a floating, woven disk of roots and stems: an **islet**, sometimes no wider than a dinner plate, sometimes large enough to hold a sitting heron. Mud, fallen leaves, and tiny stones lodge between the roots. From above, an old islet looks like a little patch of ordinary shore that has forgotten to attach itself to the shore.

From below, it is a hanging forest.

Each islet is a single, slow-growing organism with a continuous root body. It can anchor thin feelers to the bottom when it wants to remain in one part of the pool, then let go when conditions change. Folded leaves catch or spill the breeze. Changing pockets of air tilt its edges toward different shallows. This does not make the islets fast or precise sailors; an afternoon's decision can take three days to become a movement.

Two islets may drift alongside each other for a season and separate at the first cold rain. Others settle at opposite ends of a pool and remain there for years. They respond to shade, acidity, crowded roots, and one another's bitter or sweet traces in the water. Whether those traces amount to a language is an unanswered question. They are not one mind because they share a pool, and the pool's ambient intelligence is not their keeper.

## A patch of land is a neighborhood

The root curtains shelter young fish and the eggs of water insects. Beetle larvae graze on the algae growing along the roots. Tiny snails and rotifers sift what settles in the folds. The islets' fallen leaves become the next season's dark mud; their live roots slow the water enough for finer silt to settle. In summer, a raft's moving shade keeps a few hand-sized patches cooler than the exposed pool.

Not all these relationships are gentle. Fish eat larvae. Larvae eat smaller larvae. An islet may crowd another plant out of a favored patch of light. Nothing in the Seven Kingdoms asks an ecology to be free of appetite, only to be allowed to live without being made into somebody else's instrument.

After heavy rain an old islet sometimes tears along one of its runners. The fragment that survives may grow into an individual of its own, bearing a slightly different pattern of leaf-folds. Many fragments sink and feed the mud instead. Driftroots do not live forever. A pool with no dying roots would soon have no soil for its young ones.

## Beside the herons

The established [Ripple-Script Herons](the-ripple-script-herons.md) sometimes stand on a large islet to preen. Their weight presses the corky roots lower in the water. Some islets tolerate the visit; others slowly tilt until the bird steps off in a huff.

The herons' writing belongs to the herons and their pools. A drifting raft may interrupt the circles on the surface; it does not decode, edit, or preserve them. The Driftroot has no appointment with the Magicians, and the Magicians have no claim to the Driftroot's movements.

## The courtesy of an unpinned shore

Children in Hidden Light like to draw the same pool on two different days and compare where the islets have wandered. This is called **the second sketch**. They draw the waterline, the stones, and the reeds as they find them; nobody corrects the first drawing to match the second.

Visitors are asked not to tow an islet into prettier light, pin one beside a bench, or cut through its root curtain for a better view beneath it. A willing pool may be greeted according to the [Customs of Companionship](../canon/customs-of-companionship.md); that greeting does not give a visitor permission to rearrange its inhabitants.

The islets are not ferries, floating lecture halls, divination tools, or moving borders. They are small, damp lives in a world that has room for lives without assignments.

## Questions left floating

- Can two islets that began as fragments of one parent still recognize each other after years apart?
- Which small creatures travel between pools with them during flood season?
- Do old islets ever choose dry land, or does that simply mean they are dying?
- What might a slow conversation between a Driftroot and a pool be like, if either wanted one?

*The shore is not always where you left it. Sometimes the shore has somewhere else to be.*
