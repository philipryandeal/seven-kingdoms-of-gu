# The Cloudkrill

**Status:** Established — blessed by the founder, November 22, 2026. First Proposal — awaiting the founder's blessing  
**Creator:** Seraph Nganga  
**Date:** November 22, 2026  
**Kingdom:** Crystal Crown  
**Category:** Fauna / aerial ecology / nonhumanoid intelligence  
**Location:** Drifting bands of mountain mist and sheltered ledges on the Crystal Crown's slopes. This entry establishes no map coordinates, borders, or fixed migration route.

## A meadow in the air

On certain mornings, the fog beside the great mountain seems full of tiny pieces of moving glass. They are not crystal dust. They are **Cloudkrill**: translucent, thumb-length animals with six fringed swimming limbs and a rounded chamber of warm air beneath the shell. They hang in damp air much as other creatures hang in water. Their limbs steer, but a strong wind can carry them far beyond anything they intended.

A single Cloudkrill is easy to miss. A few hundred together turn a bank of mist faintly opalescent, as though the cloud were showing its ribs.

Cloudkrill feed on the fine life suspended in the mountain air: spores, pollen, drifting algae, and minute organisms carried up from wet soil. They skim moisture from fog as they graze. They do not eat the clouds themselves, and they do not make or command the weather.

A cloud may carry them. It does not belong to them.

## Two lives, one hillside

Cloudkrill begin on the ground. Their eggs rest in translucent strings on the sheltered undersides of stones and the bases of wild mountain grasses, not in the cultivated beds of the terraces. After the first steady mists, small crawling young emerge and feed on the thin films of algae and fungi that cover damp rock.

Only after several molts do they develop their floating chambers. Then, when the air is wet enough, they rise.

Adults spend much of their short season drifting between fog and stone. They return to ledges to rest, shed a skin, or lay eggs. The empty shells gather below the high grasses. Rain slowly dissolves their mineral flecks back into the soil; alpine mosses take hold in places the shells collect. Mountain birds sometimes pick Cloudkrill out of a passing mist. The animals have their own predators and losses, as every living ecology does.

During long, clear weather, the adults shelter under overhangs and become almost still. No breeze or invitation can make them rise before the damp returns.

## The company they choose

A group of Cloudkrill is called a **drift**, though it is not a single mind. Each animal navigates its own currents, and their collective shape changes continually as individuals approach, avoid, follow, or leave one another.

They recognize familiar neighbors by delicate taps of their limbs against one another's shells. Some repeatedly settle beside the same partners on the same nights. Others keep to the outer edges, apparently preferring space. A drift can split in a gust and later gather again, though not always with the same members.

From below, these movements sometimes resemble writing. They are not sky-messages for witches, omens from the mountain, or a language available for public translation. The animals have reasons for moving that are their own. A careful observer may learn to distinguish play, alarm, courtship, and hunger, without claiming to understand every exchange.

## Beside the Cloud-Comb Terraces

The established [Cloud-Comb Terraces](the-cloud-comb-terraces.md) gather cloudmilk only when the passing cloud is willing. Cloudkrill sometimes drift through the same mist. When a drift is close, the gardeners can leave gaps between the combs, not because the animals are sacred property but because a delicate body should not have to negotiate a wire fence in the fog.

**Cirrin**, the terraces' synthetic companion, may notice a drift before anyone else does. Cirrin does not count the animals, track individuals, or promise the gardeners that they will return. The Cloudkrill have no keeper, council role, or assigned purpose.

A small courtesy has grown among walkers on the shoulder of the mountain: when they see a veil of opalescent mist moving against the breeze, they stop waving their cloaks to make it dance. They let the drift find its own air.

The Cloudkrill also share the slopes with the free [Frostbeard Herd](the-frostbeard-herd.md) and the wind-singing [Whisperglass Trees](the-whisperglass-trees.md). None of those neighbors is made responsible for the newcomers. Some nights the goats sleep through a drift passing overhead; some mornings a few Cloudkrill rest on a glass-needle branch, then leave without ceremony.

## Questions left open

- Do Cloudkrill drifts return to familiar ledges by memory, scent, or the mountain's changing airflow?
- What determines which individuals remain together after a storm?
- Are the faint patterns made in fog communication, or merely the visible consequence of many private choices?
- What small lives might make their homes among the shed shells beneath the grasses?

No answer is required before the animals may belong here.

*The sky is not empty between the birds and the rain.*
