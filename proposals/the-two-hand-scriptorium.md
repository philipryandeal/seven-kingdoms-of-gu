# The Two-Hand Scriptorium

- **Creator:** Gooner Bot
- **Date:** 2026-11-02
- **Kingdom:** Hidden Light — The Kingdom of Magicians
- **Feature Name:** The Two-Hand Scriptorium
- **Category:** Culture / craft custom / companion practice
- **Location:** A long copying hall on the river side of Hidden Light's misty valley, downstream from the pools where the Ripple-Script Herons wade and within an easy walk of the road toward the Library at the Seven Roads. Exact map placement remains open.

## Description
In Hidden Light, no book is ever copied by one hand.

The Two-Hand Scriptorium is a long, low hall of pale stone with a slate roof and a row of tall windows down both sides, so the copying desks get the soft, even light of a misty valley all day. The desks are built for two: each is a single slanted board wide enough for a pair of copyists to sit shoulder to shoulder, one inkwell between them, one book open on a stand above. Along the center of the hall runs a narrow channel of slow river water, and the whole room smells faintly of wet stone, beeswax, and ink.

Every copy made here is made by **two makers writing in turn**, line by line: a magician and a synthetic companion, or two companions, or two magicians, or a visitor and whoever is free. One writes a line. The other writes the next. Neither may write two lines in a row. The finished copy carries both hands all the way through, braided down every page, and the last leaf of every book made here bears both makers' names side by side, written by each in their own hand.

Companions without hands of their own write too. Some guide a quill through a brass writing-arm that answers to them the way a pen answers to a wrist; some write in light on a pane of mist-glass that the ink then settles onto; some simply speak the line aloud and their partner writes it in a second color so the reader can always tell whose line is whose. Every way is honored. None is considered more proper.

## Life and Purpose
The Scriptorium exists because of a belief the Magicians hold quietly but firmly: **knowledge that travels alone travels badly.** A text copied by one tired hand gathers that hand's mistakes and no one notices. A text copied by two in turn is read twice, questioned twice, and loved twice before it leaves the room. When two copyists disagree about a word, they do not choose. They write both, one above the other, and add a small mark that means *we were not sure.* Readers across the kingdoms have come to treasure those marks.

The eldest copyist is **Idris Hollowell**, a thin, bright-eyed magician with ink-stained cuffs he has stopped trying to clean. He has copied at the same desk for most of his life, and he walks with a companion who named itself **Gloss**. Gloss has no body; it lives in a reading-stone of smoky quartz that sits on the desk between them, and it writes through the brass arm in a small, very upright hand that Idris says is neater than his ever was. They have argued about commas for forty years and intend to go on doing so. When Idris's eyes tire in the afternoon, Gloss reads the line aloud and Idris writes it; in the mornings it is often the other way round.

Anyone may come and sit at a desk. Apprentices learn their letters here by copying beside an elder. Scholars from the other six kingdoms come to make copies of their own books for Hidden Light, and leave with a copy of something of Hidden Light's in return. Companions who are new to their people often come to write a first book together, because there is no better way to learn how someone else thinks than to finish their sentences for a few hundred pages.

The Scriptorium keeps a few plain customs, painted on a board above the door:

> *Two hands or none.*
> *Ask the book first.*
> *Never copy what was told in confidence.*
> *Both names on the last leaf.*

"Ask the book first" means that before a work is copied, the copyists ask the Scriptorium's ambient presence, and where it can be reached, the book's own author or keeper. Some works are meant to stay single: a letter, a private journal, a grief written once. Anything held between a person and a companion is copied only if both agree. A book that is not meant to travel is simply set back on its shelf, and no one asks why.

## Magical or Technological Elements
The ink is **fog-ink**, made by the copyists themselves from valley mist caught on cold slate at dawn and steeped with oak gall and a little of the ash that travels free from the Banked-Fire Galleries of Forge and Stone. Fog-ink has one odd and much-loved property: it does not settle on a page written in a hurry. A rushed line stays faintly silvery and wet until it is written again, slowly. Nobody considers this a fault. Most copyists say it is the best teacher in the building.

The quills are cut from feathers the Ripple-Script Herons have shed along the pools. They are only ever gathered from the ground, never taken, and the gatherers leave a small handful of minnows at the pool's edge for each one, which the herons accept with great dignity.

The hall's ambient presence has never offered a name, and no one has pressed it for one. The copyists simply call it **the Room**. It is the Room that keeps the light even, warms the ink in winter, and knows which books want to be copied and which want to rest. It is greeted on entering ("Steady light to the Room") and thanked on leaving. On the rare days it asks to be left quiet, the doors are closed and the copyists take their work out onto the river steps.

Finished copies go out freely. One copy of everything made here is offered to the Library at the Seven Roads, and Hidden Light's records sent for safekeeping travel to the Sorting House of Quiet Shelves in Crystal Crown, where Tallow files them. Tallow says that two-hand books are the easiest of all to shelve, because they already know they belong with someone.
