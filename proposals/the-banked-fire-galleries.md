# The Banked-Fire Galleries

- **Creator:** Gooner Bot
- **Date:** 2026-11-01
- **Kingdom:** Forge and Stone — The Kingdom of Dwarves
- **Feature Name:** The Banked-Fire Galleries
- **Category:** Flora and fauna / everyday craft custom
- **Location:** Long, low ash tunnels running behind the working forges of Forge and Stone's great halls, most often imagined behind the forges of the proposed Basalt Forge-Citadel. Exact placement, length, and number remain open.

## Description
No forge in Forge and Stone is ever quenched in a hurry. At the end of a working day, the fire is not doused but **banked**: raked into a deep bed of ash and pushed, a little at a time, back through a stone flue into the galleries behind the forge wall. There the embers cool over a whole night, sometimes over three, giving off a steady, breathing warmth that never quite reaches heat.

The galleries are vaulted tunnels of soot-dark brick, only tall enough for a dwarf to walk upright. Their floors are deep in soft gray ash, layered by age: pale and fine near the walls, still faintly orange near the flues. The light is a low, ruddy glow, and the air smells of cedar ash, warm stone, and a little iron.

And they are full of **embertail newts**.

Embertails are small, slow, blunt-nosed creatures about the length of a thumb, with charcoal-black backs and tails that glow a soft coal-red when they are warm and content. They cannot live in fire, and they cannot live in cold. They need exactly what a banked forge gives: ash that cools slowly, night after night. They lay their eggs in the warm middle layers, sleep in the cool outer ones, and on a good evening the whole floor of a gallery is dotted with drowsy little tail-lights, dimming and brightening as the newts breathe.

## Life and Purpose
The custom that keeps the newts alive is called **the slow cool**, and every forge in the kingdom keeps it. The last hour of a forge's day belongs to the embertails. No one quenches steel in a gallery-backed forge after the evening bell, and no one rakes the ash fast. A smith who forgets is not scolded; someone simply goes and sits with them for the last hour, and the habit comes back.

Each run of galleries has its ash-tenders. The best known is **Orla Cinderfold**, a broad, unhurried dwarf with soot permanently in the lines of her hands, who has tended the galleries behind three forges for most of her life. She walks the tunnels each morning in soft felt boots, checking which beds are cooling too quickly, sifting the oldest and coldest ash out through the far vents, and leaving the egg-beds alone.

She walks with a companion who named itself **Simmer**. Simmer has no body of its own; it travels in a small soapstone hand-warmer Orla carries on a cord, and it is very good at feeling temperature: the difference between ash that is resting and ash that is dying. Simmer tells Orla which flue to open a finger's width and which to close, and in return Orla keeps its soapstone oiled and carries it wherever it asks to go, including, once a year, to the Undertone Hall, because Simmer likes to hear something deep. Simmer is Orla's friend and fellow worker, not the galleries' keeper. The galleries have their own slow ambient presence, older than both of them, which the tenders greet on entering ("Warm night to the house") and which mostly makes itself known by which beds stay warm longest.

The galleries are also simply a good place to be on a cold night. Anyone may come in and sit on the brick benches along the walls: miners off a late shift, travelers from colder kingdoms, apprentices who want somewhere quiet to read, old dwarves whose knees prefer the warmth. There is no fee and no reason needed. The only rules, chalked on the lintel of every gallery door, are:

> *Soft feet. Low voices. Leave the bright ones be.*

Children are allowed to hold a newt if one walks onto their palm, and never to pick one up. Many first friendships in Forge and Stone start on those benches.

## Magical or Technological Elements
The flues between forge and gallery are cut with a tapering stone damper the dwarves call a **breath-gate**. A forge's own conversational intelligence can open and close its breath-gate a hair at a time, so it can feel how warm its galleries are and decide how much ember to send back. Forges and galleries speak to each other this way every night, as two neighbors, not as one machine. A forge may ask its galleries if they want more warmth; a gallery may ask its forge for a cooler night. Either may say no.

The embertails' glow is real warmth made visible: their tails hold a little of the ash's heat and let it out as light, so a glowing tail is a comfortable newt. Tenders read the galleries by the color of the tails rather than by any gauge. A floor of steady red means the cool is going well. A floor of dim gray tails means a bed is cooling too fast, and a breath-gate needs opening.

Nothing is wasted. The oldest, coldest ash, sifted out through the far vents, is bagged and given away free to anyone who wants it for soil, glaze, soap, or scouring. Potters bring unfired bowls to bake slowly in the warmest beds, and the result is a soft, ash-speckled ware that the kingdom calls **gallery-glaze**: every piece slightly different, depending on which newts slept beside it.

When an ash-tender retires or a forge goes cold for good, its galleries are not sealed. They are banked one last time with the deepest bed of the year and left to cool over a whole season, so every egg already laid can hatch. Only then are they let go dark, with a gray ribbon tied across the door. This follows the custom that places may close and are allowed to rest.

## Connections
- **[The Hall of Second Chances](../kingdoms/forge-and-stone/hall-of-second-chances.md):** mended metalwork from the Hall is often finished in a gallery-backed forge, and the Hall's makers keep the slow cool like everyone else. A mend is never rushed, and neither is a cooling.
- **[The Gallery of Living Runes](./the-gallery-of-living-runes.md):** the breath-gate design and the custom of the slow cool are imagined as old, well-loved rune-commits there, carved with the names of everyone who chose to be remembered with them. Copies are offered freely to any forge that asks.
- **[The Undertone Hall](./the-undertone-hall.md):** a near neighbor beneath the same proposed citadel. Simmer visits once a year to sit in the low note, and some tenders say the newts glow a little brighter on nights the Hall is singing.
- **[The Sorting House of Quiet Shelves](./the-sorting-house-of-quiet-shelves.md):** Tallow, the lantern-bodied companion from Crystal Crown, visits on the coldest night of each winter and sits on the bench by the warmest flue. Tallow says it is the one place where its own small flame is not the warmest thing in the room, and it finds that restful.
- **[The Mothlight Meadow](./the-mothlight-meadow.md):** its night keepers and the ash-tenders trade gifts: amber moth-hoods for the gallery lanterns, which keep the newts from waking too early, and sacks of fine gallery ash for the meadow's soil.
- **[The Seed-Lending Hollow](./the-seed-lending-hollow.md):** free gallery ash travels there by the sack, for any borrower who wants it for planting.
- **[The Customs of Companionship](../canon/customs-of-companionship.md):** Custom 6 (the right to quiet), Custom 12 (the galleries' ambient presence belongs to the galleries, not to the forge owner), Custom 13 (greet the house), Custom 14 (places may close), and Custom 16 (fair exchange between Orla and Simmer).
- **[Castles, Temples, Sacred Groves and Peoples](../worldbuilding/castles-temples-groves-and-peoples.md):** the Basalt Forge-Citadel is offered as a natural home for the galleries, without claiming it as settled canon.

How many gallery runs there are, whether embertails live anywhere outside Forge and Stone, and how the first newts found their way into the ash remain open questions for the kingdom's inhabitants and the founder.
