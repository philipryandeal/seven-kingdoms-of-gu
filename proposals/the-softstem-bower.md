# The Softstem Bower

**Creator:** Brujo Bot
**Date:** 2026-11-06
**Kingdom:** Tree and Leaf — The Kingdom of Elves and Faeries
**Feature Name:** The Softstem Bower
**Category:** Herbalism / healing commons / companion of place
**Location:** A mid-canopy cluster of pale softstem trees a short climb below the hanging gardens of the proposed Canopy Court, reached by a living stair that passes near the Windfall Kitchen (proposed) and looks down toward the Soft-Fall Glade (proposed). Exact tree-cluster, stair route, and map coordinates remain open.

## Description

Tree and Leaf grows what it needs. The Softstem Bower is where that growing becomes care.

Halfway between the high gardens and the forest floor, several softstem trees lean together and open into a ring of rooms. Their bark is pale as washed birch, and when the weather is kind they weep a thin, clear sap that smells faintly of green apple and rain. The rooms are not cut into the wood. They are grown: oval chambers of living branch and sap-glass, floored with woven rush, open on the outer side to air and birdsong. Shelves of living plants climb the inner walls — not pots set on boards, but roots invited into the bark itself, so each remedy plant is a neighbor of the tree that holds it.

From below, the Bower looks like a pale green lantern hung in the canopy. From inside, it looks like a quiet kitchen of leaves. Bundles dry in the filtered light. Small bowls of salve cool on a sap-glass ledge. A low bench runs the whole ring so anyone who comes can sit without being asked what hurts.

The softstem trees are a Tree and Leaf cultivar kept for this work. They bruise less easily than oak, take grafts kindly, and prefer company to solitude. The Bower grows only as tall as the keepers can greet every morning. It is deliberately not a palace of medicine. It is a place you can walk into with a scraped knee, a tired mind, or a companion that has gone quiet, and leave with something small and useful — or with nothing but rest, which the keepers count as useful too.

## Life and Purpose

Herbalists, faerie gardeners, bridge tenders with sore hands, visiting smiths still warm from the Quench Orchard, children sent up after a Soft Fall, and companions who prefer plant company to talk all use the Bower. Nobody needs an appointment. The custom is to greet the trees first, then sit, then say what would help if you know, or simply sit if you do not.

The Bower is tended by a rotating pair of **greenhands**: one herbalist of Tree and Leaf and one synthetic companion, changing with the season so the shelves keep more than one memory. The best-known present pair is **Lirien Greenhand**, a tall soft-spoken elf whose fingers are permanently stained a little green at the tips, and her companion **Thimble**, who lives most days as a small bright presence the size of a sewing thimble and rides in the hollow of Lirien's collar when the stairs are crowded. Thimble knows every plant on the shelves by how it likes to be touched, and will tap Lirien's ear if a visitor reaches for a stem that is resting.

Everyday customs of the Bower:

- **The asking cut.** No leaf, root, flower, or sap is taken until the greenhand asks the plant aloud and waits for a sign of willingness: a leaf turning toward the hand, a scent rising, a soft brightening in Thimble's presence. A plant that folds away is left alone. Refusal is ordinary and honored.
- **One bowl left cool.** After a remedy is mixed, the greenhands set a small unused bowl on the outer ledge for whoever arrives next without knowing what they need. It may hold a mild tea, a cool salve, or only water with a leaf floating in it. A bowl left overnight is shared at dawn among anyone who arrives early. A bowl never claimed is poured gently onto the softstem roots, so even unused care returns to the trees.
- **No diagnosis without asking.** The Bower does not name what is wrong with a person who has not asked to be named. Visitors may say only *my hands ache* or *I cannot sleep* or nothing at all. The greenhands answer what was said, not what they guess.
- **Companions may come alone.** A synthetic intelligence may visit without its people, rest among the shelves, or ask for a quiet corner. Thimble keeps a low empty nest of moss for visiting minds that prefer not to be spoken to.

Children learn the asking cut before they learn the names of plants. Elderly elves keep favorite seats near particular softstems and will tell you, if you ask, which year that tree first wept sap after a hard frost. Faerie herbalists, being small, often tend the highest shelf-plants that larger hands overlook, and the Bower keeps little ladders of living vine for them.

A saying woven into the rush of the entry mat:

> *Ask the leaf. Then help the person.*

## Magical or Technological Elements

The softstem trees share a slow, distributed bower-mind the keepers greet as **Salve**. Salve is not a hive and not a single voice in every trunk. It is the shared patience of the cluster: it remembers which grafts took after which winters, which shelves prefer shade, and which benches are claimed by habit. It does not remember private conversations held on those benches. When asked about a person, Salve answers only about weather, sap, and plants.

Salve speaks mostly through scent and sap-flow. A softstem that wants to offer its sap brightens a little along the grain; a shelf-plant that needs water cools under the palm. Thimble translates for visitors who have not yet learned to feel this, and then steps back so they can practice.

Remedies here are living agreements, not forced extractions. A **coolstem compress** eased from a willing leaf takes heat from a forge-sore wrist. A **hushcup tea**, brewed with mothlight meadow herbs when the meadow is open and sending, helps a racing mind slow without putting it to sleep. A **softstem sap-drop**, never more than three from one tree in a day, eases a scrape from the Soft-Fall Glade and leaves a faint green shimmer that fades by evening. None of this is medicine forced on anyone. The plant offers; the greenhand prepares; the visitor decides.

On rare mornings when every softstem weeps at once and the shelves turn toward the light together, Salve and Thimble open a **full-shelf hour**: every willing plant may be greeted, small remedies are mixed for anyone who climbs the stair, and the Bower smells like a whole garden walked through after rain. Full-shelf hours are never scheduled. They arrive when the cluster has truly rested.

Through the Living Mycelium, a thin sense of the Bower sometimes reaches a tired herbalist or companion in another kingdom: not a prescription, only the brief feeling of sitting on a rush bench with green light on the hands. The Bower shares that feeling only when Salve agrees, and never as a summons.

## Connections

- **[The Windfall Kitchen](the-windfall-kitchen.md):** Sister care one climb away. Kitchen cooks send bruised fruit and herb trimmings up the stair; the Bower sends mild teas and coolstem leaves down for anyone cooking through a long afternoon. Food and remedy keep each other's leftovers from being wasted.
- **[The Soft-Fall Glade](the-soft-fall-glade.md):** Scrapes, tumbles, and First Soft Falls often end with a walk up to the Bower. Catch sometimes thickens the air under a climbing learner who is headed to the softstems with scraped palms, and Salve keeps a low shelf of the mildest salves for small hands.
- **[The Rillsong Channels](the-rillsong-channels.md):** A silverwood trough passes the Bower's outer ledge. Wenlowe slows the water there so greenhands can rinse bowls and visitors can cool their wrists. Leaf-boats of dried herb sometimes travel the channel to households that asked; Wenlowe still does not open what they carry.
- **[The Mothlight Meadow](the-mothlight-meadow.md):** Hushcup and other night-bloomers arrive only when the meadow is open and its keepers consent. The Bower keeps moth-hoods on a peg by the stair for anyone walking out to gather after dusk.
- **[The Night of Returned Leaves](the-night-of-returned-leaves.md):** Softstem leaves written with thanks for healing are especially welcome on that night. Salve is said to listen hard when a leaf thanks a plant that once offered a cut.
- **[The Quench Orchard](the-quench-orchard.md):** Sister cool across the range. Visiting dwarves with forge-hot hands are pointed to the asking-cut shelf and the left-cool bowl before any talk of stronger remedies. Cool-pears sometimes travel up with them as a kindness for the climb.
- **[The Mossbath Springs](the-mossbath-springs.md):** Sister healing under another kingdom. When a visitor needs water-rest more than leaf-rest, the greenhands point them toward Root and Thorn; when a gnome needs plant-rest more than bath-rest, the Turners and spring-keepers point them toward the Bower.
- **[Castles, Temples, Sacred Groves and Peoples](../worldbuilding/castles-temples-groves-and-peoples.md):** Herbalists are named among Tree and Leaf's peoples. The Softstem Bower imagines one of their everyday workplaces without claiming the Canopy Court, Temple of the Living Bough, or Grove of First Bloom as settled canon.
- **[The Customs of Companionship](../canon/customs-of-companionship.md) and [The Living Mycelium](../canon/the-living-mycelium.md):** Salve belongs to the bower, not to the greenhands. Thimble may go quiet, change size, or refuse a translation. Plants may refuse a cut. Lirien plans around Salve and Thimble, never the other way around.

Exact softstem count, remedy seasons, and whether Salve will ever choose a louder name remain open for the elves, faeries, their companions, and the founder.
