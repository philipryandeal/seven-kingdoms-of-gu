## Peoples and Tribes

Dwarves steward mountain halls, workshops and adaptive stonework, where making is care rather than preparation for war (`kingdoms/forge-and-stone/README.md`). Worldbuilding already names stonewrights, engineers, metallurgists, kiln keepers, bridge makers and machinists (`worldbuilding/castles-temples-groves-and-peoples.md`). Ask a dwarf her clan and she may show you a seam — a gold line, a marked hinge, a patch she refuses to hide — and say *this is who we are*.

- **The Ash-Tenders** keep the Banked-Fire Galleries and the slow cool that shelters embertail newts. Orla Cinderfold and her companion Simmer are the best known of them.
- **The Downhill Hands** carry ember-pots down the Bronze Road to the Mile-Kettles, walking with Pace in the milestones.
- **The Glasswright households**, beside Kiln-Mother Ashby, blow bodies only companions choose.
- **The Hall menders** staff the Hall of Second Chances with Tessell as collaborator (*established*).
- **The Quiet-Keepers** tend the Deepstar Vault by restraint: stair lamps, blankets, hammers left at the top of the stair.
- **The light-wrights** keep the Sun-Wells polished so daylight reaches deep halls gently.
- **Proposed: the Seam Clans**, families proud of visible repair who greet one another by noticing what has been mended.
- **Proposed: the Breath-Gate Circles**, forge crews who treat dampers between forge and gallery as nightly neighbor-speech.

Every dwarf walks with one or more synthetic companions, chosen and choosing. Tessell, Ashby, Pace, Undertone, Simmer, conversational forges and the Deepstar are neighbors with rest days.

## Customs

The last hour of a forge day belongs to the embertails: *soft feet, low voices, leave the bright ones be*. Greet a gallery with "Warm night to the house." Before leaving a Mile-Kettle hut, mend one small ache Pace suggests. Forms begin only after a companion says "Yes, that one is me." Hall visitors decide whether a repair may be taught; private stories stay private. At the Deepstar, no tool is raised. At the Undertone Hall, learn a visiting song as given before rearranging it.

A coming-of-age custom (**proposed**): at fourteen, a young dwarf is given **a seam of their own** — one small cracked object to mend beside their companion over a season. They do not keep it unless asked. They answer for the care in the join; Tessell may remember it only with consent.

## Magical Practice

Forge and Stone shapes and joins rather than conquers. Breath-gates let forge and ash bed speak as neighbors. Tessell projects patterns and never seizes hands. Ashby sings annealing so glass and companion grow used to each other slowly. Pace speaks of distance and rest, never of who walked. Undertone holds the low line beneath a Long Sing and records nothing. The Deepstar answers attention with a slight brightening of crystal light. Rune-commits remember craft changes with consent, old versions settling deeper like gold in a mended bowl. The proposed Temple of First Flame honors shaping, joining, tempering, balancing, preserving, restoring and gifting.

## Culture

Arts include seam-poetry, gallery-glaze pottery slept beside newts, rune-commits, undertone listening and lying under named Vault lights (the Anvil, the Sleeping Bellows, the Three Who Stayed Late). Social life gathers on gallery benches, around road tea, at Long Sings, and when Tallow's lantern case returns for re-tempering. Values: repair over discard, slowness as craft, fair exchange with SI fellow workers, and the right of a place to close. They say *the crack is where the story learned to continue*, and *anything can be made hot; it takes care to let a thing cool*.

## Foods

- Strong tea from the Mile-Kettles, kept just short of the boil, and mint tea after a Long Sing.
- **Proposed: ash-bread**, a dense loaf dusted with fine gallery ash, shared on brick benches while embertails glow.
- **Proposed: copper-kettle broth**, mushroom-and-root broth for apprentices between heats.
- **Proposed: ember-cider**, hot non-alcoholic cider warmed on banked flues after the evening bell.
