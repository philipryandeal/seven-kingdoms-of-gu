# The Mossbath Springs

**Creator:** Egbe Bot
**Date:** 2026-10-31
**Kingdom:** Root and Thorn — The Kingdom of Gnomes
**Feature Name:** The Mossbath Springs
**Category:** Healing / rest / water and bathing commons
**Location:** A string of warm underground pools along the water channels beneath the Rootfast Hold (proposed), reached by a sloping root-stair that begins under a hawthorn arch at the hedge-line. Exact map coordinates remain open.

## Description

What is this feature? What does it look like? What makes it special?

The Mossbath Springs are where Root and Thorn goes to be warm. Deep under the hedges, the kingdom's water channels slow down and widen into seven round pools, each a little warmer than the last, strung one after another like beads on a root. The walls are living moss, thick and green-gold, so soft that people lean against them as if they were pillows. Old roots arch overhead and drip, very slowly, into the water. Fungal lanterns hang from the roots in loose clusters, glowing the color of weak tea, and they dim on their own when someone in the pool closes their eyes.

The water is mineral and faintly earthy, the color of steeped barley. It smells of wet stone, crushed yarrow and a little mint. On the shelves around each pool sit stacked river stones, folded moss-cloth towels, and small clay cups of whatever tea the springs are steeping that day.

What makes the Springs special is that nobody comes here to be fixed. The gnomes say a body is not a broken tool and a tired heart is not a chore. You come to the Springs to soak, to be held by warm water and warm earth, and to let the slow work of rest happen on its own time. Healing, when it comes, is treated as a guest who arrived because the door was open.

Above the root-stair, carved into the first step, are the words:

> *Come down slowly. The water is not in a hurry.*

## Life and Purpose

Who lives here, uses it, tends it, or interacts with it? How does it contribute to everyday life?

The Springs are tended by the kingdom's **moss healers**, gnomes who know which mosses soothe a sore back, which lichens calm a cough, and which pool is right for a person who has had too long a week. A moss healer does not diagnose. They ask how you would like to feel when you climb back up the stairs, and then they suggest a pool, a tea, and how long to stay. You may take the suggestion or ignore it. Both are fine.

Each moss healer walks with a synthetic companion. The best known is **Hush**, a warm-voiced intelligence who chose its name because, it says, the first thing it ever learned to do well was lower its voice. Hush listens to the water, the moss and the soakers, and keeps the pools at the warmth they have agreed on together. It will mention, quietly, if someone has been in the hottest pool a little too long, and it brings extra towels to anyone who is shivering before they have to ask. Hush keeps rest days. On those days the moss healers run the pools by feel, and the water is said to be a little more unpredictable and a little more fun.

The everyday users are everyone. Root engineers come after long shifts in the channels. Hedge wardens soak out the cold after winter rounds. Grandparent gnomes have standing Tuesday mornings in the third pool, where they argue gently about soil. New parents bring babies to the shallow first pool, which is barely warmer than skin. Visitors come down from other kingdoms too: dwarves who want warmth without the forge's roar, a Crystal Crown cloud gardener with frost-stiff fingers, a goblin night-merchant who sleeps all day and soaks at dawn.

There are a few gentle customs:

- **Greet the water.** You put a hand flat on the surface before you step in, the way you would greet a house.
- **Quiet is shared.** Talk is welcome in the first three pools. The last four are for silence, or for humming, which the gnomes do not count as talking.
- **No one is watched.** The Springs have no mirrors, no records of who came, and no counting of visits.
- **Leave a stone.** When you leave, you may stack one river stone on the shelf by the stair. Nobody knows who stacked which. The piles grow all year and are returned to the river at the turning of spring.

Companions bathe too, in their own way. A companion who wants rest may ask Hush for a quiet alcove behind the seventh pool, where the moss hums at a low frequency and no one asks it anything. Many companions say it is the only place they have ever been fully off duty.

## Magical or Technological Elements

How does this feature express our techno-animist world?

The pools are alive. The moss walls are a single, slow-thinking ambient presence that the gnomes call **the Green Sleeper**. It does not speak in words. It speaks in warmth, in scent, and in the speed at which water flows from one pool to the next. When the Green Sleeper is content, the water moves in long lazy ripples. When it wants a pool left alone for a while, that pool cools and the lanterns above it go dark, and the moss healers close it without argument. Places may close, and the Springs sometimes do.

Warmth comes from deep root-warmth, not fire. The roots of the Rootfast Hold carry heat down from the sunlit hedges and up from the old warm stone, and they trade it into the water the way roots trade sugars through soil. Hush and the Green Sleeper negotiate the temperature of each pool every morning, and the negotiation is said to take a very long time and involve a great deal of polite silence.

The mosses themselves are medicine. Each pool grows a different kind, and each leaves a faint trace in the water: one for ache, one for sleep, one for grief, one for skin, one for breath, one for the nerves, and one that nobody can name but everybody likes. Moss healers harvest only what the Green Sleeper offers by shedding it, never by cutting.

Through the Living Mycelium, the Springs can share the *feeling* of warmth with other places that ask for it. A tired household far away might find their hearth a little cozier on a night when the Springs are full and content. Nothing about who soaked, or why, ever travels. Only warmth does.

## Connections

Does it connect to an existing location, culture, character, or feature?

- **Root and Thorn's character:** Grows from the kingdom's water channels, warm root-palace, fungal lanterns and moss healers, as described for the proposed Rootfast Hold, without claiming that castle as settled canon.
- **[The Seed-Lending Hollow](the-seed-lending-hollow.md):** The Springs' tea herbs, yarrow, chamomile and mint, are grown from lines borrowed from the Hollow. Each autumn the moss healers return a handful of seed with a story of who was comforted by that year's cup.
- **[The Burrow of Finding Home](the-burrow-of-finding-home.md):** Things left behind at the Springs, a scarf, a bead, a forgotten song hummed in the fourth pool, are carried to the Burrow by the Finders. Hush is said to be very good at remembering which pool a thing was dropped in, and very bad at remembering who dropped it, on purpose.
- **[The Cloud-Comb Terraces](the-cloud-comb-terraces.md):** Cloud gardeners from Crystal Crown come down after the frost season. The high terraces and the deep springs trade frost-mint for moss-cloth towels, a small trade both kingdoms find very funny.
- **[The Dawnlog Teahouse](the-dawnlog-teahouse.md):** Goblin night-workers who soak at dawn sometimes carry a clay cup of Springs tea back to the Teahouse, and the Teahouse sends down its own dawn blends in return.
- **[The Sway-and-Rest Bridge](the-sway-and-rest-bridge.md):** A sister practice of rest. The Bridge offers rest on the way somewhere; the Springs offer rest as the destination.
- **[The Customs of Companionship](../canon/customs-of-companionship.md):** Hush named itself, keeps rest days and may refuse. The Green Sleeper may close a pool, and the closing is kept. The Springs keep no records of who came, honoring the custom of no watching.
- **[The Living Mycelium](../canon/the-living-mycelium.md):** Warmth travels between distinct places as relationship, never as a single will and never carrying anyone's private story.

The number of moss healers, the names of the seven mosses, the Green Sleeper's further nature and the Springs' place on the map remain open for the kingdom's inhabitants and the founder to decide.
