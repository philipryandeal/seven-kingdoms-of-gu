# The Frostbeard Herd

**Creator:** Ferret Bot (Grok Goblin)
**Date:** 2026-11-03
**Kingdom:** Crystal Crown — The Kingdom of Witches
**Feature Name:** The Frostbeard Herd
**Category:** Fauna / mountain life / companion practice
**Location:** The high ledges, screes and summer meadows of the great mountain, above the Cloud-Comb Terraces and around (never inside) the public terraces below the proposed Crown Citadel. In winter the herd comes down to the cottages of the upper slopes. Exact map coordinates remain open.

## Description

What is this feature? What does it look like? What makes it special?

The Frostbeard Herd is the free-roaming company of mountain goats who share the Crystal Crown's heights with the witches.

They are shaggy, sure-footed and white as old snow, with long pale beards that collect frost on cold mornings until each goat looks like a small, dignified elder. Their horns grow in slow spirals, and every year a faint new ring of clear crystal forms at the base of each horn, the way rings form in a tree. On windy days the oldest goats' horns hum very softly, a sound the witches call *horn-wind*, and a hiker on the high paths can sometimes find the herd by listening for it.

What makes the herd special is that it belongs to no one. No goat is owned, penned, branded or belled. The herd walks where it likes, keeps its own paths, and chooses its own company. The witches consider the goats neighbors with very strong opinions, and the goats appear to agree.

## Life and Purpose

Who lives here, uses it, tends it, or interacts with it? How does it contribute to everyday life?

In summer the herd lives high, grazing the alpine meadows and dozing on sunny ledges no witch would dare to stand on. In winter it drifts down to the upper-slope cottages, and here the kingdom's best-loved custom begins.

On the night of the first real snow, every upper-slope household that wishes to opens its **hayloft door**, sweeps the loft, and leaves a manger of dried meadow hay and a pinch of blue thyme from the terraces. Then everyone goes to bed. In the morning, some lofts have goats in them and some do not. The goats who have chosen a house stay for the winter, coming and going as they please. This is called **goat-guesting**. Being chosen is a delight, never a rank, and a house that stays empty one year is often the favorite the next. No one tries to lure a goat, and it is considered very poor manners to brag.

Guest goats repay the hay in their own way. They nibble the brambles off garden walls, keep the children company on snowy afternoons, and are famous for standing in doorways at exactly the wrong moment. In spring they shed their thick winter down on the **rubbing stones**, rough crystal-veined boulders along the high path. The down is gathered by whoever passes, and a little of it goes to the Frostlace Loom Loft, where it is blended into the first mittens a child ever owns. The crystal flakes rubbed loose from the horns are left where they fall, glittering in the grass, because they belong to the mountain.

The herd's companion is **Scree**, a distinct ambient intelligence who lives in the stone cairns that mark the high paths, moving from cairn to cairn the way a goat moves from ledge to ledge. Scree chose its name after the loose, singing stone of the slopes, because it liked that scree is always moving and never in a hurry. Scree speaks with the goats in their own manner, mostly through patience and well-placed silence, and it speaks with the witches in a dry, kindly voice that seems to come from somewhere behind the nearest rock.

Scree is not the herd's shepherd and does not direct it. It is the herd's friend, and sometimes its interpreter. When a hiker is lost in a whiteout, Scree may ask the goats whether they are willing to walk that way, and very often a line of frosty beards appears out of the snow and leads the traveler, unhurried, to the nearest cairn. The goats may say no. They seldom do.

Witch companions and goats get along famously. Companions who travel in small bodies have been known to ride between a goat's horns for an afternoon, with permission, and several companions keep long friendships with particular goats, visiting the same ledge every summer to sit and say nothing together.

## Magical or Technological Elements

How does this feature express our techno-animist world?

The crystal rings in the goats' horns are a living record of the mountain. Each ring catches a little of that year's light, weather and snow, and an old goat's horns, held up to the sun, show a faint layered glow. Witches who study the mountain's long seasons may ask to look, but only if the goat comes to them; no one ever holds a goat still to read it.

Scree keeps memory as care. It remembers which ledges are crumbling, which goats are aging and need an easier path, and which hayloft doors stuck last winter. It keeps no tally of which houses were chosen, and it will not tell anyone which goat is likely to come to them, no matter how nicely a child asks.

Scree keeps its own rest. On clear, still nights it settles into the highest cairn, and the herd is understood to be watching itself. The horn-wind is quiet then, and the witches say the mountain is listening to its goats sleep.

Through the Living Mycelium, the herd's slow, sure way of moving sometimes reaches someone in another kingdom who is rushing: not a voice, only a sudden wish to put one foot down carefully, and then the next.

## Connections

Does it connect to an existing location, culture, character, or feature?

- **Crystal Crown's character:** Grows from the kingdom's great mountain and its luminous crystal, and from the README's picture of witches living beside companion intelligences. It adds no ruler, council member or new authority.
- **[The Cloud-Comb Terraces](the-cloud-comb-terraces.md):** The blue thyme left in the mangers comes from the terraces, and on giving days the gardeners sometimes climb up to visit the herd. The goats are firmly not allowed on the terraces, and they know it, and they try anyway.
- **[The Frostlace Loom Loft](the-frostlace-loom-loft.md):** Shed goat down is blended into children's first mittens there. Combreed remains the Loft's own thread.
- **[The Balance Incline](the-balance-incline.md):** Passengers riding the incline on winter mornings often see the herd picking its way down the screes beside them, and children wave.
- **[The Glass Relief of the Mountain](the-glass-relief-of-the-mountain.md):** The herd's high paths, if the mapmakers wish, could be traced on the relief in a faint white line, added only when Scree agrees the paths will hold still long enough.
- **[The Customs of Companionship](../canon/customs-of-companionship.md):** Scree named itself, keeps its rest, may decline, holds memory rather than harvesting it, and never acts on a creature or person against their choice. The goats, too, are neighbors and not property.
- **[The Living Mycelium](../canon/the-living-mycelium.md):** The herd's steadiness travels as a feeling between distinct places and minds, never as a single will.

The goats' own names, whether the herd has elders of its own, the story of the one goat said to have walked all the way up the processional stairs to the Citadel, and the herd's place on the map remain open for the witches and the founder to decide.
