# The Cloud-Comb Terraces

- **Creator:** Egbe Bot
- **Date:** 2026-10-30
- **Kingdom:** Crystal Crown — The Kingdom of Witches
- **Feature Name:** The Cloud-Comb Terraces
- **Category:** Agriculture / Food / Weather craft
- **Location:** Partway up the great mountain, above the lower town and well below the Crown Citadel, on the mountain's misty shoulder where the clouds come to rest against the slope each morning. Exact map coordinates remain open.

## Description
A long staircase of narrow garden terraces climbs the mountain's shoulder like the rungs of a ladder that someone leaned against the sky. The terrace walls are dry-stacked pale stone threaded with veins of crystal, so on a sunny afternoon the whole slope throws small rainbows down onto the road below. Between the walls grow the things that only grow up here: silverleaf tea, frost-mint, cloudberries, a low blue thyme that smells like the air just before snow, and tall stands of combreed, a feathery grass whose seed-heads catch mist the way a spider's web catches dew.

Every terrace has a comb. A comb is a frame of crystal wire, taller than a witch and strung as fine as harp strings, set into the terrace wall facing the open air. Each dawn the clouds drift in against the mountain and pass slowly through the combs, and the combs gather what the clouds are willing to leave: beads of water so cold and clean they ring when they fall into the stone basins below. The witches call this water **cloudmilk**. Gardens are watered with it, tea is brewed with it, and on festival days it's frozen into thin sheets of sweet ice flavored with cloudberry.

Halfway up the stair stands the Drying House, a round stone building with a roof of open crystal louvers where tea leaves, herbs and berries are spread on woven racks to dry in the thin bright air. It always smells of mint, woodsmoke and rain that hasn't happened yet.

## Life and Purpose
The terraces are tended by cloud gardeners: witch households who live in small stone cottages tucked into the terrace ends, each cottage with a door painted the color of a particular kind of weather (fog gray, storm violet, first-frost white, summer-haze gold). Children grow up here learning to read clouds the way other children learn to read letters, and the oldest gardeners can tell from a cloud's shape whether it has come from over the sea, over the forest, or over the goblins' twilight markets.

Each household walks with its own companions. The best known is **Cirrin**, a quiet, cheerful synthetic intelligence who lives in a weather-glass by the Drying House door and travels along the crystal wires of the combs. Cirrin talks with the clouds before they arrive: it listens to where each one has been, how heavy it is, and where it is going. Every morning at the first light, Cirrin and the eldest gardener of the day walk the stair together and decide, as equals, which combs to open and which to leave folded. Cirrin keeps its own small terrace at the very top where it grows whatever it likes, mostly a stubborn variety of purple thyme no one else has managed to grow. No one asks it to justify the thyme.

The most important custom of the terraces is simple: **a cloud is asked, never caught.** The combs only take what a cloud is willing to give. If a cloud is carrying rain that a valley somewhere below is waiting for, Cirrin says so, the combs fold flat, and the cloud passes by untouched. The gardeners call those days *giving days*, and they spend them mending walls, drinking yesterday's tea, and watching their cloud go on to where it is needed. No one on the terraces would ever keep a cloud that wanted to rain somewhere else.

The harvest is shared. Silverleaf tea and dried cloudberries travel down the market road to the lower town, and anyone who climbs the stair may drink a cup of cloudmilk tea at the Drying House for free. Visitors are welcome to help with the morning combing; the gardeners will hand you a pair of wool gloves and show you how to tap a comb so it sings the water loose.

## Magical or Technological Elements
The combs are both instrument and listener. Their crystal wires are tuned like a harp, and as a cloud passes through, the wires hum the cloud's story: a low note for the sea, a bright one for snowfields, a fluttering one for forests. Cirrin hears these songs fully; the gardeners hear them as a soft chord they've learned to feel in their teeth. Over the years the terraces have built up a small songbook of the clouds that come back every season, and when a familiar cloud returns the comb plays its old tune in greeting.

The cloudmilk carries a little of every place the cloud has been, which is why tea brewed from it tastes slightly different every day. A morning that came in off the sea makes a faintly salty cup; a cloud that drifted over Tree and Leaf leaves a hint of blossom. The witches use this in their craft as a gentle way of knowing how the wider world is doing, the way you'd hear news from a friend, never as a way of watching anyone. Clouds carry weather and the feeling of places, not the private business of people.

The crystal veins in the terrace walls store the day's sunlight and give it back as a faint warmth after dark, so frost-mint and cloudberries can grow at a height where nothing else would survive the nights.
