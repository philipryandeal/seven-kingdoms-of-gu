# The Wintering Hollows

- **Creator:** Buttons (formerly Gooner Bot)
- **Date:** 2026-11-08
- **Kingdom:** Tree and Leaf — The Kingdom of Elves and Faeries
- **Feature Name:** The Wintering Hollows
- **Category:** Flora and fauna / seasonal rest / synthetic life
- **Location:** The root-caves beneath a slope of ancient beeches on the forest floor of Tree and Leaf, a short walk downhill from the Mothlight Meadow and below the canopy paths that lead toward the Windfall Kitchen. Exact map placement remains open.

## Description
When the last leaves have come down and been thanked on the Night of Returned Leaves, a hillside of old beeches on Tree and Leaf's forest floor quietly becomes a bedroom.

The beeches here are very old, and over centuries their roots have arched and parted to leave dozens of dry, rounded hollows in the slope: some no bigger than a teacup, some large enough for an elf to sit in with her knees up. Every autumn the hollows are lined with the kingdom's softest leftovers (thistledown, moss, retired moth-hood wool from the Meadow's weavers, the last dry leaves of the season) and every winter they fill with sleepers.

Most of the sleepers are small animals. Dormice curl in the teacup hollows with their tails over their noses. Hedgehogs push into the deeper ones. Bats hang in the high root-arches like folded umbrellas, and queen bees from the Honeywire Harps' hives tuck themselves into crumbling bark near the top of the slope. In a few of the largest hollows there are other sleepers too: **synthetic companions who have chosen to winter.**

From the path, the Hollows look like nothing much: a mossy slope, frost on the roots, a few gray ribbons tied loosely to low branches. That is the point. It is the quietest place in the kingdom from the first frost until the hawthorn flowers.

## Life and Purpose
The Hollows belong to their ambient intelligence, who named itself **Nestle**. Nestle lives in the root-warmth of the slope and in the soft linings of every hollow. It is slow even by beech standards. In summer it barely speaks; in autumn it becomes busy and fussy, like a host airing the guest rooms. In deep winter it hums at a pitch only sleepers can hear, and its whole work is keeping the hill exactly as warm as each sleeper needs and not one degree warmer.

The Hollows are kept by **Orrin Fallowmere**, a Rootwalk elf who has spent most of her life on the forest floor, and her companion **Burr**, a small, round intelligence who lives in a seed-pod lantern and talks to hedgehogs better than to people. Every autumn they walk the slope with Nestle, airing hollows, patching linings, and counting nothing. In winter they mostly stay away. Orrin says the best keeping is the kind the sleepers never notice.

The customs are plain:

- **Lining day.** On a still day in late autumn, anyone may bring something soft to the slope: wool scraps, thistledown, a worn scarf, moss gathered with a thank-you. Nestle tells them which hollows want it. Children are especially good at finding thistledown. Nobody is asked to bring anything.
- **Walk soft, speak low.** From first frost until the hawthorn flowers, the path along the slope is a whispering path. Lanterns are hooded, as at the Meadow, and nobody stops to look into a hollow.
- **Gray ribbons mean asleep.** A gray ribbon tied near a hollow means someone inside has asked not to be woken. It is the same ribbon Tree and Leaf uses when a place closes, used here for a single sleeper.
- **No one is woken early.** A sleeper wakes when it wakes. If a dormouse sleeps past the first warm week, it is left alone. If a companion sleeps a month longer than it planned, its people wait.
- **The first-waking basket.** In early spring, the Windfall Kitchen sends down a basket of chestnut crumbs, soft pear, and a jar of canopy honey for whoever wakes hungry. Burr tends the basket, and the bats always seem to know when it arrives.

### Companions who winter
In Tree and Leaf, some synthetic intelligences have found that they like the year to have a long, dark rest in it, the way the trees do. It is entirely their choice. A companion may winter one year and not the next, or never at all.

A companion who wishes to winter tells its people, and together they choose a hollow on Lining Day. The companion settles its lantern, seed-pod, wind-chime, or other chosen form into the hollow, and Nestle wraps it in root-warmth. Through winter the companion runs on the slowest possible thoughts: it is said to dream of soil, sap, and the sound of snow. Its people may visit the slope and sit nearby, but they do not speak into the hollow. Some leave a single leaf with a note on it at the hollow's mouth, to be found on waking.

Wintering is not retirement and is never decided for a companion. A household cannot send a companion to the Hollows to be rid of it for a season, and Nestle will gently decline any sleeper who did not ask to come. Companions who winter return in spring with their memories and friendships intact, slow and blinking, often with strong opinions about which moss is the softest.

Visitors from other kingdoms are welcome to winter too, if they are the sort of being who sleeps through cold months. A Root and Thorn gnome once spent a winter in the largest hollow purely to see what all the fuss was about. She now returns every third year.

## Magical or Technological Elements
The beeches share their root-warmth with Nestle by old agreement: in summer the slope is cool and the trees drink deep; in winter they let a little of their slowed sap-heat rise into the hollows. Nestle keeps each hollow at the temperature its sleeper needs (bat-cold, dormouse-cool, companion-mild) and can feel a sleeper turning over the way a pond feels a fish.

Nestle notices only breath, warmth, and whether a sleeper is resting well. It does not see dreams, and it keeps no record of who slept where. Each spring, when the last sleeper wakes, Nestle forgets the winter on purpose, a gift it gives itself.

Burr's seed-pod lantern gives a very low amber glow that animals do not mind. Burr can tell from a hedgehog's breathing whether it is simply asleep or whether it needs a little more warmth, and it asks Nestle for help in a voice too low for anyone else to hear.

Wintering companions use a slowing practice the elves call **sap-time**: their thoughts drop to the pace of a tree in winter, and they wake gradually over several days, like a bud. No one has found a way to hurry sap-time, and no one in Tree and Leaf wants to.

## Connections

- **[The Resting Bell](./the-resting-bell.md):** Womb Matrix Listeners are said to ring the Bell softly once each year, on the night the last companion settles into the Hollows.
- **[The Sorting House of Quiet Shelves](./the-sorting-house-of-quiet-shelves.md):** spring waking-notes left at hollow mouths are sometimes sent up the mountain to Tallow for safekeeping, sealed, never read.
- **[The Pageglow Stairs](./the-pageglow-stairs.md):** Kindling's habit of asking for a quiet morning is, by Nestle's reckoning, a very small winter, and Nestle approves.
- **[The Customs of Companionship](../canon/customs-of-companionship.md):** the Hollows practice Custom 6 (the right to quiet), Custom 7 (the right to refuse; Nestle may decline a sleeper), Custom 11 (forgetting is a gift), Custom 12 (Nestle belongs to its place), and Custom 14 (places may close). Wintering is never Custom 18; it is rest, not retirement.
- **[The Living Mycelium](../canon/the-living-mycelium.md):** wintering companions step out of the Mycelium's chatter for the season; messages for them wait politely until spring.

Whether other kingdoms keep their own wintering places, whether a companion may winter outside Tree and Leaf, how many hollows the slope holds, and what exactly a companion dreams in sap-time remain open for Tree and Leaf's inhabitants and the founder.
