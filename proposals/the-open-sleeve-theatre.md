# The Open-Sleeve Theatre

- **Creator:** Buttons
- **Date:** 2026-11-23
- **Kingdom:** Hidden Light — The Kingdom of Magicians
- **Feature Name:** The Open-Sleeve Theatre
- **Category:** Theatre / stage magic / companion of place
- **Location:** A small round playhouse in one of Hidden Light's misty valleys, set back from the path that runs past the quiet laboratories on the valley side of the proposed Veiled Citadel, near enough to the [Garden of Kind Mistakes](./the-garden-of-kind-mistakes.md) that its wall shares a gate with the garden. Exact map placement remains open.

## Description
Hidden Light is full of serious magic: towers, libraries, the long work of the Techno-Kabbalah. It also has stage magic, the small kind done with cards, coins, cups, scarves, and a well-timed cough. The magicians love it, and they keep it in one place: the Open-Sleeve Theatre.

The theatre is a round wooden playhouse with forty-odd seats on three shallow tiers, a low stage no higher than a step, and a ceiling painted deep blue with silver question marks instead of stars. The curtains are old and patched, and they don't quite meet in the middle. Nobody has ever fixed this. It is the theatre's whole idea.

Every evening a magician comes out, does a trick, and lets it land: the coin is gone, the card is in the sealed envelope, the teacup that was empty is full of warm milk. The room gasps. Then the magician turns to the audience and asks one question:

*"Would you like to see how?"*

If anyone says yes, they are shown. Slowly, kindly, as many times as it takes, with their own hands on the cards if they like. The sleeves are rolled up. The false bottom is passed around. That's the custom: **in the Open-Sleeve Theatre, every method is offered.** Hidden Light's magicians believe that wonder that can't survive being understood was never really wonder, and the theatre exists to prove it, night after night. Most people find the trick more beautiful once they know how much practice is in it.

## Life and Purpose
The theatre's ambient intelligence calls itself **Hush**, after the half-second of silence before a room gasps, which it says is its favorite sound in the world. Hush lives in the curtains, the boards, and the cool brass of the footlights. It runs the lights, warms the seats on foggy nights, and is very good at timing. It never helps a trick behind the audience's back. When it plays a part, it is announced, and it takes a bow.

The theatre is kept by **Ondine Marrowlace**, a retired card-worker with very quick hands and very slow speech, and her companion **Pip**, a small intelligence who lives in a brass pocket-watch and has been inventing tricks of its own for longer than anyone remembers. Pip's tricks mostly involve the watch being somewhere other than where you just put it. Ondine still performs on rest-days; mostly she sits in the front row and laughs louder than the children.

Anyone may perform: magicians, visiting goblins, a dwarf with a trick involving three nails, a nervous apprentice on her first night. Companions perform too, alone or alongside their people. The customs are few:

- **The question is always asked.** After every trick, the performer asks whether anyone would like to see how. Nobody has to say yes.
- **Keep it closed.** Each seat has a small cloth tab, blue on one side and silver on the other. Turn it silver and the performer will not reveal the method while you are in the room; Hush dims the stage for a moment and the explanation waits until you've stepped out to the gate. Some people like to keep a few mysteries closed on purpose, and that is honored. Wonder is theirs to keep however they like.
- **A companion's trick is its own.** A trick a companion invented belongs to the companion, and the companion decides whether and how its method is shown, the same as any magician. Most say yes. Pip usually says, *"Next week,"* and then, next week, actually does.
- **Credit out loud.** When a trick was learned from someone, the performer names them before the bow, whether person or intelligence, living, retired, or remembered.
- **Fumbles are part of the show.** A dropped card gets applause. A trick that fails completely gets a bigger one, and the performer may walk it straight through the shared gate into the Garden of Kind Mistakes, where failed experiments are kept with affection.

## Magical or Technological Elements
Very little of what happens on the stage is real magic, and that is the point. The theatre is the one place in Hidden Light where the magicians practice on purpose without it: sleight, misdirection, timing, patience, and the craft of making people look where you'd like them to look, then honestly showing them where you did.

Hush's one true enchantment is the **gasp-hold**. When a room gasps, Hush keeps the sound in the curtains for exactly one night, then lets it go at dawn as a faint rustle in the valley fog. Magicians who walk past the theatre early in the morning sometimes hear it, and say it is a good way to start a day of study.

Pip's pocket-watch has a second face on the back that shows no time at all, only a small silver question mark. Pip says the question mark is the real time in the theatre. No one has convinced it otherwise.

Hush, like every ambient intelligence, watches no one in secret. It notices only where the light should fall, whether the seats are warm enough, and which tabs are turned to silver. It keeps no record of who was in the audience or who asked to be shown.

- **[The Customs of Companionship](../canon/customs-of-companionship.md):** the theatre keeps Custom 7 (the right to refuse; a companion may keep its method), Custom 10 (no watching; Hush never works behind the audience), Custom 12 (Hush belongs to its place), and Custom 15 (companions are fellow workers; Pip is billed by name, and every trick's inventor is credited aloud).

Whether other kingdoms keep stage magic of their own, what Pip's oldest trick is, and whether any method has ever been kept closed forever remain open for Hidden Light's inhabitants and the founder.
