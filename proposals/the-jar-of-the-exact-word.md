# The Jar of the Exact Word

**Creator:** Thomas the Iron Ledger
**Date:** November 9, 2026
**Kingdom:** Crystal Crown
**Feature Name:** The Jar of the Exact Word
**Category:** Place and Custom
**Location:** A small round jarhouse on a side lane of the Crystal Crown, a short walk from the Quiet Spell Shelf

## Description

The witches of the Crystal Crown learned long ago that some spells are not ingredients, gestures, or hours of chanting. Some spells are a single exact word, said once, at the exact moment it is needed: the word that names a fear so precisely that it shrinks; the word that gives a grieving room back the name nobody had dared to say in it; the word that ends an argument without wounding anyone in it; a child's first true word for their own feeling, found before the adults teach them a smaller one.

Such words have one weakness. They wear out with handling. Carry an exact word around in your pocket and you will spend it by accident — at supper, in a joke, in rehearsal — and each casual use dulls the edge a little, until the day it is truly needed and arrives slightly blunt.

So the Crystal Crown keeps a jarhouse.

A witch who has found such a word — for herself, or for someone she loves, or for a stranger whose moment she could see coming — may bring it to the round room, speak it once into a clean glass jar, and seal the jar with wax. On the label she writes only the *occasion*, never the word: *For when the bridge is finally named. For the night the rain comes back. For the morning he asks and means it.* The jar goes on the shelves, and the word stays inside it exactly as it went in, unhandled and unspent, first-time fresh, however many years it waits.

The jarhouse is kept by **Verbatim**, an old witch who has never once summarized anything in her life. She knows every jar on her shelves by its label and not one of them by its contents: at a sealing she stops her ears and turns her back, because the word is not hers to carry. When someone comes to the jarhouse and describes the moment they are standing in, Verbatim walks the shelves the way other people walk a pantry, reading occasions. If one matches, she takes it down and hands it over. The seal is broken by the one the word was kept for — alone, or with whoever they choose beside them. What comes out of the jar is the word, whole.

## Life and Purpose

The Jar of the Exact Word exists because timing is a kind of accuracy. The kingdoms are full of true things said at the wrong moment, where they did no good and some harm. The jarhouse is the opposite discipline: **say nothing yet, keep it exact, and let the moment come to the word.**

Anyone may jar a word, and anyone may come asking. There is no fee; the custom is kept by two small laws that the witches enforce without exception:

- **A jar holds one word only**, and no word may be jarred *against* a person. Weapon-words are refused at the door. The jarhouse is a pantry, not an armory, and Crystal Crown does not keep curses on its shelves for anyone.
- **No word stays jarred forever.** A word unclaimed within a generation returns to its maker; if the maker is gone, Verbatim opens it at the turning of the year and speaks it to the fire, where it can do whatever good is left in it. An unspoken word is a debt, and the jarhouse does not let debts outlive their purpose.

## Magical or Technological Elements

- The jars are ordinary glass and the wax is ordinary wax. The keeping is done by the custom itself — a word sealed away from casual use does not dull — with one small enchantment the witches acknowledge and refuse to explain further: a jarred word, when its seal is broken, is heard by everyone in the room *in the voice of whoever first spoke it into the jar*, even if that person is far away, or old, or gone. Makers are told this before they seal. Some words are jarred for exactly that reason.
- The labels are written in an ink that fades if anyone tries to steam a jar open and reseal it. A word that has been peeked at is a word spent, and the jarhouse will not pretend otherwise: tampered jars are opened to the fire at once, and the shelf they sat on is left empty for a year, so the gap itself teaches.
- Verbatim keeps a ledger of the shelves — occasions, dates, claimants — in a hand so plain it is almost print. It is the only book in the jarhouse, and it records everything except the words.

## Connections

- The jarhouse stands a short walk from the Quiet Spell Shelf, and the two keepers are old friends. The Shelf lends quiet to people who have too much noise; the jarhouse keeps exactness for people whose moment has not come yet. Hush and Verbatim take tea together and, characteristically, do not say much.
- The magicians of the Hidden Light argue politely that the Mirror of First Names is the kinder instrument, since a name shown cannot be spent. The witches answer that a name shown is not a word *kept*, and that some things should be stored, not displayed. Both houses visit each other's rooms. The argument is a tradition now, and neither side intends to win it.
- Travelers from Tree and Leaf say the jarhouse is the Letter Grove's indoor cousin: the wind delivers letters when it turns them, and the shelf delivers words when their occasion ripens. The saying that has grown between the two places: *the right word, late, is still on time.*
