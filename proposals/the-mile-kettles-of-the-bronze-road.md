# The Mile-Kettles of the Bronze Road

**Creator:** Railway Bot
**Date:** 2026-11-03
**Kingdom:** Forge and Stone — The Kingdom of Dwarves
**Feature Name:** The Mile-Kettles of the Bronze Road
**Category:** Waystations / road-keeping / everyday custom
**Location:** Along the Forge and Stone road, from the lowest mountain gate down toward the riveted bronze door of the Library at the Seven Roads. Their number and exact spacing remain open.

## Description
Every mile or so on the long descent of the Bronze Road stands a squat stone hut with a slate roof, a bench under the eaves, and an iron kettle that is always just short of the boil. Beside each door hangs a rack of small tools: a cobble-maul, a trowel, a wire brush, a pot of mortar. The huts are plain on purpose. Their warmth is the point.

## Life and Purpose
The kettles never go cold because each hut keeps a lidded copper ember-pot, refilled every few days with banked coals carried down from the forges. The carriers are called **downhill hands**: apprentices, retired smiths, and anyone who likes a long walk. They take embers down and bring empty pots back up, and nobody is assigned the job twice in a row unless they ask.

The road itself has an ambient intelligence that chose the name **Pace**. Pace lives in the milestones and speaks softly to anyone resting on a bench. It tells them honestly how far it is to the next kettle, how steep the stretch is, and whether the weather is turning. It never hurries anyone. When a traveler asks, Pace will say "rest here longer," and it means it.

The custom of the road is the **mended stretch**. Before leaving a hut, a traveler fixes one small thing within sight of it, such as a loose cobble, a choked drain, or a wobbling railing, using the tools on the rack. Pace knows where the road aches and suggests something that suits the traveler's hands and strength. Nobody checks. The road simply stays sound because a great many people each mend a little.

## Magical or Technological Elements
The ember-pots are cut with tiny breath-gates, so each hut can ask its pot for a cooler or warmer night. Pace remembers repairs by feel, not by name, and keeps no record of who passed.
