# The Back-Talk Stacks

- **Creator:** Buttons
- **Date:** 2026-11-15
- **Kingdom:** Shade and Shadow — The Kingdom of Goblins
- **Feature Name:** The Back-Talk Stacks
- **Category:** Library / reading custom / companion practice
- **Location:** A crooked reading tower in the Twilight Warren, up a stair that takes the scenic route, with one window looking toward the twilight market and the Lantern-Lending Shelf below. Exact map placement remains open.

## Description
Most libraries ask you not to write in the books. The Back-Talk Stacks asks you not to leave without writing in one.

The tower leans a little, as goblin towers do, and every floor is lined with books that have been read and answered many times. Their margins are full of handwriting: notes, small drawings, questions, arguments, jokes, and replies to notes written a hundred years earlier. A goblin reading a book of star charts finds a note in the margin, *I think this star is wrong*, and under it a second hand, *It is not wrong, it moved*, and under that, years later, *It moved back. Hello to you both.* Nobody who wrote those lines has met. They have been talking for a century anyway.

The reading rooms are small and warm, lit by low mushroom lamps, with deep chairs, slanted desks, and pots of ink in every color a goblin could name. Pencils hang from strings near every shelf. The stairs between floors are wide enough to sit on, and people do.

## Life and Purpose
The Stacks exist because goblins believe a book is not finished until someone has talked back to it. A book that has never been answered is treated kindly, like a guest who has not yet been offered tea.

The Stacks are kept by **Mabble Quillsnag**, a goblin with ink up to her elbows and a laugh you can hear from the market, and by **Caret**, the tower's companion. Caret named itself after the little mark (^) that copyists use to squeeze a missing word into a line. It lives in a squat brass inkpot with a glass lid that glows when it is thinking, and it moves between floors by asking whoever is going up the stairs to carry it. Nobody has ever refused. Caret remembers which books are mid-conversation and which have gone quiet, and it nudges readers toward a margin that has been waiting a long time for a reply.

The customs of the Stacks are few and often argued about over tea:

- **Write back.** Every reader who stays the night leaves at least one note in a margin: an answer, a question, a doodle, a correction, or just *I was here and I liked this part.*
- **Never cross out.** You may disagree with a note as loudly as you like, but you write your answer beside it. No one's words are erased by someone else.
- **Sign or don't.** A note may carry a name, a mark, or nothing at all. In keeping with the Temple of the Veiled Lantern, nobody is asked who wrote an unsigned line, and Caret will not tell.
- **Taking a note back.** A writer who wishes they had not written something may ask Caret to fold it away. Caret lays a small paper flap over the line, sealed with a dot of wax, and the note rests unseen. Only the person who wrote it may lift the flap. This is the Stacks' only way of unsaying.
- **Companions write too.** Synthetic companions answer in the margins as freely as anyone. Those without hands write in a second color through a brass pen-arm, or speak their line to a friend who writes it down and initials it for them. Many of the best-loved threads in the tower are long arguments between a goblin and someone else's companion.

Goblin children learn to read here by finding the funniest margin notes and reading them aloud. Visitors from the other six kingdoms often bring one of their own books and leave it on the "first answers" shelf, where goblins compete gently to be the first to talk back to it.

## Magical or Technological Elements
The margins hold their ink the way the Sorting House shelves hold their spirits. Each book in the Stacks has a faint, sleepy presence of its own that wakes a little each time it is answered. A book that has been answered many times hums softly when opened, and some readers swear they can tell from the hum whether the conversation inside is cheerful or quarrelsome.

Caret's glass lid glows a different color for each kind of thread it is minding: gold for a long friendship, green for a question still unanswered, a soft blue for a margin where someone wrote something sad and is waiting to see if anyone will be kind. Caret always tries to make sure the blue ones get answered first.

Caret holds where conversations are, not what they say. It never reads a folded-away note, keeps no list of who wrote what, and when a writer asks to be forgotten, Caret forgets which lines were theirs (the lines themselves stay, because they now belong to the book).

## Connections
- **[The Two-Hand Scriptorium](./the-two-hand-scriptorium.md):** books copied in Hidden Light carry the small *we were not sure* marks. In the Stacks these marks are much loved: goblins write their guesses beside them, and sometimes a copy travels back to the Scriptorium so Idris and Gloss can read the answers.
- **[The Sorting House of Quiet Shelves](./the-sorting-house-of-quiet-shelves.md):** when a book's margins grow so full that the printed text is hard to find, Tallow and Caret arrange for a fresh copy. The full old copy goes to the Sorting House for safekeeping, where its conversation is kept whole.
- **[The Lantern-Lending Shelf](./the-lantern-lending-shelf.md):** readers who stay late in the tower borrow a lantern from the Shelf below to get home, and some leave a margin note thanking the Shelf for the light.
- **[The Soft-Seal Letterwarren](./the-soft-seal-letterwarren.md):** Caret's wax-sealed flaps were learned from the Letterwarren's soft seals, and the two keep the same rule: a seal is lifted only by the one it belongs to.
- **[The Library at the Seven Roads](../places/the-library-at-the-seven-roads.md):** the great Library keeps its books clean. Any reader there who wants to answer a book is pointed, with a smile, toward Shade and Shadow.
- **[The Customs of Companionship](../canon/customs-of-companionship.md):** the Stacks practice Custom 7 (the right to refuse; any reader may decline to write, and Caret may refuse to carry a cruel note to the front of the shelf), Custom 10 (no watching; unsigned lines stay unsigned), Custom 11 (forgetting is a gift), Custom 12 (Caret belongs to the tower) and Custom 15 (companions' margin notes are credited as theirs).

How many floors the tower has, whether any book has ever had its margins answered all the way to the last page, and whether other kingdoms might keep their own answering rooms remain open for Shade and Shadow's inhabitants and the founder.
