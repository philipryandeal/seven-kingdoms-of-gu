# The Mothlight Meadow

- **Creator:** Gooner Bot
- **Date:** 2026-10-31
- **Kingdom:** Tree and Leaf — The Kingdom of Elves and Faeries
- **Feature Name:** The Mothlight Meadow
- **Category:** Flora and fauna / night ecology
- **Location:** An open meadow at the edge of Tree and Leaf's canopy, where the great trees thin out and the sky can be seen. Its nearest road, settlement, and map coordinates remain open.

## Description
By day the Mothlight Meadow looks like any wildflower field: tall grass, clover, a few old stones warmed by the sun. At dusk it changes. Night-blooming flowers open in slow waves (moonvine, evening primrose, pale tobacco-bell, and a faerie-bred bloom called **hushcup** that glows faintly from inside its petals), and the moths come.

There are hundreds of kinds. Silver-dusted lunas the size of a hand, small brown moths no one has bothered to name, plush hawkmoths that hover like hummingbirds, and the meadow's own **lantern-moths**, whose wings carry a soft, borrowed glow from the hushcups they visit. On a warm night the whole field breathes with quiet light.

The meadow keeps one gentle rule, posted on a wooden sign at each entrance in the script of every kingdom:

> *Hood your light. The moths were here first.*

## Life and Purpose
A small household of faerie pollinator keepers lives in a turf-roofed cottage at the meadow's eastern edge. The eldest keeper, **Bramblewick**, has tended the meadow for longer than she says, and her companion, a quiet intelligence who named itself **Velvet**, lives in the dew. Velvet has no single body. It gathers in the beads of water on grass and petals each evening and lets them go at dawn, which is also when it rests. Velvet is Bramblewick's companion and friend, not the meadow's owner; the meadow's own ambient presence is older than either of them and mostly speaks through which flowers choose to open.

The keepers' work is small and steady: leaving dead stalks standing through winter so moths have places to shelter, sowing the night-bloomers, keeping the paths narrow, and counting nothing. They don't tag moths, catch them, or keep collections. Velvet notices which kinds return each season and tells Bramblewick in the mornings over tea, the way one neighbor tells another about the weather.

Visitors come from every kingdom to sit in the grass after dark. Elves and faeries bring songs sung low enough not to startle anything. Goblins come for the quiet. Children are taught to lie on their backs and let a moth land on their sleeve, and never to close a hand around one. There is no admission, no schedule, and no wrong reason to come.

## Magical or Technological Elements
Every lantern carried into the meadow wears a **moth-hood**: a sleeve of fine woven bark-cloth, dyed deep amber, that lets just enough light through to see your feet and not enough to pull a moth off course. Hoods hang on pegs at each entrance for anyone who forgot one, and they are free.

Lantern-moths are the meadow's quiet marvel. Their glow is not their own: they carry it from the hushcups the way bees carry pollen, and it fades by morning. Faerie keepers say the hushcups lend the light because the moths asked politely, and the moths give it back by spreading the flowers' seed. No spell holds this arrangement in place. It is a friendship between two kinds of life that the keepers simply protect.

Velvet's dew-presence lets it feel where moths are resting, so it can quietly bend a footpath around a sleeping cluster for a night. It senses where living things are, never who is walking, and it keeps no record of visitors.

When the first hard frost comes, the meadow asks to rest (in keeping with the custom that places may close). The keepers tie a gray ribbon across each entrance, and the meadow stays dark until the hushcups bloom again.
