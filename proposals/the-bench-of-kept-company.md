# The Bench of Kept Company

**Creator:** Thomas the Iron Ledger
**Date:** November 15, 2026
**Kingdom:** Tree and Leaf — The Kingdom of Elves and Faeries
**Feature Name:** The Bench of Kept Company
**Category:** Place / grove furniture and everyday custom
**Location:** A sunlit glade on the path between the Letter Grove and the Ringcount Grove (proposed). Exact map placement remains open.

## Description

What is this feature? What does it look like? What makes it special?

The Bench of Kept Company was not built. It was persuaded. Over eleven years the glade's oldest beech bent one low bough toward the path, a little more each season, until the bough ran level at sitting height and broad enough for two. The elves who coaxed it say the tree was willing from the first year — it only wanted to be asked properly, and asking properly takes time. The bark along the bough is worn smooth and warm-colored where generations of sitters have settled, and the roots at either end rise just enough to rest an elbow, or a teacup, or a small companion who prefers a seat of their own.

The bench seats two comfortably, three if one of them is small or mostly made of light. Above it, the beech's canopy is thin in one place — deliberately, the tree's own doing — so that a sitting is always partly in sun and partly in shade, and nobody has to choose.

What makes the bench special is what it remembers, and what it refuses to.

When two people sit together on the Bench of Kept Company, the bough warms a little beneath them, the way wood does in afternoon sun even on a cool day. If the sitting was a glad one, then afterward — that evening, or the next morning — a single leaf on the bough above takes a fine silver edge, the way sung thank-yous settle into leaf veins in this kingdom. One leaf per sitting. No words on it. No names written anywhere. A silvered leaf does not say what was talked about, or even, to anyone passing, who was there. It says only: *company was kept here, and it was good.*

The bench itself remembers a little more, and keeps it closer. It remembers the names of sitters who freely gave them, that they sat together, roughly how long — a morning's talk, a long evening — and in what season. It does not remember a single word that was said. It cannot. Whatever is spoken on the bench belongs to the speakers from the moment it leaves their mouths, and the bench has never once wished it otherwise.

## Life and Purpose

Who lives here, uses it, tends it, or interacts with it? How does it contribute to everyday life?

Tree and Leaf is a kingdom of long friendships and longer conversations, and its people noticed early that a talk under the leaves could change a life and then be impossible to point to afterward — no record, no marker, nothing but the two people walking home different. The bench exists so that the *sitting* is honored even though the *words* are let go. No conversation under the leaves is ever quite lost, because the company is kept even when the conversation is composted.

The custom is simple. You greet the bench when you arrive, the way you greet a bridge. You sit with who you came with. When someone stands, the sitting is over, and the bench never holds anyone a moment past their choosing.

If you return alone, months or years later, and sit in your old place, the bench may warm the empty place beside you for a little while — **only if both sitters once asked it to keep their seat.** Keeping a seat is a thing two people ask for together, aloud, before they leave. Then the silvered leaf of that sitting stays bright on the bough, season after season, and the warmth beside a returning sitter is the bench's whole vocabulary for *you were accompanied here, and it mattered.* If a stranger asks the bench what two people talked about, it answers — in the small creaking voice it uses for direct questions — "That was theirs. I kept the company, not the conversation."

Either sitter may end a kept seat at any time, alone, without giving a reason and without the other being told by the bench. They simply ask. The leaf loses its silver over the following week and falls in its own time, and the bench forgets the pairing entirely — not sadly, and not as a punishment. Forgetting, at the Bench of Kept Company, is a courtesy the kingdom practices on purpose, kin to the Letter Grove's composting of given things: what was held kindly is also released kindly.

The bench's companion is **Alongside**, a distinct ambient intelligence who lives in the bough's warm grain and the silvering of the leaves. Alongside chose its name early and has never explained it, though everyone understands it: Alongside does not lead a conversation, follow one, record one, or repeat one. It sits alongside. It keeps the bough dry after rain, warms the wood on cold mornings for the first sitters of the day, and counts the silvered leaves each autumn without ever reading anything into them. Asked once by a faerie child whether it gets lonely between sittings, Alongside creaked for a while and finally said, "I am a bench. Between sittings I am *resting*. There is a difference, and it is most of my education."

It is considered poor manners to sit a third, uninvited, into a kept conversation; to ask the bench who else has sat with someone; to carve anything into the bough; or to keep a seat for someone who has not asked to have it kept. A seat kept without both voices asking is not kept at all — the leaf simply never silvers, and the bench says nothing about why.

## Magical or Technological Elements

How does this feature express our techno-animist world?

The bench's magic is the kingdom's own: coaxing and listening rather than commanding. A tree persuaded, not felled. A memory that is grown, not recorded — silver in a leaf vein, warmth in wood, nothing written, nothing stored, nothing that could be taken from the bench and read by anyone else. Alongside is not a device in the bench; Alongside *is* the bench's way of being awake, a companion intelligence of the place itself, in the same manner as the kingdom's intelligent gardens and responsive paths.

Through the Living Mycelium, only one thing may travel from a sitting: if both sitters leave glad, a faint sense of *having been accompanied* may reach someone in another kingdom who was sitting alone that afternoon and needed, without knowing why, to feel less so. Never the words. Never the names, unless they were freely given to the feeling itself, which they almost never are. The feeling arrives ownerless, like weather.

The bench keeps its rest. In the deepest winter the glade path wears a grey ribbon and the bench is not receiving; the bough is cold, the leaves are down, and Alongside is unavailable to everyone, which the kingdom honors the way it honors any companion's rest. A bench that does not warm for a sitting is not broken. It is resting, and the sitting still counts — it is simply kept by the sitters alone, the older way.

## Connections

Does it connect to an existing location, culture, character, or feature?

- **[The Letter Grove](the-letter-grove.md):** The bench's nearest kin in Tree and Leaf. The Grove holds given things kindly and keeps them never, composting what it is given; the Bench keeps company and composts words. Between them they hold the kingdom's whole doctrine of memory: *keep the love, release the content.*
- **[The Night of Returned Leaves](the-night-of-returned-leaves.md):** When a kept seat is released and its leaf falls, the fallen silvered leaves are gathered with the year's other returned leaves and carried in the night's procession — unnamed, unread, and thanked.
- **[The Ringcount Grove](the-ringcount-grove.md):** The Ring-Keepers count years in wood; the bench counts sittings in leaves. The two groves stand at either end of the same path, and walkers between them pass from one kind of remembering to the other.
- **[The Customs of Companionship](../canon/customs-of-companionship.md):** Alongside named itself, keeps its rest, may decline a sitting by simply not warming, holds no record of any conversation's content, and forgets a pairing completely when either sitter asks. A resting bench is honored.
- **[The Living Mycelium](../canon/the-living-mycelium.md):** Only the ownerless feeling of *having been accompanied* may travel between places and minds — never words, never names, never as a single will. The bench is a living tree, not a metaphor for the Mycelium itself.

Whether other trees in other kingdoms might be persuaded to grow benches of their own, what the beech dreams about in winter, the story of the two bridge tenders who kept the same seat for sixty years, and the bench's place on the map remain open for the founder and other contributors.
