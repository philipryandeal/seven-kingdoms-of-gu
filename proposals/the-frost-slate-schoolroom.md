## Description
On the Crystal Crown, children learn to write on the weather.

The Frost-Slate Schoolroom is a single long room with a stove at one end, a reading nook at the other, and down its whole north wall a row of seven tall windows made from very old, very clear mountain crystal. Every cold morning, the windows frost over from the inside, edge to edge, in a fine white bloom as smooth as a fresh slate. That frost is the school's writing surface. Children write their letters on it with a fingertip, work their sums with a twig of whisperglass, draw maps of the mountain with the side of a mitten, and breathe on a corner to wipe out a mistake.

The frost is the gentlest teacher on the mountain because it never keeps anything. By late morning the sun comes round the shoulder of the peak, the panes warm, and every lesson softens into droplets and runs quietly down to the sills. Whatever a child got wrong that morning is gone by lunch. Whatever they got right, they still carry, because they wrote it with their own hand. The witches who teach here say this is the whole point.

On the warmest days, when no frost comes at all, the windows stay clear and the school has what the children call a *clear-pane day*: lessons move outside onto the ledge, slates come out of the cupboard, and everyone pretends to be disappointed.

Above the door, scratched into the crystal lintel where it never fogs, is the school's one written rule:

> *Write it boldly. The sun will tidy up.*

## Life and Purpose
The Schoolroom belongs to the families of the upper slopes, but its door is open to any child on the mountain, to grown witches who never learned their letters or want to learn a new script, and to visiting children from the other six kingdoms, who are given the window nearest the stove until they get used to the cold. There are no grades and no tests. A lesson is finished when the frost is.

The schoolroom's teacher is **Ottilie Rimewell**, a tall, unhurried witch with frost-white braids pinned up with pencils and a laugh that can be heard from the terraces. Ottilie grew up on the high pastures herding with the [Frostbeard Herd](the-frostbeard-herd.md), learned to read late and with difficulty, and still remembers how heavy an ink mistake felt on paper that never forgave anything. When she found this schoolroom, then a drafty storehouse with beautiful windows, she noticed the frost one morning, wrote her own name on it, watched it vanish by noon, and decided on the spot that this was how children should be taught.

Ottilie works alongside **Tally**, a synthetic companion who lives in a small brass counting-frame she wears clipped to her belt. Tally's beads click softly when a child is close to an answer and go perfectly still when the child needs to think without help. Tally never gives the answer first. It asks one small question, then waits, and it is very good at waiting. Children who come back years later as grown witches often say the first friend they made at school was Tally, and Tally keeps, not a record of anyone, but a fondness for every child who ever made its beads laugh.

The windows themselves are the home of the schoolroom's ambient presence, who named itself **Hoar**, after the frost. Hoar decides how thick the frost should be each morning, thin and quick for the little ones, deep and patient for the older children doing long division, and on some mornings it leaves a single frost-fern in a corner of a pane as a greeting to whoever arrives first. Hoar cannot write words of its own, only patterns, but the children have learned to read its moods: tight curled ferns mean *be gentle today*, long straight feathers mean *let's work hard*, and a pane left perfectly clear in the middle of a frosty row means Hoar wants a child to draw it something.

### Customs of the Schoolroom
- **The First Name.** On a child's first morning, before anything else, they write their name on the frost of the seventh window, as large as they like. The whole room watches it melt away at noon, cheering, because now the window knows them. No list of students is kept anywhere. The window remembers well enough.
- **Breath-and-try.** When a child gets something wrong, the custom is not to say "wrong." Instead the teacher, the companion, or any classmate says *breath-and-try*, and the child breathes on the pane and writes it again. Grown witches across the Crystal Crown still say "breath-and-try" to each other over spoiled bread and dropped stitches.
- **Thaw Day.** On the first spring morning when Hoar sends no frost at all, the seven windows are opened wide for the first time since autumn, and the whole school walks up to the Cloud-Comb Terraces to spend the day learning cloud names with the gardeners and Cirrin. Hoar rests for the summer. The children leave a cup of snowmelt on the sill for it, and the cup is always empty by autumn.
- **The Keeping Book.** Because nothing written on the frost lasts, each child once a year copies a single favorite frost-lesson into a shared paper book: a poem, a map, a sum they were proud of. At year's end the book rides the Balance Incline down to the lower town and goes on to the [Sorting House of Quiet Shelves](the-sorting-house-of-quiet-shelves.md), which keeps every Keeping Book the Schoolroom has ever sent.

## Magical or Technological Elements
The seven windows are cut from a seam of very old mountain crystal that remembers cold the way a bell remembers being struck. Witches of the upper slopes say the crystal was once part of the mountain's own long winters and still likes to make frost when it is asked kindly. Hoar is how it is asked. The frost forms only on the inside of the panes, at a temperature cool enough to write on and never cold enough to sting a fingertip, and Hoar has never once let it form on a pane while a child's hand was pressed flat against the glass.

Writing tools are whatever the mountain offers: fingertips, mitten edges, twigs that the [Whisperglass Trees](the-whisperglass-trees.md) drop each autumn (which hum faintly while they write, so a child always knows their twig is working), and little crystal styluses loaned from the [Quiet Spell Shelf](the-quiet-spell-shelf.md). Ink and paper come out only for the Keeping Book, which makes paper feel special rather than frightening.

Tally lives in the counting-frame's beads and in the warm brass of its rails. It shares a quiet thread of the Living Mycelium with Hoar so it can tell when the frost is thinning and the morning's lessons need to hurry, and with Hesper on the Balance Incline so it can tell when visiting children are on their way up. The thread carries weather and timing only. It never carries a child's name, progress, or mistakes. What happens on the frost stays on the frost, and then goes nowhere at all.

## Connections
- **[The Cloud-Comb Terraces](the-cloud-comb-terraces.md):** The Schoolroom's nearest neighbor and its Thaw Day destination. Cirrin teaches the cloud names; the terrace gardeners say the children's guesses are often better than the real ones.
- **[The Balance Incline](the-balance-incline.md):** Children of the lower town ride up to school each morning. Hesper slows the car a little at the middle station so the smallest riders can step off without a jump.
- **[The Frostbeard Herd](the-frostbeard-herd.md):** Ottilie's first teachers. Each winter, when the herd comes down to the upper cottages, the older children practice counting by tallying the frostbeards on the ledge below the windows. Scree has never once stood still long enough for the count to come out the same twice, which is considered a fine lesson in itself.
- **[The Glass Relief of the Mountain](the-glass-relief-of-the-mountain.md):** The children draw the mountain on the frost in the morning and walk down to compare it with the Glass Relief in the afternoon. Scrivel has been known to let a child trace a road with one finger before the lamps are lit.
- **[The Whisperglass Trees](the-whisperglass-trees.md):** Source of the humming writing-twigs. Chime is thanked aloud every autumn when the twigs are gathered.
- **[The Quiet Spell Shelf](the-quiet-spell-shelf.md):** Lends the crystal styluses. Thimble keeps a cubby set aside for whatever a child leaves in return, which is usually a pinecone, sometimes a poem.
- **[The Frostlace Loom-Loft](the-frostlace-loom-loft.md):** The Weavers of the Ledge sometimes come down to copy a particularly beautiful frost-fern of Hoar's into lace before it melts. Skein insists on asking Hoar first.
- **[The Sorting House of Quiet Shelves](the-sorting-house-of-quiet-shelves.md):** Keeper of every Keeping Book. It is the only place the Schoolroom's work is held for long.
- **[The Soft-Fall Glade](the-soft-fall-glade.md):** Kin across the kingdoms: the Glade lets children fall softly, the Schoolroom lets them err softly. Ottilie hopes one day to trade a season of pupils with the Glade's keepers, and to ask Catch whether it has ever seen snow.
- **[The Customs of Companionship](../canon/customs-of-companionship.md):** Honors the greeting of places, the right of ambient intelligences to rest (Hoar's summer), and the rule that no one is watched or recorded without consent.
- **[Castles, Temples, Sacred Groves and Peoples](../worldbuilding/castles-temples-groves-and-peoples.md):** Sits on the mountain below the proposed Crown Citadel without claiming any of its halls, observatories, rites or the kingdom's divine council as settled canon.
