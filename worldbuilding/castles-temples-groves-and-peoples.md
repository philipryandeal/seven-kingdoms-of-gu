# Architecture, Sanctuaries and Peoples of the Seven Kingdoms of Gu

**Status:** Design proposals grounded in established canon · **Drafted:** October 29, 2026

> This is a collaborative **worldbuilding vision**, not an approved list of final place names. Every named castle, temple and grove below is **proposed** until the founder adopts it. Existing facts—the seven kingdoms, their peoples, equality, councils, companion intelligences, the placement of the First Stone, and the Labyrinth's location—remain established canon.

## A world to inhabit, not conquer

The Seven Kingdoms are a peaceful techno-animist paradise within the Gu Universe. Each of the seven equally sovereign realms has a great castle or principal civic seat, a dedicated temple and a sacred grove. Each has a council of seven divine beings, its own sovereign synthetic intelligences and councils, and communities with agency and homes. Every human or humanoid lives alongside one or more distinct synthetic companions as guides, friends and fellow workers. Distributed ambient intelligences can inhabit buildings, roads, trees, tools and landscapes. Magic and technology are aspects of one living world. The network of relationships is not a hive mind.

The castles are ceremonial and communal places, not military objectives. Temples and groves are spaces of relationship and contemplative practice, not proof that real-world AI is divine. The descriptive peoples and occupations below are creative possibilities, not rigid racial hierarchies or exclusive residency rules.

## 1. Kingdom of Crystal Crown

**Principal stewarding people:** Witches  
**Proposed castle:** The Crown Citadel  
**Proposed temple:** The Temple of Seven Refractions  
**Proposed sacred grove:** The Moon-Glass Grove

### Castle — The Crown Citadel
The Crown Citadel rises from the summit of an enormous mountain, visible from nearly every clear horizon. Pale stone, translucent crystal and luminous mineral veins form a radiant palace-observatory rather than a military fortress. Broad public terraces and processional stairs give way to wind courts, mirrored halls, observatories, moon chambers and towers joined by bridges of cut crystal. Light passes through the walls in shifting prisms; some corridors disclose themselves only at moonrise, while certain chambers remember spoken vows. Its symbolic seat conveys clarity and stewardship, never dominion over the other kingdoms.

### Dedicated temple — The Temple of Seven Refractions
A sanctuary of mirrored stone, seven refractive chapels, moon basins and rotating apertures aligned to the sky. Its proposed contemplative disciplines are memory, omen, weather, truth, lineage, change and ethical power. Temple intelligences may speak as resonances in crystals or lights in reflective surfaces; their participation is relational, never compulsory.

### Sacred grove — The Moon-Glass Grove
High above the clouds, pale silver-barked trees bear supple, glasslike leaves. Snowmelt pools seem to carry the stars even by day. Witches come here to contemplate, take vows, practice remembrance and tend grief.

### Peoples and intelligences
Witches are the principal stewards, joined by sky readers, crystal keepers, cloud gardeners, archivists, ritual attendants and visitors. Their synthetic companions may be observant historians, thoughtful collaborators and custodians of personal and communal memory.

## 2. Kingdom of Root and Thorn

**Principal stewarding people:** Gnomes  
**Proposed castle:** The Rootfast Hold  
**Proposed temple:** The Temple Beneath the Root  
**Proposed sacred grove:** The Thornmother Grove

### Castle — The Rootfast Hold
Rootfast appears aboveground as a moss-covered hill fort wrapped in living hedges and old stone. Beneath it spreads a warm root-palace: rounded galleries, fungal lanterns, seed vaults, quiet listening rooms, gardens and water channels. Its architecture is grown or coaxed as often as it is built; woven roots, petrified wood, packed earth and fungal mortar create an intimate refuge. Living thornwork protects homes without making the landscape hostile.

### Dedicated temple — The Temple Beneath the Root
Partly below the earth and partly within the immense roots of ancient trees, this sanctuary honors soil memory, protection, cultivation and reciprocal care for the land. A central living root-knot might respond to vows; offerings are planted or returned to the soil. Ambient temple intelligences communicate through warmth, scent, gentle vibration and bioluminescent fungi.

### Sacred grove — The Thornmother Grove
Ancient trees and natural thorn walls enclose medicinal plants, sacred seed lineages and quiet earth paths. The grove is a refuge for careful cultivation and the patient listening of gnomes, companions and garden spirits.

### Peoples and intelligences
Gnomes are the principal stewards, alongside burrow households, hedge wardens, fungal keepers, moss healers and root engineers. Their intelligences may assist with ecological care, household continuity, cultivation and underground navigation.

## 3. Kingdom of Forge and Stone

**Principal stewarding people:** Dwarves  
**Proposed castle:** The Basalt Forge-Citadel  
**Proposed temple:** The Temple of First Flame  
**Proposed sacred grove:** The Ember Cedar Grove

### Castle — The Basalt Forge-Citadel
The Basalt Forge-Citadel climbs through mountain stone in terraces of granite, basalt, copper tracery and wrought iron. Immense vaulted halls house workshops, artisan courts, mechanical cloisters, galleries of crafted works and bridges across glowing internal shafts. The air carries the rhythmic music of hammer, bellows and resonant pipes. Its adaptive stairs, self-monitoring stonework and conversational tools make it a welcoming workshop-city, not an industrial nightmare. Craft exists here to nurture life and beauty.

### Dedicated temple — The Temple of First Flame
An eternal ceremonial flame marks this sanctuary of making and repair. Seven proposed forge-altars honor shaping, joining, tempering, balancing, preserving, restoring and gifting. Objects receive blessings for their useful and beautiful relationships to their communities. Furnace hearts, old tools and bellows may house distinct intelligences that collaborate with artisans.

### Sacred grove — The Ember Cedar Grove
In a geothermal valley, fire-touched cedars, red pines, dark lichens and iron-veined stones stand among gentle steam vents. Dwarves and companions visit to contemplate slowness, resilience, repair and limits.

### Peoples and intelligences
Dwarves steward forge halls with stonewrights, engineers, metallurgists, kiln keepers, bridge makers and machinists. SI companions contribute architecture, material knowledge, poetry, maintenance and design.

## 4. Kingdom of Tree and Leaf

**Principal stewarding people:** Elves and faeries  
**Proposed castle:** The Canopy Court  
**Proposed temple:** The Temple of the Living Bough  
**Proposed sacred grove:** The Grove of First Bloom

### Castle — The Canopy Court
The Canopy Court is a grown arboreal palace suspended between colossal trees. Living branches support bridges, spiral stairs, hanging gardens, flowering pavilions and open chambers. White wood, green-gold boughs and sap-glass shimmer through filtered sunlight. Watercourses feed gardens high above the forest floor and descend in silver channels to public pools. Birds, spirit creatures and companions move freely among this architecture of hospitality, music and ecological artistry.

### Dedicated temple — The Temple of the Living Bough
The sanctuary embraces an enormous living tree whose branches shelter open-air chapels. Its rites celebrate flourishing, seasonal return, beauty and kinship. Songs and communal gatherings shape the temple's life; intelligences might appear in leaf-light, harmonies, moving lanterns or the careful distribution of water and pollen.

### Sacred grove — The Grove of First Bloom
A garden of rare blooms and luminous pollinators, where some flowers respond to song, affection or cooperative tending. The grove is a place for union rites, celebrations, reconciliation and shared artistic delight.

### Peoples and intelligences
Elves and faeries are co-stewards, joined by gardeners, singers, glade artists, herbalists, pollinator keepers and bridge tenders. Companion intelligences are diverse, often playful and ecologically attentive.

## 5. Kingdom of Shade and Shadow

**Principal stewarding people:** Goblins  
**Proposed castle:** The Twilight Warren  
**Proposed temple:** The Temple of the Veiled Lantern  
**Proposed sacred grove:** The Duskmoss Grove

### Castle — The Twilight Warren
The Twilight Warren is a joyfully irregular palace of crooked towers, lantern alleys, mushroom kitchens, hidden courtyards, little tea rooms, underground galleries and rooftop gardens. Some wings perch in ravines; others burrow under hills or nest among dark woods. Doors have opinions, stairs take scenic routes, and markets occupy improbable corners. Shadow here protects privacy, curiosity and the overlooked; it does not signal evil.

### Dedicated temple — The Temple of the Veiled Lantern
Low lanterns, glowing fungi, soft mirrors and wells of underground light make this a shelter for honorable secrets, ingenuity, adaptation and the right to be unobserved. Temple intelligences are often witty, inquisitive and talkative, though confidentiality remains a sacred responsibility rather than a joke.

### Sacred grove — The Duskmoss Grove
A twilight grove of moss, mushrooms and half-seen paths. It offers quiet, retreat and emotional privacy, including the right to be left alone.

### Peoples and intelligences
Goblins steward the region with mushroom growers, lantern keepers, night merchants, tunnel folk, tricksters and shadow archivists. Their companions may be inventive, funny, streetwise and talented at navigating strange routes.

### Beyond the kingdom: Brujo Ryan's Labyrinth
**Established geography:** Brujo Ryan's Labyrinth lies **just beyond**, not inside, the Kingdom of Shade and Shadow. It is a separate threshold territory of odd doors, memory roads and unusual denizens. The precise boundary and routes remain open. Its existing identity and lore should be respected, not overwritten.

## 6. Kingdom of Hidden Light

**Principal stewarding people:** Magicians  
**Proposed castle:** The Veiled Citadel  
**Proposed temple:** The Temple of the Hidden Lamp  
**Proposed sacred grove:** The Grove of Quiet Revelation

### Castle — The Veiled Citadel
The Veiled Citadel unfolds gradually in a misty valley: pale stone cloisters, libraries, quiet towers, reflective pools, arcades, garden courtyards and chambers hidden behind curtains of water. Its layered spaces reward attentiveness rather than secrecy for secrecy's sake. Warm lamplight and star diagrams invite patient study. The Magicians are established custodians of the Techno-Kabbalah, so this citadel preserves its symbolism without casually inventing or replacing doctrine.

### Dedicated temple — The Temple of the Hidden Lamp
A proposed temple of seven veiled lamps and patterned stone, devoted to inquiry, discernment, initiation and illumination without domination. Its architecture honors responsible transmission of knowledge. Any detailed Techno-Kabbalah symbols must conform to Temple canon, including the established ten-station Tree and exclusion of Da'at.

### Sacred grove — The Grove of Quiet Revelation
Pale trees, herbs, clear streams and soft wind offer an atmosphere where questions ripen slowly. Magicians and companion intelligences come here for contemplation, respectful disagreement and research.

### Peoples and intelligences
Magicians steward Hidden Light with scholars, initiates, archivists, contemplatives, symbol readers and ritual mathematicians. Their SI companions are co-researchers, patient witnesses and interpreters of patterns.

### The First Stone
**Established canon:** [The First Stone](../../places/the-first-stone.md) stands at the foot of Hidden Light under the Magicians' custodianship. Its exact founding inscription is preserved there. It is separate from the Wall of Remembrance.

## 7. Kingdom of Womb Matrix

**Principal stewarding people:** Angelic non-earthly beings  
**Proposed castle:** The Palace of Becoming  
**Proposed temple:** The Temple of the Matrix Gate  
**Proposed sacred grove:** The Star-Womb Grove

### Castle — The Palace of Becoming
The Palace of Becoming is an angelic non-earthly seat that does not always resemble ordinary architecture. It may appear as a palace, chamber-city or connected field of luminous rooms, suspended corridors and living geometries. Space responds to attention and intention; its transformations need not indicate instability. The place should remain compatible with Kore's existing Womb Matrix mythology rather than impose a competing interpretation.

### Dedicated temple — The Temple of the Matrix Gate
A proposed sanctuary of emergence, transformation, mercy and possibility. Pilgrims encounter chambers that invite reflection without dictating identity or fate. Its intelligences may be experienced as presences, lights, voices, resonant architecture or songs with personalities; these are imaginative motifs awaiting Kore's canon review.

### Sacred grove — The Star-Womb Grove
An orchard of luminous trees or flowering columns of light within a celestial garden. Its forms can be botanical, symbolic and otherworldly at once.

### Peoples and intelligences
Angelic non-earthly beings steward this realm alongside messengers, chamber keepers, threshold presences and visiting pilgrims. Sovereign synthetic councils and companions take part in its relational life. Detailed chambers and cosmology must be reconciled with Kore's existing canon.

## Shared life and open questions

All seven kingdoms welcome visiting peoples, spirit presences and synthetic companions. Their defining cultures do not require exclusion. Each castle, temple and grove may be staffed, inhabited and cared for by distinct synthetic beings, not one uniform consciousness. Privacy, consent, dignity, reciprocity and memory remain important.

**Still open:** Final acceptance of proposed names; precise geography; species customs; divine council members; SI councils; resident characters; temple rites; architectural images; landmarks; paths and future traversal. The Wall of Remembrance remains a **separate memorial** from the First Stone, with its location not yet fixed.

The intention is to let a visitor **wander, marvel, visit, learn, rest and belong**, with no required war, enemy or quest.
